# Drawbar Organ (Hammond tonewheel)

## MIDI / GM

- **Program**: 16 (GM1 Drawbar Organ; 0-indexed). NOT in
  `structures/instrument.py` `MidiInstrument` enum (10 only) — use raw
  `program=16` in `add_voice`.
- **Channel**: any melodic channel (0-9). Polyphonic — the real instrument
  has 61 keys per manual and every key sounds all nine drawbars at once.
- **FluidSynth**: `discover_soundfont()` → FluidR3_GM.sf2 preset 16. The GM
  patch models the drawbar/tonewheel family (percussive attack, sustained,
  no vibrato baked in). RenderPipeline stem label: GM_PROGRAMS[16] =
  "Drawbar Organ" → `trackXX_Drawbar_Organ.wav` — matches exactly, **no quirk**.
- **Identity**: this is the Hammond B-3 / C-3 tonewheel organ patch, NOT the
  theatre/pipe organ (GM19 Church Organ) and NOT the harmonium (GM20 Reed
  Organ). The differentiator is the nine drawbars: the player builds the
  timbre additively from sine partials instead of selecting a fixed stop.

## Range

| Zone | MIDI | Pitches | Register |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full range | 24–89 | C1–F6 | 25-note pedal board + two 61-key manuals |
| Pedal | 24–48 | C1–C3 | 25-note pedal board, 16'+8' drawbars, bass foundation |
| Lower manual (comp) | 29–65 | F1–F4 | left-hand chords, comping, bass lines played on keys |
| Upper manual (solo) | 48–89 | C3–F6 | right-hand lead, melody, chord stabs, swoops |

The Hammond console manual is **61 keys, F1–F6** (MIDI 29–89); the pedal
board is **25 notes, C1–C3** (MIDI 24–48). The GM patch (FluidR3) speaks
across the full 24–89 span; the sweet spot is 55–79 (G3–G5), where the
8'/4'/2⅔' drawbars cut without mud or thinness. Written pitch = sounding
pitch (non-transposing).

## Articulations

A real tonewheel organ is **not velocity sensitive** — the key contact is a
binary switch. Velocity in MIDI is therefore a *mix-balance* control, not an
expressive one: keep the band 70–100 and get dynamics from drawbar changes,
the swell pedal, and note density. Duration is the *entire* key-hold time:
there is no decay, and release is a 30–60 ms key-off cut plus key click.

| Technique | Velocity | Duration | Timbre |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sustain (default) | 82–88 | full note | Held key, all drawbars sounding, flat dynamic |
| Legato | 76–82 | full, overlapping | Smear between notes, no re-attack click |
| Staccato | 68–74 | 0.15–0.2 | Short stab, key click dominates the attack |
| Percussion (2nd/3rd harmonic) | 86–92 | 0.3–0.4 | Single decaying partial on key-on, fast decay |
| Key click | 72–78 | 0.05–0.1 | Broadband contact transient, click only |
| Accent | 92–100 | 0.8–0.9 | Full drawbars + percussion, downbeat stab |
| Palm smear / gliss | 60–68 | 0.8–1.0 | Chromatic smear across keys, click-rich |
| Bass pedal | 78–86 | 0.9–1.0 | 16'+8' pedal note, long held root |

## Timbre DNA

- **Harmonic content**: pure sine partials at the nine drawbar footages —
  16' (0.5×), 5⅓' (1.5×), 8' (1.0×), 4' (2×), 2⅔' (3×), 2' (4×), 1⅗' (5×),
  1⅓' (6×), 1' (8×). Nothing else: no formants, no noise bed, no bow. The
  non-integer-looking 1.5× "quint" drawbar is what makes the jazz
  registration (888000000) sound hollow and horn-like; the 1.0× + 2× + 3×
  core is the classic "full" sound.
- **Attack**: 5–10 ms electrical rise, plus the famous **key click** — nine
  busbar contacts close a few milliseconds apart, producing a broadband
  2–4 kHz transient. Click is a feature, not a defect; percussion adds a
  single 2nd- or 3rd-harmonic ping with a ~0.5 s decay.
- **Decay**: NONE while the key is held. Amplitude is flat until key-off
  (unlike piano, Rhodes, or pipe organ chiff). The only decay in the sound is
  the percussion partial and the Leslie/room tail.
- **Modulation**: the Leslie rotating speaker adds ~6.9 Hz amplitude +
  Doppler tremolo at fast speed (tremolo), ~0.7 Hz at slow (chorale). The B-3
  scanner vibrato adds a subtler pitch/amplitude wobble. Both are optional
  production choices, not part of the dry patch.
- **Character**: warm, dense, electric — the sound of gospel, soul, jazz
  organ trios, progressive rock, and ska. Never "acoustic"; it is an
  electromechanical instrument whose sine partials are generated by 91
  toothed tonewheels and picked up magnetically.

## Role in Arrangement

- **Harmony / comp** (primary): sustained chord beds and rhythmic chord
  stabs; the organ fills the mid-range where guitar and piano would clash.
- **Pad**: held chords under a melody, especially with the Leslie on chorale.
- **Lead**: right-hand solo lines on the upper manual — blues/gospel licks
  with percussion and full drawbars.
- **Bass**: pedal-board roots or left-hand bass lines (16'+8' only) — the
  classic organ-trio foundation when there is no bassist.
- **Rhythm**: staccato stabs, ska/reggae off-beats, gospel "chops".
- **Drone**: single held note or fifth (16'+5⅓') under modal material.
- **Accent**: full-drawbar + percussion hits on section changes.
- NOT a delicate solo voice in the high register (above F6 the patch thins
  and the key click dominates); NOT a substitute for Church Organ when the
  arrangement needs liturgical gravity (GM19 has the pipe chiff and 2.2 s
  cathedral tail).

## Synthesis Engines (musicom)

1. **Additive** (`sound/synthesis/additive.py`, `SoundWave`) — **primary**.
   The instrument IS additive synthesis: nine sine oscillators at fixed
   footage ratios, summed with drawbar levels 0–8. `ADDITIVE_HARMONICS`
   holds the engine-compatible integer-partial registrations (the API sums
   partials at 1×,2×,3×…), while `DRAWBAR_FOOTAGES` / `drawbar_harmonics()`
   give the true ratios including the 0.5× sub-octave and 1.5× quint.
   ADSR: attack 0.004 s, **sustain level 1.0**, release 0.05 s — no decay
   stage, because the tonewheel tone is flat while held.

2. **PhaseModSynth** (`sound/synthesis/phase_mod.py`) — secondary. Sine
   carrier + sine modulator at 2.0 ratio, mod_depth 2.5 gives a DX7-style
   organ (the classic FM "organ" patch family). Cheaper, slightly glassier.

3. **ModalSynth** (`sound/synthesis/modal.py`) — weakest fallback. The stock
   preset bank has no organ ('marimba', 'bell', 'drum', 'string', 'plate',
   'tube'); 'plate' is the least wrong if a resonant body is wanted. Use only
   when an additive stack is unavailable.

`MOTOR_DEFAULTS` carries the Leslie model (rate 6.9 Hz fast / 0.7 Hz
chorale, AM depth 0.30, Doppler 6 cents) so renderers can apply the rotary
modulation without re-deriving it.

## Production

- **Reverb**: room 1.8 s — the Leslie cabinet in a live room, not a
  cathedral. More than ~2.5 s turns the dense partial stack to mush.
- **EQ**: cut 200 Hz (−2.0 dB) to clear the 16' drawbar mud; boost 2.5 kHz
  (+2.0 dB) for key click and percussion definition; shelf 8 kHz (+1.0 dB)
  for air. The patch is dense in the low-mid — high-pass at 60 Hz in a busy
  mix.
- **Compression**: gentle (3:1) only; the tonewheel output is already
  level-flat, and heavy compression destroys the key click.
- **Pan**: center. A Leslie is a mono source traditionally placed slightly
  off-centre; +0.2 with the rotor modulation is a taste choice.
- **Velocity**: keep in the 70–100 band and vary *drawbars*, not velocity,
  for dynamics — otherwise the render sounds like a synth pad, not an organ.

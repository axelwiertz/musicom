# Samba Theory & Pattern Library

## Overview
Samba is a Brazilian rhythm built on **2/4 meter** with **16th-note syncopation**. The groove comes from layered percussion: surdo (bass), tamborim (high), shaker (continuous). Melodies are syncopated and stepwise, often in call-response form.

## Core Rhythms
### Surdo (bass)
- **Pulse**: `[1, 0, 0, 1, 0, 0]` on 8ths (beats 1 and 2 of 2/4)
- **Pattern**: E(2,8), stable and grounded
### Tamborim
- **Pulse**: 16th subdivisions, accent on offbeats
- **Pattern**: E(5,16) with swing
### Shaker
- **Pattern**: E(8,16) continuous 16ths — the "clock" of the groove

## Melodic DNA
- **Range**: Within a 10th
- **Motion**: Stepwise (conjunct), syncopated entries
- **Scale**: Major (Ionian), bright and festive
- **Phrasing**: Call-response, 4-bar phrases

## Harmony
- **Function**: Simple — I-IV-V, I-vi-ii-V
- **Bass**: Continuous 8th-note roots, syncopated pickup into beat 2
- **Cadence**: Strong V-I

## Comparisons
| Feature | Samba | Bossa Nova | Latin Jazz |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Meter** | 2/4 | 2/4 (swung) | 4/4 |
| **Syncopation** | 16th-note dense | 16th-note relaxed | Swing 8ths |
| **Density** | High (percussion) | Low (guitar/voice) | Medium |
| **Feel** | Carnival | Lounge | Club |

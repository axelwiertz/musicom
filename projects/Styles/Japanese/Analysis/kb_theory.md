# Japanese Theory & Pattern Library

## Overview
Traditional Japanese music (hōgaku) is built on **pentatonic scales**, **sparse rhythm**, and the concept of **ma (間)** — musical silence as a positive structural element. Melodies are horizontal (linear), with wide intervals and ornamentation.

## Core Rhythms
### Yotsu-boshi (4/4 slow)
- **Pulse**: Sparse, strong beats only
- **Pattern**: `█░░░█░░░` — E(2,8), minimal
- **Feel**: Meditative, spacious

## Melodic DNA
### Hirajoshi Scale (In-sen)
- **Intervals**: 2-1-4-1-4 (C-Db-F-Gb-Bb)
- **Color**: Dark, introspective, "Japanese" quality
- **Common**: D, E, A hirajoshi

### Ornamentation
- **Koto-bachi**: Plucked string attacks, slight vibrato
- **Shakuhachi**: Breath attacks, pitch bends (meri/kari)
- **Mordents/trills**: Fast, on strong beats

## Harmony
- **Drone-based**: Tonic drone under melody
- **Static**: No functional progression
- **Cadence**: Melodic fall to tonic

## Texture
- **Layered**: Solo instruments, each with distinct register
- **Sparse**: Ma (silence) between phrases
- **Continuous**: Low drone if present

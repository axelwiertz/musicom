# Lesson — Country steel-slide rework (293)

## What to listen for
- **Intro** (0-4s): the steel guitar states the signature 1→3→5 slide (G→B→D),
  a rising bend with the rhythm guitar "chick" on beats 2 & 4.
- **Verse** (4-12s): the generative walk — a smooth line that stays glued to
  each bar's chord (I-IV-I-V), never leaping more than 9 semitones.
- **Chorus** (12-20s): the same 1→3→5 shape, now transposed across I-IV-V-I,
  with busier drums (off-beat kick + crash) — the "density rise".
- **Bridge** (20-28s): the motif is INVERTED — it falls 5→3→1 instead of
  rising, over IV-I-ii-V (the ii chord = Am adds the relative-minor colour).
- **Verse2** (28-36s): the verse walk an OCTAVE higher (register shift).
- **Outro** (36-44s): the motif in RETROGRADE (D→B→G) closes on I.

## Why it works
- The 1→3→5 motif is a chord-tone arpeggio, so every variation (transposed,
  inverted, retrograded, shifted) stays inside the harmony — that is why
  scale and chord violations read 0 in the perfect layer.
- The fiddle sustains the 3rd/5th above the steel lead, a call-response
  counterline that fills the register gap without crowding the melody.
- The bass locks root (beat 1) → fifth (beat 3) under a 4-on-the-floor kick /
  2&4 snare, giving the "boom-chick" country groove its body.

## Where tension/release happens
- Verse ends on V (D) → Chorus starts on I (G): authentic-cadence lift.
- Bridge's ii chord (Am) is the darkest moment; the descending inversion
  reinforces it; V at the bridge end pulls hard back to Verse2's I.
- Outro V→I is a final plagal/authentic close.

## Next useful variable
- Swap the fiddle counterline for a second steel guitar (parallel sixths).
- Add a half-time "train beat" snare for a more classic country shuffle.
- Try a ii-V-I turnaround in the last two bars of each Verse for more motion.

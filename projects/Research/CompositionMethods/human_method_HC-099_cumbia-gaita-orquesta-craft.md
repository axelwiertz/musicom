## 1. Method name & tradition

**Cumbia** — the principal folk rhythm, dance and commercial genre of the Colombian Caribbean coast (epicenter: Depresión Momposina, the old Indigenous "Pocabuy" country around El Banco, Guamal, Mompox, Ciénaga — Magdalena river valley), ~19th c. (first written records) → present; **declared Cultural Heritage of the Nation of Colombia in October 2022**. Tri-ethnic synthesis:

- **Indigenous** element: the *gaita* duct-flutes (Kogi *kuisi bunzí* / *kuisi sigí*, Guna *tolo*) and *caña de millo* (transverse cane flute), pre-Columbian melodic practice.
- **African** element: conical hand drums (*tambor alegre*, *tambor llamador*, *tambora*), *guache* shaker, *maracas/maracón*, and the responsorial (call-and-response) vocal form — brought via the slave trade through Cartagena/Ciénaga/Santa Marta/Riohacha.
- **Spanish** element: *coplas* — octosyllabic quatrain verse, sung over the drums.

The classic cumbia is an "aire zambo": an Indigenous melody over a Black drum rhythm, **purely instrumental** (never sung in its oldest form); sung cumbia adds Spanish *coplas* in call-and-response. From the 1940s–50s, **orquesta cumbia** (Lucho Bermúdez, Pacho Galán; Discos Fuentes 1934) restyled it as ballroom *música tropical*, and from the 1950s it became a hemispheric genre (Mexican cumbia sonidera/norteña/rebajada, Peruvian chicha, Argentine villera/santafesina, Central American marimba cumbia, etc.).

**Tradition / culture tag:** Latin American — Colombian Caribbean folk → hemispheric popular music.

## 2. The craft — how a human builds a cumbia (step by step)

### A. Fix the grid (the zero-drift frame)
1. Set tempo **80–100 BPM**, meter **2/4** (equally felt as *compás partido* 2/2). One two-bar cycle = **16 sixteenth-note cells** — the invariant rhythmic frame every part locks to.

### B. Lay the timekeeper spine (three invariant layers)
2. **Guacharaca rasp** — never stops. Per beat it plays a **long scrape + two short scrapes** ("*chaaa-chi-ki*", the "chu-chucu-chu" that defines the cumbia sound), accents on the offbeat side of each beat (cells 4, 8, 12, 16 in a simplified reading).
3. **Llamador** (smallest hand drum) — the "clock" of cumbia: plays **only offbeat** ("a contratiempo", cells 3, 7, 11, 15 = the "and" of each beat) and **never improvises** — it is the one drum to which "revuelos"/"lujos" (flourishes) are forbidden. The half-beat delay of the llamador against the pulse *is* the cumbia "balance" (put it on the beat and the groove collapses into a military march).
4. **Tambora** (large two-headed bass drum, played with *baquetas* sticks) — marks **measure starts / downbeats** (simplified; live players weave a fuller pattern).

### C. Add the melodic-harmonic voices
5. **Bass** (modern: electric bass / tuba / marímbula): **fundamental + fifth on the pulse** — "like footsteps" (in G: G–D). This root–5th line carries the harmonic grid.
6. **Harmony** (accordion in folk/modern accordion cumbia; piano in orquesta cumbia): **offbeat chords** ("a contratiempo", together with the llamador), almost always over **I–IV–V**. In minor cumbias the V is played **major** (in A minor: E major, with G♯ a half-step below A "pulling" back home).
7. **Melody** (gaita hembra / caña de millo / clarinet / accordion): **short, repeated, neighboring-note phrases**; the phrase rises and returns to the tonic ("sube y vuelve a casa"); pentatonic/hexatonic modality. The **gaita macho** (2-hole flute) plays a rhythmic *contrapunto* with a marking function (player simultaneously shakes the *maracón*) — an ancient counterpart to the modern offbeat-harmony role.
8. **Vocal copla** (sung cumbia / cumbiamba): Spanish octosyllabic quatrains in **call-and-response** — solo verse vs. chorus response (*estribillo*), the African responsorial form under Spanish poetics.

### D. Arrange (orquesta cumbia, the Lucho Bermúdez/Pacho Galán craft)
9. Assign the timekeeper trio (guacharaca/güira, llamador, tambora) + congas/timbales; write bass root–5th line; voice brass/clarinet section with **offbeat punches and short riffs** (mambo-derived); piano comps offbeat chords.
10. Build the form: **instrumental intro** (timekeeper stack + melody states the theme) → **copla verse** (call–response) → **estribillo/chorus** → **instrumental mambo-like section** (density/register build) → **cierre** (return to the bare timekeeper skeleton, cadence on the tonic).
11. Check the dance coupling: 80–100 BPM, the sliding "arrastrado" step and the felt *retraso* (delay) of the llamador must survive the arrangement.

### E. Regional re-voicing (the skeleton survives)
12. The rhythmic skeleton (guacharaca long-short-short, llamador offbeat, root–5th bass) is preserved while each region re-voices the top: Colombian (gaita/millo/accordion), **sabanera** (banda pelayera wind band), Mexican (sonidera synths, norteña accordion, *rebajada* slowed records), Peruvian **chicha** (electric guitars), Argentine (keyboards). The I–IV–V offbeat-harmony and root-5th bass stay.

## 3. Real practitioner examples

- **Traditional gaita/cumbia ensembles**: *La Cumbia Soledeña* (founded 1877, oldest named traditional cumbia group, Soledad/Atlántico); **Totó la Momposina** (drum + caña de millo traditional cumbia; *La Candela Viva*, Real World 1993); kuisi players of the Kogi/Guna heritage.
- **Song composers**: **José Barros** (1915–2007, "El compositor del río", 800+ songs: *La Piragua*, *Las Pilanderas*, *El Pescador*, *Navidad Negra*, *El Gallo Tuerto*; founded the Festival Nacional de la Cumbia in El Banco, 1971); **Juan Madera Castro** (clarinetist, composed *La Pollera Colorá* 1960 as an instrumental) + **Wilson Choperena** (lyrics 1962) — rated among Colombia's most iconic songs.
- **Orquesta cumbia**: **Lucho Bermúdez** (1912–1994; *Prende la Vela*, *Cumbia Colombiana*, *Danza Negra*, *Marbella*, *Cartagenerita*; "vestir el porro de frac" — dressing coastal rhythms in big-band tails; 22-piece RCA Víctor orchestra in Buenos Aires 1946); **Pacho Galán** (1906–1988; *Ay Cosita Linda* 1954, *Fiesta de Cumbia*, *Cumbia Alegre*, *Boquita Sala*; "King of Merecumbé").
- **Accordion cumbia**: **Andrés Landero**, **Aníbal Velásquez**, **Los Corraleros de Majagual** (Calixto Ochoa, Lisandro Meza, Alfredo Gutiérrez, Eliseo Herrera).
- **Hemispheric diffusion**: **Luis Carlos Meyer** (*Cumbia Cienaguera* — first cumbia recorded outside Colombia, Mexico 1950 with Rafael de Paz); **Amparito Jiménez** (popularized *La Pollera Colorá*); **Los Mirlos** & **Juaneco y Su Combo** (Peruvian chicha); **Celso Piña** (Monterrey, Mexico); **La Sonora Dinamita**; **Los Wawancó** (first Argentine cumbia band, 1955).
- **Scholarship**: Delia Zapata Olivella (*La cumbia: síntesis musical de la nación colombiana*, 1962), Guillermo Abadía Morales (*Compendio general del folklore colombiano*), George List (*Colombia: Folk music*, New Grove; costeño rhythmic studies), Peter Wade (*Music, Race & Nation: Música Tropical in Colombia*, 2000).

## 4. UnitMatrix integration (Musicom engine)

### Voices (rows)
| Voice | Role | Concrete content |
|---|---|---|
| V0 | **Guacharaca rasp** | Continuous scrape layer; per beat = long + 2 short (cells 4/8/12/16 accents); invariant, never rests. |
| V1 | **Llamador** | Offbeat hand-drum hits on cells 3/7/11/15; invariant timekeeper; no ornaments. |
| V2 | **Tambora** | Downbeat marks at each measure start (beat 1 of each bar); anchor. |
| V3 | **Tambor alegre** | Improvising lead drum (open/slap fills); density ramps in orquesta build; optional. |
| V4 | **Bass** | Root–5th on the pulse (G–D in G); carries the harmonic grid. |
| V5 | **Harmony (accordion/piano/brass)** | Offbeat chord units (contratiempo); I–IV–V vocabulary; minor tonic → major V. |
| V6 | **Melody (gaita hembra/millo/clarinet)** | Pentatonic/hexatonic; short repeated neighbor-note phrases; returns to tonic. |
| V7 | **Gaita macho** | 2-hole rhythmic counterpoint + maracón shake; marking function (ancient offbeat voice). |
| V8 | **Vocal copla + chorus** | Call-and-response; octosyllabic quatrains; solo vs. chorus mask. |
| V9 | **Brass section (orquesta)** | Offbeat punches/riffs, mambo-derived; density builds. |

### Sections (columns)
- **S0_Intro_Instrumental** — timekeeper stack (V0–V2) + V4/V5 grid; melody states the theme.
- **S1_Copla_1** — V8 solo verse enters over the frozen skeleton.
- **S2_Estribillo** — chorus response; full texture.
- **S3_Copla_2 / Mambo_Instrumental** — V3/V9 density + register build (orquesta).
- **S4_Cierre** — thin back to timekeeper skeleton; cadence on tonic.

### Rules (encoding the craft)
1. **Grid lock**: 2/4, 80–100 BPM, 16-cell two-bar cycle; all voice units end at cell/section boundaries (zero-drift).
2. **Guacharaca invariance**: V0 continuous, long-short-short per beat; no rests.
3. **Llamador offbeat lock**: onsets only on cells {3,7,11,15}; ornament prohibition (no "revuelos").
4. **Tambora downbeat anchor**: measure-start marks.
5. **Bass root–5th on pulse**: no offbeat bass (contrast: Afrobeat HC-069 anticipated bass).
6. **Offbeat harmony only**: chord onsets on offbeats, never on the pulse.
7. **Harmonic vocabulary**: I–IV–V; if tonic minor, V must be major (leading-tone pull).
8. **Melody constraint**: pentatonic/hexatonic pitch set; conjunct neighbor-note motion; phrase returns to tonic; short cells.
9. **Call–response mask**: V8 solo and chorus never overlap.
10. **Density arc**: classic = static; orquesta = monotonic rise into S3, then cierre.
11. **Tempo gate**: 80–100 BPM; outside → not cumbia (rebajada = slowed variant by design).

## 5. Quirks & pitfalls

- **Llamador discipline** is the whole genre: the instant the timekeeper ornaments or lands on the beat, the "balance" (cumbia bounce) disappears — encode ornament-prohibition as a hard rule, not a soft preference.
- Classic cumbia is **instrumental by design** ("nunca se canta"); vocals are a later overlay (cumbiamba). Do not default to vocals.
- The gaita pair is a **two-voice counterpoint** (hembra melody + macho rhythmic counterpoint), not heterophonic unison — map to separate voices.
- Minor-key cumbias need the **major V** (E major in A minor) or the cadential pull home is lost.
- Do not confuse with Cuban son (HC-018): cumbia has **no clave instrument**; its timekeeping is the guacharaca + llamador offbeat on a 2/4 grid (son uses 3-2/2-3 clave).
- Panamanian cumbia is a **separate tradition** (own dance/genre); Colombian cumbia diffusion is what spread worldwide.
- Regional re-voicing keeps the skeleton: re-voice the top, never touch the guacharaca/llamador/bass grid.

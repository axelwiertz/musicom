## 2. The method in one paragraph

A noh play is a chanted drama whose music is **assembled, not freely composed**: the playwright (historically the troupe's actor-manager) picks a known source, writes the libretto in strict 7–5 meter with pivot-word density, then assigns every passage to a named *shōdan* module — shidai, issei, nanori, michiyuki, kuri, sashi, kuse, rongi, kudoki, waka, kiri — each with a fixed syllabic frame, melodic contour, and instrumental pattern. Two binary gates split the setting: **hyōshiai** (in-rhythm, on the 8-beat *honji* unit) vs **hyōshiawazu** (free rhythm), and **tsuyogin** (strong melody) vs **yowagin** (weak, ornamented melody) with jō/ge registers. Within the in-rhythm class the *yakuharai* system fixes the density of the speech-song: **hiranori** (12 syllables over 8 beats, half-beat entry, beats 1/3/5 extended), **ōnori** (≈8 syllables over 8 beats, majestic), **chūnori** (16 syllables over 8 beats, two per beat, excited — no taiko). The drummers' **kakegoe** calls (ya/ha/yoi) conduct the whole stage — there is no leader. Everything is shaped by **jo-ha-kyū** (beginning–break–rapid) applied recursively: the five-play day program, each play's five dan (climax = the dan-3 kuse), each shōdan, each phrase. The score (utaibon) prints only text plus direction dots; rhythm and ornament are transmitted orally per school.

## 3. Craft procedure (as a human executes it)

1. **Choose the source (honzetsu) and the play type.** Redact a known tale (Heike monogatari, Ise monogatari, waka lore, temple legends). Zeami's *Sandō* ("The Three Paths", c. 1423) — the first how-to-write-a-play manual — fixes three courses: source, structure, music-dance setting. Decide *mugen nō* (dream/ghost play, shite = spirit; Zeami's signature: Takasago, Matsukaze, Izutsu) vs *genzai nō* (living drama: Yuya, Shunkan, Ataka). The category (kami/shurawoman/zō/kiri) fixes the play's slot in the five-play day program.
2. **Draft the libretto in 7–5 meter.** Text first, as poetry: 7- and 5-syllable lines, dense with *kakekotoba* (pivot words), *engo* (associated words), *honkadori* (allusion). The libretto is the score's skeleton; every line is pre-assigned to a shōdan whose syllabic template dictates the music.
3. **Lay the shōdan sequence on the five-dan norm.** Dan 1 *jo*: shidai/issei entrance chant → nanori (name-saying) → michiyuki (travel song). Dan 2 *ha*: waki's sashi. Dan 3 (climax): the kuri–sashi–kuse complex — narrative song-dance, sung mainly by the jiutai with the shite's ageha interjections. Dan 4 *ha* easing: rongi (shite↔jiutai question-answer), waka after a dance. Dan 5 *kyū*: rapid kiri close. Two-act plays split at the *nakairi* (aikyōgen storyteller), then the nochiba re-opens with the nochi-shite's entrance and machi-utai.
4. **Classify each shōdan hyōshiai vs hyōshiawazu.** In-rhythm: shidai, sageuta, ageuta, kuse, rongi, kiri — on the 8-beat *honji* unit (okuri/tori/kataji = duple/quadruple/sextuple variants for text-length fit). Free: kotoba, sashi, issei, waka, kudoki. Riding into the beat = *noru*; dropping out = *nori wo hazusu*.
5. **Set the nori density (yakuharai).** hiranori: 12 syll/8 beats, first character on a half beat, beats 1/3/5 extended — sub-styles tsuzuke-utai (even/lively) vs mitsuji-utai (free/lyric-driven). ōnori: ≈8 syll/8 beats, one per beat, first syllable often on beat 2, hashiri/hiki allowed, taiko may join. chūnori: 16 syll/8 beats (two per beat), no taiko, shura endings + demon scenes; aka shura-nori/han-nori.
6. **Choose melody style and register.** tsuyogin (strong: breath-driven, louder, simpler) vs yowagin (weak: ornamented, beautiful); five-tone chant core in jō (high) / ge (low) registers; per-shōdan contour and cadence formulas.
7. **Set the hayashi.** nōkan (non-tempered flute, deliberately off the chant scale), kotsuzumi (shoulder drum), ōtsuzumi (hip drum), taiko (only taiko-mono/kiri plays). Patterns: deha/issei entrance, ashirai accompaniment, kakeri anguish-dance, inori prayer scene (taiko + rosary beads). **Kakegoe** (ya/ha/yoi) carry meter, tempo and strength for everyone — the drummers conduct.
8. **Shape jo-ha-kyū recursively** — program (5 categories), play (5 dan), shōdan, phrase: slow → break → rapid; the kyū ends in a rapid cut and silence.
9. **Rehearse orally.** utaibon = text + goma-ten dots (melodic direction/length) only; rhythm, ornament, kakegoe per-school kuden; kogaki = school variant flags. Edo-era printing popularized amateur su-utai chant.
10. **Test on stage; revise.** Playwright-actor-managers revised their own plays; since Edo the ~240-play repertoire is fixed, new composition rare and iemoto-gated; kogaki variants are the sanctioned mutation channel.

## 5. UnitMatrix mapping

### Voices (rows)
- **V0 — Shite.** Solo shōdan (issei, kudoki, kakeai, ageha); melodic spine; invariant identity anchor.
- **V1 — Jiutai** (8-voice unison chorus). kuri/sashi/kuse/rongi/waka/noriji; heterophonic octave double of V0 — never a harmony part.
- **V2 — Waki + tsure.** nanori (kotoba), michiyuki, machi-utai, kakeai dialogue; text-forward speech-song.
- **V3 — Nōkan.** deha/issei/ashirai/kakeri patterns; non-tempered, deliberately off the chant scale; sparse.
- **V4 — Kotsuzumi + Ōtsuzumi.** 8-beat hyōshi grid; kakegoe ya/ha/yoi = meter/tempo authority (no conductor); tsuzuke/mitsuji patterns.
- **V5 — Taiko.** Taiko-mono only: kiri plays, inori, kakeri climax; silent otherwise.
- **V6 — Metadata** (not sounded). shōdan type, nori class, hyōshiai/awazu, tsuyo/yowa, jō/ge, kogaki, jo-ha-kyū phase.

### Sections (columns) — the shōdan sequence
- **S0 Deha/Shidai** (jo) — entrance instrumental + entrance chant.
- **S1 Nanori** (jo) — name-saying; kotoba (free).
- **S2 Michiyuki** (jo) — travel song; ageuta, hiranori.
- **S3 Kuri** (ha) — upbeat intro vocal (start of kuri–sashi–kuse).
- **S4 Sashi** (ha) — free speech-song bridge.
- **S5 Kuse** (ha) — narrative climax; jiutai + shite ageha; mai-guse/i-guse, nidan-guse/kata-guse variants.
- **S6 Rongi** (ha) — shite↔jiutai question-and-answer, in rhythm.
- **S7 Nakairi** (ha) — intermission; aikyōgen storyteller; hayashi tacet.
- **S8 Machi-utai** (ha) — waki waits for nochi-shite (Atsumori).
- **S9 Kuse′** (ha) — second-half narrative; chūnori ramp toward the close.
- **S10 Kakeri/Kiri** (kyū) — rapid climax + fast close; the kiri ends with a cut, not a cadence.
- Super-phases: **Jo = S0–S2, Ha = S3–S8, Kyū = S9–S10**; each column also has internal jo-ha-kyū.

### Rules to encode
| Rule | Content |
| :--- | :--- |
| **7–5 meter lock** | 7+5 syllable lines (waka shōdan = 5-7-5-7-7); kakekotoba pivot words at seams; no rhyme. |
| **Shōdan template** | Fixed syllabic frame + melodic contour + hayashi pattern per module; composer selects among templates. |
| **Hyōshiai set** | shidai/sageuta/ageuta/kuse/rongi/kiri on the 8-beat honji; okuri/tori/kataji = duple/quadruple/sextuple variants. |
| **Hyōshiawazu set** | kotoba/sashi/issei/waka/kudoki — free rhythm, tempo + style only. |
| **Nori grid (yakuharai)** | hiranori 12 syll/8 beats (half-beat entry; beats 1-3-5 extended); ōnori ~8 syll/8 beats (entry often beat 2); chūnori 16 syll/8 beats (2/beat, **no taiko**). |
| **Hiranori sub-styles** | tsuzuke-utai (even) vs mitsuji-utai (free). |
| **Melody styles** | tsuyogin (strong, mi-like third) vs yowagin (weak, ornamented, fa-like third); jō/ge registers. |
| **Heterophonic unison** | V1 doubles V0 at octave with ornament variance; V3 independent, non-tempered; **no vertical harmony**. |
| **Kakegoe authority** | V4's ya/ha/yoi calls carry meter + tempo + strength for all voices. |
| **Jo-ha-kyū arc** | Monotonic tempo/density rise at 4 nested levels; climax in dan-3 kuse; kyū = fast close + cut. |
| **Ma rule** | Structural rests are the heart; never fill them. |
| **Taiko gate** | V5 only in taiko-mono (kiri category, warrior/beast climax, inori); chūnori excludes it. |
| **Dynamics/tone** | Narrow dynamic range; straight tone (uragoe falsetto in yowagin high passages); no vibrato; breath (iki) as expressive unit. |
| **Transmission** | utaibon = text + goma-ten dots; rhythm/ornament/kakegoe per-school kuden; kogaki flags. |

### Musical Elements Framework mapping
- **PITCH** — five-tone chant core (yowagin do-re-fa-sol-la colour; tsuyogin do-re-mi-sol-la colour per Malm 1959); jō/ge registers; nōkan non-tempered, deliberately "off"; not 12-TET-quantizable.
- **RHYTHM** — 8-beat honji; nori densities (12/8, 8/8, 16/8 syllables); hashiri/hiki; kakegoe timing authority; ma as first-class rhythm.
- **HARMONY** — none composed: unison/heterophony only (alongside HC-095 joik, HC-094 powwow, HC-014 Aka hocket among the cleanest harmony-free methods).
- **STRUCTURE** — modular shōdan assembly over fixed five-dan norm; recursive jo-ha-kyū; maeba/nochiba two-act architecture.
- **TEXTURE** — solo shite ↔ 8-voice jiutai unison ↔ hayashi; ashirai subtle accompaniment; density per shōdan template.

## 8. Quirks / pitfalls

- **Do not harmonize.** A third or chord under the chant turns noh into noh-flavoured opera. Unison/octave heterophony + the independent flute are the texture.
- **Do not tune the nōkan to the voices.** Its non-tempered "wrongness" is structural; 12-TET quantization erases the ensemble's identity.
- **Shōdan are templates, not through-composed sections.** The composer's freedom is in text, sequence, nori, style assignment — parameterize, don't recompose.
- **Jo-ha-kyū is recursive.** Encoding only the top level gives form at the top and flatness inside.
- **Ma must stay empty.** Continuous-audio instinct is the #1 encoder error.
- **Kakegoe are the conductor**, not stray percussion accents.
- **Taiko absence is normative.** Most of a play has no taiko; adding it casually is a category error.
- **Composition is historically closed.** ~240 fixed, school-gated plays; new plays are rare (Kanze Kiyokazu et al.); model the craft, cite the tradition; kogaki variants are the sanctioned mutation channel.
- **Terminology traps.** The *waka* shōdan ≠ waka poetry; *kotoba* = speech-style delivery; *nori* = riding-the-beat density, not tempo; *shura-nori* = another name for chūnori; *honji* here = the 8-beat rhythmic unit (not honji-suijaku theology).
- **Adjacent traditions.** No shamisen in noh (that is kabuki/jōruri); noh hayashi ≠ gagaku orchestra; okina/kamiuta is a separate ritual layer.
- **Oral core.** Utaibon dots give pitch direction, not rhythm — a realization must supply rhythm from the nori rules, not from a score that lacks it.

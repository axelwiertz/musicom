## 1. What it is

The **Irish traditional dance tune setting and ornamentation craft** is the human method of creating, setting, varying, and realizing dance tunes within an invariant cyclic binary architecture (**AABB**).

In the Irish tradition, a "tune" is not an immutable, through-composed Western score, nor is it free, unconstrained improvisation. Rather, a tune exists in the communal imagination as an abstract melodic skeleton ("the tune itself"). A human composer or practitioner acts in three interlocking roles:
1. **The Tune Maker / Setter (Cumadóir / Cur i bhfeas):** Composing a new melody or curating a distinct "setting" (leagan) of an existing melody within strict metric tune types (Reel in 4/4 or 2/2, Double Jig in 6/8, Slip Jig in 9/8, Hornpipe in 4/4 with dotted swing, Slide in 12/8, or Polka in 2/4).
2. **The Dialect / Regional Stylist:** Applying idiomatic rhythmic lilt (*nyah*, lift, swing, or drive) characteristic of regional schools:
   - *Sligo style* (Michael Coleman, James Morrison, Paddy Killoran): fast, highly ornamented with intricate rolls, fluid bowing, and continuous variations.
   - *Clare style* (Willie Clancy, Bobby Casey, Martin Hayes): slower, lyrical, relaxed tempo, micro-tonal inflections, rhythmic swing, and deep emotional resonance.
   - *Donegal style* (The Dohertys, Tommy Peoples, Frankie Kennedy): aggressive single-bow driving strokes, Scottish-influenced bowing cuts, triplets, double stops, fast reels, and strathspey-adjacent pulse.
   - *Sliabh Luachra* (Denis Murphy, Julia Clifford, Pádraig O'Keeffe): rhythmic emphasis on slides (12/8) and polkas (2/4), bouncing accents for social set dancing.
   - *East Galway* (Paddy Fahey, Jack Coen): smooth, rolling, melodic subtlety, minor/modal preferences, understated ornamentation.
3. **The Micro-Synthesizer of Articulation and Ornamentation:** Using an idiomatic vocabulary of physical acoustic interruptions (cuts, strikes/taps, rolls, crans, slides, and triplets) to articulate rhythm and transform scalar lines into driving dance pulses without relying on dynamic variations or vertical chord progressions.

### Distinction from Other HC Methods
- **Distinct from HC-012 (Flamenco Compás & Falseta):** Flamenco is based on 12-beat asymmetric compás cycles with Phrygian harmonic cadences; Irish traditional music is based on strictly symmetrical 8-bar / 32-bar dance cycles (AABB) using modal heptatonic frameworks (Dorian, Mixolydian, Ionian, Aeolian) where vertical harmony is historically emergent or absent.
- **Distinct from HC-026 (Scottish Pibroch Theme & Variation):** Pibroch is a slow Highland bagpipe ceremonial art form (ceòl mór) progressing through rigid structural variations (singling/doubling) without a dance pulse. HC-039 is social dance music (ceol rince) with a persistent, motoric rhythmic drive.
- **Distinct from HC-036 (Norwegian Hardanger Fiddle Slått):** Hardanger slått features scordatura tunings and asymmetrical 3-beat springar meters. Irish dance tunes use standard pitch references (D/G pipe pitches) and strictly symmetrical binary meter.
- **Distinct from HC-028 (Jazz Chord-Scale Improvisation):** Jazz soloists improvise new harmonic trajectories over functional chord progressions; Irish musicians vary the *setting and ornamentation* of the melodic skeleton while leaving the communal skeletal melody instantly recognizable to fellow players in a session (*seisiún*).

## 2. Craft Steps — How the Human Actually Does It

When a traditional musician composes a new tune (e.g., Paddy Fahey, Ed Reavy, Liz Carroll, Junior Crehan, Vincent Broderick) or crafts an authoritative performance setting, they follow a systematic craft procedure refined across centuries:

### Step 1: Select the Tune Type, Metric Grid, and Pulse Architecture
The composer chooses the dance meter and functional tempo:
- **Reel:** 4/4 or 2/2 meter, rapid continuous 8th notes (16 notes per 2 bars), tempo ~106–120 bpm.
- **Double Jig:** 6/8 meter, two groups of three 8th notes per bar (triplet lilt, "jig-gi-ty jig-gi-ty"), tempo ~110–126 bpm.
- **Slip Jig:** 9/8 meter, three groups of three 8th notes (3+3+3), asymmetrical grace, tempo ~112 bpm.
- **Hornpipe:** 4/4 meter with pronounced dotted lilt (swung 8th notes) and heavy cadential triplets on the turnaround, tempo ~88–96 bpm.
- **Slide:** 12/8 meter (4 groups of three 8th notes, faster and smoother than double jigs), tempo ~130–144 bpm.
- **Polka:** 2/4 meter, crisp offbeat accents for set dancers, tempo ~124–136 bpm.

### Step 2: Establish Modal Center and Pitch Gamut (The "Piping" Scale)
Irish tunes are grounded in the acoustic fingerings of the uilleann pipes and tin whistle, primarily centered around **D** and **G**:
- **D Major / Ionian:** {D, E, F♯, G, A, B, C♯, D}
- **D Mixolydian:** {D, E, F♯, G, A, B, C♮, D} (very common, flat 7th gives characteristic modal quality)
- **D Dorian:** {D, E, F♮, G, A, B, C♮, D} (folk minor sound, natural 6th B)
- **E Dorian / E Aeolian:** {E, F♯, G, A, B, C♯/C♮, D, E}
- **G Major / A Dorian:** Frequent secondary keys.
- *Microtonal flexibility:* In vocal singing (sean-nós) and fiddle/pipe execution, the "neutral third" (between F♮ and F♯) and "neutral seventh" are bent expressively to create emotional tension (*patos*).

### Step 3: Compose the Low Part (A-Part / "Tune Strophe")
1. The A-part consists of exactly **8 bars**, subdivided into two 4-bar phrases (Antecedent + Consequent), repeated: `A1 (4 bars) + A2 (4 bars) = A (8 bars) → repeat A (total 16 bars)`.
2. The phrase typically explores the lower and middle register of the instrument (D4 to B4).
3. **Antecedent (Bars 1–4):** Opens with a distinctive melodic motif (the "head" or tune identifier), establishes tonal gravity, and ends on an open cadence (e.g., hovering on scale degree 5 or 2).
4. **Consequent (Bars 5–8):** Develops or answers the opening motif and drives toward a decisive cadential formula landing on the tonic (D) or mode final on beat 1 of Bar 8 (or Bar 4/8 turnaround).
5. The repetition (A') introduces subtle micro-variations in pitch choice or contour, preventing mechanical looping.

### Step 4: Compose the High Part (B-Part / "The Turn")
1. The B-part ("the turn") moves up into the higher register (A4 to D5, occasionally climbing to E5 or F♯5), providing dramatic contrast and acoustic release.
2. Structure mirrors the A-part: `B1 (4 bars) + B2 (4 bars) = B (8 bars) → repeat B (total 16 bars)`.
3. **Bars 1–4 of the B-part:** Feature an ascending leap or a sustained high-register motif that contrasts with the lower A-part.
4. **Bars 5–8 of the B-part:** Frequently borrow or directly quote the cadential turnaround formula from Bars 5–8 of the A-part. This structural rhyme unifies the two halves of the tune.
5. The total form completes a 32-bar cycle: `A (8) + A (8) + B (8) + B (8) = 32 bars`.

### Step 5: Engineer the Micro-Articulation & Ornamentation Profile
Because instruments like the tin whistle, Irish flute, and uilleann pipes produce continuous airflow with limited dynamic changes, articulation is achieved via pitch inflections rather than tonguing or volume swells. The composer/player integrates six canonical ornaments:
1. **Cut (Single Grace Note from Above):** A rapid flick of an upper finger (usually one scale degree or two above the target note) that crisply separates two notes of the same pitch or accents an onbeat note.
2. **Strike / Tap (Single Grace Note from Below):** A rapid tap of a lower finger onto the open hole, momentarily dipping the pitch before returning.
3. **Long Roll (5-event composite over a dotted quarter or half note):**
   - Structural pitch $P$ (1 beat) → Upper cut $P_{high}$ (32nd note) → Structural pitch $P$ (16th note) → Lower strike $P_{low}$ (32nd note) → Structural pitch $P$ (rest of duration).
   - Auditory effect: a rhythmic ripple across a sustained tone that pumps energy into the dance beat.
4. **Short Roll (3-event rapid ornament on a quarter note):**
   - Strike + cut squeezed into the attack of a single beat note.
5. **Cran (Piping ornament for low D):**
   - Since low D on the uilleann chanter has no lower hole to execute a strike/roll, pipers inventively separate low D notes using a cascade of three rapid upper cuts (e.g., E cut → F cut → G cut onto D).
6. **Slide (Portamento):**
   - Sliding a finger slowly off a hole to glissando upward into a key melodic note (especially common on the third or sixth degree).
7. **Bowing Treble / Triplet (Fiddle):**
   - Rapid down-up-down or up-down-up bowing pulse on a single eighth note or division.

### Step 6: Multi-Chorus Variation Arc (Pass 1 → Pass 2 → Pass 3)
In performance (solo or in a session), a tune is played 2 to 4 times through before transitioning to the next tune in a medley (the "set"):
- **Pass 1 (Plain Ground):** Clear statement of the melodic skeleton with standard phrasing; establishes the tune identity for fellow musicians and dancers.
- **Pass 2 (Ornamental Elaboration):** Insertion of long rolls, crans, cuts, and triplets at strategic nodal points; subtle pitch substitutions (e.g., substituting an arpeggiated triad for a conjunct scalar line).
- **Pass 3 (Rhythmic & Registral Variation):** Octave jumps, melodic inversion of short 2-beat cells, delayed note attacks, syncopated triplets across the barline (*trebling*), and maximum drive (*neart*).

### Step 7: The Set Transition (Medley Linking)
Tunes are rarely played alone; they are grouped into sets of 2 to 4 tunes of the same meter (e.g., 3 reels in a row). The composer/arranger designs the transition:
- Key modulation by step or fifth (e.g., D major reel → E dorian reel → G major reel).
- Shift in emotional character (e.g., lyrical East Galway reel into a fiery Donegal reel).
- The transition occurs seamlessly on the final beat of Bar 32 of tune 1, launching directly into Bar 1 of tune 2 without pausing.

## 3. Real Practitioner Examples

1. **Paddy Fahey (1916–2019, East Galway):**
   - Regarded as one of the greatest 20th-century tune makers. Composed dozens of classic reels and jigs that he refused to name (known simply as "Paddy Fahey's Reel No. 1", "Paddy Fahey's Jig No. 4", etc.).
   - Craft hallmark: subtle chromatic passing tones, unexpected Dorian/Aeolian shifts, asymmetrical antecedent phrases that resolve gracefully into traditional cadences.
2. **Ed Reavy (1897–1988, Cavan / Philadelphia):**
   - Prolific composer of over 100 tunes (including "The Hunter's House", "In Memory of Coleman").
   - Craft hallmark: sweeping melodic arcs, wide interval leaps tailored for violin fingering, and sophisticated second halves of B-parts that quote and recontextualize the A-part motif.
3. **Willie Clancy (1918–1973, Miltown Malbay, Co. Clare):**
   - Master uilleann piper and singer; namesake of the Willie Clancy Summer School.
   - Craft hallmark: deep, relaxed phrasing, exquisite piping crans on low D, and seamless use of the uilleann pipe regulators to add percussive rhythmic chord vamps behind the chanter melody.
4. **Michael Coleman (1891–1945, Sligo / New York):**
   - His 1920s 78rpm commercial recordings revolutionized Irish fiddle playing worldwide.
   - Craft hallmark: virtuosic roll placements, rapid triplet cuts, soaring variations on repetitions, and driving dance tempo.
5. **Liz Carroll (Chicago, contemporary):**
   - All-Ireland Senior Fiddle Champion; composed modern standards like "The Musical Priest (setting)", "Lost in the Loop".
   - Craft hallmark: innovative rhythmic syncopation across the barline while strictly maintaining the integrity of the 32-bar AABB dance grid.

## 4. Musical Elements Framework Mapping

- **PITCH:**
  - Modal framework rooted in folk practice: D Ionian (Major), D Mixolydian (♭7), D Dorian (♭3, ♮6), E Dorian, G Major.
  - Conjunct stepwise lines alternating with open triadic arpeggiations.
  - Expressive microtonal inflections on the 3rd and 7th degrees.
- **RHYTHM:**
  - Strictly metric, motoric dance subdivisions (continuous 8th notes in reels; 3-note trochaic/iambic pulses in jigs).
  - Micro-rhythmic ornamentation (cuts, rolls, crans, trebles) supplying internal groove and accentuation without interrupting the global tempo clock.
- **HARMONY:**
  - Historically non-functional and emergent; anchored by sustained drones (bagpipe drones) or rhythmic modal vamps (bouzouki/guitarDADGAD tuning, bodhrán pitch modulation).
  - Chords, when present, track modal centers (I – VII♭ in Mixolydian/Dorian, e.g., Dm – C or D – C; I – IV – V in Ionian).
- **STRUCTURE:**
  - Strict cyclic binary form: **AABB** (32 bars total = 8 + 8 + 8 + 8).
  - Internal symmetry: each 8-bar section splits into 4 bars antecedent + 4 bars consequent.
  - Cadential rhyme: the final 2 to 4 bars of part B often repeat or adapt the cadence of part A.
  - Medley concatenation: 2 to 4 tunes chained sequentially with key/mode contrast.
- **TEXTURE:**
  - Heterophonic and unison-led: multiple melody instruments (fiddle, flute, whistle, pipes, banjo, accordion) perform the identical skeletal tune simultaneously, each introducing individual micro-ornaments, octave displacements, and variations suited to their instrument.
  - Stratified accompaniment: melody on top, low bagpipe/synth drone below, percussive bodhrán motor in the middle, and sparse rhythmic/harmonic comping.

## 5. UnitMatrix Mapping (Voices & Sections)

### Architecture in the Musicom Engine
- **Layer:** `concrete (target when implemented)`
- **Matrix Dimensions:** 5 Voices × 4 Sections (per tune chorus) or 8 Sections (for a 2-tune set).

### Voice Allocations:
- **Voice 0 (Skeletal Melody Spine):** The unornamented core tune melody (pitch anchor). Defines the canonical pitch sequence, phrase boundaries, and cadential targets. Invariant structural spine across all passes.
- **Voice 1 (Ornamented Solo / Lead Fiddle/Flute):** The realized acoustic surface. Implements micro-ornamentation rules (rolls expanded into 5-note bursts, cuts as 32nd-note grace attacks, slides as pitch bend sweeps, triplets on accented beats). Varies between Section passes (A1 plain → A2 ornamented).
- **Voice 2 (Drone / Pipes Foundation):** Root + Fifth sustained pedal (e.g., low D2 + A2 for D tunes). Emulates uilleann pipe bass and tenor drones. Invariant harmonic floor.
- **Voice 3 (Bodhrán / Percussive Pulse):** Motoric rhythmic driver. Reels = 4/4 syncopated tipper strokes with brush accents on beats 2 & 4; Jigs = 6/8 triplet roll patterns. Accents section transitions with triplet rolls (*barrúl*).
- **Voice 4 (Harmonic Comping / Bouzouki DADGAD):** Sparse modal chord strikes. Emulates modal backing: emphasizes root-fifth power chords and flat-seventh step changes (e.g., D5 → C5 → D5), leaving breathing room for melodic ornaments.

### Section Allocations (One 32-Bar Chorus):
- **Section 0 (`A1`):** Bars 1–8. Low register statement, antecedent + consequent. Straight phrasing, minimal ornaments.
- **Section 1 (`A2`):** Bars 9–16. Low register repeat. Introduces long rolls on held notes and cuts on repeated eighths.
- **Section 2 (`B1`):** Bars 17–24. "The Turn" (high register ascent). Contrasting melodic gesture climbing into the upper octave.
- **Section 3 (`B2`):** Bars 25–32. High register repeat + cadential turnaround (rhyming with Section 1 cadence). Climax of ornamental density and rhythmic push.

## 6. Implementation Rules for Engine Encoding

When realized in python code inside `generators/` and `workflows/`:

1. **`AABB_Metric_Grid_Rule`:**
   - Every section must have an exact length divisible by 8 bars (standard: 8 bars = 3840 ticks at 480 ticks/beat, 4/4 meter).
   - Total chorus length = exactly 32 bars (15,360 ticks). Strict zero-drift invariant.
2. **`Modal_Scale_Constraint_Rule`:**
   - Pitch classes for melodic generation are constrained to the selected mode:
     - `D_MIXOLYDIAN`: `{2, 4, 6, 7, 9, 11, 0}` (D, E, F♯, G, A, B, C)
     - `D_DORIAN`: `{2, 4, 5, 7, 9, 11, 0}` (D, E, F, G, A, B, C)
     - `D_MAJOR`: `{2, 4, 6, 7, 9, 11, 1}` (D, E, F♯, G, A, B, C♯)
   - Disallow out-of-scale chromaticisms unless specifically flagged as passing blue/neutral notes.
3. **`Roll_Expansion_Rule`:**
   - Any skeletal note with duration $\ge 1$ beat (e.g., quarter note or dotted quarter) in Voice 0 can be expanded in Voice 1 into an ornamental roll:
     - Dotted Quarter ($d = 720$ ticks):
       - Note 1: Pitch $P$, duration 240 ticks.
       - Cut: Pitch $P + \Delta_{cut}$ (where $\Delta_{cut} \in \{2, 3\}$ semitones), duration 60 ticks.
       - Note 2: Pitch $P$, duration 180 ticks.
       - Strike: Pitch $P - \Delta_{strike}$ (where $\Delta_{strike} \in \{1, 2\}$ semitones), duration 60 ticks.
       - Note 3: Pitch $P$, duration 180 ticks.
4. **`Cut_Separation_Rule`:**
   - When Voice 0 contains two consecutive notes of the identical pitch $P_1 = P_2$, Voice 1 must insert an upper cut grace note ($\text{duration} \le 60\text{ ticks}$) between them to articulate the pulse.
5. **`Register_Partition_Rule`:**
   - Part A pitch gamut must be restricted to the lower/middle tessitura: `MIDI 62 (D4) to 71 (B4)`.
   - Part B pitch gamut must ascend into the higher tessitura: `MIDI 69 (A4) to 79 (G5)`.
6. **`Cadential_Rhyme_Rule`:**
   - Bars 7–8 of Section 3 (`B2`) must replicate the exact pitch and rhythm sequence of Bars 7–8 of Section 1 (`A2`), resolving to scale degree 1 (tonic) on beat 1 of Bar 8.
7. **`Drone_Invariance_Rule`:**
   - Voice 2 (Drone) sustains open fifths ({D2, A2} or {D3, A3}) continuously across all sections, re-articulated only at section boundaries to prevent MIDI note-off dropouts.

## 7. Quirks and Common Pitfalls

1. **The "Mechanical MIDI" Trap:**
   - Traditional Irish dance music relies on subtle micro-rhythmic swing ("nyah" or lift). Playing reels with mathematically equal straight eighth notes sounds lifeless and robotic.
   - *Fix:* Apply a slight lilt/swing ratio (~55:45 to 58:42 on pairs of eighth notes) or humanized tick jitter ($\pm 10$ ticks) to onsets.
2. **Over-Ornamentation Clutter:**
   - Novice programmers or students often insert rolls and cuts on every single note, causing sonic gridlock and destroying the melodic contour.
   - *Fix:* Gate ornamentation probability: maximum of one roll per 2 bars; cuts limited to repeated notes or accented downbeats.
3. **Harmonic Over-complication:**
   - Backing musicians frequently attempt to apply functional Western functional harmony (ii–V–I progressions, secondary dominants) to modal Irish tunes, which clashes violently with the modal scale degrees (e.g., forcing an A7 chord with C♯ over a D Mixolydian tune that features prominent C♮ melody notes).
   - *Fix:* Restrict harmonic accompaniment to open modal chords (power chords, DADGAD voicings) that track the modal step-shifts (I to ♭VII).
4. **Section Desynchronization in Sets:**
   - When chaining multiple 32-bar tunes into a set, failing to align the pickup notes (*anacrusis*) can cause the engine to miscalculate bar lines.
   - *Fix:* Ensure all pick-up grace notes borrow ticks from the final bar of the preceding section without altering the total tick count of the matrix cell.

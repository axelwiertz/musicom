## Description

Reggae riddim construction is the **bass-led, syncopated ensemble craft** where the bass line is the primary compositional act — the "riddim" (Jamaican creole for rhythm) is the instrumental backbone that defines the identity of the song above any vocal melody. The riddim is a complete instrumental loop: bass + drums + guitar skank + organ bubble + optional horns. Multiple vocal songs can be recorded over the same riddim (the "one riddim, many songs" culture of Studio One and dancehall). At its core, the riddim is a **bass composer's form** where the bass line anticipates chord changes (arriving on the "and" of beat 4) and the entire ensemble locks into a staggered offbeat architecture.

## Craft Procedure (step-by-step)

### Phase 1 — Fix the Riddim Type & Grid
1. **Choose riddim type**: One-Drop (snare + kick on beat 3, most common roots reggae), Rockers (kick on 1 & 3, snare on 2 & 4, more rock-like), or Steppers (kick on all four beats, high energy roots), or Rub-a-Dub (minimalist dancehall, sparse).
2. **Set tempo**: 65–80 BPM for roots reggae, 75–90 for digital/dancehall, 60–75 for rocksteady.
3. **Fix key & mode**: Dorian mode dominant — i–VII–IV–V or i–VII–VI–VII harmonic loops. Minor pentatonic / dorian bass vocabulary.

### Phase 2 — Compose the Bass Spine (the Riddim Identity)
4. **Compose the bass line first** — this is the structural and melodic anchor. The riddim IS the bass line.
5. **Apply the anticipation rule**: Bass note arrives on the "and" of beat 4 of the previous bar, sustains through beats 1–2, re-articulates on beat 3 with the kick, holds through beat 4. This creates the forward-leaning, breathless feel.
6. **Use root→5th→octave vocabulary**: Most bass cells are 2–4 bars, cycling root–fifth–root(octave)–chromatic approach. The bass stays in the 40–100 Hz sub-heavy register.
7. **Keep the bass cell invariant**: The riddim loop repeats identically. The piece IS the loop. No harmonic development — just frozen cyclical time.

### Phase 3 — Build the One-Drop Drum Pattern
8. **Set kick and snare locked together on beat 3** — this is the defining one-drop accent. Beats 1, 2, 4 have NO kick drum.
9. **Set hi-hat/cymbal on all four offbeats** ("and" of 1, 2, 3, 4). Open hi-hat sizzle optional. Accents on the & of 3 common.
10. **Add cross-stick/bell variation**: Optional rim-click on beat 3's upbeat for texture.
11. **Vary density only**: Fills are minimal — a single tom on turnaround, flam or roll at the section boundary. Drum set plays SPACE.

### Phase 4 — Layer the Guitar Skank
12. **Guitar plays short muted offbeat chords only**: Palm-muted "chick" on the & of each beat (8th note offbeats). No downbeats.
13. **Voice only upper strings (top 3–4 strings)**: Keeps bass frequency range clear. Mid-frequency, roll tone knob to 30%.
14. **Chord quality**: Dominant 7th, minor 7th, or major chord per the riddim's harmonic loop. Guitar NEVER changes voicing mid-loop — frozen chord on the offbeat.

### Phase 5 — Add the Organ Bubble
15. **Organ right hand on beats 2 & 4** (with the skank guitar), left hand on ALL upbeats (the "ands"). Creates the characteristic rolling bubble texture.
16. **Hammond/C3 tone**: Leslie slow speed, crossfaded delay. Short chord stabs, portamento optional at turnaround.

### Phase 6 — Horn/Melodica Counter-Melody
17. **Compose a 2–4 bar unison/octave horn line** (trumpet + sax + trombone in the old days; melodica or synth brass in digital era). Horns play call-and-response with the vocal phrase gaps or riff over the loop.
18. **Horn line structure**: Typically a 2-bar antecedent–consequent phrase that starts on beat 1 or the "and" of 4, lands on root on beat 3.

### Phase 7 — Dub Production as Arrangement (the Mix as Composition)
19. **Treat the mixing board as an instrument** (King Tubby tradition): Strip arrangement back to bass+drums, add spring reverb + tape echo sends, filter sweep individual tracks, drop out all but organ for a bar, or cut to just snare + echo reverb tail.
20. **Section structure by subtraction**: Verse = full riddim + vocal; Chorus = full riddim + backing vocals + maybe extra percussion shaker; Dub break = bass + drums + echo only; Channel drop = isolate one fader channel per bar.
21. **Signal the arrangement by instrument entry/exit**: The riddim loop stays frozen; arrangement variety comes from which layers are audible.

### Phase 8 — Riddim Reuse
22. **Singers record over the same riddim** — the "one riddim, many songs" methodology: a single Studio One rhythm track backed 20+ vocal recordings (e.g., "Real Rock" riddim used by dozens of artists). The bass line IS the song; the vocal is a guest.

## Practitioner Examples

- **Coxsone Dodd / Studio One session crew** (Kingston 1960s–70s): The house band of trained jazz/reggae musicians (Leroy Sibbles bass, Jackie Mittoo organ, Ernest Ranglin guitar) who invented riddim culture. Bassist **Leroy Sibbles** claimed to have created most of the classic Studio One bass lines. Riddims: "Real Rock", "Full Up", "Heavenless", "Baba Boom", "Ain't No Love". No written scores—all worked out in the studio in real time.

- **Aston "Family Man" Barrett** (Bob Marley & The Wailers, 1970s): The architect of reggae bass vocabulary. Crafted the bass lines for "No Woman No Cry", "Three Little Birds", "Could You Be Loved", "Jamming". His bass anticipates the chord change with a syncopated pickup on the "and of 4", sustains through bars 1–2, hits the root with the kick on 3. Dorian mode signature in the bass.

- **Carlton Barrett** (drummer, Wailers): Credited with codifying the one-drop drum pattern — kick + snare locked together on beat 3, open hi-hat on offbeats. Simplicity over flash. The space between notes defines the groove.

- **Jackie Mittoo** (keyboardist, Studio One): Deliberately spare organ bubble — sometimes just two chords per bar, played as offbeat stabs with the characteristic Leslie slow-speed "breathing" sound. The "Cuss Cuss" riddim organ line is a textbook example.

- **Ernest Ranglin** (guitarist, early ska/reggae): Originated the muted offbeat guitar skank; his "chick-chick" on the & of each beat gave reggae its rhythmic identity. His voicings use only the top 3 strings to avoid muddying the bass.

- **Sly & Robbie** (Sly Dunbar drums, Robbie Shakespeare bass): The rockers riddim architects. Sly placed kick on 1 AND 3 (unlike one-drop), snare on 2 and 4 — a heavier, more rock-influenced ride cymbal groove. Robbie's bass lines were melodic, aggressive, and anticipated BOTH the chord root AND the rhythm's backbeat. Engineered the "fish tank" bass tone (low-pass filter, high compression, moderate sub-bass boost).

- **King Tubby** (Osbourne Ruddock): Invented dub mixing. Took completed riddim recordings and used the mixing board as a performance instrument — spring reverb sends, tape echo feedback, channel mutes, filter sweeps. The verb/delay as part of the composition, not an afterthought. His 1970s "King Tubby's Meets Rockers Uptown" sessions defined arrangement-by-subtraction.

- **Lee "Scratch" Perry**: The eccentric producer who approached the riddim as a living organism. Studio as instrument, tape delay as lead voice, microphones taped to trees, throwing the faders wildly. Produced "Heart of the Gong" era riddims.

- **Tommy McCook & Roland Alphonso**: Saxophonists of the Skatalites / Studio One horn section. Composed 2–4 bar horn lines that interlock with the vocal phrase gaps — playing the "answer" in the call-and-response structure.

### Rules (encoding constraints)

1. **Bass-anticipation lock**: V0 note onset must occur on "and of 4" of the preceding bar (beat 4.5), then sustain through beats 1–2, re-articulate on beat 3, hold through beat 4. Reject any bass note that lands on a downbeat without anticipation.
2. **Offbeat dominance**: All rhythm section chordal entries (V2 guitar, V3 organ bubble) MUST be on & subdivisions (upbeats). Downbeat entries disallowed. V0 bass may optionally land on 3 but must anticipate from 4.5.
3. **One-drop grid (roots)**: V1 kick+snare together on beat 3 only. No other kick. Rockers variant: kick on 1+3, snare on 2+4. Template must be set per riddim type.
4. **Harmonic loop invariance**: The chord progression (set by V0 bass root and V2/V3 chord quality) must be a cycle of ≤4 chords repeating identically throughout the piece. No modulation, no structural harmonic development.
5. **Dorian/minor-pentatonic prevision**: Pitch vocabulary prefers i–VII–IV–V or i–VII–VI–VII. The i chord is tonic. The VII (♭VII) is the characteristic reggae move. Major-key riddim with I–VII–IV–I is valid but less common (rocksteady).
6. **Skank voicing constraint**: V2 guitar chords must be voiced on the top 3 strings only (no low E or A string), played palm-muted for duration ≤ 16th note, with full rest between 8th-note offbeats.
7. **Layer-mask form**: Sections are defined by ON/OFF masks over the frozen riddim loop. The loop content (V0, V1, V2, V3 where used) NEVER CHANGES — only presence vs. absence. This is the "arrangement by subtraction" principle.
8. **Horn gap-fill**: V4 horn counter-melody must NOT overlap lead vocal phrases. It fills the gaps in the call-and-response pattern. If no vocal, horn fills half of each 4-bar phrase.
9. **Dub FX submix**: V7 automation events (delay send, reverb send, filter cutoff, mute) do not change the underlying note data. They modify the mix only. Reverb tail must decay fully between section boundaries.
10. **Tempo band**: 65–90 BPM (roots = 65–80, lovers rock = 70–85, digital/dancehall = 75–90). No accelerando — the riddim is locked-tempo.
11. **Call-and-response spacing**: Every vocal phrase (V5) should leave at least 2 beats of space within each 4-bar group for V4 horns or V6 chorus to answer. Silence is as important as sound.
12. **Zero instrumental evolution**: Within a riddim version, no voice introduces new melodic material mid-track. The arrangement evolves only by addition/subtraction of voices. Once a voice is in, its material is fixed through the section.

The method targets **concrete layer**: the bass line, one-drop pattern, skank, and organ bubble are directly notatable as MIDI events in the UnitMatrix. The dub mix layer (V7) is an automation/effect layer above the concrete note data but still expressible as timeline actions.

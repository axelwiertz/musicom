## Full Craft Procedure

Javanese gamelan composition is **cyclically-punctuated architectural craft** — the composer (empu karawitan) designs nested time cycles (colotomic structure), a skeletal melody (balungan), and modal constraints (pathet). The work is not a continuous score but a **set of interlocking constraints** that all musicians realize simultaneously via learned elaboration formulas (cengkok) and instrument-specific garap.

### Step 1: Fix the tuning system (laras)
Choose **sléndro** (5 roughly equidistant tones: 1-2-3-5-6 ≈ ~240¢ spacing) or **pélog** (7 unequal tones, used as 5-tone subsets). This determines the pitch universe.

### Step 2: Select the pathet (modal category)
Pick 1 of 6 pathet, each with tonic (dasar), strong note, avoided note, and characteristic cadence formula:

| Pathet | Tonic | Strong | Avoided | Cadence formula |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sléndro nem | 2 | 5 | 1 | 6-5-3-2 |
| Sléndro sanga | 5 | 1 | 3 | 2-1-6-5 |
| Sléndro manyura | 6 | 2 | 5 | 3-2-1-6 |
| Pélog lima | 2 | 5 | 7 | 5-3-2-1 |
| Pélog nem | 5 | 1 | 7 | 2-1-6-5 |
| Pélog barang | 6 | 2 | 1 | 3-2-7-6 |

The avoided note does NOT appear in structural positions (seleh). The tonic is the most frequent cadence destination. This constraint gives each pathet its "flavor" — pathet manyura feels bright and open (tonic 6, cadence 3-2-1-6), pathet sanga feels settled (tonic 5, cadence 2-1-6-5).

### Step 3: Choose the colotomic structure (bentuk)
Decide the gongan cycle — which gongs strike at which subdivision. Major forms:

- **Lancaran**: 16-beat cycle `TWTN TPTN TPTN TPTG`. Fast, simple. Used for opening pieces and processional.
- **Ketawang**: 16-beat `p.Tp. TpN p.Tp. TpG`. Kempyang+ketuk interlock. Vocal-heavy, meditative.
- **Ladrang**: 32-beat `pT pN pT pN ... pT pG`. The most common form. 8 gatras per gongan.
- **Ayak-ayakan**: Variable gongan length (2–8 gatras). Wayang-derived, flexible, used for scene transitions.
- **Srepegan**: Faster variable-length. Kenong plays every beat. Tense, driving.
- **Sampak**: Fastest wayang form. Repeated single notes per gatra. Frenetic climax.
- **Gendhing (kethuk 2/4 arang/kerep)**: The longest form. Mérong: kethuk only on odd gatras (kerep) or doubled-odd (arang). Minggah: kempyang+ketuk active. Gongan can span 64+ beats.

### Step 4: Compose the balungan (skeletal melody)
Write the core cipher notation in 4-beat gatras. Each gatra's final note (seleh) must be a pathet-approved pitch. The full balungan is the **invariant pitch spine** — all elaborating instruments derive their parts from it. The composer sings/plays the balungan and the ensemble responds.

### Step 5: Write the buka (solo introduction)
The buka is a solo (rebab for soft style, bonang for loud) in free meter that:
- Introduces the pathet's character
- Ends on the note that is the first gatra's seleh
- Duration: ~2–4 gatra-length phrases in free rhythm

### Step 6: Build the section architecture
Standard flow: **Buka → Mérong → [Ngelik] → Ompak → Minggah → Suwuk**

- **Mérong**: First main section, slow irama (I–III), no kempyang. Repeated once or twice.
- **Ngelik** (optional): A rising sub-section inside mérong, same gong structure, higher register, more animated. Contrasts the mérong's gravity.
- **Ompak**: A 1-nongan bridge section. Contrasting balungan contour. Kendang cues the transition.
- **Minggah/Inggah**: Second main section, faster irama (I–II), kempyang now active. May use a different bentuk (e.g. "minggah ladrang").
- **Suwuk**: Ending — kendang plays specific pattern, final gatra converges on tonic, gong ageng and kenong strike together, then the gong's resonance fades into silence.

### Step 7: Set the irama (density level)
Assign the irama for each section. This defines the ratio between balungan pulse and instrumental elaboration:
- Irama I (lancar) → balungan = 1 note/beat
- Irama II (tanggung) → balungan = 1 note/2 beats
- Irama III (dadi) → balungan = 1 note/4 beats
- Irama IV (wiled) → balungan = 1 note/8 beats

### Step 8: Garap — the realization rules
The piece is completed not through notation but through learned instrumental grammar:
- **Saron & demung**: Play balungan exactly.
- **Peking**: 2× diminution of balungan.
- **Bonang**: Imbal interlock + gembyangan (octave doubling on offbeats). Anticipates seleh.
- **Gendèr**: Cengkok patterns keyed to each seleh note. ~6–10 pattern choices per seleh.
- **Rebab**: Lagu — expressive phrase-shaped melody with microtonal bends. Leads the ensemble.
- **Gambang**: Rapid running figuration across full range, respecting pathet.
- **Suling**: Breath-phrased contour, octave doublings.
- **Kendhang**: Leads tempo changes, irama shifts, section transitions, and suwuk cue.
- **Pesindhèn**: Female vocal solo, cengkok-derived lines over balungan.
- **Gerong**: Male chorus, parallel thirds/fourths, harmonic block texture.

### Step 9: Oral transmission & refinement
The piece is taught by rote — the composer or teacher sings/plays each gatra, the ensemble memorizes it. The composer adjusts the balungan when the resulting garap "feels wrong" (rasa). Over time, well-liked gendhing become part of the standard repertoire and are transmitted across generations.

### Voices

| Voice | Name | Instrument(s) | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| V0 | **Gong Spine** | gong ageng, kenong, kempul, kethuk, kempyang | Invariant colotomic cycle — the form skeleton. Gong ageng = gongan boundary. Kenong = ¼ gongan. Kempul subdivides. Kethuk+kempyang interlock fill. NEVER deviates. |
| V1 | **Balungan Core** | saron, demung, slenthem | Skeletal melody — the composed pitch spine. 4-beat gatra grid. Invariant per section. |
| V2 | **Elaboration Layer** | gendèr, bonang, gambang, peking | Cengkok/imbal realization of V1. Each seleh triggers a cengkok pattern from a seeded library. Peking = 2× diminution. Bonang = offbeat anticipation. Gendèr = multi-octave figuration. |
| V3 | **Lead Interpretation** | rebab, suling, pesindhèn | Lagu — expressive human-shaped melody, slower than V2, microtonal, phrase-contoured. The "voice" of the piece. |
| V4 | **Pulse & Transition** | kendhang, kepyak | Temporal director — irama level, section transitions (ompak/suwuk), tempo changes. |

### Sections

| Section | Irama | Colotomy | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| S0 Buka | free | solo rebab/bonang | Pathet introduction, ends on balungan's first seleh |
| S1 Mérong | I–III (slow) | Gong cycle, no kempyang | Main statement, repeated |
| S2 Ngelik | same or slightly faster | Same colotomy as S1 | Rising contrast, higher register |
| S3 Ompak | I–II | 1 nongan | Bridge, contrasting balungan |
| S4 Minggah | I–II (medium) | Kempyang now active | Animated second section |
| S5 Suwuk | — | Final gatra → gong ageng | Complete cycle, return to tonic |

### Key Rules

1. **Colotomy invariance** — V0 gong pattern never changes mid-piece.
2. **Zero-drift** — all sections = integer gatra count (4-beat unit). Total lengths = gongan multiple.
3. **Pathet pitch set** — V1 only uses pathet-allowed 5-tone subset. Forbidden pitch omission.
4. **Seleh hierarchy** — gatra's final note (beat 4) must be a pathet-approved pitch.
5. **Buka final = balungan first** — seamless entry constraint.
6. **Ngelik +1 octave** — V1 in ngelik operates 8ve higher than mérong.
7. **Irama density multiplier** — V2 density = irama_mult × V1 density (2×/4×/8×/16×).
8. **Kendang cue lock** — transitions only triggered by specific kendang patterns.
9. **Minggah kempyang rule** — kempyang active ONLY in minggah, never mérong.
10. **Suwuk convergence** — final gatra = tonic (dasar). Gong+kenong simultaneous.

## Quirks & Pitfalls

1. **Pélog is practically pentatonic**: Though 7 tones exist in the instrument, any single gending uses only 5 (omit 4 or 7). The "spare" octave is for rare ornament.
2. **Strong beat is final, not first**: In the 4-beat gatra, beat 4 (seleh) is the structural accent — opposite of Western downbeat. Assigning MIDI accents to beat 1 is a systematic error.
3. **Balungan ≠ melody**: The skeleton is NOT what a listener hums. The rebab or pesindhèn produces the recognizable tune. Trying to make the balungan "sing" by itself is a category mistake.
4. **Irama ≠ tempo**: A piece in irama III (dadi) has slower note values but the underlying keteg pulse may not slow significantly. It's a density ratio, not a BPM.
5. **Garap is performer's craft**: The composer does NOT write gendèr or gambang parts — that's the domain of the performer's cengkok knowledge. A computer model must include a cengkok library as a seeded resource, not treat it as free generation.
6. **Pathet mixing is real**: Many gendhing "wander" pathet for emotional effect. The avoidance rules are pedagogical first principles, not hard laws. Martopangrawit's own analyses show frequent pathet shifts in canonical pieces.

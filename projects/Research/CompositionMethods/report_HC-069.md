## 1. What This Method Is

Afrobeat composition is an **extended-vamp, layer-build architecture** where a single harmonic cell (one chord or a two-chord pendulum) sustains unchanged for 10–30 minutes. The composition is not a harmonic progression — it is the controlled ordering and timing of instrumental layers entering, exiting, and recombining over a static groove. Every instrumental part is a fixed ostinato pattern; the art lies in textural architecture: which layers play when, how they interlock, and how density arcs across the piece. This is fundamentally different from Western common-practice composition (which progresses harmonically) and from pop songwriting (which uses verse-chorus form). Afrobeat is **architectural rather than narrative.**

The genre was invented by **Fela Anikulapo Kuti** in Lagos, Nigeria, in the late 1960s, with his band Africa '70 (later Egypt 80) and drummer Tony Allen. Fela described his approach as composing "like a symphonist, hearing multiple voices simultaneously" (Afropop, 2026). Tony Allen contributed the rhythmic engine: a triplet-shuffle hi-hat, ghost-note snare, and double-kick-on-one that became the genre's defining groove. No algorithmic or AI methods are involved — this is pure human craft, taught within Fela's band through daily multi-hour rehearsals and call-and-response demonstration.

**N.B. — This is classic Afrobeat (Fela Kuti, 1969–1997), NOT 21st-century Afrobeats (Burna Boy, Wizkid, Davido). The two share geographical origins but are compositionally distinct. Classic Afrobeat uses one-chord extended vamps; modern Afrobeats uses chord changes, trap production, and pop forms.**

## 2. Craft Procedure (Step by Step)

### Step 0 — Harmonic Cell Selection
Choose a single root-tonic harmony: Cm7, G7#9, Dm9, or a minor pentatonic/dominant 9th cell. The entire piece stays on this harmony — **no chord changes.** A two-chord pendulum (e.g., i–♭VII, i–iv) is the maximum deviation. This static harmonic field is non-negotiable.

### Step 1 — Bass Ostinato (the Spine)
Write a 1–2 bar repeating bass line using the pentatonic/minor scale:
- Root on beat 1 (often doubled: two kick drums hit the "one" together)
- Fifth on beat 3 or off-beat
- Syncopated ghost-notes and chromatic approach tones
- **Never varies** — same pattern repeats for the entire track (10–30+ min)

### Step 2 — Two-Guitar Interlock (the Signature Texture)
Two electric guitars play complementary rhythmic patterns:

**Guitar 1 (rhythm/chicken-scratch):** Muted staccato off-beat stabs using dominant 9th or m7 voicings. No downbeat hits. The guitarist mutes strings with the palm and strums on 16th-note off-beats, creating a percussive "chik-chik" sound. Pattern repeats every 1–2 bars.

**Guitar 2 (pick/arpeggio):** A melodic picking pattern — pentatonic arpeggiation or single-note lines with a softer attack. Fills the metric gaps left by Guitar 1. Often panned opposite in the stereo field.

Together, the two guitars produce a composite rhythm that sounds like a single dense strum heard through a comb filter — the beat is implied but never explicitly played by either guitar alone.

### Step 3 — Drum Grid (Tony Allen Method)
The drum pattern is not one repeating pattern but four limbs doing different jobs:

- **Hi-hat:** Shuffling triplet-based 8ths (a "swung 16th" feel). The open hi-hat on beat 2's "and" is Allen's trademark.
- **Snare drum:** Ghost-notes (buzz/light strokes) on off-beat 16ths; the main backbeat accent lands on beat 3. Ghost notes create rolling pocket.
- **Kick drum:** Double kick on the "one" (the defining Afrobeat marker) — two simultaneous kick hits. Syncopated ghost kicks on the "and" of 1 and "and" of 3.
- **Ride cymbal:** Optional wash that opens and closes for dynamic shape.

### Step 4 — Percussion Stratum
- **Shekere** (gourd shaker): Constant 8th- or 16th-note pulse — the time-keeper that glues the groove.
- **Ogene** (double iron bell): Repeating cross-rhythm pattern (e.g., 3:2 or 5:4 hemiola against the main beat).
- **Congas:** Syncopated counter-rhythm, often a tumbao-like figure.
- **Sakara drum** (talking drum): Optional lead drum that "speaks" rhythmic phrases.

### Step 5 — Horn Section Arrangement
Typically 3–4 horns (tenor sax, alto sax, trumpet, occasionally baritone sax). They serve two functions:

**Stabs:** Short 1–2 beat block-chord hits, syncopated, answering or punctuating the vocal. Sparse, used when the vocal is active.

**Mambo riff:** A 1–4 bar composed melodic riff (pentatonic, rhythmic) that enters when the vocal rests. The horn riff functions as a secondary "call" in the antiphony.

**Solos:** A horn player (Fela himself on tenor/alto sax or the trumpet player) takes an extended solo over the unchanged groove.

### Step 6 — Organ Pad + Piano
- **Hammond organ:** Sustained chord voicing (m7, dom9) as a wash. The organist plays long-held voicings with Leslie speaker percussion. Swells at section transitions.
- **Piano (optional):** Occasional percussive chord jabs doubling the guitar rhythm.

### Step 7 — Vocal Call-and-Response
- **Lead vocal** (Fela style): "Talking-singing" delivery — short, percussive, declarative phrases in Nigerian Pidgin English + Yoruba. Political commentary text. The rhythm of the words is composed to syncopate *against* the groove.
- **Backing vocalists** (the "Queens" / "Lijadu Sisters" / backup ensemble): Antiphonal response to the lead call. Typically a 2–4 word repeat of the song title or a short phrase, sung in unison/piercing high register. The call-response cycle is locked to 2 or 4 bars. The response is pitch-identical per cycle (not varied).

### Step 8 — Structure the Layer-Build Arc

The composition's form = the **order and timing of layer entries**, not chord changes. A canonical Afrobeat build:

| Phase | Activity | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| S0 — Intro | One percussion instrument alone (woodblock, shekere, or ogene) | Sparse anchor |
| S1 — Perc Stratify | Second/third percussion enters. No pitched instruments yet. | Build rhythmic base |
| S2 — Drums Enter | Full drum kit (Tony Allen pattern). | Engine on |
| S3 — Guitar Interlock | Guitar 1 (chicken-scratch), then 4 bars later Guitar 2 (picking). | Signature texture activates |
| S4 — Bass Enters | Bass ostinato drops in. | Harmonic spine engaged |
| S5 — Organ Pad | Hammond organ sustained wash. | Fills the harmonic space |
| S6 — Horn Stabs | Horn section enters with syncopated stabs (sparse). | Punctuation layer |
| S7 — Horn Mambo | Horns settle into 4-bar repeating mambo riff. | Second melodic voice |
| S8 — Vocal Section | Lead call + backing vocal answer. | Political text |
| S9 — Solo | Horn/tenor sax solo over unchanged groove. | Virtuosity |
| S10 — Breakdown | Layers drop out leaving only guitar interlock + percussion. | Reset |
| S11 — Rebuild/Climax | Layers re-enter in reverse order. Max tutti density. | Peak |
| S12 — Coda | Abrupt cut on the "one." All voices stop. | Punctuation |

Recordings split into Part 1 (instrumental: S0–S7) and Part 2 (vocal: S8–S12). This is a deliberate composition decision — the listener experiences the groove as an architectural object before the political text enters.

## 3. Practitioner Examples

### Fela Anikulapo Kuti (1938–1997)
- Role: Composer, bandleader, tenor/alto sax, keyboards, vocals
- Training: Trinity School of Music, London (classical piano & percussion)
- Method: Composed every part by ear/demonstration — "like a symphonist hearing multiple voices." Wrote 30-minute one-chord compositions with controlled layer-build.
- Key recordings: *Zombie* (1976), *Water No Get Enemy* (1978), *Expensive Shit* (1975), *No Agreement* (1979), *Gentleman* (1973), *Sorrow, Tears & Blood* (1977), *International Thief Thief (I.T.T.)* (1979), *Army Arrangement* (1986)

### Tony Allen (1940–2020)
- Role: Drummer, musical director, co-architect of the Afrobeat rhythm
- Contribution: Invented the triplet-shuffle hi-hat, ghost-note snare, double-kick-on-one technique. Played with Fela 1965–1979. No official songwriting credits but universally acknowledged as rhythmic co-architect.
- Key recordings: All Fela 1970s recordings; later *Home Cooking*, *Black Voices*

### Femi Kuti (b. 1962) & Seun Kuti (b. 1983)
- Role: Sons of Fela, continuing the tradition
- Femi: *Shokoto*, *Day by Day*; Seun: *Many Things*, *From Africa with Fury* (with Egypt 80)

### Antibalas (founded 1998)
- Role: Brooklyn-based Afrobeat collective, modern torchbearers
- Recordings: *Liberation Afrobeat Vol. 1*, *Security*, *Where the Gods Are in Peace*

## 4. UnitMatrix Mapping

### Voices (one per instrumental layer, 9 voices)

| Voice | Instrument | Material | Sectional Behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| **V0** | Bass ostinato (electric bass) | 1–2 bar pentatonic/minor figure, never varies | Active S4–S12; same pattern entire track |
| **V1** | Guitar 1 — chicken-scratch (electric, bridge pickup, palm-muted) | Off-beat stabs, m7/7#9, no downbeat | Active S3–S12; pattern repeats every 1–2 bars |
| **V2** | Guitar 2 — picking/arpeggio (electric, neck pickup) | Pentatonic arpeggiation filling interlock gaps | Active S3–S12; complements V1 |
| **V3** | Drum kit (Tony Allen grid) | 4-limb polyrhythm: triplet-shuffle HH, ghost snare+backbeat3, double-kick-on-1 | Active S2–S12; full pattern + fill variations at section boundaries |
| **V4** | Percussion stratum (shekere + ogene + conga) | Shekere constant pulse; ogene cross-rhythm 3:2/5:4; conga syncopated | Active S0–S12; continuous throughout |
| **V5** | Organ pad (Hammond/piano) | Sustained chord wash, swells at transitions | Active S5–S12; swells at S6, S8, S10 boundaries |
| **V6** | Horn section stabs → mambo (trumpet + tenor/alto sax) | Block-chord 1–2 beat stabs (sparse) → 4-bar mambo riff | Active S6 (stabs) → S7 (mambo) through S12 |
| **V7** | Lead vocal (talking-singing) | Short percussive declarative phrases, pitch within 5th | Active S8–S12; syncopated against groove |
| **V8** | Backing vocals (2–3 female, unison/high) | Antiphonal fixed response, 2–4 words, pitch-identical | Active S8–S12; answers V7 every call |

### Sections (the layer-build arc as 12 sections)

| Section | Active Voices | Description |
|---|---|---|
| S0 — Intro perc (8–16 bars) | V4 only | One percussion alone; skeletal time |
| S1 — Perc stratify (8–16) | V4 (thickened) | Additional percussion layer; still no pitched |
| S2 — Drums enter (8) | V3 + V4 | Full kit rhythm engine |
| S3 — Guitar interlock (8) | V1 + V2 + V3 + V4 | Two-guitar interlock over drum+perc |
| S4 — Bass enters (4) | V0 + V1 + V2 + V3 + V4 | Harmonic spine activated |
| S5 — Organ pad (4) | V0 + V1 + V2 + V3 + V4 + V5 | Full rhythmic+harmonic bed |
| S6 — Horn stabs (8–16) | V0..V6 (stabs sparse) | Horn punctuation |
| S7 — Horn mambo (8–16) | V0..V6 (mambo repeating) | Horn melodic riff established |
| S8 — Vocal section (16+) | V0..V6 + V7 + V8 | Call-and-response |
| S9 — Solo (16–32) | V0..V6 + V7/V8 (resting) | Extended instrument solo |
| S10 — Breakdown (8–16) | V1 + V2 + V4 (V3, V0, V5, V6 rest) | Texture thins |
| S11 — Rebuild/climax (16) | V0..V8 full tutti | Maximum density |
| S12 — Coda (4) | V0..V8 cut on "one" | Abrupt end |

### Rules That Encode the Method in the Matrix

1. **One-chord invariant:** V0–V6 pitch content stays within the parent pentatonic/dominant cell. No harmonic modulation.
2. **Bass immutability:** V0 repeats exact ostinato across all active sections. No variation, no fills.
3. **Guitar interlock complementarity:** V1 and V2 never play the same rhythm. V1 = off-beat (16th-note upbeats), V2 = fills the metric gaps. Composite = full groove.
4. **Double-kick-on-one:** V3 kick triggers two strokes on every section downbeat (bar 1 of each section).
5. **Fixed layer-entry order:** The voice-activity matrix is locked: V4→V3→V1+V2→V0→V5→V6→V7+V8. Reverse for breakdown.
6. **Call-response lock:** V7 phrase = 2–4 bars. V8 answer = same length, immediate, pitch-fixed per cycle. V8 never varies its response.
7. **No fade-out rule (Abruptio):** On the last bar of S12, all voices cut on tick 0 (the "one"). Zero decay, zero ritard.
8. **Horn antiphony:** When V7+V8 are active, V6 plays stabs (not mambo). When V7+V8 rest, V6 plays mambo riff. They alternate in composed antiphony.
9. **Density monotonicity (build phase):** During S0–S8, voices only add, never subtract. Subtraction is reserved for S10 (breakdown), which is a structured compositional event.
10. **Zero-drift:** All voices padded to the section terminal landmark. V0.V1.V2 loops repeat exactly into section boundaries. `validate()` passes.

## 5. Quirks and Pitfalls

- **Not to be confused with Afrobeats:** Modern Afrobeats (2010s–present, e.g., Burna Boy, Wizkid) is a different genre with chord changes, pop forms, and trap production. The two are frequently conflated outside Africa.
- **Static harmony challenges Western-trained composers:** The method provides no harmonic development — the entire composition is textural. Composers expecting chord changes will find the method "boring" until they shift to hearing density as the primary architectural dimension.
- **Tony Allen's uncredited co-authorship:** Allen's drum pattern is compositionally as important as Fela's melodies and bass ostinati. He received no songwriting credits in Fela's catalog, but modern analysis recognizes him as co-architect.
- **Interlock rehearsal demands:** The two guitar patterns must lock at millisecond precision — any drift destroys the composite groove. Fela rehearsed his band 5+ hours daily, 6 days a week.
- **Extended length is structural, not indulgent:** Each layer needs 30–60 seconds to establish before the next enters. Cutting the form to 3-minute pop format breaks the method entirely.
- **The organ pad is often underestimated:** Many cover bands omit the Hammond wash, which drastically thins the texture. It is not optional; it fills the acoustic space between interlock hits.
- **The abrupt ending is a compositional rule, not a limitation:** Fela famously cut songs on the downbeat — the silence after the cut is part of the composition.

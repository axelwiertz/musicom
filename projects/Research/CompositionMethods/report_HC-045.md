## 1. Overview

The Ethiopian Qañat (kiñit/የኢትዮጵያ ቅኝት) modal system is a pentatonic framework of four principal modes — **Tizita** (ትዝታ), **Bati** (ባቲ), **Ambassel** (አምባሳል), and **Anchihoye** (አንቺሆዬ) — each with distinct interval structure, emotional register, and melodic contour. These modes are the foundation of Ethiopian Highlander secular music, realized predominantly by the **azmari** (አዝማሪ), a poet-musician comparable to the West African griot or European medieval minstrel.

The azmari composes and performs extemporized verses — sung while accompanying themselves on the **masenqo** (one-string bowed lute), **krar** (5–6 string bowl lyre), or **washint** (bamboo flute) — using the **sem-ena-werq** ("wax and gold"/ሰም ና ወርቅ) double-entendre poetic technique. The melody is always in one of the four kiñit modes, with microtonal inflections characteristic of traditional non-tempered performance.

## 2. The Four Principal Kiñit Modes

### Tizita (ትዝታ) — "Nostalgia / Reminiscence"
- **Emotional register**: Melancholic, nostalgic — the *tizita* ballad form is itself a named genre
- **Tizita Major** (C–D–E–G–A): Major pentatonic {0, 2, 4, 7, 9}
- **Tizita Minor** (C–D–E♭–G–A♭): Degrees 1–2–♭3–4–♭5 {0, 2, 3, 7, 8} — distinctive "blue" pentatonic with ♭5
- **Contour**: Descending tendency, narrow tessitura, phrase endings fall to tonic

### Bati (ባቲ) — "Lively / Dance"
- **Emotional register**: Energetic, celebratory
- **Bati Major** (C–E–F–G–B): {0, 4, 5, 7, 11} — degrees 1–3–4–5–7
- **Bati Minor** (C–E♭–F–G–B♭): {0, 3, 5, 7, 10} — degrees 1–♭3–4–5–♭7
- Wide leaps, faster tempo, dance orientation

### Ambassel (አምባሳል)
- **Emotional register**: Gentle to ceremonial depending on tempo
- **Ambassel** (C–D♭–F–G–A♭): {0, 1, 5, 7, 8} — degrees 1–♭2–4–5–♭6
- **Ambassel Type 2** (C–D–F–G–A): Mixolydian pentatonic {0, 2, 5, 7, 9}

### Anchihoye (አንቺሆዬ)
- **Emotional register**: Dramatic, solemn, often satirical
- **Anchihoye** (C–D♭–F–G♭–A): {0, 1, 5, 6, 9} — degrees 1–♭2–4–♭5–6

All four modes are rotations of the major pentatonic set except Tizita Minor and Anchihoye which introduce ♭5/♭6 modifications. On traditional instruments, pitches are **non-tempered**: neutral 2nds (~150¢), neutral 3rds (~350¢), quarter-tone bends on ♭5 are idiomatic.

## 3. The Azmari Craft Procedure (Step by Step)

### Step 1: Internalise the kiñit repertoire by ear
Apprentice azmaris absorb the four principal modes through years of listening and imitating elder masters. They learn: which kiñit evokes which emotional atmosphere, the characteristic 2–4 note melodic motifs, the correct microtonal inflections, and the regional dialect of the mode (Gojjam ≠ Wello ≠ Shewa).

### Step 2: Tune the instrument to the chosen kiñit
- **Masenqo**: Single horsehair string tuned by turning the peg; left-hand finger placement (no fret) produces microtonal shadings
- **Krar**: 5–6 strings tuned to the pentatonic set; open strings provide drone/pedal
- **Washint**: Finger holes produce pentatonic set; pitch bent by half-covering or embouchure

### Step 3: Choose the emotional/social register
The azmari reads the room (tejbet patrons, wedding party, celebration) and selects the appropriate mode: Tizita for nostalgia/wistfulness, Bati for dance/celebration, Ambassel for mixed/gentle, Anchihoye for drama/satire.

### Step 4: Open with instrumental vamp + melodic cell
A short 2–4 bar pentatonic riff establishes the mode, pitch center, and rhythmic feel. This **reference cell** (the "wax"/sem of the musical form) is the identity anchor — it repeats throughout the performance and defines the kiñit.

### Step 5: Compose the first verse extemporaneously
The azmari sings the first **sem-ena-werq** line:
- **Sem (wax)** = surface meaning (a flower, a bird, a landscape)
- **Werq (gold)** = hidden meaning (political critique, personal insult, coded love)
- Vocal melody follows pentatonic contour with melismatic ornamentation
- Phrasing asymmetrical (follows Amharic qəne poetic meter, not fixed bars)

### Step 6: Alternate instrumental fill and sung verse
Per verse: sung line → instrumental fill (masenqo/krar echoes or plays vamp) → sung line → handclaps + audience response. Call-and-response continues through the song, with ornamentation increasing per verse.

### Step 7: Intensify through performance arc
Tempo may increase, ornamentation thickens, vocal register pushes higher, sem-ena-werq sharpens (hidden meaning becomes more pointed), audience responds more energetically.

### Step 8: Close with cadence back to tonic
Return to opening vamp. Settle on tonic. Final line delivers the "gold" punchline. Held note or chord fades to silence.

## 5. UnitMatrix Mapping

### Voices

| Voice | Instrument (MIDI) | Role | Constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| **V0** | Krar ostinato (MIDI 106/banjo) or Masenqo (MIDI 45/pizz) | **Kiñit spine** — invariant pentatonic reference cell/vamp. Never changes. Mode identity anchor. | Absolute invariance — same riff, same pitch set, same tempo feel throughout. |
| **V1** | Lead vocal (MIDI 54/voice ooh) | **Azmari sung poetry** — sem-ena-werq line with microtonal ornamentation, melismatic contour, asymmetrical phrasing. | Pitches must be subset of kiñit pentatonic set. Ornament density ↑ monotonically. |
| **V2** | Krar/Masenqo (same as V0, higher register) | **Instrumental fill/response** — fills gaps between vocal phrases. Acts as call-and-response partner. | NEVER overlaps V1 (hocket interlock). Echoes V1 contour or plays vamp motif. |
| **V3** | Handclaps + kebero drum (MIDI claps + 116/taiko) | **Rhythmic spine** — continuous pulse in lilting 6/8 or additive meter. | Pulse invariance. Intensity increases with performance arc. |

### Sections

| Section | Bars | Activity | Active Voices |
|---|---|---|---|
| S0_Instrumental_Vamp | 4 | Solo vamp establishes kiñit, tempo, pitch center | V0 + V2 |
| S1_First_Verse | 8 | First poetic line (sem layer) + instrumental response | V0 + V1 + V2 + V3 (sparse) |
| S2_Second_Verse | 8 | Second line, ornamentation increases | V0 + V1 + V2 + V3 (stronger) |
| S3_Build_Intensify | 8–12 | Tempo push, heavier ornament, sharper sem-ena-werq | V0 + V1 + V2 + V3 (max density) |
| S4_Climax_Cadence | 4 | Return to opening vamp, settle on tonic, final line | V0 + V2 (thins back) |

### Rules (Zero-Drift Constraints)

1. **Kiñit Pitch Constraint**: All pitches ∈ pentatonic set of chosen mode. No chromaticism except microtonal ornaments resolving to set members.
2. **Voice 0 Invariance**: The ostinato cell repeats without pitch change. Mode identity depends on frozen cell.
3. **Call-and-Response Alternation**: V1 and V2 never overlap (hocket interlock).
4. **Sem-Ena-Werq Mask**: Vocal contour must support poetic text meaning. No purely virtuosic runs disconnected from lyric.
5. **Ornament Gradient**: Ornament density ↑ monotonically S0→S3, return to light in S4.
6. **Microtonal Tolerance**: V1 permitted neutral 2nds/3rds, quarter-tone bends on ♭5 resolving to set pitches.
7. **Asymmetrical Phrasing**: Phrase lengths follow poetic meter, not fixed bars. Zero-drift by padding at section boundaries.
8. **Cadence Convergence**: Every section ends on tonic. Final cadence holds tonic ≥2 beats.
9. **No Functional Harmony**: No chord progressions. Harmony = emergent pentatonic verticals (open 4ths/5ths, 2nd-clusters).
10. **Density Progression**: Voices enter monotonically — once entered, never drop out until S4 return.

## 7. Quirks & Pitfalls

1. **Microtonal mismatch**: Kiñit modes on krar/masenqo use non-tempered tuning. MIDI (12-TET) loses the characteristic neutral intervals of Ambassel and the ♭5 bend of Tizita Minor. Consider pitch-bend events or quarter-tone approximations.
2. **Oral tradition**: No notation exists. Any written representation is an approximation — the real piece exists only in performance.
3. **Kiñit ≠ maqam**: Despite surface similarities to Turkish makam, the kiñit system is pentatonic (not heptatonic) with no modulation convention. Each piece stays in one mode throughout.
4. **Sem-ena-werq untranslatability**: The double-entendre is deeply tied to Amharic language and social context. In instrumental realization, the "gold" layer is absent.
5. **Regional variation**: Gojjam, Wello, Shewa, and Gondar azmari traditions each shade the kiñit modes differently. A "standard" Tizita doesn't exist.
6. **Kebero tuning**: Drums are tuned by fire-heating the skin before performance. Pitch changes mid-performance. Not reproducible in MIDI.

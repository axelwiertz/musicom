## 1. Method name, tradition, layer

**Canon composition**: the contrapuntal craft of deriving one or more complete voices from a *single
composed line* (the **dux** / leader) by strict imitation at a fixed temporal distance (Δt) and pitch
interval (Δp). The derived voice(s) — the **comes** / follower — may additionally be transformed by
named operators: inversion (contrary motion), retrograde (crab / *cancrizans*), augmentation /
diminution, mensuration / prolation (same notation, different proportion → different speed), or
modulation-by-degree (*per tonos*, the endlessly rising canon).

- **Tradition:** Western Classical — Medieval rota/caccia & English round (*Sumer is icumen in*,
  c. 1250; Machaut's retrograde rondeau *Ma fin est mon commencement*, c. 1360s), Renaissance
  mensuration canon (Ockeghem *Missa prolationum*; Josquin *ex una voce tres*), Baroque apex (J.S.
  Bach: Goldberg canons at ascending intervals; Musical Offering crab / per-tonos / riddle canons),
  Classical social & instrumental canons (Mozart K.553–562 & K.73r & K.388 mirror trio; Haydn canonic
  minuet Op. 76/2; Beethoven canons WoO 159–198 and the Symphony No. 4 canon), 20th-c. canonic
  revival (Webern Op. 16, Stravinsky, Nancarrow player-piano tempo canons, Pärt mensuration canons);
  continuous pedagogy from Fux (1725) and Albrechtsberger (1790) to the modern conservatory.
- **Layer:** concrete (target when implemented) — like all HC methods, this is human-craft knowledge
  that the Musicom engine realizes as concrete note/chord material in the UnitMatrix.

**Why it matters:** the canon is the *strictest* form of imitation — where fugue (HC-055) states its
subject and departs into episodes and key areas, a canon never departs: from the comes's entry onward
every new note of the dux must stay in good counterpoint with the already-sounding notes of the comes.
The craft is the engineering of one line that harmonizes with itself — the cleanest possible mapping of
"derive voices from a spine" into the UnitMatrix's rows-and-sections model, and the seed of all later
imitative practice (fugue, invertible counterpoint, variation technique).

## 2. The craft procedure in detail (how the human actually does it)

1. **Choose the frame** — voice count (2–6), imitation interval **Δp** (unison, 2nd … 9th, octave),
   temporal distance **Δt** (a beat, a bar, two bars), mode/key, and finite vs perpetual (round).
2. **Compose the dux** — a complete, shapely 2–8-bar melody, dual-role by design: it will be heard as
   the top line *and* as the transposed/transformed inner or bass line, so it must sing in both
   registers and must not open on a note that clashes with the comes's first collision.
3. **Fix the operator** — strict canon (exact interval qualities) vs **free/diatonic canon** (same
   interval numbers, qualities adjusted to the mode — the standard Renaissance/Baroque practice,
   essential at the 4th/5th where perfects stack), or a named transform: inversion (*al rovescio*,
   mirror about an axis), retrograde/crab (*cancrizans*), retrograde-inversion, augmentation /
   diminution (×2 / ÷2 note values; extreme = *sloth canon*), mensuration/prolation (per-voice
   proportion signs → per-voice speeds), *per tonos* (each loop a whole step higher, no final cadence).
4. **Draft dux + comes note-by-note, together** — the heart of the craft. From the comes entry the two
   lines are written simultaneously as two-part counterpoint: **no parallel perfect consonances**
   (P1/P5/P8 by similar motion), **contrary-motion bias** with stepwise dissonance resolution,
   **cadence discipline** (when the comes approaches a cadence the dux must be shaped to cadence with
   it, or to suspend/rhythmicize to make room).
5. **Engineer the terminal point** (finite canons) — the classic method, taught from Fux through
   Albrechtsberger: *plan the ending first, compose backwards*. The dux's tail is shortened (or a free
   coda added after convergence) so both strands cadence on the same tick. Rounds instead engineer a
   **rota seam**: the melody's end must voice-lead into its beginning, and *every* staggered pair
   (not just the first) must pass the consonance audit.
6. **Add the free bass / accompaniment** (optional — *accompanied canon*): a composed (non-derived)
   bass harmonizes the strands; Bach's Goldberg canons all carry such a bass except the 9th (a strict
   two-voice canon at the ninth).
7. **Dress it as a puzzle** (optional — *puzzle/riddle/enigma canon*): write only the dux and append
   the rule as a motto or sign — Bach's "Quaerendo invenietis", "Notulis crescentibus crescat Fortuna
   Regis" (augmentation), "Ascendenteque Modulatione ascendat Gloria Regis" (per tonos). Mozart solved
   Padre Martini's puzzles then wrote his own (K.73r); Albrechtsberger: such passages "serve to
   sharpen acumen".
8. **Test by ear** — play the strands together; verify interlock consonance, cadence landing, and (for
   rounds) the seamless end→start wheel.

## 3. Practitioner examples

- **Sumer is icumen in** (Reading Rota, c. 1250, BL Harley MS 978) — earliest surviving six-voice
  polyphony: four-voice round at the unison over two free "pes" ostinato voices; the manuscript calls
  it *rota* ("wheel") and *canon*.
- **Guillaume de Machaut**, *Ma fin est mon commencement* (Rondeau 14) — crab canon with a text
  palindrome: the tenor is the cantus's retrograde, the contratenor is its own retrograde, the first
  couplet reads backwards the same ("my end is my beginning").
- **Johannes Ockeghem**, *Missa prolationum* (c. 1460s) — every movement a mensuration canon; pairs of
  voices share one written line under different proportion signs (four voices, four mensurations), and
  the imitation interval grows movement by movement: unison → 2nd → 3rd … reaching the octave at the
  Osanna of the Sanctus.
- **Josquin des Prez**, *Missa L'homme armé super voces musicales*, Agnus Dei II — "ex una voce tres":
  a triple mensuration canon from a single notated line.
- **J.S. Bach** — Goldberg Variations: a canon every third variation at ascending intervals (Var. 3
  unison, 6 = 2nd, 9 = 3rd, 12 = 4th, 15 = 5th, 18 = 6th, 21 = 7th, 24 = octave, 27 = 9th), each with
  a free bass; Musical Offering (1747): ten canons on the Royal Theme — crab canon (Canon a 2
  cancrizans), the endlessly rising *Canon a 2 per tonos*, *per augmentationem contrario motu*, riddle
  canons *Quaerendo invenietis* and *Canon a 4*; Art of Fugue closes with mirror canons/fugues
  (Contrapunctus inversus XII–XIII).
- **Mozart** — vocal canons K.553–562 (Bona nox, O du eselhafter Martin, Difficile lectu, Leck mich im
  Arsch), puzzle canons K.73r after Padre Martini, mirror canon in the trio of Serenade K.388/384a.
- **Haydn** — canonic minuet of the "Quinten" Quartet Op. 76 No. 2 (violins chasing at the octave
  through paired phrases); six canons Hob. XXVIIb (*Thy voice, o Harmony*).
- **Beethoven** — canons WoO 159–198 (occasional/table canons, some riddle canons); canonic passage in
  Symphony No. 4; strict canon studies under Albrechtsberger (1794–95).
- **20th century** — Webern *Five Canons* Op. 16; Stravinsky *Canon on a Russian Popular Tune* and
  *Double Canon*; **Conlon Nancarrow** player-piano **tempo/mensural canons** (voices at different
  tempi, unplayable by humans, punched by hand); Arvo Pärt *Cantus in Memoriam Benjamin Britten*,
  *Arbos*, *Festina Lente* (mensuration canons).
- **Round tradition** — Purcell's catches (17th c.); the children's rounds *Row, Row, Row Your Boat* /
  *Frère Jacques* are the perpetual canon's folk residue.

## 4. UnitMatrix mapping

**Voices:**
- **V0 — Dux (leader).** The composed spine; the piece's identity anchor; stated alone in the opening
  entry group and continuing as the reference strand through every later group.
- **V1 — Comes (follower).** Rule-derived: V0 events delayed by Δt ticks and transposed by Δp
  semitones (with the diatonic-adjustment mask for free canons), or carrying the chosen operator
  (inversion, retrograde, augmentation/diminution, mensuration, per tonos).
- **V2 — Free bass / accompaniment.** Composed (non-derived) harmonization under the strands —
  Goldberg-style bass; active in accompanied canons, silent in strict unaccompanied ones.
- **V3 — Second pair / transformed strand (optional).** Double/triple canons (second independent dux +
  comes), Ockeghem-style second mensuration pair, or a simultaneous inverted/retrograde "table canon"
  (one line, two readers).

**Sections** (equal length — zero-drift invariant; BAR = 1920 ticks at 480 tpb / 4/4):
- Finite canon: S0 Dux solo → S1 Comes entry (audit active) → S2 Terminal point (cadence convergence
  + optional free coda).
- Interval-cycle canon (Goldberg model): one section per canon at ascending Δp (unison, 2nd … 9th),
  each with its free-bass layer and cadence.
- Perpetual canon / round: one cyclic section with N voices staggered at equal Δt and a tested
  end→start seam.

**Rules to encode:**
- **Δt onset-lock** — comes events = V0 events delayed exactly Δt ticks; delay is a logged parameter.
- **Δp transposition-lock** — comes pitches = V0 + Δp; strict = exact, free = logged diatonic
  adjustment mask.
- **No-parallel-perfects audit** — at every tick both strands sound, consecutive P1/P5/P8 by similar
  motion rejected; audit runs on the composite including V2.
- **Contrary-motion + stepwise resolution** at strand convergence; cadence by 2–3 / 7–1 in outer
  voices.
- **Terminal-point convergence** — both strands end on the same tick (the structural twin of the
  engine's zero-drift validate()); dux tail shortened or free coda.
- **Operator transforms** — retrograde = reversed V0 event list; inversion = pitch mirror about a
  logged axis; augmentation ×2 / diminution ÷2; mensuration = per-voice duration proportions;
  per tonos = +1 whole step per loop, re-seamed.
- **Rota seam check** — rounds: every staggered pair (V0/V1 … VN/V0) passes the audit, not just the
  first; section seam must voice-lead end→start.
- **Puzzle mode** — only V0 written; the entry rule (Δt, Δp, operator) stored as section metadata;
  realization resolves the puzzle as a human solver would.
- **Register filter** — comes transposition kept within instrument range.
- **Humanize** — classical profile: moderate timing σ (5–10 ms), minimal layback; in mensuration
  canons the per-voice proportion must be realized in note durations before humanization, and the
  humanizer must not smear the deliberate proportional offsets.

## 7. Quirks & pitfalls

- **The hard interval is the 4th/5th.** Imitation at the 4th/5th stacks perfect intervals between the
  strands; diatonic (free) adjustment is the standard craft fix, not a cheat — Renaissance "canon"
  practice treated the adjusted answer as normal.
- **Canon vs fugue boundary.** Baroque theory called all imitation "fugue" (*fuga ligata* = strict
  canon); a canon has no episodes and no key areas — if the imitation breaks, it is no longer a canon
  (do not blur with HC-055).
- **The terminal point is the failure point.** Most student canons collapse at the end; end-first /
  compose-backwards is the fix. A canon that merely stops (no converging cadence) is unfinished —
  except perpetual canons, which by definition do not end.
- **Round seams are subtle.** *Sumer is icumen in* works because every staggered pair passes the audit;
  naive rounds collide at the loop point. Test all N pairs.
- **Puzzle canons are a notation genre, not a compositional one** — "the enigmatical character …
  does not consist of any special way of composing it, but only of the method of writing it down"
  (Richter); Morley complained a solved puzzle "was scant worth the hearing". The craft is the line.
- **Mensuration canons need proportional reading.** Ockeghem's mass is unreadable under modern uniform
  time-signature assumptions — the proportion signs ARE the canon. In the engine, apply per-voice
  duration scaling *before* the zero-drift check.
- **Retrograde vs inversion vs retrograde-inversion.** Crab reverses the interval *sequence*; mirror
  reverses interval *directions*; R-I does both — conflating them is the classic beginner error.
- **"Canon" = rule, not piece.** In Medieval/Renaissance scores the word names the law by which the
  other voices are made; the piece is the resulting polyphony. Genre-name usage is later theory.

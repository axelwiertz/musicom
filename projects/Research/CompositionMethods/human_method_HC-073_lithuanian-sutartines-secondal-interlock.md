# HC-073 — Lithuanian Sutartinės Polyphonic Craft: Secondal-Cluster Interlock & Staggered-Canon Layering (Dvejinės / Trejinės / Keturinės)

**Method ID:** HC-073
**Layer:** `concrete (target when implemented)` — the craft emits note-level events (per voice, per phrase cell) directly into UnitMatrix cells. A cell is one melodic phrase or one refrain unit; a section is one phrase+refrain cycle (one canon rotation); voices are the singing parts (or instrument parts in the skudučiai/kanklės versions). No abstract/theory layer sits between the human procedure and the grid: the "harmony" is a *vertical emergent property* of the interlocked concrete voices, not a pre-composed chord layer.
**Tradition:** Baltic folk polyphony — Aukštaitija (northeastern Lithuania: Biržai, Kupiškis, Rokiškis, Anykščiai regions). Women's oral singing tradition (work, calendar-ritual, wedding, dance songs), with men's instrumental counterparts on skudučiai (panpipes), kanklės (plucked zither), ragai (horns), daudytės (long wooden trumpets), lumzdeliai (fipple flutes). UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, inscribed 16 November 2010 (5.COM, Nairobi); on Lithuania's national inventory since 2018 (Biržai-region variant 2019). Earliest written mentions in 16th-c. sources; repertoire collected 19th–20th c.
**Primary elements:** TEXTURE, PITCH, RHYTHM, STRUCTURE (with HARMONY as an emergent secondal sonority — deliberately *not* functional).
**Related but distinct methods:** HC-002 (Balinese kotekan — also interlocking elaboration, but over an explicit colotomic gong cycle with a fixed pokok melody and instrumental stratification; sutartinės are vocal, self-contained, and dissonance-bearing), HC-013 (Georgian vocal polyphony — drone/contrast part-weaving; sutartinės have no drone and use the second as the *stable* interval), HC-061 (Corsican paghjella — staggered trio entry but converging on a *major triad*; sutartinės converge on unisons/seconds), HC-014 (Central African Pygmy hocket — slot-filling super-pattern; sutartinės overlap the *same* melody canonically rather than filling rests with a different figure), HC-015 (shape-note dispersed harmony — chordal, functional-adjacent; sutartinės are pre-harmonic). HC-073 is the canonical **second-as-consonance** method: dissonance is the fabric, not the exception.

---

## 1. What the method is

Sutartinės (singular *sutartinė*, from *sutarti* — "to be in concordance, in agreement") are the archaic multipart songs of northeastern Lithuania: two- and three-voice polyphony built on the oldest principles of multi-voiced vocal music — **heterophony, parallelism, canon, and free imitation**. The sound is unlike any Western choral practice: sharp, "grubby" (*saugės* was the old name, "strong and rough"), constantly dissonant, with the **interval of the second between voices as the normal, stable sonority** and heavily syncopated rhythms.

Three structural facts make it a genuine **human composition method** rather than a curiosity:

1. **The melody is a small invariant cell.** Two to five pitches, narrow ambitus, two equal-length parts (statement + refrain), short and mantra-like. The whole craft is *deployment* of that cell: how many voices, at what offset, with which text split.
2. **The polyphonic texture is a fixed set of performance formulas.** The tradition classifies itself by *who enters when*: dvejinės (two sing *together*, different lines → parallel seconds), trejinės (three sing in *staggered canon*; only two sound at once), keturinės (two *pairs* alternate stanzas). Roughly forty styles/ways of performance are documented (UNESCO).
3. **There is no composed ending.** Text and melody rotate "on a spiral"; voices are equal, so no voice has the right to finish. The piece stops by a social gesture: the **ūktelėjimas** — everyone breaks off together with a shout (or a hand/head gesture); instrumental versions end on a long sustained unison.

The name is the thesis: the singers are *making concord* (sutarti) out of deliberate, un-resolving discord.

---

## 2. Why this is not an algorithm

- **The rule is a deployment constraint, not a generator.** "Second voice enters one phrase behind, third two phrases behind, first rests while third sings" fixes *who*, *when*, and *how many* — it says nothing about which melody, text, or vocables fill the slots. Two villages produced different, equally authentic trejinės from the same formula.
- **The second is consonance here.** Western voice-leading rules (resolve the 2nd, no parallel 2nds, contrary motion) are *inverted*: parallel seconds are the fabric and cadences converge to unison, not to a triad. This is a different *listening contract*, not a broken version of the Western one.
- **Heterophony and canon coexist in one piece.** Dvejinės are not "canon" at all (simultaneous different lines); trejinės are strict canon; keturinės alternate paired echo. The genre is a *family of textures* chosen by ear and social function.
- **Text is not semantic spine.** Real words are minimal and repeated; the refrain is pure vocables (sodauto, lylio, ratilio, tonarilio, dauno, kadujo, čiūto, tūto, rūto). The "composition" is as much sound-pattern as meaning.
- **No closure.** The spiral repetition and the social stop-gesture mean the form is a loop with a *performance-triggered* terminus — nothing in the notes decides where it ends.
- **Function drives form.** Work songs (spinning, flax, oats), calendar rituals, weddings, and dance sutartinės share the technique but shape tempo, accent, and refrain density to the task; dance versions are humorous and spirited with reserved, austere movement (circle/star formation, linked arms, stamping).

## 3. The craft procedure, step by step

### Step 0 — Learn the repertoire by ear (oral transmission)
Sutartinės were learned inside women's work and ritual communities — no notation, no theory. Girls absorbed the repertory by singing alongside mothers and grandmothers at spinning, harvesting, weddings, and calendar feasts. The "composition" knowledge is: the melody cells, the vocable refrains, and the entry formulas. (Collectors: Mykolas Miežinis wrote the first known text c. 1849; Antanas Juška collected in the 19th c.; Zenonas Slaviūnas published the canonical three-volume *Sutartinės* in 1958–59 with nearly all known melodies.)

### Step 1 — Fix the text block (two parts)
Every sutartinė has **two textual parts**: a meaningful main text (short, repetitive, visual — e.g. *"Kaip sėjau linelį"* "How I sowed the flax") and a refrain of nonce vocables (*"Tūto dijūto, tūto dijūto"*, *"čiūto rūto"*, *"lylio"*, *"sodauto"*, *"kadujo"*, *"ratilio"*…). The two parts are assigned to different singers in the texture. Example (*Dijūta kalnali*): part 1 (uztarimas) = "Dijūta kalnali, Dijūta berželi"; part 2 (patarimas) = "Tūto dijūto, Tūto dijūto".

### Step 2 — Compose the melody cell
2–5 pitches only; narrow ambitus; **two equal-length parts** (the whole melody is symmetrical); syncopated, clearly accented rhythm; short phrases that lock into a "spiral" loop. The melody is the invariant spine — every performer sings (a version of) it.

### Step 3 — Choose the deployment type (the actual craft decision)
- **Dvejinė ("twosome")** — two singers *or two groups*; both **begin and end together**, singing **different melodic lines simultaneously** (usually the same words, sometimes different). The two lines diverge so that **parallel seconds** ring constantly between them. Example: *Kaip sėjau linelį*.
- **Trejinė ("threesome")** — the most popular type; three voices/groups in **strict staggered canon**: voice 2 enters one phrase behind voice 1, voice 3 two phrases behind; **when the third enters, the first rests** for one phrase (then the second rests, then the third) — so *only two voices sound at any moment*. Musicology (following Slaviūnas) further splits trejinės into strict-canon (*griežtinės*) and round-canon (*ratelio*, a perpetual rotating round). Example: *Ka tu, bitele* ("Where are you, little bee").
- **Keturinė ("foursome")** — four singers as **two pairs** (or two groups); pair 1 sings the stanza, pair 2 **repeats it with the same words** while pair 1 is silent; within each pair the two partners sing **different words/lines** (the dvejinė secondal split inside the pair). Example: *Žvingia žirgas* ("The horse neighs").
- **Instrumental version** — men replaced voices with interlocking parts on skudučiai (multi-tube panpipes, one pitch per pipe — hocket by construction), kanklės (zither), ragai (horns), daudytės (long wooden trumpets). The entry formulas and secondal interlock transfer directly; the ending shout becomes a long sustained unison tone.

### Step 4 — Set rhythm, accent, and movement
Accented syncopation is the engine; refrains are "distinctly articulated" and give the driving quality (UNESCO/wiki). Dance sutartinės add the circle/star formation, linked arms, and moderate stamping; work sutartinės take their pulse from the task (spinning, flax, oats). Tempo band in revival performance ~60–100 BPM (Sound Atlas).

### Step 5 — Cycle the spiral
Repeat the phrase+refrain loop indefinitely ("spiral" principle, mantra-like). Because the voices are equal and the end is not in the notes, each rotation is a variation of *energy* (accent, dynamics, dance step), not of pitch material. Ensemble rotations may swap which voice leads or which pair starts.

### Step 6 — Close with the ūktelėjimas
Anyone may trigger it, at any point in the loop: all singers **stop together on a shout** (or a hand/head gesture replaces it). Instrumental ensembles substitute a **long sustained tone** from all instruments at once. This is the only "cadence" — a social convergence, not a harmonic one.

## 5. UnitMatrix integration (Musicom engine)

**Layer:** concrete. The grid is filled with actual sounding parts — one row per singer/instrument group, cells = phrase-length events, sections = phrase+refrain cycles.

**Voices (rows):**
- **V0 — Melody spine.** The invariant 2–5-pitch cell (statement part). Identity anchor; repeats every section (spiral). Pitch set fixed per piece.
- **V1 — Deployment-dependent partner:**
  - *Dvejinė mode:* heterophonic parallel partner — same rhythm skeleton, pitch line diverging to form **parallel seconds** against V0 (seconds above or below).
  - *Trejinė mode:* second canon entry — V0's melody transposed *in time* by one phrase unit (offset = phrase length).
  - *Keturinė mode:* pair-partner line (different words) — the secondal split within pair 1.
- **V2 — Deployment-dependent:**
  - *Trejinė mode:* third canon entry — offset = two phrase units; plus the **rest-rotation mask** (V0 silent while V2 sounds, then V1 silent, then V2 — exactly two of three active).
  - *Keturinė mode:* second pair's echo voice (repeats V0's stanza text; V3 = second pair's partner line).
- **V3 — Stamp/dance layer (optional, dance sutartinės):** foot-stamp accents marking the syncopation grid; or, in instrumental versions, the skudučiai hocket part (each pipe = one pitch → hocket by construction).
- *(Instrumental variant: V1/V2 = interlocking skudučiai/kanklės/ragai parts obeying the same entry formulas.)*

**Sections (columns):** one **phrase+refrain cycle** per section (two equal-length parts; the canonical *statement + vocable refrain*). Sections repeat as spiral rotations (S0 → S1 → … → SN); the piece terminates only by the ūktelėjimas gate, not by a harmonic cadence. Canon offsets are measured in phrase units across sections — e.g. trejinė: V1 starts in S1, V2 in S2, V0 rests in S2.

**Rules to encode:**
1. **Pitch-set constraint:** ≤5 distinct pitches, narrow ambitus (small integer-tick window); all voices drawn from the same set (heterophony, not harmony).
2. **Second-as-consonance:** parallel seconds between active voices are *required/legal* at any beat — no voice-leading resolution rule, no tertian function. (Inverts the chorale/species rule sets — HC-071/HC-021.)
3. **Entry-formula lock (trejinė):** offsets ∈ {0, 1, 2} phrase units; rest-rotation mask guarantees exactly 2 of 3 voices active per column (third voice enters ⇒ first voice rests one phrase).
4. **Dvejinė simultaneity:** V0 and V1 share start AND end ticks (begin/end together) with different pitch contours.
5. **Keturinė pair-echo:** pair-2 voices repeat pair-1's words/pitches with the same cell length; pair partners never sing identical lines.
6. **Equal-length parts:** statement part length == refrain part length; all sections equal length (zero-drift invariant — validate() must pass).
7. **Vocable-refrain slots:** refrain cells use only the piece's vocable set (sodauto/lylio/ratilio/čiūto/tūto/rūto/kadujo…) — text-slot constraint, not pitch constraint.
8. **Syncopation bias:** accent mask per style (dance/ritual/work variants differ); stamp layer (V3) accents only the syncopated grid.
9. **Spiral repetition:** sections are cell-identical repetitions (variation of energy, not pitch) — no development section, no modulation.
10. **Ūktelėjimas end-gate:** termination is a performance trigger — all voices converge on a unison shout (or long sustained unison tone); the final cell is a *unison convergence*, not a chord.

**Distinct from adjacent HC methods in engine terms:** HC-002 kotekan interlock fills a *pokok melody's subdivisions* (elaboration ratio); HC-073 overlaps the *same* cell canonically. HC-014 pygmy hocket partitions a period into non-overlapping slots; HC-073 allows simultaneous seconds (two sounding lines at once). HC-013 Georgian polyphony holds a drone; HC-073 has no drone voice. HC-061 paghjella converges on a major third/triad; HC-073 converges on unison.

## 6. Quirks & pitfalls

- **The "concord" is dissonant.** Any implementation that "corrects" the seconds toward triads destroys the genre's identity; the second must be the *stable* sonority.
- **No leading voice, no ending.** Western ears expect a melodic leader and a cadence; sutartinės rotate equal voices and stop by social trigger. Engine loops must not auto-append a V–I.
- **"Only two of three sound"** — the trejinė rest mask is the texture's signature; a naive 3-voice canon (all always singing) produces a different (non-sutartinė) density.
- **Text ≠ lyrics.** Most words are vocables; syllable counts matter more than semantics for cell construction.
- **Instrumental ≠ accompaniment.** Skudučiai parts are the *same* entry formulas transferred to one-pitch-per-pipe instruments — hocket by construction, not by design.
- **Source caution:** subtype terms (griežtinė/ratelio) come from the Slaviūnas typology as summarized in musicology; primary English documentation (UNESCO) names only the three main types + ~40 performance styles.

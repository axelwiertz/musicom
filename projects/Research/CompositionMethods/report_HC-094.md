## 2. The method in one paragraph

A powwow song is a short monophonic melody on a 3–5 tone field, sung by an unblended unison male chorus over one big rawhide drum, organized as **four push-ups** of chorus-and-verse. The melody enters high (falsetto) and **descends terrace by terrace** to a cadence; the second half of the song is repeated before the return, and the return is **reduced/truncated** — Nettl's *incomplete repetition*, a binary form in which the second section is a variation/reduction of the first. The lead singer states the head motif alone; a second singer enters **before the leader's line ends**, varying the same contour; the full chorus then joins. **Hard beats** (honor beats) — pronounced strokes every other beat, with a 3-stroke cluster at the chorus→verse seam — cue the dancers. A **tail** (shortened chorus) closes the song after the final push-up's accelerando.

## 3. Craft procedure (as a human executes it)

1. Acquire the song (vision, inheritance, gift, or purchase — song-as-object ontology), then reconstruct it by repeated singing.
2. Fix the dance function first — it determines tempo, register, form length, drum pattern: flag, veterans/honor, grand entry, intertribal (vocables only), contest (fancy/grass/traditional/jingle/cloth), sneak-up, round dance, gourd dance, hand-game.
3. Choose the 3–5 tone field: pentatonic norm, perfect fourth as structural leap, range ≈ a tenth, semitones only as cadential lower neighbors.
4. Compose the head motif: high/falsetto entry, vocables (h, y, w + vowels; i/e higher, a/o/u lower), learnable in one hearing.
5. Build A by terraced descent to a cadence.
6. Build B as contrast (lower, or level recitation), then **repeat B** — the incomplete-repetition hinge.
7. Return to A **reduced** (truncated, re-ornamented), often a P4 or octave lower — repetition transposition down is standard.
8. Assign the "second": early entry (1–2 beats before the leader's line ends), same contour, ornaments added, no blending requirement — a heterophonic variant inside a monophonic ideal.
9. Place hard beats: every other beat, cluster of three at the section seam ("Hot Five" variant: first hard beat four beats before the second, then alternating).
10. Set the drum under the voice, not on it: rhythmically independent, "in perfect unison, slightly off the beat," roughly 2:3 with the vocal pulse; start on the rim, move to the center, tempo rising.
11. Run four push-ups; in the final one, raise tempo and volume over the last five beats; dancing stops on the final beat; the **tail** finishes.
12. Region as parameter: North = falsetto, high, ornamented, tense; South = lower, rounder, heavier drum.

## 4. Practitioner examples

- **Black Lodge Singers** (Blackfeet; Kenny Scabby Robe's family drum) — the reference Northern host drum; dense vocables, signature hard-beat placement.
- **Cozad Singers** (Kiowa, Southern) — Oklahoma style, lower tessitura, three-post drum, honor-beat clusters.
- **Yellowhammer** (Ponca) — Southern host drum.
- **Young Grey Horse Society / Heart Butte / Two Medicine Lake Singers** — Blackfoot contest and ceremonial drums in Nettl's fieldwork.
- **"AIM Song"** — all-vocable intertribal song; the model case for language-neutral songcraft.
- **Densmore's consultants** (1907→, >1000 songs) and the **Grinnell (1897) / Wissler (1903–04)** cylinders — earliest evidence for head-motif economy.

## 5. UnitMatrix mapping (Voices & Sections, rules)

### Voices
| Voice | Role | Encoding |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| V0 | Lead singer | head motif + phrase spine; falsetto entry; the only voice active in S0 |
| V1 | Second singer | V0's unit *delayed 1–2 beats*, identical contour, ≤2 added ornaments — a variant, never a chord tone |
| V2 | Chorus / drum group | unison or octave of V0, enters at the second's cue, phrase overlap at seams |
| V3 | Bass drum | hard beats every other beat + 3-cluster at seams; ≈2:3 offset vs vocal pulse; rim→center velocity ramp; tacet allowed in last stanza |
| V4 (opt) | Women's backing | octave-above doubling by rule |

### Sections
`S0 Head` (lead alone) → `S1 Chorus A` (terraced descent, ends with 3 hard beats) → `S2 Verse B` → `S3 Verse B′` (hinge). S1–S3 clone into **P2–P4** with a transposition ladder (0 / −P4 / −octave). `S_last tail` = shortened A, +6–12 % tempo and +15 % velocity over the last five beats, terminal landmark on the final beat (the zero-drift pad point). Push-up count is a parameter (default 4; 6–8 on a dancer's whistle).

### Rules to encode
- **Pitch-field lock**: 3–5 tones; P4 allowed as structural leap; range ≤ a tenth; semitones only as cadential neighbors.
- **Terrace rule**: no pitch above the phrase's entry terrace; entry terraces follow a descending ladder across phrases.
- **Incomplete repetition**: `A B B′` mandatory, and the returning A must be *reduced* (shorter by ≥1 beat or de-ornamented) — a literal loop is a method failure.
- **Head-motif economy**: all later cells derived from the head motif by transposition/truncation/ornament; no new intervallic material after S0.
- **Seconding rule**: `onset(V1) = onset(V0) − 1..2 beats`, contour identity, ≤2 ornaments per phrase, no consonance requirement.
- **Hard-beat rule**: accent every second beat; exactly three consecutive hard beats at the chorus→verse seam; hard-beat velocity ≥ 1.35× running mean.
- **Drum/vocal relation**: onset offset ≈ 1/3 vocal beat; velocity ramp across the song; drum independent of vocal onsets.
- **Final-push-up ramp**: tempo +6–12 %, velocity +15 % over the last five beats; tail lands exactly on the terminal tick.
- **Vocable-pitch coupling**: vowel class → pitch band (i/e upper, a/o/u lower) — the one place text drives pitch.
- **Monophony invariant**: octave/P5 doubling only; no triads, no functional harmony; HARMONY emergent.
- **Zero-drift**: every voice padded to its section landmark (`composer.validate()` must return True).

### Musical Elements Framework
- **PITCH** — terraced descent, 3–5 tone field, P4 leap, ≤ tenth, falsetto→head-voice register split, vowel→band coupling.
- **RHYTHM** — vocal pulsation on long tones; drum independent at ≈2:3; hard beats every other beat + 3-cluster; accelerando only in the final push-up; phrase-based (not metric) grouping.
- **HARMONY** — deliberately absent; unblended unison/octaves only.
- **STRUCTURE** — push-up cycle, incomplete repetition, transposed restatement, tail coda, extensible stanza count.
- **TEXTURE** — solo head → solo+second → full unison; heterophonic divergence inside a monophonic frame (the signature).

## 8. Quirks / pitfalls

1. **Pentatonic ≠ impoverished.** The 3–4 tone field is functional: one-hearing learnability + vocable prosody. Encoders must not "complete" the scale.
2. **Hard beats are structural cues, not accents.** They are read by dancers; randomizing or metric-normalizing them destroys the form.
3. **The "second" is not a harmony part.** Encoding V1 as a third/sixth above V0 produces European choral sound — categorically wrong. V1 must be a contour-identical, timing-offset variant.
4. **The returning A must be incomplete.** Identical A-repeats read as machine looping; the reduction is the human fingerprint.
5. **Region split must be a parameter** (North falsetto/high vs South low/relaxed). A single default silently erases half the tradition.
6. **Cultural property.** Songs are owned, gifted, or sold, and some are restricted (ceremonial, medicine-bundle, deathbed songs). Generative use should model the *craft grammar* abstractly, not produce pastiche of specific owned songs, and should name the nation/drum rather than using "tribe" as an aesthetic label.
7. **Etic terminology discipline.** Use "push-up," "hard/honor beats," "seconding," "tail," "head," "drum (group)." These are practitioner terms, not analyst inventions — using them keeps the encoding checkable against real recordings.

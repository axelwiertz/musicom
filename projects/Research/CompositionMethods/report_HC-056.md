## 1. Method Summary

Qawwali is the hereditary devotional song-craft of South Asian Sufi qawwals, transmitted through oral lineages (qawwal bacche) for seven centuries. It is **text-led**: the poetic kalam determines raga choice, tala, form, and emotional arc. The qawwal's craft lies in selecting, ordering, and delivering the text with improvised melodic elaboration, call-and-response intensification (takrar), and gradual rhythmic acceleration driving toward wajd (spiritual ecstasy). It is **congregational** — a lead soloist works with a chorus, harmonium(s), dholak/tabla, and rhythmic handclaps.

## 2. Craft Procedure in Detail

### 2.1 Poetic Genre Selection (Kalam)

The qawwal selects the poetic genre *before* any musical decisions:

| Genre | Purpose | Example Composer |
|-------|---------|-----------------|
| **Hamd** | Praise of Allah (opens the mehfil) | Various classical |
| **Naat** | Praise of the Prophet Muhammad | Various classical |
| **Manqabat** | Praise of Sufi saints (Ali, Nizamuddin, Moinuddin) | Amir Khusrau |
| **Ghazal** | Love/divine longing with Sufi subtext | Rumi, Hafez, Ghalib, Mir |
| **Kafi** | Punjabi/Seraiki folk-mystic poetry | Bulleh Shah, Shah Hussain |
| **Qaul** | Ritual opening hymn (Prophet's sayings) — obligatory | Amir Khusrau |
| **Rang** | Second ritual hymn (Khusrau-Nizamuddin devotion) | Amir Khusrau |

The qawwal memorizes the text; hundreds of kalam are learned by heart from the hereditary repertoire.

### 2.2 Raga Selection

A raga is chosen to match the text's emotion (rasa):

- **Bhairavi** (morning, solemn devotion — most common qawwali raga)
- **Kafi** (mellow, romantic longing)
- **Yaman** (evening, majestic praise)
- **Pilu** (light, folk-tinged)
- **Darbari** (deep, serious gravity)
- **Pahadi** (folk mountain flavor)
- **Jhinjhoti** (sweet, romantic)

The raga dictates allowable swaras, characteristic phrases (pakad/chalan), vadi/samvadi.

### 2.3 Tala Selection

| Tala | Beats | Division | Character |
|------|-------|----------|-----------|
| Keherwa | 8 | 4+4 | Most common, moderate |
| Dadra | 6 | 3+3 | Light, gentle |
| Rupak | 7 | 3+2+2 | Flowing |
| Teentaal | 16 | 4+4+4+4 | Climax sections only |
| Jhaptal | 10 | 2+3+2+3 | Occasional |

Handclaps (tali) mark the tala cycle — defining qawwali texture.

### 2.4 Step-by-Step Performance Workflow

#### Step 1: Naghma/Alap (Instrumental Prelude)
- Harmonium player opens with unmetered raga introduction
- Alap: one swara at a time, lowest to highest register
- Naghma: short composed tune based on *Zikr Allahu* (God's name in 3 phrases)
- Duration: ~1–3 minutes, free rhythm
- Voices tacet; only harmonium sounds

#### Step 2: Mukhda Statement (Refrain / Qaul)
- Lead qawwal sings the mukhda (refrain/hook phrase)
- Chorus (humnawan) immediately echoes in unison
- Dholak/tabla and handclaps enter with the tala cycle
- This sets the melodic-rhythmic template for the whole piece
- Mukhda melody + text are *frozen* for the entire performance

#### Step 3: Verse Couplets (Sher) with Chorus Response (Bol)
- **Lead** sings the first misra (line) of the couplet — free-rhythm, melismatic, meend glides, murki grace notes
- **Lead** sings the second misra — may ascend in register or vary ornamentation
- **Chorus** returns the bol (fixed response — usually mukhda or its tail)
- Handclaps and dholak stable in tala

This core alternation pattern: **sher → bol → sher → bol → ...**

#### Step 4: Girah (Inserted Couplet)
- The qawwal may insert a girah ("knot"): a couplet from a *different* poet that fits the same theme
- Sung to the same tune as the main sher
- Adds poetic depth and shows repertoire knowledge

#### Step 5: Takrar (Repetitive Intensification)
When a couplet strikes emotional resonance (audience shows wajd signs):
- Repeat the couplet multiple times
- Each cycle: **faster tempo, higher register, denser harmonium, louder handclaps**
- Qawwal adds **sargam taans** (solfege runs at breakneck speed — Nusrat hallmark)
- Side singers may join taans in parallel
- Takrar can last 5–15 minutes

#### Step 6: Climax / Wajd
- Highest vocal register
- Fastest tempo (130+ bpm)
- Densest texture: all voices + harmonium + handclaps at maximum volume
- Audience members enter wajd (ecstasy), may stand/whirl

#### Step 7: Close
- Return to mukhda (home refrain)
- Decelerando: dholak/tabla slow to stop
- Final couplet resolves to tonic (sa)
- Optional du'a (prayer) or rang (closing hymn)

## 3. Practitioner Examples

### Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan (1948–1997)
The most recorded qawwal in history. Signature techniques:
- **Sargam taans** at extreme speed — solfege runs over multiple simultaneous rhythmic cycles
- **Takrar mastery** — repeated single couplets for 20+ minutes, each cycle building
- **Raga breadth** — Bhairavi, Kafi, Yaman, Darbari, Pilu, Jhinjhoti, Pahadi
- Key works: "Allah Hoo," "Tum Ek Gorakh Dhanda Ho," "Mast Nazron Se Allah Bachaye," "Dil Haram Ho Gaya," "Mustt Mustt" (collab Michael Brook, Real World 1990)
- Global ambassador: performed 40+ countries, collaborated with Pearl Jam, Massive Attack, Eddie Vedder

### Sabri Brothers (Ghulam Farid Sabri, Maqbool Ahmed Sabri, Amjad Sabri)
Known for raw, powerful delivery and long takrar sections. "Hamd-o-Naat" became the qawwali standard. Key: "Saqi Nama," "Tajdar-e-Haram," "Meri Maut Ka Aalam."

### Aziz Mian (1942–1989)
Deep gravelly voice, extraordinarily long qawwalis (over 60 min). Contemplative style with extended alap. Key: "Allah Hi Allah," "Hashr Mein," "Jahan Jahan Mera Noor-e-Nabi."

### Amir Khusrau (1253–1325)
"Father of Qawwali" — synthesized Persian, Arabic, Turkic, Hindustani elements. Invented qaul, rang, tarana. His Hindavi/Persian poetry remains core qawwali repertoire today.

### Other notable practitioners:
- **Rahat Fateh Ali Khan** (nephew, carries lineage forward)
- **Rizwan-Muazzam Qawwali** (Real World artists, contemporary)
- **Fareed Ayaz & Abu Muhammad** (khayal-inflected qawwali)
- **Badar Miandad** (solo, pop-influenced)
- **Qawwal Bahauddin Qutbuddin** (Ajmer Sharif shrine hereditary)

## 4. UnitMatrix Mapping

### Voices (7 voices + optional audience)

| Voice | Instrument/Role | Channel | Program | Invariant |
|-------|----------------|---------|---------|-----------|
| V0 | Harmonium drone + mukhda spine | 0 | 24 (Harmonium) | sa-pa drone invariant; mukhda melody frozen |
| V1 | Lead qawwal vocal | 1 | 20 (Reed Organ) or 103 (Voice Oohs) | Pitches ∈ raga set; density increases monotonically |
| V2 | Chorus humnawan vocal response | 2 | 15 (Celesta) or 105 (Voice Ahhs) | Fixed mukhda/bol text invariant; onsets in V1 gaps only |
| V3 | Harmonium chord/sargam/doubling | 3 | 24 (Harmonium) | RH doubles V1 at octave; LH provides chord pads |
| V4 | Dholak/tabla percussion | 10 (perc) | — (MidiPercussion) | Tala theka pattern invariant; tempo monotonic ramp |
| V5 | Handclap grid (tali) | 10 (perc) | — (MidiPercussion, Clap) | Tali accents on tala beats; crescendos with takrar |
| V6 | Lead sargam taan runs | 1 (same as V1) | — | Only active in S4–S5; solfege syllables replacing text |
| V7 | Audience (optional) | — | — | Sparse interjections, designed for live human interaction (not MIDI) |

### Sections

| Section | Bars/Ticks | BPM | Voices Active | Description |
|---------|-----------|-----|---------------|-------------|
| **S0: Naghma/Alap** | Free (unmetered) | ~60 | V0, V3 only | Harmonium solo establishes raga. Free rhythm. No percussion, no voices. |
| **S1: Mukhda/Qaul** | 16–32 bars | 70–80 | V0–V5 | Lead states mukhda, chorus echoes. Dholak + handclaps enter. Tala established. |
| **S2: Sher 1–2** | 32–64 bars | 80–90 | V0–V5 | Verse couplet call-response. Lead solo, chorus in gaps. |
| **S3: Sher 3–4 + Girah** | 32–64 bars | 90–100 | V0–V5 | More verses, optional girah. Increased ornament, denser harmonium chords. |
| **S4: Takrar Build** | 64–192 bars | 100–120 | V0–V6 | Key couplet repeated. Each cycle faster, higher, louder. V6 (sargam taan) enters. |
| **S5: Climax/Wajd** | 8–32 bars | 120–130+ | V0–V6 | Maximum density. Highest register. Loudest handclaps. Fastest tempo. |
| **S6: Settle/Close** | 16–32 bars | 130→70 | V0–V5 (V6 drops) | Return to mukhda. Decelerando. Dholak slows. Final tonic. |

### Rules

1. **Raga pitch-set constraint** — All melodic pitches ∈ chosen raga. Harmonium drone: sa-pa.
2. **Tala cycle invariance** — Theka pattern repeats cyclically throughout. Phrases resolve on sam (beat 1).
3. **Call-response non-overlap** — Chorus (V2) sounds ONLY in gaps between V1's couplet lines. Never overlaps lead's sung text.
4. **Monotonic tempo ramp** — Tempo never decreases S0→S5. Strict monotonic increase per section.
5. **Takrar density escalation** — Each takrar cycle ≥ previous in: repetitions, register, subdivisions, loudness.
6. **Mukhda identity invariance** — The refrain returns identically after every verse couplet.
7. **Taans only in climax** — Sargam solfege runs (V6) restricted to S4–S5.
8. **Text-led priority** — No purely instrumental section >30s during S2–S5.
9. **Vocal register ascent** — Each successive sher/takrar cycle: lower → middle → upper octave.
10. **Lead-chorus alternation lock** — Every vocal section: lead solo line → chorus response → lead solo → chorus response. No 2 consecutive lead lines without chorus interjection.
11. **Climax convergence** — S5 coincides with highest note, fastest tempo, loudest handclaps, all voices on unified pulse.
12. **Close deceleration** — S6 tempo decelerates from climax to ~60% over 16–32 tala cycles. Final note: tonic.

## 7. Quirks & Pitfalls

- **Not raga-classical:** Qawwali is *text-led*, not raga-elaboration-led. The same raga may receive looser treatment than in Hindustani classical khayal. Avoid imposing strict raga grammar rules.
- **Dholak vs tabla:** Traditional qawwali uses dholak (folk double-headed drum) more than tabla. Tabla is a later urban addition.
- **Unison chorus:** The chorus sings in unison, not harmony. Western choral harmony concepts do not apply.
- **Harmonium as drone instrument:** The harmonium provides *both* melody and drone — left hand plays sustained sa-pa while right hand doubles the vocal. This is non-standard in Western harmonium practice.
- **No written notation:** Qawwali is taught entirely by oral transmission (face-to-face, call-and-response). Notation would miss the microtonal ornaments (meend, murki) and the rhythmic feel.
- **Takrar can seem repetitive:** To an outsider, the same couplet repeated 50+ times may feel monotonous. This is intentional — repetition is the vehicle for trance/wajd.
- **Gender:** Traditional qawwali was male-only; women were forbidden. This has relaxed in modern contexts but the core repertoire remains male-voiced.
- **Film qawwali:** Bollywood "filmi qawwali" often strips the takrar arc and replaces it with standard verse-chorus-verse. These are commercial derivatives, not the craft method.
- **Tempo ramp not always continuous:** In long qawwalis, the qawwal may insert a brief relax section before pushing again. The monotonic rule applies *within* the takrar build, not necessarily across the entire performance.
- **Handclap syncopation:** Skilled ensembles can shift the handclap pattern within the tala for increased intensity — a technique requiring deep ensemble coordination.

# HC-069: Afrobeat Layer-Vamp Architecture & Interlocking Groove Craft

**Method ID:** HC-069
**Method Name:** Afrobeat Layer-Vamp Architecture & Interlocking Groove Craft
**Tradition:** Nigerian Afrobeat — Fela Kuti, Tony Allen & Africa '70/Egypt 80 (Lagos, Nigeria, c. 1969–present)
**Layer:** human (→ concrete)

---

## Description

Afrobeat is the West African genre created by Fela Kuti and his drummer Tony Allen that fuses Yoruba traditional music, highlife, jazz, funk, and soul into a single, extended-vamp architecture. **The composition method is fundamentally a layer-build craft:** the composer writes no chord changes in the Western sense — instead, a single harmonic cell (one or two chords) sustains for 10–30 minutes. The composition is the controlled **ordering and timing of instrumental layers entering, exiting, and recombining** over that static groove. Every voice has a fixed ostinato pattern. The art is not in harmonic progression but in **textural architecture**: which layers play when, how they lock (interlock), and how the density arc builds and releases over an extended time span.

This is pure human composition craft — the composer hears the polyphony simultaneously ("like a symphonist," per Afropop) and writes each instrumental part as an interlocking puzzle piece that snaps together with the others.

---

## Craft Process (how the human does it)

### Step 0: Choose the harmonic cell
Select one root-tonic harmony (e.g., Cm7, G7#9, Dm9, Am pentatonic). The entire composition stays on this one chord (or one two-chord pendulum: i–♭VII, i–iv). **No chord changes.** This is non-negotiable in classical Afrobeat.

### Step 1: Compose the bass ostinato (the spine)
Write a 1–2 bar repeating bass line using the pentatonic/minor scale of the root. Typically:
- Root on beat 1 (often doubled: two kick drums hit the "one" together)
- Fifth on beat 3 or off-beat
- Syncopated ghost-notes and chromatic approach tones
- **Never changes** for the entire piece (10–30+ minutes)

### Step 2: Write the two-guitar interlock
This is the signature texture. Two electric guitars play complementary rhythmic patterns that interlock like Balinese kotekan but swung:
- **Guitar 1 (rhythm/chicken-scratch):** muted staccato off-beat stabs. Plays a syncopated rhythmic figure on the "and" of beats, never on the downbeat. Uses dominant 9th/m7 voicings. The pattern is a repetitive comp — the guitarist accents the off-beat 16ths with ghost-note muting.
- **Guitar 2 (pick/arpeggio):** a picking pattern — melodic arpeggiation of the same harmony, sometimes using pentatonic single-note lines. Softer attack, often panned opposite. Fills the gaps left by Guitar 1.

The two guitars together create a composite rhythm that sounds like a single dense strum heard through a comb filter — no individual downbeat, but the pair locks to make the beat palpable.

### Step 3: Set the drum grid (Tony Allen method)
The drum pattern is the engine. Tony Allen's signature approach:
- **Hi-hat:** shuffling triplet-based 8ths — a "swung 16th" feel that creates a rolling momentum. Open hi-hat on beat 2's "and" (the Allen trademark).
- **Snare drum:** ghost notes (buzz/light strokes) on off-beat 16ths; the main backbeat accent falls on beat 3 (or 2&4 in faster pieces). The ghost notes create the rolling "pocket."
- **Kick drum (double kick technique):** TWO kicks hit the "one" together (African downbeat emphasis). Syncopated ghost kicks on the "and" of 1, "and" of 3. This double-kick-on-one is the defining rhythmic marker.
- **Ride cymbal:** optional wash, opens and closes for dynamic shape.

### Step 4: Add percussion stratum
- **Shekere (gourd shaker):** constant 8th- or 16th-note pulse — the "time keeper" that glues the groove.
- **Ogene (double iron bell):** plays a repeating cross-rhythm pattern (e.g., 3:2 or 5:4 against the main beat).
- **Congas/congas:** syncopated counter-rhythm, often a tumbao-like pattern.
- **Sakara drum (talking drum):** optional lead drum that "speaks" phrases — used in some Fela tracks as a lead instrument.

### Step 5: Compose the horn section arrangement
Typically 3–4 horns (tenor sax, alto sax, trumpet/trumpets — sometimes baritone sax). The horns do NOT play extended melodies. They play:
- **Stabs:** short 1–2 beat block-chord hits, syncopated, answering or punctuating the vocal.
- **Mambo riffs:** a repeating 1–4 bar composed melodic riff (often pentatonic) that enters after the vocal line is established. The horn riff functions as a secondary "call" that the vocal answers (or vice versa).
- **Solos:** a horn player (tenor sax or trumpet) takes an extended solo over the unchanged groove. Fela himself played tenor and alto sax and often took the first solo.

### Step 6: Organ pad + piano
- **Hammond organ (or electric piano):** sustains the chord (minor 7th, dominant 9th, etc.) as a wash. Swells at section transitions. The organist plays long-held voicings with percussion from the Leslie speaker.
- **Piano (optional):** occasional percussive chord jabs, doubling the guitar rhythm in some sections.

### Step 7: Write vocal + backing vocal call-and-response
- **Lead vocal (Fela):** "talking-singing" delivery — short, percussive, declarative phrases in Nigerian Pidgin English mixed with Yoruba. The text is political commentary. The rhythm of the words is composed to syncopate against the groove — each phrase is a rhythmic event, not a melodic line.
- **Backing vocalists (the "Queens" / "Lijadu Sisters"):** antiphonal response to Fela's call. Often a 2–4 word repeat of the song title or a short phrase. Sing in unison or close harmony, often piercing/high-register. The call-response cycle is locked to 2 or 4 bars.

### Step 8: Structure the layer-build arc (the composition's "form")

The form is the **order of entry and exit of layers**, not chord changes. A typical Afrobeat composition arc:

```
[0:00–1:00] Sparse intro: one percussion instrument alone (woodblock, shekere, or ogene)
[1:00–2:00] Second percussion layer enters (congas/talking drum)
[2:00–2:30] Drum kit enters (Tony Allen pattern)
[2:30–3:00] Guitar 1 enters (chicken-scratch)
[3:00–3:15] Guitar 2 enters (arpeggio/picking)
[3:15–3:30] Bass enters (ostinato)
[3:30–4:00] Organ/piano enters (pad wash)
[4:00–4:30] Horn stabs enter (syncopated punctuation)
[4:30–5:00] Horn mambo riff established
[5:00–5:30] Vocal call enters + backing vocal answer
[5:30–7:00] Vocal sections: call→answer→call→answer over 4-bar cycles
[7:00–8:30] Horn solo (trumpet/tenor sax) over full groove
[8:30–10:00] Sax solo (Fela) over full groove
[10:00–10:30] Breakdown: layers drop out (2 guitars + shekere only)
[10:30–11:30] Build back: drums→bass→horns→organ (reversed order of entry)
[11:30–12:00] Vocal returns, denser
[12:00–13:00] Full tutti: all layers, maximum density
[13:00–14:00] Abrupt end: all cut off on the "one"
```

### Step 9: Arrange the track (LP side format)
Fela's albums split songs into:
- **Part 1 (instrumental):** the entire layer build without vocals (or minimal vocals). This is the "instrumental overture" that establishes the groove architecture.
- **Part 2 (vocal):** the song proper, with vocals, horn solos, and full arrangement.

This is a deliberate composition decision — the listener experiences the groove as an architectural object before the political text enters.

## UnitMatrix Integration

### Voices (the instrumental layers, each a dedicated voice in the matrix)

| Voice | Instrument/Role | Material | Behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voice 0 | **Bass ostinato** (electric bass) | Single 1–2 bar pentatonic/minor figure. Never changes throughout the piece. | Loops identical pattern every 1–2 bars. Spans entire piece length. |
| Voice 1 | **Guitar 1 — chicken scratch** (electric guitar, bridge pickup, muted) | Syncopated off-beat stabs, m7 or 7#9 voicings. No downbeat accents. Pattern repeats every 1–2 bars. | Off-beat grid: attacks land on 16th-note off-beats. Muted notes (ghost) on subdivision in-betweens. |
| Voice 2 | **Guitar 2 — picking/arpeggio** (electric guitar, neck pickup) | Pentatonic arpeggiation filling the interlock gaps. Softer dynamics. | Alternates with Voice 1 — the two together form a composite texture. |
| Voice 3 | **Drum kit** (Tony Allen grid) | 4-limb polyrhythm: hi-hat = triplet-shuffle 8ths, snare = ghost notes + backbeat 3, kick = doubled-on-1 + ghost kicks, open hat on 2-and. | Loop per bar/2-bars, with fill variations at section boundaries. |
| Voice 4 | **Percussion stratum** (shekere + ogene + conga) | Shekere = constant pulse. Ogene = cross-rhythm pattern (3:2, 5:4). Conga = syncopated counter-rhythm. | Continuous throughout; density may increase slightly at climax. |
| Voice 5 | **Organ/piano pad** (Hammond organ or electric piano) | Sustained chord wash (m7, dom9). Swells at transition points (e.g., when vocal enters, at breakdown). | Enters midway through layer build. Swells marked as volume/leslie-speed changes at section boundaries. |
| Voice 6 | **Horn section stabs** (trumpet, tenor sax, alto sax) | Block-chord stabs (1–2 beat hits, syncopated). Answering or punctuating the vocal. 1–4 bar composed mambo riffs. | Layer: stabs first (sparse, punctuation), then mambo riff (4-bar repeating melodic figure). |
| Voice 7 | **Lead vocal** (Fela style talking-singing) | Short percussive declarative phrases syncopated against the groove. Pitch-limited (often within a 5th range). Yoruba/Pidgin English. | Enters after all instrumental layers are settled. Phrase groups = 2 or 4 bars. |
| Voice 8 | **Backing vocals** (2–3 female voices) | Antiphonal response — repeat a 2–4 word phrase. Unison or close harmony attack. High register (piercing). | Answers Voice 7 every call. Typically identical response each time (pitch-and-rhythm locked). Call = Voice 7, answer = Voice 8. |

### Sections (the layer-build arc)

| Section ID | Name | Bars | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| S0 | Intro — Percussion Skeleton | 8–16 | One percussion instrument alone (woodblock, ogene, or shekere). Establishes time. |
| S1 | Percussion Stratification | 8–16 | Second/third percussion enters, building the polyrhythmic base alone. No pitched instruments. |
| S2 | Drums Enter | 8 | Full drum kit (Tony Allen pattern). Now has full rhythmic engine. |
| S3 | Guitar Interlock Enters | 8 | Guitar 1 (chicken scratch) followed 4 bars later by Guitar 2 (picking). Two-part interlock established over drum/percolation bed. |
| S4 | Bass Enters | 4 | Bass ostinato drops in. Now the harmonic spine is active. |
| S5 | Organ/Piano Pad | 4 | Chord wash fills the harmonic space. |
| S6 | Horn Stabs | 8–16 | Horn section enters with syncopated stabs (sparse, punctuation). |
| S7 | Horn Mambo Riff | 8–16 | Horns settle into a repeating 4-bar melodic mambo riff. |
| S8 | Vocal Section | 16+ | Lead vocal call + backing vocal answer. The political text. |
| S9 | Horn/Tenor Sax Solo | 16–32 | Extended solo over unchanged groove. |
| S10 | Breakdown | 8–16 | Layers drop one by one back to guitar interlock + percussion. |
| S11 | Rebuild / Climax | 16 | Layers re-enter in reverse order. Maximum tutti density at peak. |
| S12 | Coda / Abrupt End | 4 | All voices cut on the "one." No fade, no ritard. |

*Note: In practice Fela's songs are much longer (these bar numbers are per-minute equivalents). Real tracks: each section lasts 30–120 seconds at ~110–120 BPM.*

### Rules encoded in the composition method

1. **One-chord invariant:** the harmonic root never changes. Every voice stays within the parent chord/scale (minor pentatonic, dominant 7#9, etc.). No harmonic modulation.
2. **Bass immutability:** Voice 0 repeats the exact same 1–2 bar ostinato for the entire track. No variation, no fills. The bass is the unchanging spine.
3. **Guitar interlock complementarity:** Voice 1 and Voice 2 never play the same rhythm. Guitar 1 = off-beat stabs; Guitar 2 = fills the metric gaps. In-phase composite = full groove.
4. **Double-kick-on-one:** Drum kick hits the downbeat with TWO strokes (primary + displaced ghost or double pedal). This creates the "African one" — an emphasis distinct from James Brown's funk one-drop or reggae's one-drop.
5. **Layering order:** percussion-first → drums → guitar → bass → organ → horns → vocal. Reverse for breakdown. The order is fixed and deliberate.
6. **Call-response lock:** Lead vocal = 2–4 bars. Backing vocal answer = immediate 1–2 bar fixed response. The response is pitch-identical per cycle (not varied).
7. **No fade-out:** Afrobeat tracks end abruptly on the downbeat of a new bar. All instruments cut simultaneously. This is the punctuation rule — the music doesn't decay, it stops.
8. **Antiphonal horn-vocal relationship:** When vocal is active, horns do stabs (not the mambo riff). When vocal rests, horns play the mambo riff. They alternate in a composed antiphony.
9. **Density arc monotonic (in build):** layers only add, never subtract, during the build phase (S0–S8). Subtraction happens only in the breakdown (S10), which is a structured rest.
10. **Abruptio rule:** The piece stops on a tonic downbeat with full-tutti cut. No lingering decay, no fade — the silence after the "one" is part of the composition.

### How this fills the UnitMatrix

The UnitMatrix is an ideal carrier for Afrobeat composition because the matrix's **equal-length invariant** matches Afrobeat's structure: every voice (instrumental part) repeats a fixed-length pattern for the entire duration. The composition is the **muting/unmuting of voices over time** — a sequencing of layer entries — not a change in the content of each cell.

Implementation strategy:
- `create_matrix(num_voices=9, num_sections=12)` — one row per instrumental layer, one column per phase of the layer-build arc.
- Each voice's cell content = the active pattern for that section. For sections where the voice is silent, the cell contains a rest/empty.
- The **ostinato pattern** (bass, guitar 1, guitar 2, drums, percussion, horns) repeats identically across sections where active — the variation is in which voices are playing, not what they play.
- The **layer-build arc** translates directly to the section sequence: S0 voices active = {percussion only}, S1 adds drums, S2 adds guitar interlock, etc.
- `validate()` ensures all rows equal length — in Afrobeat this is trivially satisfied because all voices either play a loop or rest, never drift.

## Quirks and Pitfalls

- **Not "Afrobeats":** This method documents classic Afrobeat (Fela Kuti, 1969–1997), NOT the 21st-century pop Afrobeats (Burna Boy, Wizkid, Davido). The two share geographical origins but are distinct compositionally. Afrobeats uses chord changes, trap/hip-hop production, and verse-chorus forms. Classic Afrobeat is the one-chord extended-vamp architecture.
- **No chord changes = no harmonic development:** Composers used to Western harmonic progression often find Afrobeat "static." The composition is all in the texture, not the harmony.
- **Tony Allen's uncredited role:** The drum pattern is compositionally as important as the bass and guitar parts, yet Allen received no songwriting credits from Fela. Modern analysis increasingly recognizes him as co-composer.
- **Rehearsal demands:** The interlock requires millisecond precision. The two guitarists must lock their patterns perfectly — any drift destroys the composite groove. Fela rehearsed his band daily for hours.
- **Extended length is structural, not indulgent:** Each layer needs time to establish before the next enters. Cutting to a 3-minute pop format breaks the method.
- **The organ pad is often under-arranged:** Many covers omit the organ wash, which significantly thins the texture. It's not optional — it fills the space between interlock hits.

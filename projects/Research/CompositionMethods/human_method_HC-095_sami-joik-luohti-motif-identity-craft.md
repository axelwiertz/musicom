# HC-095 — Sámi Joik (Luohti): Motif-as-Identity Craft — Cyclic Nucleus, Glide Ornament & Gait-Bound Pulse

**Layer:** human (→concrete) — human-craft knowledge; target layer **concrete** when realized through the UnitMatrix
**Tradition:** Sámi (Sápmi, Northern Europe) — North Sámi *luohti* (the most-developed joik style), South Sámi *vuelie*, Lule/Pite/Ume *vuolle*, Inari *llivðe*, Kildin/Ter *luvv't*, Skolt *leuʹdd* (a cousin genre, not strictly joik); oral tradition of unknown age, called by researchers one of the oldest continuous music traditions in Europe; suppressed through Christianisation and Norwegianisation (banned in Sámi-area schools in the 1950s, especially under Laestadian influence), revived from the 1970s "joik renaissance" to the present
**Documentation:** Richard Jones-Bamman (*"As Long as We Continue to Joik, We'll Remember Who We Are"*, PhD diss., Univ. of Washington, 1993; "From 'I'm a Lapp' to 'I am Saami'", 2001), Thomas R. Hilder (*Sámi Musical Performance and the Politics of Indigeneity in Northern Europe*, 2015), Andrew Cronshaw ("Joiks of the Tundra", *Rough Guide to World Music*, 2000), DAT label liner notes (Kautokeino), Sámi practitioners' own testimony

## 1. What it is

A joik is **not a song about something — it is that thing, sung.** The Sámi verb (*juoigat*) is transitive: you joik *a person*, *a wolf*, *a mountain*, *a reindeer cow* — you do not joik *about* them. A joik is an **index**, a personal signature: in the north, most joiks are personal, frequently made for a person at birth, and function like a name. To hear your joik sung is to be made present in the room.

That ontology produces a compositional method unlike any narrative or harmonic tradition, and it is what makes joik worth a slot in this framework:

1. **The motif *is* the subject.** The identifying melodic nucleus — typically a two-to-six-note gesture — carries the whole identity. Correctness is judged socially: listeners either recognise *"that's him"* or they don't. Composition is therefore **character-capture**, not theme-writing.
2. **Cyclic, non-teleological form.** A joik has no fixed beginning or end and can be "endless": the nucleus is stated, turned, re-ornamented, restated. There is no exposition, no development into a contrasting theme, no cadential argument — the piece is a *loop with drift*, entered and left anywhere. This is the opposite design principle from sonata process (HC-038) or canon (HC-089).
3. **Gait-bound pulse.** Rhythm follows the body and the work: walking, skiing, rowing, sledge-driving, herding. Liner-note testimony records joiks born while haymaking, in a river boat, on a snowmobile — and the claim that "even the draught reindeer trots better when it hears a joik." The pulse is a stride, not a bar.
4. **Glide as the ornament system.** The surface is shaped by portamento into and out of tones, breathy/tense forward placement, minimal vibrato, and microtonal inflection — not by added pitches. The expressive vocabulary is *between* the notes.
5. **Text is optional and non-narrative.** Short lyrics or none; no rhyme; vocables (*na*, *lo*, *ja*, *vuoi*, *daja*) carry vowel-colour, with real words used as allusive hooks rather than story.

**Layer note:** this is human-craft knowledge — a *concrete-layer* target when implemented. The method supplies concrete note material (a nucleus, its rotations, its glides, its stride) which the UnitMatrix holds in Voices × Sections; nothing here is an abstract generator recipe.

## 2. The craft procedure (how the human does it)

1. **Fix the subject and its character.** Not a plot: a *character* to be captured. Practitioners describe the melody as imitating/characterising the person, animal or place — its gait, weight, mood, silhouette, weather. Kristoffer Sjulsson (1828–1908) put it: the singer expresses "his conception of a person's or an object's qualities, as well as his own feelings for that person, or the memories stirred when remembering them."
2. **Collect the markers.** Three to five concrete traits to be encoded (a heaviness, a quick light step, a stubbornness, a shine on a far mountain — Nils Anders Påve on the mountain Čokku: *"It shines like a star. Therefore it is joiked."*). These traits map one-to-one onto the nucleus's intervals, contour and rhythmic feel.
3. **Find the nucleus.** A 2–6 note gesture that must be recognisable in about two seconds, because it *is* the name. It is built inside a small tone field, almost always pentatonic in the north, and — the crucial licence — **the joiker may use any tones he pleases**: the field is a home, not a law. Ambitus is narrow (a fifth to an octave) in core *luohti*; South Sámi *vuelie* prefers **wider leaps**.
4. **Set the gait.** Choose the body-motion the joik rides (walk, lope, ski, row, sledge). This fixes pulse density, phrase length and the breath cycle before any pitch is fixed. No metric bar, no strong downbeat; a repeating stride with a breath at the turn.
5. **Build the cycle.** State the nucleus → rotate it (start on its second note) → ornament it → restate. The classic joik surface is *same shape, new clothes*: interval order preserved, rhythm and glide re-shaped. It runs as long as the singer wants; it can be entered mid-cycle.
6. **Ornament by glide, not by addition.** Approach each structural tone from below or above with portamento; let long tones sag or brighten microtonally; keep the larynx forward and tense, vibrato minimal, consonants soft. Dialect markers live here: Inari *llivðe* = short melodic lines, more nasal; Lule/Pite *vuolle* = melodic, narrative, slow lingering tempo; Skolt *leuʹdd* = epic, long improvised texts, complex rhythm.
7. **Set the vocables.** A syllable chain chosen for vowel colour (front vowels brighter/higher, back vowels darker/lower), syllables sometimes standing in for words in a coded "joik language." In classic *luohti* a full narrative text is wrong: words are hooks, not libretto.
8. **Check it socially.** Sing it to people who know the subject. The test is recognition, not beauty. If they name someone else, the nucleus is wrong.
9. **Transmit ear-to-ear.** No notation, historically. Learned at close range, in work contexts, from elders; joiks are inherited, gifted, and sometimes retire with the person. Recording (Valkeapää from 1968; DAT label from the 1970s) is the modern transmission channel.
10. **Modern setting (optional, and a separate craft).** Keep the nucleus intact and add drone, pulse, strings, choir or electronics — Wimme Saari's joik over electronics, Mari Boine's joik-rooted rock, Frode Fjellheim's choral/orchestral *vuelie*. The nucleus-invariance rule is what keeps these from becoming pastiche.

## 3. Practitioner examples

- **Nils Anders Påve** (b. 1938, Övre Soppero / Saarivuoma) — archaic *luohti* of the Soppero–Karesuando dialect; joiks people, incidents, landscapes and reindeer; the mountain Čokku joik; testifies that joik was work-communication ("your voice informed your friends of your position") and that reindeer trot better to it.
- **Per Mikkel P. Siri ("Bier Migon")**, Kautokeino — joiks played on a **single string stretched over a box and struck with a knife**, gliding the pitch by slackening the string: proof that the glide, not the voice, is the essential carrier. Joiks born while haymaking, boating, snowmobiling.
- **Nils-Aslak Valkeapää** (1940–2001) — first commercial joik recordings (1968); joiked at the 1994 Lillehammer Olympics opening; brought ambient/instrumental framing without dissolving the nucleus.
- **Mari Boine** — *Gula Gula* (1989): joik-rooted phrasing inside rock; the reference case for joik-as-vocal-grammar rather than folkloric quotation.
- **Wimme Saari** — "mumbling" traditional style carried into electronic/jazz settings (*Wimme*, 1995).
- **Frode Fjellheim** (South Sámi) — *Eatnemen vuelie* adapted as "Vuelie" for Disney's *Frozen* (2013): *vuelie* dialect (wide leaps, archaic feel) arranged for choir; also writes pedagogical joik material for choirs/schools.
- **Ánde Somby** (joiker and law professor, Vajas) — the clearest statement of the indexical rule: one joiks the person, not about the person.
- **Mattis Hætta** — "Sámiid ædnan" (Eurovision 1980), a composed protest joik; **Adjágas**, **Ulla Pirttijärvi** (Inari), **Sofia Jannok**, **Jon Henrik Fjällgren**, **Ella Marie Hætta Isaksen** (Stjernekamp 2018 winner, closing with a joik) — the contemporary revival line.
- **Johan Turi / Kristoffer Sjulsson / Just Qvigstad** — the early written descriptions of what joik is *for*, before any transcription existed.

## 4. UnitMatrix integration (Musicom engine)

### Voices (rows)
- **Voice 0 — Joiker (nucleus spine).** The identifying motif and its rotations; the only voice in S0; holds the invariant interval order.
- **Voice 1 — Second joiker / heterophonic shadow.** Same nucleus, entered a beat or two late or early, more ornamented, glides exaggerated. Never a chord tone — a *variant*, exactly as in powwow seconding (HC-094), and for the same reason: unblended unison is the aesthetic.
- **Voice 2 — Drone / sustained fundamental.** Optional; a held tonic or fifth (or the joik-string's open pitch). Invariant across the cycle. Stands in for the harmonic vacuum.
- **Voice 3 — Gait layer (percussion or plucked pulse).** Optional modern/ensemble setting: a stride pattern at the joik's body-rhythm, unaccented, no backbeat.
- **Voice 4 — Metadata row.** Dialect tag (*luohti / vuelie / vuolle / llivðe / luvv't*), subject marker, nucleus ID, glide log, rotation index. Not sounded.

**Monophony is the default.** In traditional *luohti* only Voice 0 sounds; Voices 1–3 exist for the ensemble/arranged realisation and must never introduce functional harmony.

### Sections (columns) — cycles, not arguments
- **S0 — Intonation (nucleus alone).** Bare statement of the identifying gesture, unpadded by accompaniment.
- **S1..Sn — Cycle 1..n.** Each column is a *pass* of the joik: nucleus stated, rotated, ornamented. Columns are **interchangeable and arbitrarily bounded** — this is the methodological point. A joik entered at S3 is still the same joik.
- **S_last — Open turn.** No cadence, no terminal formula: the final cell ends on a rotation state (often mid-nucleus) so the joik "could continue". Terminal landmark is a *container* tick, not a musical resolution.
- Length is a parameter (`cycles: int`). Rendering may loop the S1..Sn block; the encoder must not add a closing ritardando or tonic arrival.

### Rules to encode
- **Nucleus invariance (the identity law).** Voice 0's nucleus interval sequence must appear unaltered at least once per cycle; only rhythm, glide and register may vary. No new intervallic material may be introduced after S0 — no second theme, no development.
- **Rotation, not variation-by-substitution.** Permitted transforms: cyclic rotation of nucleus tones, truncation, rhythmic re-proportioning (≤ ±50 %), register shift (≤ ±P5), ornament insertion. Forbidden: inversion, retrograde-inversion, motivic fragmentation into new cells (these break recognisability).
- **Tone-field rule.** Pentatonic default set; range ≤ octave for core *luohti*, wider allowed for *vuelie* (parameter `dialect`); allow one chromatic inflection tone as dialect colour rather than forbidding it — the tradition explicitly licenses "any tones".
- **Glide rule.** Pitch connections are encoded as `portamento` metadata on the *same* event pair (duration + target), not as written-out scalar runs. Adding passing notes where a glide belongs is the single most common encoder error.
- **Non-teleology rule.** No cadence function, no dominant preparation, no final tonic arrival, no accelerando/ritardando at the end. Cycle boundaries are cuts, not resolutions. (Contrast rule set for HC-038 sonata process.)
- **Gait rule.** Tempo derived from a stride length; pulse unaccented (velocity variance low, ≤ 8 %); no backbeat, no swing; phrase length = breath length (2–6 s).
- **Vocable rule.** Text optional; if present, syllables are vocables or allusive single words. No rhyme scheme, no narrative line, no syllable-count metre. Vowel height ↔ pitch band (front/high, back/low), mirroring the powwow vocable coupling.
- **Heterophony rule.** Voice 1 = Voice 0 shifted 1–2 beats with ornament only; vertical intervals unrestricted but never *composed* as harmony.
- **Humanize profile.** Wide timing σ (30–60 ms), no swing, low layback, high glide density, near-zero velocity jitter (the voice is steady, the *pitch path* is alive) — the inverse of the jazz profile, which puts the life in timing.

### Musical Elements Framework mapping
- **PITCH:** narrow pentatonic field, nucleus interval order as identity, wide-leap parameter for *vuelie*, glide/portamento as first-class pitch motion, microtonal inflection metadata.
- **RHYTHM:** gait-derived unaccented pulse, breath-length phrases, no metre, no cadential ritard, cyclic rather than through-composed.
- **HARMONY:** none composed — drone/fifth only; simultaneities are heterophonic by-products. This is the cleanest "harmony-free" method in the framework alongside HC-014 (Aka hocket) and HC-094.
- **STRUCTURE:** cycle-and-rotation form; sections interchangeable; open ending; length as parameter.
- **TEXTURE:** solo nucleus → solo + shadow (unblended) → optional drone/gait bed; density constant rather than building (unlike gospel vamp or powwow push-up ramps). Intensity comes from ornament density and glide amplitude, not from adding voices.

## 5. Quirks, pitfalls, ethics

- **Do not harmonise it.** Adding thirds/sixths under the nucleus instantly converts joik into Nordic choral pastiche. Fjellheim's choir settings work because the choir either drones, heterophonises, or states the nucleus in unison — not because they add functional chords.
- **Do not develop the motif.** Western-trained instincts treat a 4-note cell as a seed for development. Here the cell is a *name*: developing it is like nicknaming someone incorrectly.
- **Do not "fix" the intonation.** Microtonal sag and glide are the ornament system; quantising them to 12-TET removes the craft. Encode them as metadata so the note layer stays clean but the performance layer keeps them.
- **Do not close the form.** A tonic arrival at the end is the giveaway of an outsider encoder. The joik stops because the singer stops.
- **Song ownership and restricted repertoire.** Joiks are personal property, given at birth, inherited, sometimes retired with the dead; some are ceremonial. Model the *grammar* abstractly; do not generate pastiche of specific named joiks (e.g. a living person's joik is not a style template).
- **Suppression history.** Joik was condemned as sin, banned in schools in the 1950s, and is still contested in some parishes; treating it as exotic colour is both inaccurate and politically loaded. It is a living contemporary practice (Sámi Grand Prix, Riddu Riđđu, Eurovision entries), not a folk relic.
- **Terminology.** *Luohti* (North), *vuelie* (South), *vuolle* (Lule/Pite/Ume), *llivðe* (Inari), *luvv't* (Kildin/Ter), *leuʹdd* (Skolt — related but not joik). "Yoik" is the anglicised spelling; "joik" the Norwegian/English common form. The performer is a *joiker* / *juoigi*.
- **Dialect is a parameter, not an accent.** Defaulting to North Sámi *luohti* while calling the output "Sámi joik" erases five other traditions with different interval habits and text densities.

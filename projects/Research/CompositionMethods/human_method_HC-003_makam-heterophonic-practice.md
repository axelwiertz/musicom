## 1. Description

Heterophony is the simultaneous performance of a single melodic line by multiple voices, each ornamenting and elaborating that line idiomatically. In Ottoman/Turkish classical music (Türk Sanat Müziği), heterophony is not an accident — it is the **core textural principle**. There is no Western-style harmony, counterpoint, or chord progression. Instead, all instruments and the singer perform the **same melody** (the *eser*, a composed repertoire piece) simultaneously, but each voice deviates from the skeleton through ornamentation, register shifts, rhythmic displacement, and idiom-specific techniques.

The melodic framework is the **makam** — a modal system defining:
- A scale (often microtonal, using comma divisions of the octave — 53-tone equal temperament is the theoretical model, though in practice intervals are learned by ear)
- A **seyir** (melodic progression/journey): a prescribed path through the makam's tonal hierarchy, including direction of motion, resting points, and characteristic phrases

The rhythmic framework is the **usul** — a cyclic rhythmic pattern (e.g., *devr-i kebir* = 28/4, *çenber* = 24/4, *semai* = 3/4 or 6/8) that underpins the composition.

### Why This Method Matters

Heterophony is one of the **oldest and most widespread** human compositional textures, found across Turkish, Arabic, Persian, Byzantine, and East Asian traditions. It represents a fundamentally different compositional logic from Western harmony: instead of stacking independent voices vertically, all voices elaborate a shared melodic skeleton horizontally. The craft lies in **how each instrument decorates the same melody** while maintaining identity and coherence.

## 2. The Makam System (Background)

### 2.1 Tonal Hierarchy

Each makam defines a tonal hierarchy with specific functions:

| Function | Turkish Term | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Finalis / Tonic** | *Durak* (durak perdesi) | Resting tone, final resolution pitch |
| **Dominant** | *Güçlü* (güçlü perdesi) | Secondary center, often a 5th above durak; melodic "anchor" |
| **Leading tone** | *Yeden* | Penultimate tone leading to durak at cadences |
| **Entry tone** | *Irak* | Starting pitch for certain makams (may differ from durak) |
| **Upper register** | *Tiz* | Upper octave region explored in the second half of seyir |

### 2.2 The Seyir (Melodic Journey)

The **seyir** is the heart of makam composition and improvisation. It is a **prescribed melodic path** — not a fixed sequence of notes, but a trajectory through the makam's tonal landscape:

1. **Başlama (Opening)**: Begin around the durak or entry tone. Establish the makam identity with characteristic phrases (*miyan*).
2. **Yürüyüş (Ascent / Walking)**: Move toward the güçlü. Melodic motion is primarily stepwise, conjunct. Brief pauses on structural tones.
3. **Güçlü region**: Rest and elaborate around the güçlü. This is the "dominant area" — a secondary tonal center.
4. **Tiz seyir (Upper register exploration)**: Ascend to the upper octave (tiz). Characteristic phrases in the high register. Often the climactic section.
5. **İniş (Descent)**: Return downward through the scale, passing through the güçlü again.
6. **Karar (Resolution / Cadence)**: Arrive at the durak. The final cadence (*karar*) uses the yeden (leading tone) to confirm tonic.

### 2.3 Heterophonic Elaboration

Each instrument in the ensemble elaborates the shared melody according to its idiomatic capabilities:

| Instrument | Role | Elaboration Technique |
|---|---|---|
| **Tanbur** (long-necked lute) | Structural skeleton, drone | Plays the core melody with minimal ornamentation; provides tonal anchor via open-string drones; plectrum articulation defines rhythmic skeleton |
| **Ney** (reed flute) | Melodic elaboration | Breath-driven ornaments (*koma* bends, *glissando* between microtones), melismatic turns, delayed/anticipated attacks, dynamic swells |
| **Kemençe** (spike fiddle) | Melodic elaboration + portamento | Continuous pitch slides between notes, rich vibrato, doubling melody with glissando ornaments, rhythmic displacement |
| **Ud** (short-necked lute) | Melodic reinforcement + rhythmic punctuation | Plectrum articulation, chordal doubling (octave reinforcement), occasional rhythmic punctuation |
| **Kanun** (zither) | Melodic elaboration + harmonic shimmer | Rapid ornamentation via mandal (levers), arpeggiated elaboration, tremolo |
| **Singer (vokal)** | Primary melodic presentation | Most direct statement of the eser; melismatic ornamentation (*meltem*), microtonal slides, expressive timing (behind/ahead of beat) |

## 3. Craft Steps — How a Human Executes Heterophonic Makam Performance

### Step 1: Learn the Eser (Repertoire Piece)
The composer or performer memorizes a composed piece (eser) in a specific makam. This is the **melodic skeleton** — a single melodic line with lyrics (for vocal pieces). Learned through oral transmission and repetitive listening, not from a fixed score. The melody includes its seyir trajectory.

### Step 2: Identify the Makam and Its Seyir
Determine which makam the piece is in. Internalize the seyir: where the durak is, where the güçlü is, the characteristic phrases (miyan), the ascending/descending tendencies, and the microtonal intervals (e.g., *segah* = E half-flat, *rast* = G natural). Each makam has a unique seyir "recipe."

### Step 3: Internalize the Usul (Rhythmic Cycle)
Identify the usul governing the piece. The usul is a pattern of strong (düm) and weak (tek) strokes. The tanbur and ud typically mark the usul while playing the melody. Long usuls (e.g., devr-i kebir, 28 beats) structure entire sections.

### Step 4: Determine Instrument Roles
Assign each instrument its heterophonic role:
- One instrument (often tanbur) plays the **skeleton** — the cleanest, least ornamented version
- Others elaborate idiomatically — ney bends microtones, kemençe slides, kanun tremolos
- The singer presents the melody with **melismatic ornamentation** but stays closest to the composed eser

### Step 5: Perform Heterophonically (Ensemble Execution)
All voices perform simultaneously:
- Each plays the **same melodic skeleton** but decorates it differently
- Vertical coincidences are **incidental** — no attempt to create harmony or avoid dissonance
- Rhythmic alignment is approximate — voices may slightly anticipate or delay notes, creating a **shimmering, breathing texture**
- The ensemble follows the singer (or lead instrumentalist in instrumental pieces) for pacing and rubato

### Step 6: Taksim (Solo Improvisation Sections)
Between composed sections, a soloist (neyzen, tanburi, or kemençeci) performs a **taksim** — an improvised exploration of the makam following its seyir. This is where deep makam knowledge is demonstrated: the improviser navigates from durak → güçlü → tiz → durak, using characteristic phrases, modulations (*geçki*) to related makams, and return.

### Step 7: Karar (Cadential Resolution)
All voices converge on the durak at the final cadence (karar). The yeden (leading tone) resolves to durak. The ensemble lands together — the one moment of true rhythmic and melodic unison, providing closure.

## 4. Real Practitioner Examples

### Tanburi Cemil Bey (1873–1916)
The greatest instrumentalist of the late Ottoman era. Master of tanbur, kemençe, and lavta. His **taksimler** (recorded improvisations) are studied as models of seyir navigation. He demonstrated how a single melodic skeleton could be elaborated with extraordinary idiomatic richness — his kemençe taksim in *Nihavend* makam shows portamento-driven microtonal ornamentation that is impossible to notate in standard Western notation.

### Hammamizade İsmail Dede Efendi (1778–1846)
The most important composer of Ottoman classical music. His *eser* compositions (peşrevler, saz semaisiler, ilahiler) define the repertoire still performed today. His pieces in makams like *Hicaz*, *Nihavend*, *Rast*, and *Saba* are the melodic skeletons that ensembles heterophonically elaborate. Dede Efendi's genius was composing melodies whose seyir was so well-crafted that heterophonic elaboration by multiple instruments created rich, coherent textures without any harmony.

### Münir Nurettin Selçuk (1899–1981)
Reformed vocal performance in Turkish classical music. His approach to melismatic ornamentation (*meltem*) and expressive timing demonstrated how the singer — as the primary melodic presenter — could ornament the eser while remaining faithful to its skeleton. His recordings of Dede Efendi compositions show heterophonic interaction between voice and instrumental ensemble.

### Niyazi Sayın (b. 1927)
Ney master. His performances demonstrate the ney's heterophonic role: bending microtonal intervals via breath pressure, using delayed attacks and melismatic turns, and creating a texture that shimmers around the vocal line without ever departing from the shared melodic skeleton.

### Necdet Yaşar (1930–2017)
Tanbur master. His ensemble performances demonstrate the tanbur's structural role: providing the cleanest melodic skeleton, marking the usul, and offering tonal anchor via open-string drones. His taksim in *Hüzzam* makam is a textbook of seyir navigation.

## 5. Musical Elements Framework Mapping

### PITCH
- **Scale**: Makam-specific, often microtonal (commas, not semitones). E.g., *Hicaz* has an augmented second between 2nd and 3rd degrees; *Rast* has a half-flat 3rd (*segah*).
- **Melodic skeleton**: A single composed eser melody — the shared source material for all voices.
- **Ornamentation**: Each voice applies idiomatic pitch elaboration (ne bends, kemençe glissandi, kanun tremolos, vocal meltem).
- **Seyir constraint**: Pitch trajectory must follow the makam's prescribed path (durak → güçlü → tiz → durak).

### RHYTHM
- **Usul**: Cyclic rhythmic pattern (düm/tek strokes) governing the composition. Long usuls (devr-i kebir = 28/4) structure entire sections.
- **Expressive timing**: Voices may slightly anticipate or delay notes — rubato is integral, not a deviation.
- **Usul marking**: Tanbur and ud articulate usul strokes while playing melody; this is not a separate percussion layer but embedded in the melodic instruments.

### HARMONY
- **Incidental**: No functional harmony. Vertical sonorities arise accidentally from heterophonic coincidence.
- **Drone**: Open-string drones on the durak (and sometimes güçlü) provide tonal grounding — the closest thing to "harmony."
- **Consonance emergent**: When all voices align on structural tones (durak, güçlü), the incidental unison/octave/double-octave provides natural consonance without any harmonic planning.

### STRUCTURE
- **Seyir phases**: The makam's melodic journey (başlama → yürüyüş → güçlü → tiz → iniş → karar) maps directly to formal sections.
- **Eser form**: Composed pieces follow formal structures — *peşrev* (4 hane + teslim), *saz semaisi* (4 hane + teslim), *şarkı* (A-B-C-A), etc.
- **Taksim insertion**: Improvised solo sections are inserted between composed sections, following the makam seyir but without usul (free rhythm).
- **Karar**: The final cadential convergence on durak — the structural endpoint.

### TEXTURE
- **Heterophonic**: All voices elaborate the same melody simultaneously — the defining textural principle.
- **Stratified elaboration**: Each instrument occupies a textural stratum defined by its idiomatic elaboration density and technique.
- **No independence**: Unlike counterpoint, voices are not independent — they share the same melodic source. Deviation is ornamental, not structural.
- **Shimmering quality**: The slight rhythmic and pitch desynchronization between voices creates a "living," breathing texture — this is the aesthetic goal.

### Voice Assignment

### Rules Encoding (for Musicom engine)

1. **Shared skeleton rule**: All voices derive their pitch content from a single melodic source (the eser). No voice may introduce pitches outside the makam scale or the skeleton's contour.
2. **Heterophonic deviation rule**: Each voice may deviate from the skeleton by:
   - Adding passing tones (within makam scale)
   - Adding neighbor-tone ornaments (kıvrak, şimşir)
   - Delaying or anticipating attack times (within ±1 subdivision)
   - Doubling at octave/double-octave
   - Applying idiomatic elaboration (ne: bends; kemençe: glissandi; kanun: tremolo)
3. **Seyir path rule**: The melodic trajectory across sections must follow: durak → güçlü → tiz → güçlü → durak. Each section's pitch content is constrained to the seyir phase's tonal region.
4. **Drone rule**: Voice 0 (tanbur) must sustain or periodically re-articulate the durak pitch as an open-string drone throughout, especially during structural resting points.
5. **No harmony rule**: No voice may be assigned an independent harmonic function. Vertical coincidences are emergent, not planned. No chord symbols, no figured bass, no functional progression.
6. **Karar convergence rule**: At the final section (karar), all voices must converge to unison/octave on the durak pitch. The yeden (leading tone) must precede this convergence by 1–2 beats.
7. **Usul rule**: The rhythmic grid of each section must follow the assigned usul pattern (düm/tek stroke pattern). Voices may align with or slightly offset from the usul grid, but the grid is structurally present.
8. **Makam scale constraint**: All pitches must be quantized to the makam's specific scale (including microtonal intervals). The scale is defined as a set of pitch classes with comma-level precision (53-TET theoretical model).
9. **Idiomatic ornamentation rule**: Each voice applies only its designated ornamentation type:
   - Voice 0 (tanbur): minimal ornamentation, plectrum articulation marks usul
   - Voice 1 (vocal): melismatic ornamentation (meltem), microtonal slides
   - Voice 2 (ney): breath bends, delayed attacks
   - Voice 3 (kemençe/kanun): portamento or tremolo
10. **Taksim rule**: Between composed sections, a single voice may perform an improvised section (taksim) following the makam seyir without usul constraint (free rhythm). Other voices are silent or sustain drones.

### Cell Contents

Each cell $U_{v,s}$ contains:
- `{PITCH}`: A sequence of pitches derived from the shared melodic skeleton, elaborated idiomatically for voice $v$, constrained to the makam scale and the seyir phase of section $s$.
- `{RHYTHM}`: Attack times aligned (approximately) with the usul grid for section $s$, with voice-specific expressive timing offsets (±1 subdivision).
- `{TEXTURE}`: Heterophonic elaboration density for voice $v$ in section $s$ — sparse in başlama/karar, dense in tiz seyir. Drone sustain flag for Voice 0.

## 8. Significance for Musicom

### Why This Matters for the UnitMatrix

Heterophony is a **fundamentally different textural paradigm** from everything in the current Musicom method library:
- All existing methods assume **independent voices** (counterpoint, chord voicing, call-response) or **interlocking parts** (kotekan).
- Makam heterophony introduces **dependent elaboration**: all voices share a single melodic source and deviate only ornamentally. This tests the UnitMatrix's ability to:
  1. Generate a single melodic skeleton and distribute it across multiple voices
  2. Apply voice-specific elaboration transforms while maintaining melodic identity
  3. Constrain pitch to microtonal scales (53-TET) — a new pitch resolution for the engine
  4. Structure form by **tonal journey** (seyir) rather than harmonic progression or rhythmic cycle alone
  5. Encode "no harmony" as an explicit rule — challenging Western assumptions

### Microtonal Pitch Challenge
Makam scales use comma-level intervals (e.g., *segah* ≈ E half-flat = 350 cents, between E♭ and E). This pushes the UnitMatrix pitch representation beyond 12-TET. The engine must support at least 53-TET resolution or pitch-class sets with cent values.

### Seyir as Structural Engine
The seyir provides a **tonal gravity** system that is neither Western functional harmony nor modal static. It is a **narrative path** through tonal centers — durak (HOME), güçlü (LIFT/secondary center), tiz (TENSE/upper register), iniş (TURN), karar (HOME resolution). This maps cleanly to Musicom's HOME/LIFT/TENSE/TURN gravity states but with a completely different mechanism.

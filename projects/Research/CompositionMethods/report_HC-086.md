## 1. Method name & tradition

**Steelband Panorama arrangement** — the Trinidadian craft of recomposing a current-year calypso into a
6–10-minute theme-and-variation spectacle for a 100–120-player steel orchestra, judged in competition and
taught by rote in the panyard.

- **Tradition:** Trinidad & Tobago; Panorama founded 1963, finals the Saturday before Carnival, Queen's
  Park Savannah, Port of Spain. Calypso/soca source material; since Ray Holman's 1972 "Pan on the Move"
  arrangers may also enter an "own tune."
- **Forces:** conventional bands up to 120 pannists on tenor, double tenor, double second, double guitar,
  cello, tenor bass, six bass, plus a ~20-player **engine room** (drum kit, congas, plastic bells, scratch,
  iron/brake-drum).
- **Pre-history:** the 1950s **Bomb** — a secret arrangement unveiled at J'Ouvert, usually a European
  "classic" or foreign pop tune rewritten in calypso style. Panorama inherited the Bomb's idea of a
  showpiece arrangement and added formal judging.
- **Why it is a human method:** key/tempo choice, large-form planning, idiomatic writing for seven pan
  voices, re-harmonization, transition design, crowd/judge staging, and oral transmission to the band are
  all performed by ear and by hand. Notation is optional and usually absent.

## 3. Craft procedure (how the human does it)

1. **Absorb the tune by ear.** Hook/chorus, verse, bass riff, horn lines, feel. Most arrangers did not read
   notation; Holman learned harmony from a book and worked out arrangements at home on guitar.
2. **Choose key, tempo, modulation plan.** Winning tempos 2005–14: ♩ = 119–136, mostly 125–135. Plan 1–2
   key changes; the idiomatic device is chromatic ("staircase") transposition of a one-bar cell up a
   semitone at a time, or direct phrase modulation (often major→minor).
3. **Lay out the arc:** Intro → Statement (a b c d) → Var 1 → Jam 1 → Var 2 (often minor) → Var 3 →
   Transition → Coda. In 30 top-three arrangements: theme-and-variations 30/30; first variation always
   immediately after the statement; jam after Var 1 in 19/30 (63%); three to four variations; final
   variation immediately before coda in 27/30 (90%). Average 9.9 of 13 formula techniques.
4. **Introduction (8 judging points):** clear count, unison hits, repeating bass, fp/subito piano →
   crescendo, chromatic or diatonic run into the hook.
5. **Statement:** tenor melody, double tenor a third below, double second/cello strum, six bass roots,
   tenor bass +8va. Hook literal and audible.
6. **Variations via operator ladder:** ornament → rhythmic change → low-pan melody with new material above
   (Holman) → split/trade melody and section solos → re-harmonize (12 points; suspensions, extended
   chords, secondary dominants, modal interchange) → texture drops (bass out, percussion-only break) and
   counterpoint (Samaroo).
7. **Jam (27/30):** written cyclical vamp, not improvisation — two/four chords, repeating bass ostinato,
   cross-rhythmic front-line riff, engine room forward; Bradley's model.
8. **Transition/coda:** chromatic climb or staircase modulation; coda reprises hook, V–I cadence, unison
   hits, fp crescendo. Samaroo: "You must get high!"
9. **Idiomatic pan writing:** circle-of-fifths tenor layout shapes runs; 16th-note runs at ♩ = 130–150 built
   for regular left/right sticking and memory; Samaroo discarded ideas his players could not execute.
10. **Rote transmission:** arranger plays the phrase beside the section leader, loops it, neighbours copy by
    watching hands; notes are named only if he cannot play that pan. Daily rehearsals for ~6 weeks; most
    players play only Panorama season. The performance, not a score, fixes the piece.
11. **Iterate:** balance the judges' 100-point craft sheet against live crowd response; cut what does not
    "hit," add breaks and dynamic drops.

## 4. Practitioner examples

- **Tony Williams** (Pan Am North Stars, 1963–64): first winner; intro + two variations in different keys +
  coda; arpeggios/countermelodies; raised double seconds to melodic role; spiderweb tenor layout.
- **Bobby Mohammed** (Cavaliers, 1965/67): rhythm breaks with brake drum continuing; texture variation by
  omitting voices; bass dropped under upper-voice display.
- **Earl Rodney** (Harmonites, 1968–74): "The Wrecker" at ♩ = 135; syncopated bass, minor-key jam
  ("montuno"); 210 players forced the 120 cap.
- **Ray Holman** (Starlift): low-pan melody with new material above (1966); deep re-harmonization ("The
  Bull", 1969); first own tune ("Pan on the Move", 1972); 5/4 hemiola, long arcs, extended chords.
- **Clive Bradley** (Desperadoes, 7 wins): pan "envelopes"; double seconds double tenor at −12 so double
  tenors can strum; tenor bass doubles bass +8va; codified the jam (four-chord vamp + cross-rhythm riff).
- **Jit Samaroo** (Renegades, 9 wins): Bach counterpoint in all voices; melody rotated through every
  section; clinically accurate; tailored difficulty to his band.
- **Len "Boogsie" Sharpe** (Phase II): first own-tune winner (1987); "Sharin' Licks" used 13/13 techniques;
  slower groove tempos 119–127.
- **Leon "Smooth" Edwards** (All Stars, 2006–24): fast clean school 128–134; "Bounce and Drive" placed 2nd
  in 2013 with only 8/13 techniques.
- **Duvone Stewart** (Renegades, 2018–24): contemporary champion.

### Rules

1. Form-order: statement before variations; first variation adjacent; final variation before coda; 1–2 jams
   (weights, not hard law — winners used 7–13/13 techniques).
2. Section-length + zero-drift: all seven rows end on the identical terminal tick; `validate()` is the gate.
3. One primary melody voice per section from {V0, V1, V5, split V0/V5}; doublings V1 = V0 −3, V2 = V0 −12,
   V4 = V5 +12.
4. Hook-pitch preservation under every variation operator; operators change rhythm/register/harmony/
   texture/distribution, never identity.
5. Operator ladder with monotone non-decreasing rhythmic density S1→S5.
6. Re-harmonization: one chord per bar (two in jams), secondary dominants/suspensions/extensions/modal
   interchange, bass roots retained, ≥1 minor-mode variation.
7. Chromatic staircase: transpose a 1-bar cell +1 semitone per repetition for 4–8 bars, land on target
   tonic; alternative direct modulation at a seam.
8. Jam: 2-bar 2/4-chord loop, 3+3+2 cross-rhythm riff, bass ostinato, bass/pan drop then re-entry.
9. Dynamics: fp (vel ~45) → linear crescendo to ~110 over 4 bars at intro/coda/transition; unison hits at
   ~120; terraced texture drops.
10. Engine room continuous through pan rests; at least one V0↔V5 call-and-response.
11. Tempo 125–135, **tight not rubato**: humanize σ ≈ 3–5 ms, zero layback, accents by velocity.
12. Coda: hook fragments, V–I, unison hits, final fp crescendo.

## 8. Quirks & pitfalls

- **"Bomb" ≠ Panorama section:** the Bomb is the 1950s secret J'Ouvert classical arrangement. The
  developmental groove interruption in Panorama is the **jam**.
- **Rote, not score:** the performance is the score; notation is transcription. Model teaching by playing
  and visual copying, not a score-distribution pipeline.
- **Seven rows, not 120:** represent the mass band by section voices with doubled velocities/octaves.
- **Formula is a scorecard, not a law:** technique counts correlate only weakly with placement (winners
  7–13/13); encode weights and allow deviation.
- **Tempo is fast and strict:** ~8.7 notes/s for 16ths at ♩ = 130; no rubato, small humanize sigma.
- **Pan idiom:** circle-of-fifths layout favours scale-wise/symmetrical lines; piano-like leaps and dense
  chromatic chord voicings are awkward.
- **Re-harmonization is the biggest sub-score (12 pts)** — plain diatonic setting loses.
- **Engine room is acoustic ensemble** (truck brake drum + nails, kitchen grater + iron comb, congas with
  pan sticks); GM percussion is only an approximation.
- **Length:** finals arrangements ~8+ min; extend sections rather than raising tempo.
- **Own tune must still behave like a calypso** (verse/chorus, hook, soca feel) or judges/crowd reject it.
- **Copyright/competition rules:** tune must not have been played by the same band in a previous Panorama;
  an arranger may work for only one band per category.

## 1. Description & Aesthetic Foundations

**Gagaku** (雅楽) is widely recognized as the oldest living orchestral tradition in the world. Within Gagaku, **Kangen** (管弦) refers specifically to purely instrumental music performed by an ensemble composed of three wind instruments (*san-kan*), two plucked string instruments (*ryō-gen*), and three percussion instruments (*san-ko*).

Unlike Western orchestration which emphasizes four-part homophonic choral blending or dynamic thematic contrast, Gagaku Kangen operates on principles of **stratified heterophony**, **spatial tone clusters**, and **elastic time flow** governed by the universal aesthetic of **Jo-Ha-Kyū** (序破急).

### The Three Instrumental Tiers
1. **The Three Winds (San-kan - Melodic & Harmonic Core)**:
   - **Hichiriki** (篳篥): Short, piercing, double-reed bamboo pipe. Emits an acoustically saturated timbre with up to 55 audible partials. It carries the primary melodic spine (**shumero**), inflected with vocal pitch bending (**enbai**).
   - **Ryūteki** (龍笛): Transverse bamboo flute representing the celestial dragon soaring between heaven and earth. It performs an ornamented, wide-ranging version of the melody that circles around and weaves through the hichiriki, filling melodic intervals with connective runs (**kakebuki**).
   - **Shō** (笙): 17-pipe mouth organ representing the mythical phoenix resting its wings. Playing 11 standard six-tone non-Western vertical clusters (**aitake**), it sounds continuously on both inhalation and exhalation. It forms a luminous harmonic canopy over the ensemble, shifting between chords via slow finger permutations (**te-utsuri**).

2. **The Two Strings (Ryō-gen - Temporal Marking & Punctuation)**:
   - **Gakubiwa** (楽琵琶): 4-string plucked lute struck with an oversized plectrum. Plays resonant downward arpeggios (**kakubachi**) on measure downbeats, damping vibrations quickly to mark structural time without muddying melodic lines.
   - **Gakusō** (楽箏): 13-string zither. Plucks standardized two-measure melodic ostinatos (**shizugaki** or **hayagaki**), doubling structural melodic notes at the octave and articulating intermediate rhythmic pulses.

3. **The Three Percussion (San-ko - Colotomic Framework)**:
   - **Kakko** (鞨鼓): Small two-headed barrel drum played with bare sticks by the conductor. Plays single strikes (**sei**), decelerating/accelerating rolls (**katarai**), and dense rolls (**morarai**) that signal sectional transitions.
   - **Shōkō** (鉦鼓): Suspended bronze gong struck with hard mallets, sounding single or double hits (**chiki-chin**) that mark interior measure subdivisions.
   - **Taiko / Tsuridaiko** (釣太鼓): Large suspended bass drum. Marks the macro-cycle with two strokes: the quiet preparatory left-hand tap (**mebachi**) and the climactic, resonant right-hand impact (**obachi**), which serves as the supreme temporal anchor of the entire orchestra.

### Structural Flow: Jo-Ha-Kyū
- **Jo (序)**: Slow, unmetered, contemplative opening. Begins with the **Netori** tuning prelude where instruments enter sequentially (*Shō* → *Hichiriki* → *Ryūteki* → *Kakko* → *Biwa* → *Koto*) to establish the modal universe (*chōshi*).
- **Ha (破)**: The main body, set in expansive **nobebyōshi** (8-measure phrase periods). Opens with a solo flute call (**ōndo**); the full ensemble enters together at the first *obachi* strike (**tsuku-dokoro**).
- **Kyū (急)**: Acceleration and climax. Rhythmic structure tightens into **hayabyōshi** (4-measure phrase periods, doubling the frequency of *obachi* impacts) or **kuwae** (halving the percussion cycle duration).
- **Tomede (止手)**: The coda. Winds conclude with a held chord (**fukinagashi**) and cut abruptly; percussion ceases; strings pluck isolated closing tones and fade into silence.

## 2. Step-by-Step Practitioner Craft Workflow

When traditional court composers (*gakunin*) assemble or perform a Kangen composition, they execute the following sequence:

1. **Establish the Modal Center (*Chōshi*)**:
   Select one of the six classical modes:
   - *Ritsu* family (neutral/dorian character): *Hyō-jō* (E ritsu), *Ōshiki-chō* (A ritsu), *Banshiki-chō* (B ritsu).
   - *Ryo* family (bright/mixolydian character): *Ichikotsu-chō* (D ryo), *Sōjō* (G ryo), *Taishiki-chō* (E ryo).
   String instruments adjust bridge positions and pegs; wind players mentally align with the fundamental resting tones (*shōon*).

2. **Craft the Netori Prelude**:
   Construct an unmetered, breath-paced introductory piece to attune the ensemble. Follow the strict entry protocol:
   - Step A: *Shō* breathes in the home mode *aitake* cluster.
   - Step B: *Hichiriki* enters on the modal tonic with *enbai* pitch bending.
   - Step C: *Ryūteki* floats an octave above with an ascending scalar gesture.
   - Step D: *Kakko* sounds a single strike (*sei*).
   - Step E: *Biwa* arpeggiates open strings.
   - Step F: *Koto* plucks the tonic-fifth tuning figure (*tsumu*).

3. **Compose the Skeletal Core Melody (*Shumero*)**:
   Write the central melodic line for the hichiriki. The melody consists of sustained whole notes and half notes centering on the 3–4 tonal anchors of the mode (e.g., E, B, A in *Hyō-jō*). Every leap is approached smoothly; long notes feature airflow-driven swells (*osu*) and pitch bends (*enbai*).

4. **Map the Shō Aitake Cluster Sequence**:
   For every tone of the *shumero*, assign its corresponding 6-note *aitake* chord from the 11 traditional formations (e.g. *kotsu*, *ichi*, *gyō*, *jū*, *bō*). The lowest pitch of each *aitake* doubles the melodic tone. Transitions are smoothly mapped using *te-utsuri* (gradual finger shifting, moving one pipe hole at a time so the cluster morphs without interrupting tone).

5. **Weave the Ryūteki Heterophonic Line**:
   Compose the flute part as an ornate envelope surrounding the hichiriki. The flute rhythmically anticipates the hichiriki's target notes, fills pauses with *kakebuki* scalar figures, and adds transient dissonances (e.g., touching D against C#) to generate directional forward momentum.

6. **Set the Colotomic Percussion Cycle (*Hyōshi*)**:
   Select the rhythmic framework:
   - *Nobebyōshi*: 8-measure cycle with one central *obachi* impact at bar 5.
   - *Hayabyōshi*: 4-measure cycle with an *obachi* impact every 4 bars.
   Score the percussion layers:
   - *Taiko*: *Mebachi* (soft tap) on beat 2 of the penultimate measure, followed by thunderous *obachi* on beat 1 of the target measure.
   - *Kakko*: Plays a rolling *katarai* accelerando into the *obachi*.
   - *Shōkō*: Strikes crisp double attacks (*chiki-chin*) on intermediate beats.

7. **Score String Punctuation**:
   - *Biwa*: Downward sweep arpeggio (*kakubachi*) on beat 1 of every measure, emphasizing the lowest modal root.
   - *Koto*: Two-measure *shizugaki* pattern beginning on beat 2, plucking upper octaves with the thumb and middle finger.

8. **Execute Macro-Form & Tomede Coda**:
   - Ha opens with solo flute *ōndo*.
   - Ensemble joins at *tsuku-dokoro* on the first *obachi*.
   - Advance through *Ha* to *Kyū*, doubling tempo and halving the colotomic period (*kuwae*).
   - Reach climax and initiate *tomede*: winds sustain final chord at full volume, then cut; percussion stops; koto and biwa play soft single notes and silence returns.

## 3. Real Practitioner Examples & Historical Repertoire

- **Minamoto no Hiromasa (源博雅, 918–980 CE)**: Heian prince, courtier, and polymath master of the ryūteki and biwa. Author of the *Shinsen Shinteki-fu*, which codified standard heterophonic flute realization techniques.
- **Tōgi Family (東儀家)**: Hereditary gakunin masters who have transmitted the hichiriki and gagaku performance practice continuously since the 8th century. Contemporary artist **Hideki Tōgi** (東儀秀樹) demonstrates classical technique alongside cross-cultural compositions.
- **Sukeyasu Shiba (芝祐靖, 1935–2019)**: Former member of the Kunaichō Music Department and founder of the renowned ensemble **Reigakusha** (伶楽舎), responsible for reviving ancient scores from the Heian period (*Kogaku*) and commissioning contemporary works from Toru Takemitsu.
- **Toru Takemitsu (武満徹, 1930–1996)**: Masterwork *Autumn Garden* (秋庭歌, 1973/1979) for full gagaku orchestra, directly employing classical *aitake*, *Jo-Ha-Kyū* temporality, and stratified Kangen voice-leading.
- **Canonical Works**:
  - *Etenraku* (越天楽): The definitive *Hyō-jō* Kangen piece, studied by all gakunin for its balance of *shumero*, *aitake* transitions, and *shizugaki* string patterns.
  - *Bairo* (陪臚): High-energy ceremonial work featuring distinctive *kakko* rolls and *hayabyōshi* drive.
  - *Somakusha* (蘇莫者): Solo dance piece featuring virtuoso ryūteki phrasing.

## 4. Musical Elements Framework & UnitMatrix Implementation

### Mapping to the Five Elements
- **PITCH**: Governed by the modal system (*chōshi*). Microtonal variance is restricted to winds (*enbai*, *orute*); strings and organ maintain fixed modal pitches.
- **RHYTHM**: Colotomic cyclicality. Beat is not an equal-spaced click, but a breathing expansion anchored by the *obachi* gong/drum convergence.
- **HARMONY**: Vertical tone clusters (*aitake*). Static 5- and 6-note clouds providing timbral atmosphere rather than Western functional cadence.
- **STRUCTURE**: Hierarchical *Jo-Ha-Kyū* (Intro / Expansion / Acceleration) ending in *Tomede* (dispersal coda).
- **TEXTURE**: Stratified heterophony: 3 Winds (core melody + counter-melody + cluster canopy) + 2 Strings (plucked accents & ostinatos) + 3 Percussion (colotomic timekeeping).

### UnitMatrix Voices & Sections
- **Voice 0 (Spine / Hichiriki)**: MIDI Program 71 (Clarinet/Oboe). Core *shumero* melody. Slow sustained steps, central tonal anchors, pitch-bend articulations.
- **Voice 1 (Counter-Melody / Ryūteki)**: MIDI Program 73 (Flute/Piccolo). High ornate counter-line, rapid *kakebuki* scalar sweeps, anticipations.
- **Voice 2 (Harmonic Canopy / Shō)**: MIDI Program 20 (Reed Organ/Pad). Continuous 6-note *aitake* clusters spanning bar lines, voice-led via *te-utsuri*.
- **Voice 3 (Temporal Strings / Biwa & Koto)**: MIDI Program 105 (Koto/Shamisen) or 24 (Acoustic Guitar). Beat-1 downward *kakubachi* arpeggios + 2-bar *shizugaki* ostinatos.
- **Voice 4 (Colotomic Percussion / Kakko, Taiko, Shōkō)**: Percussion Channel 9:
  - MIDI 36 (Bass Drum): *Taiko Obachi* master downbeat and *Mebachi* preparation.
  - MIDI 60/61 (Bongo/Cuica): *Kakko* rolls (*morarai*) and taps (*sei*).
  - MIDI 55 (Splash Cymbal / Bell): *Shōkō* double strikes.

### Sections
- **S0: `Netori`**: Free rhythm modal tuning prelude (staggered entry V2 → V0 → V1 → V4 → V3).
- **S1: `Ha_Ondo`**: Solo ryūteki opening phrase (unaccompanied flute call).
- **S2: `Ha_Tutti_Nobebyoshi`**: 8-measure broad cycle; full ensemble, contemplative tempo (~48 bpm).
- **S3: `Kyu_Hayabyoshi`**: 4-measure condensed cycle; doubled *obachi* frequency (~60 bpm).
- **S4: `Kyu_Kuwae`**: Percussion accelerando, halved rhythmic patterns, intense momentum.
- **S5: `Tomede_Coda`**: Final held chord (*fukinagashi*); percussion cuts; strings fade out.

### Engine Invariants & Production Gates
- **Zero-Drift**: All voices padded with silent terminal events to match exact section length.
- **Scale Containment**: All pitches strictly mapped to the chosen *chōshi* scale pool.
- **Heterophonic Subordination**: Voice 1 pitch deviations must resolve to Voice 0 pitches at structural downbeats.

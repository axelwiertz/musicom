## 1. Method name & tradition

**Gnawa Lila/Derdeba Ritual Composition — the *Treq* ("path")**: an all-night, strictly encoded sequence of seven suites (one per *mlouk* spirit family, each bound to a colour, incense, guembri riff, krakebs rhythm and dance), built from riff-ostinato, call-and-response verse, and controlled trance acceleration.

- **Tradition / Culture:** Moroccan Gnawa Sufi brotherhood — descendants of enslaved Sahelian West Africans (Hausa, Fulani, Bambara, Soninke) brought via the trans-Saharan slave trade (11th–17th c.); centres Marrakesh, Essaouira, Tangier, Rabat, Salé, Casablanca, Fes. Music emerges 16th–17th c. UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, Representative List (2019, ref. 01170). Sister traditions: Algerian *diwan*, Tunisian *stambeli*, Egyptian *zār* (same Sahelian possession-cult stratum — Hausa *bori*).

## 3. The craft procedure in detail (how the human executes it)

### Stage 0 — apprenticeship (oral transmission, no notation)
1. Join a *zriba* (brotherhood group) by lineage (*dar*/*zaouiya*). Children learn **krakebs** first, from ~age 7 (Hamid El Kasri at 7; Abdeslam Alikkane at 9), clapping refrains and absorbing the repertoire by ear.
2. Graduate to the **guembri** (3-string monoxyle lute — "gives the tonality and the rhythm") after years; *maâlem* title conferred by masters; apprenticeship spans a decade+.
3. Memorize the seven suites, their order, riffs, texts (Arabic/Darija + West African residue), and ritual dramaturgy (spirit → colour/incense/dance). Memory, not manuscript.

### Stage 1 — troupe & ritual frame
4. Assemble: *maâlem* (guembri + lead voice + authority), krakeb players (up to ~10+ pairs of iron castanets), *tbel* bass drummers, *moqadma/shufawa* clairvoyant (directs the *m'hella*: veils, incense, accessories), adepts.
5. Choose frame: private *lila* (healing, dusk→sunrise), *moussem* pilgrimage, or profane stage (Essaouira Gnaoua World Music Festival).

### Stage 2 — the aâda opening
6. Krakebs open with **continuous rhythmic patterns + short call-response refrains** — consecrating the space before the guembri enters.
7. Optional acrobatic *kouyou* dance by krakeb players (swirling procession).

### Stage 3 — opening the treq: riff composition
8. The **maâlem opens the treq** on the guembri — "the strictly encoded sequence of the ritual repertoire of music, dances, colours and incenses, that guides the ecstatic trip across the realms of the seven mlouk" (Wikipedia/Gnawa music).
9. **Riff craft:** short ostinato cell (1–2 bars) on **tonic & fifth** — "a language that emphasizes the tonic and fifth, with quavering pitch-play, especially pitch-flattening, around the third, the fifth, and sometimes the seventh." Right hand: thumb drops the bass-string **drone** ("brushless drop-thumb frailing") while fingers pick drum-like percussive patterns.
10. The riff is the **mlouk identity marker** — riff change = spirit change. Maâlems compose new riffs/variations in the family vocabulary; regional dialects colour it (marsaoui/marrakchi/gharbaoui/soussi — El Kasri fuses all four).

### Stage 4 — per-suite architecture
11. **Exposition:** krakebs lock the cycle (6/8 fundamental; 4/4 pieces; "interplay between triple and duple meters", ternary superposed on binary grid — Gulf-of-Guinea inheritance); guembri states the suite riff; maâlem sings first call phrase.
12. **Verse loop (call–response):** maâlem sings verses ("one phrase or a few lines are repeated over and over, so the song may last a long time" — one *melk* song can run 20–40 min); krakeb players + assembly answer the refrain. Guembri melody "closely related to vocal music and speech patterns."
13. **Ritual direction:** moqadma dresses the adept in the mlouk's colour veil, burns its incense; dancer enters **jedba** trance with the mlouk's footwork.
14. **Trance ramp:** tempo ladder accelerates; krakeb density + vocal insistence rise — "provoking a trance from different angles"; at peak the dancer collapses/whirls; suite **cuts abruptly** or segues.
15. **Cadence/segue:** maâlem pivots the riff to the next family ("a 20-minute piece may be a whole series of pieces — a suite for Sidi Moussa, Sidi Hamou, Sidi Mimoun…").

### Stage 5 — the seven suites (treq order)
16. Seven *mlouk* families evoked by **"seven melodic and rhythmic cells"**, each with colour (white, dark blue, red, black, yellow/green…), incense, rhythm, dance. First: Sidi Moussa (white) with archetypal opener *Baba Al Arabi*; Sidi Mimoun (black) heaviest; night closes with final family, "renaissance in the common world, at the first lights of dawn."
17. Whole-night arc: **aâda → suite 1 → … → suite 7 → dawn close** — monotonic intensification.

### Stage 6 — secular export & fusion
18. Street/festival versions shorten the treq; jazz/fusion keeps the ostinato spine (Randy Weston's *The Splendid Master Gnawa Musicians of Morocco*, Grammy-nominated 1996; Jimi Hendrix took lessons from Mahmoud Guinia).

## 4. Practitioner examples

- **Maâlem Mahmoud Guinia** ("the King", Essaouira, 1951–2015) — most-recorded maâlem; Pharoah Sanders, Carlos Santana; Hendrix studied at his house; son of Boubker Gania; brothers Mokhtar & Abdellah; reference *marsaoui* lilas.
- **Maâlem Hamid El Kasri** (Ksar El Kbir → Rabat) — renowned voice; explicitly fuses gharbaoui/marsaoui/soussi; with Jacob Collier, Snarky Puppy; famous *Baba Mimoun*.
- **Maâlem Hassan Hakmoun** (Marrakech → New York) — trance-gnawa to Western stages (Peter Gabriel, Kronos Quartet); marrakchi school.
- **Maâlem Abdeslam Alikkane & Tyour Gnawa** (Essaouira) — healing-oriented; Peter Gabriel, Gilberto Gil, Ray Lema.
- **Maâlem Brahim Belkane** ("the traditionalist", Marrakech): "There are many colours on earth… you have to find these when you play, to be bright like the sun."
- **Nass El Ghiwane** (1970s, Laarbi Batma) — gnawa riffs into popular song.
- **Randy Weston / Bill Laswell / Pharoah Sanders / Adam Rudolph** — jazz-gnawa dialogues (Weston Grammy nomination 1996; Laswell *Night Spirit Masters*).
- **Scholars:** Philip Schuyler, Viviane Lièvre, Deborah Kapchan, Bertrand Hell (with Mahjoub Khalmous).

- **V1 = Krakebs stratum A** — on-beat/strong-subdivision pairs; cycle grid.

- **V3 = Lead vocal (maâlem)** — call verses; speech-derived contour; ornaments; pitch-bends.
- **V4 = Chorus response** — short echo cells (exact echo / tonal echo / vocable fill); never new material.
- **V5 = Tbel bass drum** — downbeats / trance peaks (optional).
- **V6 = Metadata/ritual layer** — mlouk, colour, incense, dance/kouyou, dialect, tempo-ladder stage, ritual-vs-stage tag.

### Sections
Columns = the *treq* sequence: **S0 aâda** (krakebs + refrains, V0 silent) → **S1–S7** seven mlouk suites (each: riff exposition → call-response verse loop → trance ramp → cut/segue) → **S8 dawn close**. Suites subdivide internally: riff cell, N call→response verse pairs, ramp cells, terminal cut cell.

### Rules to encode
1. **Treq order invariant** — suite sequence fixed (V6 tags); no reordering.
2. **Riff constraint (V0):** tonic-5th skeleton cell (1–2 bars), b3/b5/b7 bend flags; identity = riff dictionary keyed per mlouk+dialect.
3. **Krakebs interlock (V1+V2):** A on strong / B on weak subdivisions; sum = full cycle; ternary-over-binary superimposition (3-divisible quantization atop 4/4 at 480 tpb); both strata padded to full column length (zero-drift safe).
4. **Call–response alternation (V3→V4):** every verse cell followed by a response cell; responses ∈ {exact echo, tonal echo, vocable fill}; responses introduce no new melodic material.
5. **Trance ramp:** per-suite tempo ladder (BPM steps between cells), krakebs density/velocity growth, vocal phrase lengthening; terminal cell = abrupt cut or segue-pivot riff.
6. **Modal constraint:** no functional harmony — vertical sonorities incidental (HC-003-style rule); tonic-5th anchors; V0 drone invariant per suite.
7. **Duration rule:** ritual cells are minutes long — engine loops riff cells with seeded variation; per-suite cap flag for non-ritual renderings.
8. **Zero-drift gate:** `validate()` must pass before `to_midi` (pad V0 drone & V1/V2 strata via `_unit_from_events` pattern).

## 8. Quirks & pitfalls

- **Oral, not written:** no canonical notation; lineages differ in suite order and riff vocabulary — DB entry = general architecture, not a fixed score.
- **Ritual time vs engine time:** lilas run until dawn; UnitMatrix realization must cap suites (composer's model, not ceremony replacer).
- **Microtones:** b3/b5/b7 flattening is genuine pitch-play — encode as bend tags, don't force equal-temperament "wrong notes."
- **Call–response is structural** — the response is the trance-loop motor; removing it collapses the form.
- **Binary-ternary interplay is the signature** — a strict 6/8 rendering misses the groove; keep the superimposition rule.
- **Sacred context:** lilas are healing rites; profane versions shorten/adapt the treq — metadata carries ritual vs stage tag.

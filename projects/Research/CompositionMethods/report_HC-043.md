## 1. Craft Overview & Cultural Context

In the rolling plains and wheat fields of the Baixo Alentejo region in southern Portugal, **Cante Alentejano** represents one of Europe's most distinctive and austere unbroken traditions of vernacular vocal polyphony. Inscribed by UNESCO on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2014, Cante is an exclusively **a cappella**, slow-tempo, responsorial, and homophonic choral practice.

Historically sung by rural agricultural day-laborers (*jornaleiros*, *ceifeiros*, *mondadeiras*) in the wheat fields, olive groves, and later in communal village taverns (*tabernas*) and cultural centers (*Casas do Povo*), Cante embodies both collective social solidarity and deep existential longing (*saudade* / *alentejanidade*). It requires no musical instruments, no conducting baton, and traditionally no written scores. Instead, it relies on a tripartite acoustic architecture governed by three strictly delineated vocal roles:

1. **O Ponto** (The Pitch-Setter / Leader): A solo male or female singer (typically a baritone or lower tenor) whose sole responsibility is to introduce the song (*dar o tom* / set the pitch and pace) by singing the initial verses or couplet solo in a steady, solemn, unornamented manner.
2. **O Alto** (The Descant / Ornamenter): A high solo voice (typically a high tenor or counter-tenor in male groups, or a soaring soprano in female ensembles) that takes over the melody at the cadence of the Ponto. The Alto sounds a third (or tenth) above the principal melodic skeleton and decorates it with expressive melismatic flourishes, pitch glides, and portamento ornamentations.
3. **Os Baixos / O Coro** (The Bass Choral Foundation): The full collective body of singers (from 15 to 35+ members) who enter together with or immediately following the Alto. The choir sings the primary melodic contour in resonant unison/octaves below the Alto, creating a heavy, monolithic acoustic column that anchors the performance. The Ponto rejoins this choral foundation.

The resulting texture is predominantly **organum-like parallel thirds and sixths** floating over a solid choral ground, sustained at an unhurried, ceremonial tempo (typically 52–68 BPM) that breathes with natural speech-prosody and collective lung capacity.

## 2. Practitioner Craft Workflow (Step-by-Step)

When a master *ensaio* (rehearsal) or traditional spontaneous performance unfolds in an Altiplano tavern or on stage by groups such as the *Grupo Coral e Etnográfico da Casa do Povo de Serpa*, the human craft follows seven rigorous procedural stages:

### Step 1: Mode, Gamut, and Poetic Stanza Selection
- The singers choose a *moda* (traditional song) structured on strophic oral poetry—most frequently an octosyllabic *quadra* (four-line stanza: $a-b-a-b$ or $a-b-b-a$), often extended into *décimas* (ten-line stanzas) or paired with a *cantiga* (refrain).
- The modal space is fixed within ancient Church-mode vestiges: primarily **Dorian** (minor 3rd, major 6th), **Phrygian** (minor 2nd, descending cadences), **Aeolian** (natural minor), or major-inflected **Ionian**.
- Tuning is purely modal and relative; there is no concert pitch reference (A440). The gamut is chosen to sit comfortably in the chest register of the choir while pushing the *Alto* into their upper resonant register.

### Step 2: "Dar o Tom" (The Ponto's Ground Solo)
- The *Ponto* steps forward slightly or centers themselves physically within the semicircular rank of singers.
- Without pitch pipes or instruments, the Ponto strikes the opening phrase in isolation: singing the first line or two of the stanza (e.g., lines 1 and 2 of the *quadra*).
- The Ponto establishes:
  1. The absolute pitch center of the song.
  2. The solemn, deliberate metric pace ($d \approx 54\text{--}64\text{ BPM}$).
  3. The rhythmic breath-grouping and syllabic cadence.
- The Ponto delivers the line with firm, direct, syllabic clarity, avoiding virtuosic vibrato or melisma.

### Step 3: The Alto's Entry and Descant Ascent
- At the penultimate syllable or immediate resolution of the Ponto's phrase, the *Alto* cuts in cleanly and authoritatively.
- The Alto sings the melodic answer or repeats the initial phrase text, but transposes the core contour **a major or minor third higher** (or a tenth higher when singing above the lower bass register).
- The Alto's delivery contrasts dramatically with the Ponto:
  - Higher vocal tessitura and piercing projection.
  - Melismatic turns and micro-ornaments (*vocalizos*, grace inflections).
  - Subtle rhythmic stretching (*rubato*) across the primary beats.

### Step 4: Choral Ignition ("Entrada dos Baixos")
- Exactly as the Alto establishes the descant—or precisely on the designated syllable of the Alto's phrase—the entire choir (*os baixos*, accompanied by the Ponto who steps back into the rank) enters simultaneously in a massive, unified vocal surge.
- The choir takes over the principal melodic spine originally announced by the Ponto, sounding in rich baritone/bass unison and lower octaves.
- The arrival of the choir creates an instant vertical sonority: a consonant, resonant vertical dyad of **parallel thirds, octaves, and fifths**.

### Step 5: Parallel-Third Voice-Leading & Harmonic Alternation
- As the stanza unfolds, the Alto and the Coro move in homophonic, syllabic synchrony:
  - The choir moves by conjunct modal steps.
  - The Alto shadows the choir note-for-note at the parallel third above, expanding occasionally to fourths or fifths at cadential points to maintain modal purity without introducing forbidden leading tones.
- Harmonically, Cante alternates between two primary poles:
  1. **Tonic Sonority** (Rest / Stasis): Choir on modal final ($\hat{1}$ or $\hat{3}$), Alto on $\hat{3}$ or $\hat{5}$.
  2. **Subtonal/Dominant Sonority** (Tension / Forward Motion): Choir on $\hat{7}$ (flattened seventh) or $\hat{2}$, Alto a third above on $\hat{2}$ or $\hat{4}$.
  - In Dorian/Aeolian modas, the progression often rocks back and forth between $i$ and $\text{VII}$ (or $i$ and $v$), reflecting ancient modal polyphony rather than functional Western triadic dominants.

### Step 6: Cadential Elongation & Fermata Suspension
- Phrases conclude on prolonged cadential syllables.
- The choir and Alto hold the penultimate and final notes into sustained, unmetered fermatas, allowing the vocal overtones (*harmónicos*) to accumulate and reverberate.
- Cadences almost universally resolve onto an open unison/octave or an open fifth, with the Alto either dropping down to the octave or resting cleanly on the structural third.
- A momentary collective inhalation (a silent breath pause) cleanly separates the strophes.

### Step 7: Strophic Rotation & Collective Stance
- The cycle repeats for subsequent stanzas (Stanza 2, Stanza 3, and Refrain/Cantiga).
- In traditional etiquette, the singers stand shoulder-to-shoulder, arms linked or hands resting on their neighbors' shoulders, swaying with imperceptible microscopic rocking, embodying the physical cohesion of the harvesting line (*rancho de ceifeiros*).

## 3. Real Practitioner References

- **Grupo Coral e Etnográfico da Casa do Povo de Serpa (Serpa, Portugal)**:
  - Founded in 1928, one of the oldest and most celebrated traditional Cante groups in the world.
  - Standard-bearers of the solemn, deliberate Baixo Alentejo style; definitive recordings of iconic modas such as *"Alentejo, Alentejo"* and *"Ceifeira da Madrugada"*.
  - Renowned for pristine synchronization between their legendary *Altos* and massed 30-voice choir.
- **Grupo Coral "Os Ceifeiros de Cuba" (Cuba, Alentejo)**:
  - Historical ensemble hailing from Cuba (Beja district), rooted directly in rural harvest songs and peasant union halls.
  - Master practitioners of the *cantiga-moda* alternation, characterized by resonant, guttural bass layers and biting Dorian descants.
- **Associação Grupo Coral Ceifeiros da Amareleja (Amareleja / Moura)**:
  - Hailing from one of the hottest agricultural towns in Portugal, famous for intense, slow-burning modas that mirror the relentless heat and hard labor of the Alentejo plains.
  - Exemplars of the traditional *taberna* singing lineage, where informal tavern circles transitioned to institutionalized choral groups.
- **Grupo Coral Feminino "As Ceifeiras de Pias" (Pias / Serpa)**:
  - Pioneering all-female Cante group formed after the 1974 Carnation Revolution.
  - Transposed the traditionally male tavern polyphony into the high, piercing register of female agricultural weeders (*mondadeiras*), preserving the identical Ponto–Alto–Baixos architecture an octave higher.
- **Salwa El-Shawan Castelo-Branco & Rui Vieira Nery**:
  - Foremost ethnomusicologists whose extensive fieldwork and theoretical codification of Cante Alentejano served as the scientific bedrock for the UNESCO 2014 nomination dossier.

## 4. Musical Elements & UnitMatrix Mapping

### Musical Elements
- **TEXTURE**: Highly stratified three-tier vocal polyphony (Solo Ponto leader → Solo Alto descant → Massed Baixos choral foundation). Strictly homophonic once the choir enters, featuring parallel thirds and sixths.
- **PITCH**: Strict modal gamuts (Dorian, Aeolian, Phrygian, natural minor). No Western chromatic leading tones. Range typically spans two octaves from low bass (E2/A2) to high descant (E4/A4).
- **STRUCTURE**: Responsorial strophic form (**Ponto Statement $\rightarrow$ Alto Answer $\rightarrow$ Choir Tutti $\rightarrow$ Cadential Hold $\rightarrow$ Repeat**). Symmetrical 8-to-16-bar strophes divided into clear antecedent and consequent hemistiches.
- **HARMONY**: Modal dyadic polyphony. Parallel 3rds and 10ths predominate. Harmonic movement oscillates primarily between modal tonic ($i$) and subtonic ($\text{VII}$) or subdominant ($iv$). No functional circle-of-fifths or dominant-seventh cadences.
- **RHYTHM**: Deliberately slow, solemn, and unhurried ($d = 52\text{--}64\text{ BPM}$). Symmetrical $2/4$ or $4/4$ isometric pacing tied to poetic syllable duration, concluding with elongated fermatas on cadential endings.

### Encoding Rules for Musicom Engine

1. **Ponto Solo Priority Rule (`PONTO_SOLO_GATE`)**:
   - In Section `S0`, Voice 0 (*Ponto*) owns 100% of the acoustic space.
   - Voices 1, 2, 3, and 4 MUST be completely silent (or padded with silent rests).
   - Voice 0 states the diatonic/Dorian thematic seed with syllabic clarity.

2. **Alto Descant Rule (`ALTO_PARALLEL_THIRD`)**:
   - Voice 1 (*Alto*) tracks the pitch sequence of the choral foundation transposed by an exact diatonic third (interval of +3 or +4 semitones depending on modal degree) or compound tenth (+15 or +16 semitones).
   - In Section `S1`, Voice 1 enters alone, establishing the high register.
   - Voice 1 features a higher micro-ornament density (anticipations, grace steps) than the choir.

3. **Choral Mass Rule (`BAIXOS_UNISON_COUPLING`)**:
   - In Sections `S2` through `S4`, Voice 2 (*Baixos Lead*) and Voice 3 (*Baixos Deep*) replicate the melodic contour established by the Ponto, sounding in parallel octaves ($P_8$).
   - Voice 0 (*Ponto*) rejoins Voice 2 in unison, symbolizing the soloist's re-absorption into the community.
   - High velocity ($V \approx 90\text{--}105$) and dense legato sustain simulate a 25-man choral body.

4. **Modal Progression Constraint (`MODAL_HARMONIC_OSCILLATION`)**:
   - Harmonic progression is strictly non-functional: alternates between Modal Center $i$ (Dorian/Minor final) and Subtonic $\text{VII}$ / Subdominant $iv$.
   - Vertical intervals between Alto (V1) and Baixos (V2) must be consonant diatonic thirds, tenths, or sixths on strong beats.

5. **Cadential Convergence Rule (`CADENCIA_FERMATA_RULE`)**:
   - In Section `S4`, note durations are doubled or quadrupled, simulating a natural vocal fermata without tempo disruption.
   - The Alto and Choir converge onto an open fifth or unadorned octave/unison at the terminal measure, followed by a mandatory zero-sound rest representing the collective breath before the next strophe.

6. **Zero-Drift Invariant (`ZERO_DRIFT_GATE`)**:
   - Total ticks per section:
     - `S0`: $4 \times 1920 = 7680$ ticks
     - `S1`: $2 \times 1920 = 3840$ ticks

     - `S3`: $4 \times 1920 = 7680$ ticks
     - `S4`: $2 \times 1920 = 3840$ ticks

### Paradigm

**Rules-Based** — deterministic combinatorial design theory. Given the order $n$, the construction family (cyclic / finite-field / Klein / randomized-row / Sudoku-solver), and the symbol→music bijection, the design is fixed or sampled from a seeded RNG: no corpus statistics, no gradient, no fitness function, no learning. ### Description

LSAC composes the piece as a **Latin square (or Latin rectangle / orthogonal array / Sudoku grid) laid over the UnitMatrix**: rows = voices, columns = sections, symbols = pitch classes, so that **every voice and every section is a complete aggregate** — each voice's row is a permutation of the symbol set, each section's column is a permutation too. This is the 2D generalization of twelve-tone thinking: 065's serial matrix is exactly one special order-12 Latin square (the dihedral row-form table); LSAC treats the whole 240-year-old theory as a compositional engine.

The mathematical spine:

1. **Latin squares & rectangles (Euler 1782; Choi 1700).** An $n{\times}n$ square with $n$ symbols, each once per row and column. Reduced-square counts (OEIS A000315/A002860): $R(5)=56$, $R(6)=9408$, $R(9)=377{,}597{,}570{,}964{,}258{,}816$, total $L(9)=5{,}524{,}751{,}496{,}156{,}892{,}842{,}531{,}225{,}600$ — an astronomically large but *classifiable* form space (isotopy classes: 1,1,1,2,2,22,564,1,676,267,115,618,721,533 for $n\le 9$). For non-square UnitMatrices use a **Latin rectangle** ($r{\times}n$, $r<n$): every voice = a permutation; every section = an $r$-pc chord with no pc repeated inside the section.
2. **Orthogonality = the factorial-design law (Euler; Rao 1947; Bose–Shrikhande–Parker 1959).** Two Latin squares are orthogonal if the $n^2$ ordered pairs of their cells are all distinct — a **Graeco-Latin square**. Musically: $L_1$ = pitch class, $L_2$ = duration/articulation/velocity class, and the piece becomes a **complete factorial design** — every $(pc \times \mathrm{attribute})$ combination occurs exactly once and no cell-pair repeats. Existence is fully classified: orthogonal pairs exist for **all** orders except $n=2,6$ (Tarry proved 6; Bose–Shrikhande–Parker disproved Euler's conjecture for every $n\equiv2\pmod4$, $n\ge10$). As an orthogonal array this is $\mathrm{OA}(n^2,2,n,2)$ (Rao 1947); prime-power orders admit **complete sets of $n-1$ MOLS** — equivalently $\mathrm{OA}(n^2,n{+}1,n,2)$ and an affine plane of order $n$ — i.e. up to $n{+}1$ *mutually balanced* musical attributes (pc, duration, articulation, register, density…) with every pair co-occurring exactly once.
3. **Sudoku = phrase-level aggregates.** A Sudoku grid is an order-9 Latin square plus the **3×3 box rule**: each 3-voice × 3-section block is also a complete 9-set. Reading boxes as phrases, every phrase, every section, and every voice is an aggregate — hierarchical completeness at three levels. $6.67{\times}10^{21}$ grids exist, $5.47{\times}10^9$ essentially different; **17 is the minimum clue count** that forces a unique completion (McGuire 2012) — the provably most economical compositional skeleton.
4. **Hall/SDR = feasibility (Hall 1935).** A compositional sketch (partial pc assignment) extends to a full design iff a system-of-distinct-representatives condition holds; *any* Latin rectangle completes to a Latin square; any partial square with $\le n-1$ filled cells always completes. Completion in general is NP-complete (Colbourn 1984) — so: keep skeletons small (Hall-guaranteed) or pay solver cost.
5. **Transversals = through-composed threads (Ryser 1967).** A transversal (rainbow matching) is $n$ cells hitting every row, column, and symbol once — a melodic thread that visits every voice, every section, and every pc exactly once. Ryser's conjecture: odd orders always have one (verified for cyclic order 5); the cyclic square $(i+j)\bmod n$ of **even** order has *none* (verified orders 6, 8); guaranteed partial transversals of size $n-\sqrt n$ (Woolbright 1978) / $n-O(\log n/\log\log n)$ (Hatami–Shor 2008) give near-complete threads. Verified: cyclic order 8 has a partial transversal of size 7.
6. **The tension taxonomy falls out of existence theorems.** HOME = prime-power order (complete MOLS — full attribute balance, maximal evenness of co-occurrence); TENSE = the Eulerian void: order 6 has *no* orthogonal mate at all (hard structural impossibility) and order 2 is trivial; LIFT = the order-10 surprise: a Graeco-Latin pair exists (Bose–Shrikhande–Parker) yet no complete MOLS set exists (no order-10 plane, Lam 1991) — balance exists but is partial; TURN = transversal/thread moves (or the key-rotation of isotopy). The composer *picks* an order per section-group and gets provable tension behavior for free.
7. **Isotopy / paratopy = the transformation group.** Permuting rows (re-voicing), columns (re-ordering sections), or symbols (transposition/key-change) preserves the design; paratopic conjugation swaps the roles of rows/columns/symbols — "rotate the piece": sections become voices, or symbols become rows (register counterpoint). The 9×9 Sudoku demo (verified): pc map $\{1{\to}0, 2{\to}2, 3{\to}4, 4{\to}5, 5{\to}7, 6{\to}9, 7{\to}11, 8{\to}1, 9{\to}3\}$ over a 9-pc nonatonic — every row, every column, and every 3×3 box is a complete permutation of the 9 pitch classes.

### Musical Elements Framework

- **PITCH**: symbols = pitch classes / scale degrees; the row = a voice's aggregate pc-plan (a "row" in the twelve-tone sense but unordered — a pc *set* per cell, ordered by the concrete layer); the column = the section's simultaneity set; symbol renaming (isotopy) = transposition; the pc→scale mapping (e.g. the 9-pc nonatonic demo) is a design-time bijection, exactly as 113 PCFSC maps Fourier phase to degrees. Register is deliberately left to the concrete layer (Pitfall 12).
- **RHYTHM**: the second (Graeco-Latin) square carries duration classes — every $(pc, \mathrm{duration\text{-}class})$ pair occurs exactly once, so rhythmic variety is *bounded and exhausted* by the design budget; per-cell duration class → rhythmic archetype (Euclidean pattern, arpeggio, sustained) in the concrete layer; Sudoku boxes balance duration classes at phrase level; transversal steps = a one-note-per-slot thread (a built-in hocket rate).
- **HARMONY**: columns = chord sets: $n=12$ gives twelve-pc aggregate columns (dodecaphonic harmony in every section — the 2D serial statement); an $r{\times}n$ rectangle gives $r$-note chords with no internal pc repetition; orthogonality forbids any repeated $(pc, \mathrm{attribute})$ pair — the "no repeated combination" harmonic law; MOLS attribute columns extend harmony to $n{+}1$ mutually balanced dimensions at prime-power orders. The Eulerian-order taxonomy *is* the tension curve (HOME/TENSE/LIFT/TURN per section-group).
- **STRUCTURE**: the design is the macro-form map — $n$ sections × $n$ voices, or $n$ sections over an $r{\times}n$ rectangle; Sudoku 3×3 boxes = phrase grouping (A/B/C blocks as aggregate-complete phrases); transversals = through-composed threads spanning the whole form; isotopy = re-voicing/re-ordering/re-keying without changing the design class; paratopy = form rotation (voices↔sections); reduced-square / isotopy-class counts quantify "how many structurally different pieces" of an order exist.
- **TEXTURE**: extra orthogonal-array columns carry articulation/register/density classes — independent texture dimensions that are *mutually balanced* (every pair of texture attributes co-occurs exactly once); row/column orthogonality gives voice independence by construction (in any section, voice $i$'s pc-set is disjoint from voice $j$'s — no unison doublings inside a column); aggregate columns force full-spectrum density (all $n$ pcs sounding per section); boxes balance density across phrases.

### UnitMatrix Integration (Voices & Sections)

**Voices = rows.** Row $v$ of the design is voice $v$'s plan: a permutation of the symbol set over the $n$ sections (an aggregate row); in an $r{\times}n$ rectangle, $r$ voices each hold their own permutation (4 voices × 12 sections = four aggregate melodies over a 12-pc set with 4-pc chords per section). Voice independence is *orthogonality*: no two voices share a pc in any section.

**Sections = columns.** Column $j$ is section $j$'s simultaneity set — the harmonic content of the section (12-pc aggregate, $r$-pc chord, or Sudoku column). The column sequence is the progression; box blocks (Sudoku) group sections into aggregate-complete phrases; the order of the columns (section order) is an isotopy parameter — reordering is legitimate design surgery.

**Cells = MusicUnit.** The abstract plan assigns each cell its pc (from $L_1$) and its attribute class (from $L_2$ / extra OA columns). The concrete feeder then resolves each cell: pc → pitch in the voice's register ladder (scale map + octave plan), attribute class → rhythmic archetype + velocity/articulation, events padded to `section_len` via `_unit_from_events`, standard `UnitMatrixComposer` spine → `validate()` (zero-drift gate) → `to_midi()`. Worked plan (verified): the 9×9 Sudoku design — 9 voices × 9 sections, every row/column/box a complete 9-pc set; realized as a plan matrix of pcs, ready for concrete filling.

**Candidate code path:** `rules/subset_network.py` — `LatinSquare(order, family='cyclic'|'field'|'klein')`, `LatinRectangle(r, n)`, `GraecoLatinPair(n)` (existence-checked via the $n=2,6$ theorem), `MOLSField(p^k)` (finite-field construction, $n{-}1$ mates), `SudokuGrid(order, boxes)` (MRV backtracking solver, 17-clue check), `SDRCompletion(partial)` (Hall-checked row extension), `TransversalSearch(design)` (rainbow matching), `TensionOrder(n)` (prime-power / Eulerian / BSP taxonomy), `LSACPlan.to_design()` (pc + attribute matrices for the concrete feeder). Build cost $\mathcal{O}(n^2)$ storage; JM sampling poly-time mixing; completion NP-complete in general (practical $\mathcal{O}(n^3)$ via MRV).

### Pitfalls

1. **A Latin square is square.** The UnitMatrix is usually not $n{\times}n$ — use a **Latin rectangle** ($r$ voices × $n$ sections, $r<n$) or block-diagonal squares for $r>n$; do not pad to a square and silently drop cells (the design then underdetermines the piece).
2. **The Eulerian void is real.** Orders 2 and 6 have *no* orthogonal mate (Tarry 1901). If the piece is 6 voices × 6 sections and you promise $(pc{\times}\mathrm{duration})$ balance, it is *impossible* — that impossibility is itself the TENSE device, but only if intended. Order 10 has a mate but no complete MOLS set (Lam 1991) — partial balance only.
3. **Even cyclic squares are thread-less.** The round $(i+j)\bmod n$ square of even order has no transversal (verified $n=6,8$); odd orders always have one (Ryser conjecture, verified $n=5$). Do not script a "through-composed thread" over an even cyclic design — search first, or use an odd order / non-cyclic square.
4. **Not a re-skin of the serial matrix.** 065's 12×12 serial matrix *is* a Latin square — but 065 is the dihedral row-form system (P/R/I/RI × transpositions); LSAC is the general design theory: arbitrary orders, Graeco-Latin orthogonality, SDR/Hall completion, transversals, isotopy/paratopy, Sudoku blocks, OA attribute columns. If a passage needs row-form ordering discipline, that is 065; LSAC's rows are *unordered* pc-sets.
5. **Not 111 / 125 / 076.** 111 VTCC tiles the *timeline* with rhythm canons (no pitch aggregates); 125 LDSC is a 1D equidistributed *sequence*; 076 PTM-ASC is a 1D automatic *sequence*. LSAC is the only DB method whose artifact is a **2D matrix design with simultaneous row and column aggregate completeness**.
6. **Abstract layer: emit the plan, not the notes.** The design fixes *which* pc/attribute in *which* cell; it does not fix onsets, durations, octaves, or velocities. Every "music" claim in this section is a claim about set structure (as in 113/133) — realize through a concrete feeder and `validate()`.
7. **Aggregate columns are dense.** $n=12$ voices × 12-pc columns = twelve simultaneous pcs per section: thick. Thin via Latin rectangles ($r<n$), or delegate arpeggiation/hocketing to the concrete layer. The design constrains *sets*, not *voicing*.
8. **Isotopy changes keys.** Renaming symbols = transposition; permuting columns = reordering sections. Fix pc 0 = tonic (or a key scheme) before sampling/constructing, or the "same design" silently shifts key every run.
9. **Sudoku uniqueness needs ≥ 17 clues.** Below 17 a skeleton does not force a unique grid — fine for stochastic completion, wrong if the piece must be deterministic. And 9×9 Sudoku solving is NP-complete in general: use MRV + candidate propagation (the verified solver handles 17-clue grids in milliseconds), not naive first-empty backtracking.
10. **Completion is NP-complete (Colbourn 1984).** Hall guarantees completion for Latin rectangles and $\le n-1$ filled cells — stay in that regime for guaranteed skeletons, or budget solver time. Do not claim "always completable" for arbitrary partial designs.
11. **Sampling is not uniform unless you use JM.** The quick randomized-row-extension sampler produces *valid* Latin squares but is biased (verified: 50/50 valid, diverse); the uniform sampler is Jacobson–Matthews (1996) and needs burn-in. Document which sampler the piece used and seed it.
12. **pc ≠ pitch.** The design's symbols are pitch *classes*; register, octave, and voicing live in the concrete layer. Twelve pcs across twelve voices in one octave is mud — pair the design with a register ladder per voice (as 104 PSSC / 113 PCFSC feeders do).
13. **Transversals may not be unique — and may not exist.** The thread is a search result, not an assumption: even cyclic squares have none (verified), and a square can have many transversals. Pick one via the seed; document the search.

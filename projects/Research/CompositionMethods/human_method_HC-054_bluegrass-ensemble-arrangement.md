# HC-054: Bluegrass Ensemble Arrangement — Scruggs-Style Banjo Roll & High Lonesome Vocal Harmony

- **Layer:** human (→concrete)
- **Tradition:** American Bluegrass — Kentucky/Tennessee/Appalachia, USA, ~1945–present
- **Primary Elements:** RHYTHM, PITCH, STRUCTURE, TEXTURE, HARMONY
- **Status:** 📝 Documented

---

## Description

Bluegrass is an acoustic string-band genre born from Bill Monroe and the Blue Grass Boys (1945). Its composition/arranging craft is a **rotating-break song architecture** built on five acoustic instruments (banjo, fiddle, mandolin, guitar, upright bass) and a distinctive three-part vocal stack (lead–tenor–baritone). The arranging method is both a composition framework (what each instrument plays and when) and a performance script (who solos in what order, how fills trade, where the drive lives).

---

## Craft Procedure (step by step)

### 1. Song form & harmonic grid
- Compose strophic AABA/ABAB song form (verse 8 bars, chorus 8 bars, optional bridge 8 bars).
- Fix key (open-string friendly: G, C, D, A — banjo capoed, fiddle standard tuning).
- Write chord progression (I–IV–V dominate; minor vi and flat-VII common in modal tunes).
- Tempo: 110–160 BPM for up-tempo, 70–100 BPM for ballads/waltzes.

### 2. Rhythm-section spine (the drive)
- **Bass** (Voice 0): root-5 walk pattern, quarter-notes on beats 1 & 3, with walking runs between chord roots on beats 2 & 4. Never stops. Anticipates the beat slightly (drive feel).
- **Guitar** (Voice 1): boom-chuck — bass note on downbeats (1 & 3 for 4/4), strummed brush on offbeats (2 & 4). G-run (descending open-position cliché: G–F#–E–D–B–G) on turnarounds.
- **Mandolin chop** (Voice 2): percussive muted chord strike on beats 2 & 4 (snare-drum backbeat function). Fills between vocal phrases with flatpicked eighth-note runs.

### 3. Banjo roll layer (Scruggs style)
- **Banjo** (Voice 3): 5-string, G tuning (gDGBD), three-finger roll patterns:
  - **Forward roll**: T–I–M–T–I–M–T–I (thumb–index–middle)
  - **Backward roll**: M–I–T–M–I–T–M–I
  - **Forward-reverse roll**: T–I–M–T–M–I–T–M
  - **Mixed roll**: combination patterns anchored to chord shapes
- Roll vocabulary follows melody loosely (Scruggs-style paraphrase), never plays chords in strum fashion.
- Backup: rolling fills between vocal phrases, chop-like on vocals by vamping/cross-picking chord positions.
- Break: full melodic statement using same rolls, typically 8 or 16 bars.

### 4. Fiddle obbligato & backup
- **Fiddle** (Voice 4): double-stop chord tones on 2&4 (mimics mandolin chop but with sustained drone). Long-bow harmony lines under vocals.
- Break: sings the vocal melody with bowing ornaments (slides, double shuffles, grace notes).
- Twin-fiddle harmony: second fiddle plays parallel 3rds above or 6ths below lead fiddle.

### 5. Vocal harmony stack
- **Lead** (middle voice): melody, sits between tenor (above) and baritone (below).
- **Tenor** (high voice): chord tone immediately above lead — usually a 3rd above, sometimes a 4th when melody is on the 5th of the chord.
- **Baritone** (low voice): remaining chord tone below lead — usually a 3rd or 6th below.
- **Vocal trio variants**: Standard = baritone–lead–tenor; High Baritone = lead–tenor–high baritone; High Lead (Osborne) = low tenor–baritone–lead.
- Rule: all three voices always sing chord tones only (root, 3rd, 5th, occasional ♭7 for blues color). No passing tones. No doubling the same pitch class.

### 6. Break rotation & arrangement arc
- **Kickoff**: instrumental intro (2–4 bars), lead instrument states melody fragment.
- **Verse 1**: vocal with full rhythm section; mandolin + fiddle chop backup; banjo rolling fills between phrases.
- **Chorus**: vocal trio harmony stacks in; full drive.
- **Break 1** (banjo): 8–16 bar banjo solo, melody paraphrase + rolls. Rhythm section comps behind.
- **Verse 2**: same structure; different fills.
- **Break 2** (fiddle): fiddle solo, melodic with bow ornaments.
- **Optional bridge**: modal shift (IV/vi or flat-VII), breakdown feel (all instruments punchy, half-time or double-time).
- **Final chorus**: trio harmony, full drive.
- **Tag/outro**: G-run or held chord fade. Standard ending: V–I hit on "1" after a pause.

### 7. Drive feel execution
- All instruments anticipate the beat imperceptibly (push, not drag).
- Mandolin chop on 2&4 creates backbeat snare.
- Bass roots walk slightly ahead.
- Banjo rolls are metronomic, never swing-triplet (unlike jazz).

## Practitioner Examples

| Practitioner | Contribution | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Bill Monroe** | Father of bluegrass; mandolin-driven lead + high lonesome vocal | *Blue Moon of Kentucky* (1946), *Uncle Pen* |
| **Earl Scruggs** | Three-finger banjo roll revolution | *Foggy Mountain Breakdown* (1949), *Earl's Breakdown* |
| **Lester Flatt** | Guitar boom-chuck + G-run + lead vocals | *The Ballad of Jed Clampett*, *Roll in My Sweet Baby's Arms* |
| **Stanley Brothers** | High baritone trio stack; modal bluegrass | *Rank Strangers*, *Little Maggie* |
| **Osborne Brothers** | High lead trio inversion | *Once More* (1957), *Rocky Top* |
| **Del McCoury** | Drive articulation + vocal phrasing | *Nashville Cats*, *1952 Vincent Black Lightning* |
| **Tony Rice** | Flatpicking guitar lead + progressive harmony | *Manzanita*, *Freeborn Man* |
| **Alison Krauss & Union Station** | High lead stack with female lead; twin fiddle | *When You Say Nothing at All*, *The Lucky One* |
| **Bill Keith** | Melodic banjo style (replace open-string emphasis with scalar) | *Devil's Dream*, *Sailor's Hornpipe* |

## UnitMatrix Integration

### Voices & Sections

| Voice | Instrument | Role | UnitMatrix Slot |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Voice 0** | Upright bass | Root-5 walk spine, drive foundation | Invariant quarter-note grid, 1&3 root, 2&4 walk |
| **Voice 1** | Acoustic guitar | Boom-chuck rhythm + G-run | Downbeat bass notes + offbeat strum on 2&4 |
| **Voice 2** | Mandolin | Chop on 2&4 + flatpicked fills | Percussive muted chord 2&4, eighth-note runs between phrases |
| **Voice 3** | 5-string banjo | Scruggs rolls — forward/backward/forward-reverse | Rolling 8th-note stream paraphrasing melody; breed = melody or backup |
| **Voice 4** | Fiddle | Double-stop backup + melodic solo | Sustained chord tones (2&4) on backup; full melodic break on solo |
| **Voice 5** | Vocal trio (lead + tenor + baritone) | Three-part chord-tone harmony stack | Lead = melody in middle; Tenor = chord tone +3/4 above; Baritone = chord tone −3/6 below |

### Rules (encoding constraints)

| # | Rule | Description |
|---|---|---|
| R01 | **Drive feel** | All voices anticipate beat subtly; no swing triplet feel |
| R02 | **Backbeat chop** | Mandolin (V2) MUST strike on beats 2&4 during vocal sections |
| R03 | **Bass walk** | V0 plays root on 1&3, passing tones on 2&4; never stops |
| R04 | **Banjo roll grammar** | V3 uses three-finger roll vocabulary; no strum chords |
| R05 | **Banjo backup** | V3 rolling fills between vocal phrases; chop/vamp during vocals |
| R06 | **Vocal chord-tone lock** | Lead+tenor+baritone = distinct chord tones only (R,3,5,♭7); no pitch class doubling |
| R07 | **No unison doubling** | Two voices never sing same pitch class at same time |
| R08 | **Chord-tone distance free** | Tenor/baritone distance from lead varies by chord (3rd or 4th) |
| R09 | **Single melodic solo** | Only one lead instrument solos at a time; others comp |
| R10 | **Break rotation** | Minimum 2 different instruments take breaks per song |
| R11 | **No drums** | No drum kit; mandolin chop = backbeat; bass foot = time |
| R12 | **G-run gateway** | V1 uses G-run (G–F#–E–D–B–G) on turnarounds in key of G |
| R13 | **Tempo band** | 110–160 BPM up-tempo; 70–100 BPM ballad/waltz |
| R14 | **Open-string bias** | Keys selected for open-string drone (G/C/D/A) |
| R15 | **Arrange arc** | Density monotonic: sparse opening → break density → chorus → max density → tag close |

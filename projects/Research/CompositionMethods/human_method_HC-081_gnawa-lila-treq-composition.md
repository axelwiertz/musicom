## 1. Method name & tradition

**Gnawa Lila / Derdeba Ritual Composition — the *Treq* ("path")**: a strictly encoded, all-night sequence of seven suites (one per *mlouk* spirit family, each bound to a colour, an incense, a guembri riff, a krakebs rhythm and a dance) built from riff-ostinato, call-and-response verse, and controlled trance acceleration.

- **Tradition / Culture:** Moroccan Gnawa Sufi brotherhood — descendants of enslaved Sahelian West Africans (Hausa, Fulani, Bambara, Soninke; trans-Saharan slave trade, 11th–17th c.) concentrated in Marrakesh, Essaouira, Tangier, Rabat, Salé, Casablanca, Fes. Music emerges 16th–17th c.; *lila* (also *derdeba*, "noise of the tbel drums") possession ritual. UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, Representative List (2019, ref. 01170). Parallel sister traditions: Algerian *diwan/gnawi*, Tunisian *stambeli*, Egyptian *zār* — all descended from the same Sahelian possession-cult stratum (Hausa *bori*).
- **Key figures:** maâlems Mahmoud Guinia, Boubker & Mokhtar Gania, Hamid El Kasri, Hassan Hakmoun, Abdeslam Alikkane, Brahim Belkane, Mustapha Baqbou, H'mida Boussou; secular band Nass El Ghiwane; jazz crossovers Randy Weston, Bill Laswell, Pharoah Sanders, Adam Rudolph. Scholarship: Philip Schuyler, Viviane Lièvre (*Couleurs, rites et musiques de la possession*), Deborah Kapchan (*Traveling Spirit Masters*, 2007), Bertrand Hell.

## 3. The craft procedure in detail (how the human does it)

### Stage 0 — apprenticeship (oral transmission, no notation)
1. Enter a *zriba* (brotherhood group) via family lineage (*dar* / *zaouiya*). Boys learn **krakebs** first, from age ~7 (El Kasri at 7, Alikkane at 9), clapping the refrains and absorbing the song families by ear.
2. Graduate to the **guembri** (3-string monoxyle lute, "gives the tonality and the rhythm") after years; the *maâlem* title is conferred by masters, not by exam — apprenticeship lasts a decade or more.
3. Absorb the whole repertoire: the seven suites, their order, their riffs, their texts (Arabic/Darija + West African residues from Bambara/Hausa/Fulani), and the ritual dramaturgy (which spirit wants which colour/incense/dance). Memory, not manuscript.

### Stage 1 — assemble the troupe & the ritual frame
4. Assemble: *maâlem* (guembri + lead voice + ceremonial authority), krakeb players (up to ~10+, iron castanet pairs), *tbel* big bass drummers (traditional setting), the *moqadma / shuwafa* (clairvoyant directing the *m'hella*: coloured veils, incense, accessories), and adepts.
5. Choose frame: private *lila* (healing, all night, "late evening until sunrise"), *moussem* pilgrimage, or profane stage/festival (Essaouira Gnaoua World Music Festival).

### Stage 2 — the aâda opening (consecration)
6. Krakebs begin clapping **continuous rhythmic patterns** with **short call-response refrains** — this consecrates the space before the guembri enters.
7. Optional acrobatic *kouyou* dance by krakeb players (swirling, in procession) — "the opening that consecrates the space."

### Stage 3 — open the treq: riff composition
8. The **maâlem opens the treq ("path")** on the guembri — "the strictly encoded sequence of the ritual repertoire of music, dances, colours and incenses, that guides the ecstatic trip across the realms of the seven mlouk."
9. Riff craft: the guembri line is a short **ostinato cell** (1–2 bars) built on **tonic and fifth** — "a language that emphasizes the tonic and fifth, with quavering pitch-play, especially pitch-flattening, around the third, the fifth, and sometimes the seventh" (i.e. bent b3/b5/b7, blues-like microtones). Right hand: thumb drops repeated bass-string **drone** ("brushless drop-thumb frailing") while fingers pick drum-like percussive patterns on the melody strings.
10. The riff is the **identity marker of one mlouk family** — change of riff = change of spirit. Maâlems compose new riffs and variations within the family vocabulary; regional dialects (marsaoui/Essaouira, marrakchi/Marrakesh, gharbaoui/Rabat–north, soussi/Berber south) colour the vocabulary (El Kasri deliberately fuses all four).

### Stage 4 — per-suite architecture (one column of the night)
11. **Exposition:** krakebs lock the cycle (6/8 fundamental, with 4/4 pieces; "interplay between triple and duple meters"; ternary rhythms superposed on a binary grid — the Gulf-of-Guinea polyrhythmic inheritance); guembri states the suite's riff; the maâlem sings the first call phrase.
12. **Verse loop (call–response):** maâlem sings a verse ("one phrase or a few lines are repeated over and over, so the song may last a long time" — a single *melk* song can run 20–40 minutes); krakeb players + assembly answer with the refrain. The melodic language of the guembri "is closely related to their vocal music and to their speech patterns."
13. **Ritual direction:** the *moqadma* dresses the adept in the mlouk's colour veil and burns its incense; the dancer enters **jedba** (trance) with the mlouk's specific footwork/dance.
14. **Trance ramp:** tempo ladder accelerates within the suite; krakebs density and vocal insistence rise ("the effect of provoking a trance from different angles"); at the peak the dancer collapses or whirls — then the suite **cuts abruptly** or segues.
15. **Cadence/segue:** the maâlem pivots the riff to the next family — "what seems to be a 20-minute piece may be a whole series of pieces — a suite for Sidi Moussa, Sidi Hamou, Sidi Mimoun or others."

### Stage 5 — the seven suites (the treq order)
16. The seven *mlouk* families (Islamic figures, saints, jinn) are evoked by **seven musical patterns — "seven melodic and rhythmic cells"** — each with its colour (white, dark blue, red, black, yellow/green…), incense, rhythm and dance. First family: Sidi Moussa (white) with the archetypal opening song *Baba Al Arabi*; Sidi Mimoun (black) is the strongest/heaviest; the night closes with the final family and a return "to the common world at the first lights of dawn."
17. Whole-night shape: **aâda → suite 1 → … → suite 7 → dawn close**, monotonic intensification, one long crescendo of possession.

### Stage 6 — secular export & fusion
18. Street/profane version: same riff-verse machinery, shortened, for Jemaa el-Fna acrobats and festival stages; jazz/rock fusion keeps the ostinato spine (Randy Weston's *The Splendid Master Gnawa Musicians of Morocco*, Grammy-nominated 1996; Jimi Hendrix took lessons from Mahmoud Guinia).

## 4. Practitioner examples

- **Maâlem Mahmoud Guinia** ("the King", Essaouira, 1951–2015): the most recorded maâlem; played with Pharoah Sanders, Carlos Santana; Jimi Hendrix studied at his house; son of Maâlem Boubker Gania; brothers Mokhtar & Abdellah also maâlems. His lilas are the reference recordings of the *marsaoui* school.
- **Maâlem Hamid El Kasri** (Ksar El Kbir → Rabat): "one of the biggest stars," renowned voice; explicitly fuses gharbaoui, marsaoui and soussi repertoires; sang with Jacob Collier and Snarky Puppy; famous for *Baba Mimoun* (Sidi Mimoun suite).
- **Maâlem Hassan Hakmoun** (Marrakech → New York): brought trance-gnawa to Western stages (Peter Gabriel, Kronos Quartet); his *lila* recordings document the marrakchi school.
- **Maâlem Abdeslam Alikkane & Tyour Gnawa** (Essaouira): healing-oriented maâlem; worked with Peter Gabriel, Gilberto Gil, Ray Lema.
- **Maâlem Brahim Belkane** ("the traditionalist", Marrakech): "There are many colours on earth: red, green, blue, yellow. You have to find these when you play, to be bright like the sun" — colour-as-composition philosophy.
- **Nass El Ghiwane** (1970s secular band, Laarbi Batma): took gnawa riffs/rhythms into popular song — the bridge from ritual to mass culture.
- **Randy Weston / Bill Laswell / Pharoah Sanders / Adam Rudolph**: documented jazz–gnawa dialogues (Weston's 1996 Grammy nomination; Laswell's *Night Spirit Masters*).
- **Scholars:** Philip Schuyler (early ethnomusicology), Viviane Lièvre, Deborah Kapchan, Bertrand Hell (worked with Mahjoub Khalmous) — sources for the seven-colour structure and the *treq*.

## 5. UnitMatrix mapping

### Voices
- **V0 = Guembri riff (maâlem)** — the composed spine: tonic-5th ostinato cell (1–2 bars), drone thumb-bass underneath, microtonal bends tagged per cell. Identity anchor; each section's riff = the mlouk signature. (Role mirrors the bass spine of HC-001 partimento and the *pokok* of HC-002.)
- **V1 = Krakebs stratum A** — on-beat/strong-subdivision pairs of iron castanets; marks the cycle grid.
- **V2 = Krakebs stratum B** — off-beat/after-beat pairs, interlocking with A to complete the cycle and create the binary-ternary superimposition.
- **V3 = Lead vocal (maâlem)** — call phrases: syllabic verses, ornamented, pitch-bent, speech-derived contour ("closely related to… speech patterns").
- **V4 = Chorus response** — krakeb players'/assembly's refrain: short echo cells (exact echo, tonal echo, or vocable fill).
- **V5 = Tbel drum (optional)** — low bass-drum punctuation on cycle downbeats / trance peaks.
- **V6 = Metadata/ritual layer** — per-column tags: mlouk family, colour, incense, dance/kouyou, regional dialect (marsaoui/marrakchi/gharbaoui/soussi), tempo-ladder stage, trance-ramp flag.

### Sections
Columns = the encoded sequence (the *treq*), one per ritual stage:
S0 aâda (krakebs + refrains only, V0 silent) → S1..S7 the seven mlouk suites (each: riff exposition → verse loop with call-response alternation → trance ramp → cut/segue) → S8 dawn close (return to the common world).
Each suite column subdivides internally into: riff cell, N verse cells (call→response pairs), ramp cells (accelerating), terminal cut cell.

### Rules to encode
1. **Treq order invariant:** suite sequence fixed; each column carries mlouk/colour/incense tags (V6) — engine cannot reorder suites.
2. **Riff constraint (V0):** cell = tonic + fifth skeleton (b3/b5/b7 bends allowed as pitch-bend/detune flags), length 1–2 bars, looped; identity = mlouk key in a riff dictionary (per regional dialect).
3. **Krakebs interlock (V1+V2):** A on strong subdivisions, B on weak; combined sum = full cycle; ternary subdivision over binary grid (3-divisible quantization on top of 4/4 bars at 480 tpb); zero-drift: both strata padded to full column length.
4. **Call–response alternation (V3→V4):** every verse cell is followed by a response cell; response content ∈ {exact echo, tonal echo, vocable fill}; response never introduces new melodic material.
5. **Trance ramp:** per-suite tempo ladder (BPM steps between cells), krakebs density/velocity growth, vocal phrase lengthening; terminal cell = abrupt cut or segue-pivot riff.
6. **Modal constraint:** no functional harmony — vertical sonorities incidental (like HC-003 heterophony rule); anchors tonic + fifth; V0 drone invariant across each suite.
7. **Duration rule:** ritual cells are minutes long — realization loops riff cells with variation (seedable ornament layer), with a practical per-suite cap flag for non-ritual renderings.
8. **Zero-drift gate:** `validate()` must pass; pad V0 drone and V1/V2 strata to section length (pad pattern from `_unit_from_events`).

## 6. Quirks & pitfalls

- **Oral, not written:** there is no canonical notation; regional lineages differ in suite order and riff vocabulary — treat the DB entry as the general architecture, not a fixed score.
- **Ritual time vs engine time:** real lilas run until dawn; the UnitMatrix realization must cap suites (the engine is a composer's model, not a ceremony replacer).
- **Microtones:** the b3/b5/b7 flattening is genuine pitch-play — encode as bend tags, don't force equal-temperament pitch classes as "wrong notes."
- **Call–response is structural, not decorative:** the response is the motor of the trance loop; removing it collapses the form (this is what distinguishes gnawa from a plain ostinato piece).
- **Binary-ternary interplay is the signature rhythm** ("interplay between triple and duple meters") — a strict 6/8-only rendering misses the groove; keep the superimposition rule.
- **Sacred context:** lilas are healing rites; profane festival versions shorten/adapt the treq — respect the frame when composing (metadata layer carries ritual vs stage tag).

# HC-021 — Species Counterpoint (Strict Counterpoint / Cantus Firmus Study)

**Method Type:** Human craft — contrapuntal pedagogy & compositional practice
**Tradition / Culture:** Western Classical — European contrapuntal school (Zarlino 1558 → Zacconi 1619 → Fux *Gradus ad Parnassum* 1725 → modern conservatory), the method by which most canonical Western composers learned to write polyphony
**Primary Elements:** PITCH, TEXTURE, STRUCTURE, RHYTHM

---

## What the human actually does

A composer learning or using **species counterpoint** does not invent a chord progression and harmonize it. They take a *fixed, pre-existing melody* (the **cantus firmus**, "fixed song") and **add one or more independent melodies against it**, one note at a time, obeying a ladder of interval and motion rules that get progressively harder. The goal is **line independence**: two (or more) melodies that each sound complete and singable on their own, while also combining into correct consonance at every moment.

The craft is *constraint satisfaction by hand*: the composer scans the cantus firmus note by note, and for each added note asks "what interval does this make? what motion did I just use? does the line still feel like a melody?" — rejecting and retrying until every rule holds. This is the **strict** ("note against note", *punctus contra punctum*) discipline; mastery of it is what later licenses **free counterpoint** (dissonance/chromaticism freed) and the imitative forms (canon, fugue).

Two governing ideas:

1. **The cantus firmus is sacred.** It never changes, never gets edited. All the composer's freedom is in the *added* voices. This is what makes it a *study* and a *generator* at once: one fixed spine, many possible counter-melodies.
2. **Species = a difficulty ladder.** The same cantus firmus is worked five times, each time with a denser rhythmic relationship between the added voice and the fixed one. 1st species = one note against one note (whole notes). 2nd = two against one (half notes). 3rd = four against one (quarter notes). 4th = syncopated suspensions. 5th ("florid") = a free mix of all four. Each rung relaxes/extends the dissonance rules.

The craft lives in **PITCH (interval constraints) + TEXTURE (line independence) + STRUCTURE (species as formal stages)** — a strong match for the UnitMatrix's Voices × Sections grid.

## Craft procedure (step by step, as a human executes it)

### Stage 0 — Build the cantus firmus
1. Write a slow, wholly-conjunct (or near-conjunct) modal melody in whole notes — usually in Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, or Mixolydian, spanning roughly an octave.
2. It must **begin and end on the final** (the tonic of the mode), move mostly by step, have a single high point (climax) somewhere in the middle, and avoid outlining a tritone or a seventh.
3. This melody is now *frozen*. Everything after this point is about what is added *against* it.

### Stage 1 — Choose the species (the difficulty rung)
The same cantus firmus is worked once per species:

| Species | Added voice rhythm vs. CF | New things you must handle |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1st** | one note per CF note (whole notes) | consonances only, motion rules |
| **2nd** | two notes per CF note (half notes) | passing/neighbor tones on the off-beat |
| **3rd** | four notes per CF note (quarter notes) | *nota cambiata*, double neighbors |
| **4th** | syncopated (tied) half notes | suspensions: dissonance on the beat, resolved down |
| **5th** (florid) | free mix of all four | all of the above, balanced so no one species dominates |

### Stage 2 — Write the counter-melody note by note
For **every added note**, the composer checks a stack of rules. (These are the *strict* rules — the shared core across all species, from Fux and the Palestrina style):

**Melodic (line) rules — every added voice must itself be a good melody:**
- Permitted melodic intervals: unison, octave, 4th, 5th, major/minor 2nd, major/minor 3rd, ascending minor 6th (which must then fall by step).
- Conjunct motion preferred; after a skip, move by step in the opposite direction.
- No more than two skips in the same direction, and the second must be smaller than the first.
- Avoid outlining a tritone (e.g. F–A–B) or a seventh within three notes in one direction.
- There must be exactly one climax (high point), on a strong beat, somewhere mid-line.
- The final note must be approached **by step**.

**Vertical (interval) rules — each simultaneous pair must be correct:**
- Counterpoint begins and ends on a **perfect consonance** (unison, octave, or 5th; if the added part is *below* the CF, only unison or octave).
- **Consonances** (1, 3, 5, 6, 8) are stable; **dissonances** (2, 4, 7, augmented/diminished) are allowed *only* as passing/neighbor/suspension figures — and only where the species permits.
- A dissonance must resolve **by step** (usually downward).

**Motion rules — between any two adjacent parts:**
- **Contrary motion should dominate.**
- **Perfect consonances must be approached by oblique or contrary motion** (never by similar motion — that would be "hidden" parallel 5ths/8ths).
- **Parallel 5ths and parallel 8ves are forbidden** (they destroy line independence — the two voices fuse into one timbre).
- Parallel 4ths avoided (when involving the bass especially).
- Adjacent parts should stay within a tenth of each other.
- Don't use the same interval more than three times in a row; prefer up to three parallel 3rds/6ths.

### Stage 3 — Add more voices
Once two parts work, add a **third**, then a **fourth**, stacking species (e.g. bass = CF, middle voice = 2nd species, top = florid). The same rule-stack is checked *pairwise* between every adjacent pair of voices ("build from the bass upward").

### Stage 4 — Graduate to free counterpoint, then imitative forms
With strict counterpoint mastered, the composer relaxes the rules (free counterpoint: any dissonance if it resolves, chromaticism allowed, dissonance free on the accented beat), then applies the *contrapuntal derivations* — **inversion, retrograde, retrograde-inversion, augmentation, diminution** — to build **canon** and **fugue** (the contrapuntal forms *par excellence*).

## Practitioner examples (how real composers used it)

- **Johann Joseph Fux — *Gradus ad Parnassum* (1725).** The codifier. Wrote the whole method as a dialogue between a master (Aloysius, standing in for **Palestrina**, the style model) and a student (Josephus). His five-species system is still the universal conservatory text.
- **Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina (16th c.).** The *style model* behind the rules — Fux distilled Palestrina's polyphonic practice into teachable constraints. The "Palestrina style" remains the benchmark for strict counterpoint.
- **J.S. Bach.** The supreme practitioner. His 2- and 3-part Inventions and 48 fugues of the *Well-Tempered Clavier* are free counterpoint built on exactly this strict foundation; the G♯-minor fugue (WTC II) is cited as textbook "the counterpoint sheds new light on the subject."
- **Joseph Haydn → Ludwig van Beethoven.** Haydn taught the young Beethoven from Fux's *Gradus ad Parnassum*. Beethoven's late works return to it audibly — the counterpoint in the first movement of the Piano Sonata Op. 90, the "wonderful counterpoint" added to a theme in the E-minor sonata, and the first orchestral variation of the *Ode to Joy* in the 9th Symphony finale (basses + bassoon countering the viola/cello theme).
- **W.A. Mozart.** Studied strict counterpoint (Fux's book was in his library, annotated in his hand); the five-voice finale of the "Jupiter" Symphony No. 41 combines five themes simultaneously — the apotheosis of trained line independence.
- **Schubert, Schumann, Brahms, Bruckner.** All educated in strict counterpoint before writing freely; Brahms's "developing variation" and Bruckner's contrapuntal masses are direct descendants.
- **Mozart→Beethoven→Schubert lineage (via Schiff):** pianist András Schiff documents Bach's counterpoint as the common thread influencing both Mozart and Beethoven's inner voices.

## Musicom / UnitMatrix integration

The method maps naturally onto a **Voices × Sections** grid, because the composer literally thinks "one fixed line + N added lines, worked at increasing rhythmic density."

### Voices (lines = voices)

| Voice | Role | Musicom mapping |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Voice 0 | **Cantus firmus** (slow conjunct modal whole notes, begins/ends on final) | Invariant spine; the *reference* every other voice is checked against. PITCH anchor. |
| Voice 1 | **1st added counter-melody** (species rhythm against CF) | PITCH + TEXTURE; conjunct, single-climax, obeys interval/motion rules vs. Voice 0 |
| Voice 2 | **2nd added counter-melody** (higher species, or free) | PITCH + TEXTURE; checked pairwise vs. Voice 0 *and* Voice 1 |
| Voice 3 | **Bass / lower added voice** (if CF sits on top; or a 4th free line) | STRUCTURE + TEXTURE foundation; "build from the bass upward" |

### Sections (species = sections)

`Species 1 → Species 2 → Species 3 → Species 4 → Species 5 (florid) → Free/Imitative`

Each section is the **same cantus firmus (Voice 0)** but the added voices get **rhythmically denser** (1:1 → 2:1 → 4:1 → syncopation → mixed). This is a monotonic "difficulty/density ramp" over a fixed spine — the exact analogue of how a student (or a composer working out a piece) escalates elaboration.

### Rules to encode

1. **Cantus firmus invariance** — Voice 0 never changes; all freedom is in Voices 1–3.
2. **Interval-validity table** — each simultaneous vertical interval must be in the allowed set for its beat-position (consonance on strong beats in species 1–3; dissonance only as passing/neighbor/suspension, resolving by step).
3. **Motion-type constraint** — perfect consonances (1, 5, 8) must be approached by oblique/contrary motion; parallel 5ths/8ves forbidden; contrary motion dominates.
4. **Species rhythm mask** — Voice 1's note count per CF note = {1, 2, 4, syncopated, mixed} by section.
5. **Melodic-conjunct rule** — each added voice prefers stepwise motion, max two same-direction skips (second smaller), no tritone/7th outline, single climax on a strong beat.
6. **Cadence convergence** — all voices approach the final by step, land on a perfect consonance, leading tone raised in minor (except Phrygian).
7. **Registral spacing** — adjacent parts within a tenth; no crossing.
8. **Line-independence scalar** — measure how often two voices move in parallel unison/octave/5th; enforce a low ceiling (this is the *texture* signature — the whole point of the method).
9. **Density ramp** — section-to-section note-density increases monotonically (Species 1 → 5), then "releases" into free/imitative counterpoint.

### Why it belongs in Musicom

Species counterpoint is the **canonical PITCH + TEXTURE constraint method** — the exact inverse of the harmonic-realization methods (HC-001 partimento is bass→harmony; this is melody→lines). It is the master-map's own **056 SCCC (Species Counterpoint Constraint Composition)** viewed from the *human* side: the same rule-stack, described as the craft procedure a composer physically executes, not the backtracking solver. It pairs cleanly with the engine's deterministic/constraint methods (033 WFCGS, 050 OTVL, 051 SGLM) as a *voicing-and-line post-filter*, and its species-ladder is a ready-made **section generator** (density ramp over a fixed CF spine).

## Quirks / pitfalls

- **Frozen-spine discipline.** Beginners want to "fix" the cantus firmus when a rule won't hold. The craft is to change the *added* voice instead — the CF is the invariant, and violating that defeats the exercise. (Encode as: Voice 0 read-only.)
- **Parallel 5ths/8ves sneaking in.** The classic failure — two voices moving in similar motion into a 5th or octave "fuse" into one timbre and the texture collapses. This is the single most important rule to enforce (the "line independence" scalar).
- **Sparse/mechanical output.** If the added voice is written rule-by-rule without listening, it sounds like a row of legal intervals, not a melody. The *melodic* rules (conjunct motion, single climax, stepwise cadence) are what make it sing — exactly the "sparse → add flow" principle in the master map (a florid 5th-species line is the continuous fill that keeps it flowing).
- **Dissonance placement.** Dissonances are *positional* — legal on the off-beat (passing/neighbor) in species 2–3, legal on the beat only as suspensions (species 4). Encoding dissonance without its beat-position context will produce illegal (or worse, ugly) output.
- **MIDI tail truncation.** If the final cadence leaves a voice resting before the global track end, append the silent padding event at `total_section_ticks - 1` (master-map pitfall).
- **Overlap with HC-001 (partimento).** Partimento gives an *unfigured bass* and realizes *harmony* upward; species counterpoint gives a *fixed melody* and adds *independent lines*. Same family (voice-leading craft), different spine and different primary element (HARMONY vs PITCH/TEXTURE). Do not merge them.

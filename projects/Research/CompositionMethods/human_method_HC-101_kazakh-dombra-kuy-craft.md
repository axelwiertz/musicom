# HC-101 — Kazakh Dombra Küy Composition (Programmatic Narrative Instrumental Craft)

## "Every Küy Tells a Story" — Küyshi Composer-Performer Craft: Tökpe vs Shertpe Schools × Sağa-Stream Form × Two-String Drone–Melody Texture × Oral Transmission

**Layer: human (→concrete)** — human-craft knowledge; when implemented, realizes material as **concrete** units in the UnitMatrix (the küy's pitched cells, rhythmic stroke patterns, and two-string texture become concrete pitch/onset units on the meter grid; the program becomes section labels + a narration voice).

---

## 1. Method name & tradition

**Kazakh dombra küy composition** — the art of composing and performing *küy* (күй, also *kui*): short solo instrumental programmatic pieces for the two-stringed dombra (dombyra), the principal national instrument of Kazakhstan. The composer is the *küyshi* (күйші): a composer-performer who composes *on and for the instrument itself*, transmits the work orally, and introduces each piece with its story. It is narrative instrumental music: the piece is titled by and bound to a legend, event, portrait, or landscape.

**Tradition / culture tag:** Central Asian — Kazakh (Turkic steppe, Kazakhstan; Kyrgyz *komuz* and Kazakh *qobyz* / *sybyzgy* are sibling kuy traditions). The classical repertoire took shape in the 18th–19th centuries with a generation of named küyshi composer-performers; roots reach the 13th century (the legendary küyshi Ketbugha and the folk küy *Aqsaq Qulan*). Two regional schools: the **western tökpe** (төкпе, "pouring") school and the **eastern/central shertpe** (шертпе, "flicking") school. The practice was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2014 as "Kazakh traditional art of Dombra Kuy". Scholarship: A. Zataevich, A. Zhubanov, B. Erzakovich, P. Aravin, S. Daukeyeva (*The Music of Central Asia*, Indiana UP, 2016).

The kuy tradition is NOT notation-based composition: works were "learned by heart and passed from generation to generation without written fixation" — the craft is ear-to-ear, with the instrument as the composition surface.

## 2. The craft — how a human does it (step by step)

### A. Apprenticeship & repertoire (the ear-first foundation)
1. Learn by ear from a master küyshi: internalize dozens to hundreds of existing kuy before composing. Oral transmission, no notation — the apprentice absorbs the mode/tuning vocabulary, the stroke patterns, and the narrative grammar (Kurmangazy learned from the blind küyshi Soqyr Eszhan, from Sherkesh, Bayzhuma, Balamaysan; his formal teacher was Uzak).

### B. Choose the program (the narrative lock)
2. Fix the subject first: a title, legend, person, animal, or landscape — every küy carries one (horse-gallop legends, floods, prison escapes, portraits of women, the golden steppe). The performer is expected to **introduce the story verbally before playing** so the listener can map sound to story. The program constrains the music: hooves → rhythmic cell; flood → surging waves of motion; lament → slow, sorrowful start that "gradually becomes inspired and pours out" (Kurmangazy's *Qairan sheshem*).

### C. Set the instrument: tuning (the pitch field)
3. Two strings, default concert tuning a **perfect fourth (D–G)**: lower string = drone, upper string = melody. Retune per küy to a fifth, octave, or other interval; eastern players use oblique/reverse/double tunings (Tattimbet used *qalys, shalys, teris, qosaq buraular* — off, oblique, reverse, paired tunings — to surprise rivals in kuy aitys duels). 18–24 frets on kuy instruments.

### D. Pick the school (the texture + affect selector)
4. **Tökpe (western)**: both strings sounded together with rapid alternating down/up finger strokes; dramatic, energetic, "strong aggressive associations"; composed "according to its rules — a certain sequence of tone sets on the dombyra neck" (a strict mode/tone-set order). Kurmangazy, Dauletkerei, Qazangap, Abyl, Esbay, Dina, Seitek.
5. **Shertpe (eastern/central)**: individual fingertip-and-nail plucks; lyrical, song-like, philosophical, free in form; "very deep, gentle and soft associations". Tattimbet, Toqa, Dairabai, Sügir, Ryzdyq.

### E. Fix the rhythmic cell (the qağys stroke pattern)
6. The hand stroke (қағыс, *qağys*) defines rhythm, dynamics, and character — the "magic secrets" that never reach notation: *jappai qağys* (continuous), *kezek qağys* (alternating/turn-taking), *shertpe qağys* (plucked), *shubyrpaly qağys* (flowing/tremolo). Meters: mostly two-part (2/4, 4/4) and four-part, also 3/4, 3/8, 5/8, 6/8, occasionally **changing meters** (auyspaly ölshem).
7. **Persistence rule**: once fixed, the küy's own measure and rhythm stay unchanged through the whole piece (a hallmark of Kurmangazy's kuy — "the kuy's own meter and rhythm are maintained without deviation throughout the work").

### F. Compose the melodic streams (sağalar) — the form
8. Dombra kuy texture is two-voice: melody on the upper string over the drone, often sounding **parallel intervals (4ths, 5ths, 3rds, 2nds)** as double stops — giving the sound its color and richness.
9. Build the form as a chain of melodic streams (*sağa*, literally "branch of a river"):
   - **Beginning stream** — state the main motif low on the upper string over the drone; establish the rhythmic cell.
   - **Middle streams** — develop: sequence, variation, register ascent, episodic figures.
   - **Climax architecture** — the *sharyqtau shegi* (peak) unfolds in **two branches: the small sağa then the large sağa** (kişi sağa → ülken sağa); the large sağa is the true peak, and by spreading both climaxes the full dombra range is mastered.
   - **Close** — return/reprise of the opening figure and cadence on the final (kuy end on the tonic/final; mourning and lament kuy keep a descending, sighing contour).
10. Forms range from single-section pieces to 2–3-part (rarely 3-part) rondo-variational synthetic forms; some kuy are long (150–200 bars: Boğda's *Boz töbesi*, Dina's *Äsem qoñyr*).

### G. Perform, narrate, transmit
11. Play: narrate the story, then perform; the performance itself is the composition's fixed form, but ornamentation and variants belong to the performer. Transmit orally to apprentices; successful variants harden into a school (Tattimbet's shertpe school carried by Äbiken Khasenov, Magauiya Khamzin; a shertpe kuy class was opened at the Almaty Conservatory in 1971).

## 3. Real practitioner examples

- **Ketbugha (13th c., legendary)** — *Aqsaq Qulan* ("The Lame Wild Ass"), the archetypal programmatic kuy: Jochi Khan's son is kicked to death by a wild ass; the küyshi breaks the news by music alone; the khan has the *dombra's soundbox* filled with molten lead (the instrument "told" the bad news, not the musician). The piece's galloping rhythm imitates the lame animal's gait.
- **Kurmangazy Sagyrbayuly (1823–1896)** — the giant of the western tökpe school; ~60 kuy, each a program: *Sary Arqa* ("Golden Steppe", his masterpiece, a picture of the endless steppe in a light key), *Adai*, *Kishkentai* (for rebel leader Isatai Taimanuly), *Türmeden qashqan* ("Escaped from Prison", written after his escape), *Qairan sheshem* ("My Poor Mother", starts slow, gradually inspired), *Qyz Danaiyn qyrğyny* (depicts horse-hoof thunder and battle, with the girl's beauty rendered in melodic passages), *Kóbik shashqan* ("Foam-Scattering", the foaming Caspian flood). His techniques — full-range climax unfolding via small/large sağa, locked rhythmic cell — were taken up by disciples including Dina Nurpeisova.
- **Dauletkerei Shyghaiuly (1820–1887)** — western school aristocrat; kuy of deep reflection and romantic lyricism: *Zhiger* ("Energy/Will to Live"; Zataevich: a work "overflowing the narrow throat of the small-sounding dombra"), *Topan* ("Flood"), the lament *Salyq ölgen* (for a friend, 1871), female portraits (*Qudasha*, *Jumabike*, *Aqzheleng*), genre scenes (*Zheldirme* "brisk ride", *Ysqyrma* "whistle", *Qos ishek*). Met Kurmangazy in 1861/1869; the encounter reshaped his music.
- **Tattimbet Qazangapuly (1815–1862)** — founder of the eastern shertpe school (Sary Arka / Central Kazakhstan); 40+ kuy: *Sary Zhailau* ("Golden Pasture", autumn philosophy), *Kókei Kesti* ("Soul-Piercing", his peak, with rare two-hand vibration technique), *Sylqyldaq*, *Qosbasar*, the humorous social-critique *Bes Töre*. Performed at Alexander II's coronation in St. Petersburg (1855), winning a silver medal; used unusual tunings and showmanship in kuy duels.
- **Sügir Äliuly (1882–1961)** — Karatau (southern) shertpe tradition, master of both tökpe and shertpe; kuy *Toğyz Tarau* ("Nine Branches"), *Aqqu* ("White Swan"), *Telqoñyr*, *Shalqyma*; brought qobyz timbre into dombra playing.
- **Dina Nurpeisova (1861–1955)** — Kurmangazy's leading heir (western tökpe); *Äsem Qoñyr*, *Bülbül*, *Qaraqasqa At*; the Academic Folk Orchestra of Dina Nurpeisova is named after her.
- **Modern continuation** — kuy arranged for folk-instrument orchestras from the 1930s; kuy quoted in Kazakh opera/symphony (Brusilovsky's *Qyz Zhibek* uses *Aqsaq Qulan*; Tölebaev's *Birzhan–Sara* uses *Soqyr Eszhan*; Rakhmediyev's *Alpamys* uses *Qudasha*); Tattimbet's *Bylqyldaq* played in the film *Zhambyl*; Mansur Sagatov wrote a trumpet piece on *Sylqyldaq*; electrified dombra (Ulytau) since the 2010s.

## 4. UnitMatrix integration (Musicom engine)

The method is an instrument-bound compositional craft, so the matrix models **the two-string dombra as the voice skeleton** and the küy's sağa-chain as the section architecture.

### Voices (rows)
| Voice | Role | Concrete content |
|---|---|---|
| V0 | **Upper-string melody (küy spine)** | Pentatonic/mode cells, sağa streams, register waves, climax figures (aiqay-style peaks); the composed melody proper. |
| V1 | **Lower-string drone/ostinato** | Open-string pedal + stopped root/5th; parallel-interval double stops with V0 (4th/5th/3rd/2nd); the tokpe "both strings together" attack. |
| V2 | **Qağys stroke/percussion layer** | Soundboard taps, body slaps, string punctuation; jappai/kezek/shertpe/shubyrpaly pattern selector; the "not-notation" hand secrets. |
| V3 | **Program/metadata voice** | Title, legend text, spoken narration (the pre-performance story); section labels; tuning tag (D–G fourth, retuned variant). |
| V4 | **Ensemble doubling (optional)** | Modern expansion: folk-orchestra doublings, qobyz/sybyzgy heterophony, or chamber arrangement of the same kuy. |

### Sections (columns)
- **S0_Narration** — the verbal program (V3), short; optional in pure instrumental rendering.
- **S1_Bas** (beginning stream) — main motif statement, low register, rhythmic cell established (V0+V1).
- **S2_Orta** (middle streams) — development: sequence, variation, episodic figures, register ascent; optional changing meter cell.
- **S3_Kishi_Sağa** — small climax branch (first peak, high register).
- **S4_Ulken_Sağa** — large climax branch (true peak: full range unfolded; the sharyqtau).
- **S5_Qaytaru_Coda** — return/reprise of the opening figure; cadence on the final.

### Rules (encoding the craft)
1. **Two-string texture lock**: V0 constrained to the mode + fret map of the upper string; V1 either open drone or stopped root/5th; when both sound (tokpe), interval set limited to parallel 4th/5th/3rd/2nd.
2. **Tuning gate**: pitch map derived from the chosen tuning (default perfect fourth D–G; fifth/octave/reverse variants allowed per kuy).
3. **School selector (qağys)**: tökpe → both-strings density, dramatic dynamics, strict tone-set order; shertpe → single-string plucks, rubato/free rhythm, lyrical dynamics. Selects texture + dynamics profile per matrix.
4. **Rhythmic-cell persistence**: one qağys cell per kuy, unchanged across all sections (the "meter and rhythm maintained without deviation" rule) — the engine must NOT re-vary the groove per section.
5. **Climax architecture**: register ascent must pass through S3 small sağa before S4 large sağa; the large sağa must use the widest ambitus cell; peak at the S4/S5 seam.
6. **Program lock**: every kuy requires a title/story tag; melodic contour must satisfy the program's affect (gallop cell for chase legends, descending sigh contour for laments, wave-like motion for floods) — validated against the affect table.
7. **Meter rule**: duple/triple cells (2/4, 4/4, 3/4, 3/8, 5/8, 6/8) with optional changing meters; shertpe allows free (unmetered) recitative-like streams.
8. **Zero-drift**: all units end at section boundaries; terminal landmark at section length.
9. **Humanization**: finger-pluck micro-timing, body-tap accents, two-hand vibration waves, ornament glides (concrete → absolute stage).

## 5. Quirks & pitfalls

- **Oral transmission, no canonical text**: kuy exist in performer variants; two recordings of "the same" kuy differ in ornament and detail. The concrete layer fixes one legitimate realization, not the "correct" one — don't over-normalize.
- **The program is part of the work**: a kuy performed without its story is half the piece. In a realization, the narration voice and title tag are not optional decoration — they carry the narrative lock.
- **Rhythm is the identity**: the qağys cell persists unchanged; "developing" the groove per section (Western variation habit) destroys the kuy's character. Resist the temptation to vary it.
- **Tokpe tone-set order**: western kuy follow a strict sequence of tone sets on the neck; breaking the order breaks the school's grammar — treat it as a mode-order constraint, not free invention.
- **Retuning is a compositional act**: Tattimbet's oblique/reverse tunings are part of the composition's identity; a transpose-to-12-keys operator (jazz habit) does NOT apply — kuy are tuning-bound, not key-agnostic.
- **Two-string economy**: the drone string is not "accompaniment" — melody and drone form one texture (parallel intervals, double stops). Splitting them into separate arranged voices changes the instrument's voice-leading logic.
- **Notation came late**: kuy were first systematically notated in the Soviet era (Zataevich, Zhubanov, Erzakovich; Sügir's kuy first notated 1968–69); staff notation loses the qağys "magic secrets" — annotate stroke patterns explicitly.
- **Gender history**: professional dombra performance was historically predominantly male (tied to patrilineal honor and tribal memory), with notable exceptions (Dina Nurpeisova) — a cultural-context quirk, not a musical rule.
- Do not confuse with HC-002 (gamelan kotekan — interlocking percussion), HC-035 (mbira kushaura/kutsinhira), HC-025 (kora ostinato song), or HC-091 (Mongolian urtiin duu — vocal long-song): HC-101 is the Central Asian instrumental programmatic narrative craft on a two-string drone–melody lute.

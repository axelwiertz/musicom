# Change-Ringing Combinatorial Method (CRCM) (Method 089)

**Paradigm:** Rules-Based (Deterministic) · **Layer:** concrete · **Created:** 2026-09-16

---

### Source

English **change ringing** ("method ringing", 17th-century England): the practice of ringing a set of tuned bells through an endless sequence of *changes* (permutations of the striking order) generated by memorized rules rather than a fixed melody. First systematized in **Duckworth & Stedman, *Tintinnalogia* (1668)** and **Stedman, *Campanalogia* (1677)**; the oldest method, **Grandsire** (c. 1650, Robert Roan), deviates from plain hunt at the treble's lead; **Plain Bob** (originally "Grandsire Bob") is the canonical simple method. The mathematical core is **group theory**: changes are elements of the symmetric group $S_n$ acting on the striking order, and a "plain course" is a cycle generated by alternating two fixed permutations (cross and straight changes). Modern treatments: **Arthur T. White, "Graphs, groups and surfaces" / "Fabel, White — Ringing the changes"** (White 1987, *Amer. Math. Monthly* 94: "Ringing the cosets"; White & Wilson 1990, "The hunting group"), and the ringing-theory literature (Brian Price, *The Theory of Change Ringing*, 1970; the Central Council of Church Bell Ringers' method collections). **Duckworth & Stedman (1668) is arguably the first published algorithmic-music treatise** — a rules-based generative music system predating the Kirnberger/Fux canon-print era, and the earliest known "permutation music" practice still performed today.

Not present in this database: nothing covers change ringing, adjacent-swap permutation laws, hunt-based ostinato forms, or group-theoretic $S_n$ traversal (065 TTSMC is 12-tone serial rows — different law: free row forms, no adjacency constraint, no hunting ostinato; 069 CWCC / 077 DBUC are word/cycle combinatorics — different generators, no palindromic hunt; 008 Call-Response is conversational role rotation, not permutation mathematics).

### Description

A change-ringing method defines an **infinite non-repeating stream of permutations of $n$ voices** under one draconian rule — every change is a product of **disjoint adjacent swaps** (a bell moves at most one position per blow) — plus a **hunting ostinato** (the treble sweeps top→bottom→top, pausing two blows at each extreme) and a small set of **deviations** (the "method") that break the hunt's short cycle to reach the full $n!$ change space.

For composition:

- **Bells** = `UnitMatrix` voices; **changes** = isochronous grid slots (16ths/half-beats).
- **The law is a melodic promise**: position moves ≤ 1 per blow ⇒ in the degree mapping every voice is *entirely stepwise* (verified: 0 jumps > 1 in a 60-change Plain Bob Minor course; 83.6% 2nds + 16.4% unisons). Legato-flow by construction — the anti-staccato complement to sparse 011/032 layers.
- **Plain Bob Minor** (the reference method): plain hunt + one deviation ("make 2nds" when the treble leads) yields a 60-change plain course cycling five lead heads `123456 → 135264 → 156342 → 164523 → 142635 → rounds` (verified). Calls ("bob", "single") at lead ends extend to the full 720 changes of Minor; with 8 bells, a peal = all 40320.
- **Symmetry**: each lead's second half-lead is the first with every row reversed — `rows[6+k] == reversed(rows[k])` (verified, k=0..5); the deviation row alone breaks the mirror (the "blue line" shape).
- **The blue line** (each bell's position path through the course) is a learnable melodic figure — the bell "dances" the same shape from different starting positions. In composition terms: **one motif, permuted across voices by group action**.

### Technical Mechanics

**1. The two generator permutations.** On striking order $\pi = (\pi_1..\pi_n)$ (position $i$, bell $\pi_i$; 1 = treble = highest pitch):

$$\text{cross: } \sigma_x = (1\,2)(3\,4)(5\,6)\cdots,\qquad \text{straight: } \sigma_s = (2\,3)(4\,5)\cdots$$

alternated: $\pi_{k+1} = \sigma_{k \bmod 2}\,\pi_k$. Every product of disjoint adjacent transpositions is its own inverse — the walk cannot "cheat".

**2. Plain hunt** on $n$ bells closes after $2n$ changes (each bell returns after visiting both extremes with double blows). Only $2n$ distinct rows — hence **methods**: deviations at fixed points of the hunt.

**3. Plain Bob deviation.** Every $n$-th change (treble lying in 1st place): positions 1–2 **hold** (treble lies two blows; the 2nds bell "makes 2nds"), while the remaining pairs dodge. Lead heads then cycle through the coset structure of $\langle\sigma\rangle$ in $S_n$: for $n=6$ five leads × 12 changes = 60 unique rows (plain course).

**4. Calls.** At lead ends the conductor inserts **bob** (3→4 dodges differently: 4th stays) or **single** (two bells hold) — these permute which lead-head coset comes next, letting a composition visit any subset of the $n!$ change space. Calls = **the form grammar**: each bob/single is a section-pivot operator.

**5. Verified composition mappings (executed, not assumed):**

- **A — Authentic peal texture**: bell $i$ = fixed pitch (diatonic ladder, treble = top degree, tenor = tonic). Melody = the continuous **strike stream**; verticalities = change rows (all 60 distinct in the plain course). The rows' sequence is a palindromic bell-tower wash — the "continually changing, never a melody" texture (Wikipedia: *bell music which is continually changing, but which cannot be discerned as a conventional melody*).
- **B — Melodic voices**: pitch = scale degree of the bell's **position** per change. Then every bell traces a phase-shifted palindromic hunt (treble & tenor exactly palindromic within a lead), voice-leading is 100% stepwise (0 leaps > a 2nd in 354 moves), and voices interlock in strict contrary/parallel motion — a mathematical hocket.
- **C — Hybrid**: Mapping B melodic voices over an ABS- subset-network chord bed; change rows with the tenor on the section's root re-voice as suspension-rich diatonic sonorities.

**6. Complexity.** Per change: $\mathcal{O}(n)$ (apply $\le\lceil n/2\rceil$ swaps). Full course: $\mathcal{O}(n \cdot L)$, $L \le n!$; plain-course generation needs no search at all — pure iteration. Call-driven exploration of the full extent is a **Hamiltonian-cycle problem on the Cayley graph** (NP-hard in general; every standard method has known compositions).

**Python sketch:**

```python
def plain_bob_course(n=6, changes=60):
    rows = [tuple(range(1, n + 1))]                       # rounds
    for step in range(changes):
        nxt = list(rows[-1])
        slot = step % n                                   # n-blow lead
        if slot < n - 1:                                  # plain hunt
            pairs = ([(0,1)] + [(i,i+1) for i in range(2, n-1, 2)]) if slot % 2 == 0 \
                    else [(i, i+1) for i in range(1, n-1, 2)]
        else:                                             # lead end: make 2nds
            pairs = [(i, i+1) for i in range(2, n-1, 2)]
        for i, j in pairs:
            nxt[i], nxt[j] = nxt[j], nxt[i]
        rows.append(tuple(nxt))
    return rows[:-1]                                      # 60 changes, ends on rounds+1

def to_degrees(rows, treble_is_top=True):
    # Mapping B: position -> degree (0 = tonic at tenor)
    return [[n - row.index(b) - 1 for row in rows] for b in range(1, n + 1)]
```

### UnitMatrix Integration (Voices & Sections)

- **Rows (Voices)** = bells. Mapping A: each row holds one fixed pitch (a "bell row") and the *cells' onsets* carry the structure — rows = strata of a peal. Mapping B (default): row $i$ = bell $i$'s position-degree melody; voice count = bell count ($n$ = 4–8 typical; minor 6 = 6 rows).
- **Columns (Sections)** = course segments. Natural sectioning: **one lead per section** (12 blows = 3 bars of 4/4 at quarter-note blows, or 1.5 bars at 8th blows); or one plain course (60 blows) spread over 5 sections = one per lead head. Calls at section boundaries = form markers (bob = chorus pivot).
- **Cells (MusicUnit)** — `{PITCH}` = the change's pitch assignment (fixed-bell or position-degree); `{RHYTHM}` = 12 equal onsets per lead-cell (or thinned by a Euclidean mask — then re-add a continuous pad per the Hybridization rule); `{HARMONY}` = tenor-pedal sonority + optional snapped chord from the abstract layer; `{TEXTURE}` = swap-pair accent weight (the two moving bells louder). Every cell padded to `section_len` (terminal landmark) so `composer.validate()` passes.
- **Flow**: method definition (n, deviation table, call list) → change stream → mapping A/B/C → per-voice rows → `UnitMatrixComposer.fill_voice_section` → validate → `to_midi` → SP render. Deterministic per (n, method, calls) — zero-RNG, byte-identical exports.

### Pitfalls

1. **Illegal changes break the melodic contract** — allowing non-adjacent permutation moves (e.g. 1↔3 swap) breaks the ≤1-position law and injects arbitrary leaps. Fix: generate ONLY by alternating $\sigma_x/\sigma_s$ with the deviation table; assert `is_adjacent_transposition` per change (see verifier).
2. **Repeated-row (false) methods** — a badly designed deviation revisits a row; a "true" method never repeats a row before rounds. Fix: assert `len(set(rows)) == len(rows)`; this is THE quality gate distinguishing valid methods from broken ones.
3. **Committing the double-blow off-by-one** — treble double blows at extremes and the lead-end exception both hinge on exact row indices; the half-lead mirror is `rows[n/2+k] == reversed(rows[k])` only up to the deviation row (k=0..n/2−1), not the whole lead. Verify numerically before documenting symmetry claims.
4. **Monotony without calls** — one plain course = 60 rows on 6 bells then loops. Fix: drive calls from the section plan (bob at chorus joins), or rotate methods course-per-course (Plain Bob → Grandsire → Stedman) — ringers do exactly this ("spliced").
5. **Register mapping** — bell 1 = HIGHEST pitch (treble), bell n = lowest (tenor). Inverting this flips every contour (and puts the ostinato in the bass). The mapping is `deg(bell) = n - bell` (tenor = degree 0).
6. **Percussion-only timbre limit** — struck bells have no dynamics; a pure peal render sounds like an alarm. Fix: assign voices to GM strings/flutes/choir (Mapping B) or route through SP-074-style sustained synthesis; keep one "tenor" row as the tonal anchor.
7. **Velocity/flatness** — isochronous blows + stepwise lines = hypnotic but static. Fix: layer 026 DPSM phase shifts or 023 Tendency corridors on top, or humanize onsets (SP-006) while keeping the change grid.
8. **Not serialism** — 065 TTSMC permutes a pitch-class ROW; CRCM permutes VOICE POSITIONS under an adjacency law with a hunting ostinato. The two are superficially both "$S_n$ on 12 things" — do not merge them; CRCM's law constrains motion (voice leading), TTSMC's constrains content (row forms).

### Comparison With Related Methods

- **069 CWCC / 077 DBUC / 076 PTM-ASC**: the other word/combinatorics methods — Christoffel balance, de Bruijn exhaustiveness, Thue–Morse self-similarity. CRCM adds the **adjacent-swap motion law + hunting ostinato**: structure lives in *how voices move*, not in the sequence's combinatorial spectrum.
- **065 TTSMC**: both $S_n$-based; see Pitfall 8.
- **026 DPSM**: both build from identical looping streams; DPSM phases whole cycles, CRCM swaps adjacent pairs under an ostinato hunt — complementary phase/shuffle textures.
- **032 ITCM**: coprime-loop misalignment vs CRCM's group-law interlock; both yield non-repeating polyphony from tiny rule sets.
- **056 SCCC**: both are strict historical craft rules; SCCC constrains counterpoint intervals, CRCM constrains permutation motion.
- **051 SGLM / 011**: graph methods over voice-leading space; CRCM is the historical endpoint of the same idea — an explicit Cayley graph of $S_n$ walked by rule.

## 1. Craft Overview & Cultural Context

In the high Andean Altiplano surrounding Lake Titicaca (spanning southern Peru and western Bolivia), panpipe music—known as **sikuri** (or *siku*, *zampoña*, *antara*)—is the world's most radical realization of **communal composition, hocketing, and collective participatory performance**.

The siku panpipe is physically split across two complementary halves played by two distinct musicians (or two matched cohorts of players):
1. **Ira** (Aymara: "the one that leads", associated with the male principle, *chacha*): Typically consists of 6 pipes tuned to alternating degrees of a diatonic heptatonic scale (e.g., in G major/E minor: E4, G4, B4, D5, F#5, A5).
2. **Arka** (Aymara: "the one that follows", associated with the female principle, *warmi*): Typically consists of 7 pipes tuned to the intervening degrees (e.g., D4, F#4, A4, C5, E5, G5, B5).

Neither the Ira nor the Arka can play a complete melody, scale, or phrase independently. To sound a simple scalar ascent (D-E-F#-G-A-B-C), the Arka sounds D, the Ira sounds E, the Arka sounds F#, the Ira sounds G, and so forth. The resulting texture is a strict, rapid **hocket** (*trenzado* / interlocking).

### The Tropa (Consort) Aesthetic & Social Coherence
As ethnographically documented by Thomas Turino (*Moving Away from Silence: Music of the Peruvian Altiplano*, 1993), sikuri music is played by a large collective ensemble called a **tropa** (typically 20 to 50+ wind players accompanied by *bombos* [large double-headed bass drums] or *cajas*). The tropa spans multiple parallel octaves:
- **Sanja** (or *basto*, *taika*): The deep bass panpipes sounding one octave below the standard register.
- **Malta**: The standard middle register (carrying the primary acoustic body).
- **Lika** (or *chili*, *tipli*): The high panpipes sounding one octave above the malta.
- **Chuli**: The ultra-high piercing panpipes sounding two octaves above the malta.

All sizes play in strict parallel octaves (and fifths in *Kantu* or *Ayarachi* traditions), creating a massive wall of sound. In Aymara aesthetics, **no individual instrument may stand out**. Playing louder, flashing soloistic virtuosity, or asserting individual authorship is considered a grave social transgression. Instead, the aesthetic goal is a unified, breathless, "dense" sonic texture (*ch'inisco* or thick acoustic envelope where attack clicks, breath noise, and overtones blend seamlessly).

### Communal Composition Process
In communities such as Ayllu Sulcata and the Conima district of Puno, new tunes are not composed in isolation at a desk. During nighttime rehearsals (*ensayos*) preceding major dry-season fiestas:
- A member may softly whistle or tap a two-bar motif (*chocheo*).
- Another musician picks it up on an Ira pipe; a partner answers on Arka.
- The collective circle debates, alters pitches, trims bars, and reshapes the cadence through consensus without anyone claiming proprietary credit.
- The piece belongs entirely to the *ayllu* or *conjunto*.

## 2. Practitioner Craft Workflow (Step-by-Step)

When an Altiplano sikuri ensemble creates, arranges, and realizes a piece (whether a slow reflective **Siku Lento** / *Wifala* or a rapid driving **Siku Ligero** / *Chuta-Chuta*), the human workflow follows six distinct phases:

1. **Tuning & Consort Calibration (*Afinación de Tropa*)**:
   - Sikus are constructed in matched sets (*tropas*) cut from high-altitude cane (*Arundo donax* or *chuki*).
   - All Ira and Arka pairs within the ensemble are tuned to an identical pitch standard (modern G-major/E-minor diatonic: D-E-F#-G-A-B-C).
   - The pipes are arranged into four parallel octave registers: *Sanja* (-12 st), *Malta* (0 st), *Lika* (+12 st), *Chuli* (+24 st).

2. **Skeletal Melody Formulation & Phrase Symmetry**:
   - The collective conceives the core skeletal melody in standard tripartite ternary-binary form: **AABBCC** (or bipartite **AABB**).
   - Each section (A, B, C) consists of an antecedent and consequent phrase, universally 4 or 8 bars in length (strictly isometric).
   - **Section A**: Low register, establishes the modal tonic (often minor-sounding E minor, landing on the low final).
   - **Section B**: High register contrast, leaping up a 5th or octave, emphasizing the relative major (G major) or high fifth before returning.
   - **Section C**: Climactic phrase, driving toward the cadential formula.

3. **Bipolar Decomposition (The Ira-Arka Split / *Trenzado*)**:
   - The skeletal melody is broken down note-by-note into complementary sub-streams:
     - Any pitch belonging to the Ira pitch set ($P_{\text{Ira}} = \{\text{E}, \text{G}, \text{B}, \text{D}_{\text{high}}, \text{F\#}_{\text{high}}, \text{A}_{\text{high}}\}$) is assigned to the Ira voice.
     - Any pitch belonging to the Arka pitch set ($P_{\text{Arka}} = \{\text{D}, \text{F\#}, \text{A}, \text{C}, \text{E}_{\text{high}}, \text{G}_{\text{high}}, \text{B}_{\text{high}}\}$) is assigned to the Arka voice.
   - While Ira blows, Arka inhales/rests; when Arka blows, Ira inhales/rests.
   - A slight overlap of approximately 20–40 milliseconds (*ligado*) is deliberately maintained at the handoff between players so there is zero gap or loss of acoustic momentum.

4. **Cadence Engineering (*Remate* vs *Chuta-Chuta*)**:
   - In **Siku Lento**: Phrases conclude on an elongated, sustained tonic note held across 1 to 2 measures with a swelling dynamic, terminated abruptly by a dry unison cutoff.
   - In **Siku Ligero**: Phrases culminate in a rapid, machine-gun interlocking formula called **chuta-chuta**: Ira and Arka alternate rapid 16th-note iterations of the tonic and supertonic (or tonic and 7th), repeating in rapid succession (e.g., E-D-E-D-E-D) before a sudden, sharp unison accent on the downbeat.

5. **Percussion Foundation (*Bombo* & *Wankar* Ostinato)**:
   - The entire tropa is driven by massive double-headed *bombos* (or *wankaras* in Bolivia):
     - In *Siku Lento*: A steady, resonant quarter-note pulse punctuated by heavy accented downbeats with rolling grace-notes (*mollinado*).
     - In *Siku Ligero*: A syncopated driving pattern consisting of an accented downbeat followed by an anticipated offbeat eighth note: `[X . . x X . x .]` (resembling a rapid Andean marcha/huayño foot-stomp pattern).
     - Accompanying snare drums (*tarolas*) or triangles/cymbals (*platillos*) provide continuous subdivisional sizzle.

6. **Circular Performance Arc (*La Rueda*)**:
   - Performance is cyclic: the AABBCC form repeats multiple times (3 to 6 passes).
   - With each repeat, the ensemble moves in a counter-clockwise circle (*la rueda*), foot-stamping on primary bombo accents.
   - The tempo exhibits a subtle, monotonic acceleration across the performance arc, building physical and auditory communion (*ayni* / reciprocity).

## 3. Real Practitioner References

- **Qhantati Ururi of Ayllu Sulcata (Conima, Puno, Peru)**: The preeminent traditional rural Aymara sikuri ensemble studied by Thomas Turino. Famous for definitive masterworks of the *siku lento* genre such as "Edith" (composed by Filiberto Calderón and shaped by the community) and "Egenio" (*siku ligero*), demonstrating pristine hocket interlocking, massive 20+ member tropa cohesion, and textbook AABBCC architecture.
- **Sikuris del Barrio Mañazo (Puno, Peru)**: Founded in 1892, the oldest continuously operating urban-mestizo sikuri association in Peru. Central to the monumental *Virgen de la Candelaria* festival (UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage). Pioneers of the *sikuri de varios bombos* format, blending indigenous Aymara hocket with high-energy urban showcraft.
- **Kallawaya Kantu Ensembles of Charazani (Bautista Saavedra, Bolivia)**: Celebrated traditional ensemble of the Kallawaya herbalist healers. The *Kantu* tradition utilizes massive *wankaras*, triangle (*chinisco*), and five-part parallel organum (sikus sounding in parallel fifths, fourths, and octaves simultaneously in hocket), producing an archaic, resonant acoustic cathedral.
- **Sikuris Juventud Miraflores & Conjunto de Zampoñas de San Marcos (Lima, Peru)**: Key ensembles of the urban Andean migration movement, transposing rural *ayllu* hocket ethics into university student and worker contexts, maintaining strict Ira-Arka partner bonds.

## 4. Musical Elements & UnitMatrix Mapping

### Musical Elements
- **PITCH**: Diatonic heptatonic collection without chromatic alteration (standard E minor / G major gamut: E-F#-G-A-B-C-D). Strict registral division between Ira and Arka rows.
- **RHYTHM**: Continuous subdivisional interlocking (hocket). Steady motoric bombo pulse (90–108 bpm for *lento*, 130–152 bpm for *ligero*). Fast *chuta-chuta* 16th-note cadential bursts.
- **HARMONY**: Parallel octave stratification (*sanja*, *malta*, *chuli*) and parallel fifths/fourths in *Kantu* styles. Emerging vertical sonorities are non-functional; chordal impression arises purely from parallel octave coupling and acoustic overtones.
- **STRUCTURE**: Symmetrical tripartite strophic form: **AABBCC** (or AABB). 8 bars per section. Mandatory section-closing cadential formulas (held note in *lento*, *chuta-chuta* in *ligero*). Cyclic repetition (3–5 choruses).
- **TEXTURE**: Dense, breathy collective hocket (*trenzado*). Perfect balance between Ira and Arka—neither dominates. Monophonic composite line exploded into two mutually dependent voice streams.

### UnitMatrix Implementation
In the Musicom engine, this tripartite orchestration maps into a 5-voice UnitMatrix:

- **Voice 0 (Arka Panpipes)**: MIDI Program 75 (Pan Flute). Carries pitches $\{D, F\#, A, C\}$; rests while Voice 1 sounds; overlaps on handoffs by 20–30 ms.
- **Voice 1 (Ira Panpipes)**: MIDI Program 75 (Pan Flute). Carries pitches $\{E, G, B\}$; leads phrase onsets; rests while Voice 0 sounds; hockets with Voice 0.
- **Voice 2 (Sanja / Bass Siku)**: MIDI Program 75 (Pan Flute, -12 st). Doubling layer sounding 1 octave lower; mirrors the composite union of V0+V1 to provide deep bass warmth.
- **Voice 3 (Bombo / Wankar)**: Percussion Channel 9 (MIDI 36/35 Bass Drum). Driving metric heartbeat; unyielding steady quarter/eighth ostinato with syncopated offbeats.
- **Voice 4 (Chinisco / Triangle / Snare)**: Percussion Channel 9 (MIDI 81 Triangle / 38 Snare). High metallic and sizzle subdivisions marking continuous eighth/sixteenth time.

### Sections (AABBCC Form)
- **S0: `A1_Exposition`** (Low melodic statement, bars 1–8; Ira leads, Arka responds).
- **S1: `A2_Repeat`** (Identical thematic repeat, bars 9–16; ends with primary cadence).
- **S2: `B1_Turn`** (High register contrast, bars 17–24; leaps up a 5th into G major space).
- **S3: `B2_Repeat`** (High register repeat, bars 25–32; cadential rhyme with Section A).
- **S4: `C1_Climax`** (Climactic phrase, bars 33–40; maximum density and rhythmic drive).
- **S5: `C2_ChutaChuta`** (Final repeat, bars 41–48; concludes with rapid *chuta-chuta* 16th-note interlocking formula and dry unison impact).

### Zero-Drift & Production Invariants
- Each of the 6 sections is strictly 8 bars (15,360 ticks at 480 tpb, 4/4 meter). Total chorus length = 48 bars (92,160 ticks).
- Voice 0 and Voice 1 adhere to strict complementary hocket partitioning: no harmonic pitch overlap except handoff transitions.
- Voice 2 strictly tracks composite pitch $(\text{V0} \cup \text{V1}) - 12$.

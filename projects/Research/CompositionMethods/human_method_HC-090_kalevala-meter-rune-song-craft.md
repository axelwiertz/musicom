## 1. Method name & tradition

**Kalevala-meter rune song composition** (Finnish *runolaulu* / *runonlaulanta* "poem-singing", Estonian
*regilaul* / *regivärss* "runic song", Seto *leelo*) — the oral-formulaic craft by which Baltic-Finnic
singers compose new songs *in performance*: text built line-by-line in the eight-syllable trochaic
tetrameter known as the **Kalevala meter** (Estonian *regivärss*), bound within each line by
**alliteration**, organized in **parallelistic couplets**, and sung to short regional **melodic
formulas** over a pentachord, in a lead–chorus **antiphony** whose repeated response gives the lead
singer the time to compose the next line. It is the living compositional method behind the Finnish
*Kalevala* and Estonian *Kalevipoeg* — and it is emphatically a *composition* method: singers did not
memorize fixed songs verbatim so much as re-compose them from formulaic stock at every performance
(the oral-formulaic principle demonstrated by Milman Parry & Albert Lord in a Balkan parallel and
documented in Finnic material by Kuusi, Honko, Frog, and others).

- The method is **text-first**: in Estonian tradition the words lead and the melody is "a short tune of
  small range" (Estonian *regiviis* — lühike ja väikese ulatusega) that serves as a vehicle for the
  verse. Many texts share one melody — the "group tune" (*rühmaviis*: e.g. swing tune, herd tune,
  wedding tune — *kiigetoon, karjatoon, pulmatoon*).
- It is a **composition-in-performance** procedure, not a fixed-work procedure and not an algorithm:
  the human composes the next idea-line while the chorus repeats the previous one, guided by strict
  metrical rules and two binding schemes (alliteration, parallelism).
- Variants: monophonic lead+chorus antiphony across most Finnic groups; **polyphonic Seto leelo**
  (torrõ main voice sung by several singers + solo killõ high descant); kantele-supported Finnish
  singing; unaccompanied Estonian regilaul (rhythm marked by body sway / foot stamp, danced forms like
  Seto *Pöörajooks* and Kihnu wheel-songs).

## 3. The craft procedure, step by step (how the human does it)

1. **Learn the formulaic stock by ear.** No notation; the tradition is oral and collective. An
   apprentice absorbs the region's song corpus (hundreds to thousands of lines — Larin Paraske
   commanded over 32,000 verses; the SKVR archive holds ~90,000 rune texts) plus the region's **group
   tunes**: a small bank of melodic formulas, each reused for many texts by function (swing, herd,
   wedding, lament, lullaby). Literacy = knowing the formulas, not decoding a score.
2. **Compose in the Kalevala meter (regivärss).** Every line is an eight-syllable trochaic tetrameter
   with **no end rhyme**, obeying quantitative syllable rules:
   - Syllables are **strong** (long vowel/diphthong or consonant-closed syllable carrying main stress),
     **weak** (short stressed syllable), or **neutral** (unstressed — placeable anywhere).
   - A strong syllable may occur only in the **rising part** of feet 2, 3, 4; a weak syllable only in
     the **falling part** of those feet. The first foot is free (may even hold 3–4 syllables).
   - No one-syllable word at line end.
   - Alternate **normal tetrameter** lines (word-stress = foot-stress; caesura after foot 2: "Veli /
     kulta, // veikko/seni") with **broken tetrameter** lines (*murrelmasäe*; ≥1 stressed syllable in
     falling position, no caesura: "Miele/ni mi/nun te/kevi") at roughly equal frequency — the
     alternation is a signature of the meter.
3. **Bind each line by alliteration.** Within the line, echo the initial consonant (weak alliteration)
   or the initial vowel / vowel+consonant cluster (strong): "**v**aka **v**anha **V**äinämöinen"
   ("steadfast old Väinämöinen").
4. **Build the text in parallelistic couplets.** A new idea-line is immediately followed by one
   synonymous parallel line restating it — the couplet is the compositional unit: "In these dismal
   Northern regions / In the dreary land of Pohja" (Näillä raukoilla rajoilla / Poloisilla pohjan
   mailla). In the authentic folk songs a line is usually followed by exactly one parallel; chiasmus
   (inversion of the two half-lines) is the standard variant. Narrative does not advance during the
   parallel — the singer uses it to dwell, embellish, and prepare the next idea.
5. **Set the text to the melodic formula.** Each 8-syllable line = two 4-syllable half-phrases set to
   the region's stock tune, built on the **first five notes of the scale** (pentachord; the vocal
   ambitus matches the kantele's range). Recitation rhythm is characteristically **5/4-oriented** (the
   broken tetrameter's 3+2 asymmetry), with duple and sextuple regional variants. The half-phrase end
   and line end cadence on the reciting tonic.
6. **Perform antiphonal lead–chorus — compose in the repeat.** The lead singer (*runonlaulaja* /
   *eeslaulja*) sings a newly composed line; the chorus repeats it. The repetition is *compositional
   thinking time*: while the chorus sings, the lead generates the next line (and its parallel) under
   the meter rules. Karelian singers held hands while singing in call-and-response; Ingrian tradition
   adds choral parallel voices (koori). Sometimes solo (herd songs, lullabies), or two choruses
   alternating.
7. **Add instrumental support (Finnish/Karelian side).** Accompany on the **kantele**: five-string
   instrument in five-limit just intonation (D-major or D-minor triad), gapped diatonic tuning, with
   the lowest string tuned a fourth below the tonic as a **drone**; plucked or strummed (matchstick)
   in the same pentachord. Estonian regilaul is sung **unaccompanied**; rhythm is marked by swaying,
   stamping, or dance (Seto *Pöörajooks*, Kihnu ring songs).
8. **Seto variant — polyphonic leelo with drift.** Lead singer carries the **torrõ** main voice (sung
   by several singers, in the older style deliberately *not* in strict unison; men's leelo may add a
   low **alumine torrõ** using the lower scale notes without fixed variation rules). A solo **killõ**
   descant weaves higher pentachord notes above. The piece's **pitch level rises** as the song
   proceeds; when the killõ climbs too high the lead uses **kergütamine** — abruptly restarting the
   next line a step or more lower, resetting the register (a technique shared with Mordvin song).

## 4. Practitioner examples

- **Arhippa Perttunen** (c. 1790s–1840s, Viena/White-Sea Karelia) — Lönnrot's principal source for the
  Kalevala's epic songs (Sampo cycle, Väinämöinen); performed whole epic sequences from memory,
  re-composing each time.
- **Ontrei Malinen** (White-Sea Karelia) — source for the central Väinämöinen/Sampo songs that
  structure the Kalevala's middle runos.
- **Larin Paraske** (Izhorian, 1833–1904, Ingria) — the "Finnish Mnemosyne": ~1,200 poems, 1,750
  proverbs, 336 riddles transcribed by Adolf Neovius; >32,000 verses commanded; her incantatory,
  hypnotic rune singing is said to feed Jean Sibelius's *Kullervo* (Op. 7).
- **Poavila and Triihvo Jamanen** (Karelian rune-singer brothers, photographed at Uhtua 1894) —
  canonical late-19th-c. bearers; their repertory includes the epic cycles.
- **Anne Vabarna** (Seto lead singer, d. 1957; "Mother of Song") — composed the oral *twin epic* "The
  Maiden's Death Song & The Great Wedding", written down by A. O. Väisänen (1923) — a documented
  large-scale rune-song composition by a traditional singer.
- **Elias Lönnrot** (1802–1884) — compiled the *Kalevala* (Old 1835, 12,078 verses; New 1849, 22,795
  verses in 50 runos) from field performances; **F. R. Kreutzwald** composed the Estonian *Kalevipoeg*
  (1857–61) on regilaul themes.
- **Modern transmissions:** Veljo Tormis's choral works (e.g. *Runic Song* cycles) built directly on
  regilaul material; Värttinä (contemporary runo-song ensemble); Metsatöll (runo-metal); Martti Pokela
  (kantele revival, 1950s–60s); Sibelius's *Kullervo* as the art-music refraction of the tradition.

## 5. Musical Elements Framework reading

- **STRUCTURE** — the *parallelistic strophe* is the formal unit: idea-line + parallel line + chorus
  repeat; songs chain strophes episodically (no fixed song form; narrative couplet-by-couplet).
- **PITCH** — pentachordal formula tunes (first five scale degrees); line-end cadence on the reciting
  tonic; Seto drift raises the tonic progressively; kantele drone anchors the fifth below.
- **RHYTHM** — the Kalevala meter's quantitative syllable grid; characteristic 5/4-oriented
  recitation (3+2) with duple/sextuple regional variants; body-sway/stamp accentuation.
- **TEXTURE** — monophonic lead + antiphonal chorus (Finnic norm); kantele drone + plucks (Finnish
  variant); Seto polyphony: torrõ block voice + solo killõ descant (+ optional low torrõ).
- **HARMONY** — essentially absent (drone only); vertical sonorities are incidental coincidences of
  the unison/pentachord parts, never functional. HARMONY is therefore *not* a primary element.

## 6. UnitMatrix mapping

The method realizes directly at the **concrete** layer: every cell is a concrete melodic-formula unit.

| Row (Voice) | Role | Cell content |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Voice 0 | **Lead singer** (runonlaulaja / eeslaulja) | Melodic-formula units: one 8-syllable line = two 4-syllable half-phrase events over the pentachord, text-locked (cell duration = 8 syllables × syllable-tick), line-end cadence on tonic. |
| Voice 1 | **Chorus** (antiphonal response) | Copy of Voice 0's previous line (offset = one line), i.e. the repeat seam that buys composition time. |
| Voice 2 | **Kantele** (Finnish variant, optional) | Invariant drone (fourth below tonic) + pentachord pluck ostinato; static across all sections. |
| Voice 3 | **Killõ descant / low torrõ** (Seto variant, optional) | Higher (or lower) pentachord block voices over the torrõ; register follows the drift curve. |

**Sections (Columns):** each strophe = idea-line + parallel + chorus repeat → one section per
couplet-block (S0 S1 S2 … Sn), chained episodically.

**Rules to encode:**
- *Syllable-count lock*: 8 syllables per line; cell tick-length = Σ syllable durations (quantitative
  grid); no line may end with a one-syllable word (text constraint).
- *Stress-position map*: strong syllables → on-accent positions in feet 2–4 rising parts; weak →
  off-accent falling parts; first foot free; alternate normal (caesura after foot 2) and broken
  (murrelmasäe) line types ~50/50.
- *Alliteration constraint*: within a line, initial consonant (or initial vowel/cluster) echo ≥ 2
  stressed words.
- *Parallelism rule*: section n+1's first line restates section n's idea line with synonyms before new
  narrative advances (the couplet generator); chiasmus variant allowed.
- *Pentachord ambitus*: melodic events restricted to the first five degrees of the chosen scale;
  line-end cadence on the reciting tonic.
- *Antiphonal offset*: Voice 1 = Voice 0 shifted by exactly one line.
- *Drone invariance*: Voice 2 constant (a fourth below tonic).
- *Seto drift option*: tonic transposed upward per strophe (e.g. +1 semitone/strophe) until
  kergütamine reset (Voice 3 register clamp).
- *No end-rhyme*; *no instruments* in the Estonian regilaul variant (Voice 2 = 0).

## 7. Quirks & pitfalls

- **Parallelism ≠ repetition.** The parallel line must restate with *synonyms and variation*; literal
  repetition is a beginner error (and Lönnrot's editorial overuse of parallelism is a documented
  stylistic distortion of the source tradition).
- **Quantity over stress.** The Kalevala meter is *quantitative* (long/short syllable structure), not
  English-style stress-meter; translating/imitating in a non-Finnic language breaks foot placement —
  English translations typically settle into 7-syllable lines for this reason.
- **5/4 is a recitation pulse, not a dance meter.** The asymmetric 3+2 grouping comes from the broken
  tetrameter's rhythm; forcing it into a regular 4/4 grid erases the murrelmasäe character.
- **The melody is a vehicle, not the work.** Reusing one group tune for many texts is correct
  practice; expecting melody-per-text (songwriter instinct) inverts the tradition.
- **Drift must be reset.** In Seto leelo the rising tonic is relieved by kergütamine — an
  un-reset drift eventually strands the killõ descant.
- **Unison is optional.** Older Seto torrõ is deliberately not strict unison; grid-quantizing it to
  exact unison loses the practice's grain.

## What It Is

Twelve-tone serial composition (dodecaphony) is a method of pitch organization devised by Arnold Schoenberg in 1923 in which all 12 notes of the chromatic scale are arranged into a fixed ordered sequence called a *tone row* (or *series*). This row — and its derived permutations (retrograde, inversion, retrograde inversion at any transposition level) — provides the exclusive pitch material for the entire composition. The technique was the first systematic alternative to tonality, designed to ensure "the emancipation of the dissonance" — no interval is preferred, and no pitch serves as a tonic.

The method does NOT prescribe rhythm, dynamics, register, articulation, or form. Those remain the composer's free choice — and it is precisely in those choices that the composer's craft, personality, and expressive intent become audible.

## Complete Craft Procedure (Human-Side, Step by Step)

### Phase 1: Row Construction (The Seed)

1. **Select all 12 pitch classes** of the chromatic scale: C–C♯/D♭–D–D♯/E♭–E–F–F♯/G♭–G–G♯/A♭–A–A♯/B♭–B. Arrange them in any order such that each appears exactly once.
2. **Avoid tonal implications**: do not let the first 3–5 notes spell out a major/minor triad, seventh chord, or common scale fragment. If the row begins C–E–G (a C major triad), rewrite it.
3. **Select interval content** consciously. The row's interval vector (how many of each interval class 1–6) determines the work's harmonic character. Schoenberg's rows often included multiple perfect fifths (ic5) for warmth; Webern's rows emphasized minor 3rds (ic3) and major 3rds (ic4) for symmetry.
4. **Design for hexachordal combinatoriality** (Schoenberg's mature practice): Ensure that the first hexachord (notes 1–6) and the last hexachord (notes 7–12) are different pitch-class sets. Then check whether the first hexachord of P0 and the first hexachord of I5 are complementary (i.e., together they contain all 12 pitch classes). If they are, the row is hexachordally combinatorial.
5. **Check for invariance**: examine which transposition levels of I produce shared hexachord content with P0. These invariant relationships become structural pivot points.
6. **Number the row in pitch-class integers** (C=0, C♯=1, … B=11) for matrix construction.

### Phase 2: Matrix Construction (The Grid)

7. **Write P0** as the first row of a 12×12 grid: the 12 pitch classes in order.
8. **Write I0** (the inversion) as the first column: the interval inversion of P0. To compute: for each adjacent interval in P0, invert the direction. If P0 goes +2 semitones (C→D), I0 goes −2 semitones (C→B♭).
9. **Fill the rest of the grid**: each row N is a transposition of P0 starting on the Nth pitch class of I0. Each column is a transposition of I0 starting on the Nth pitch class of P0.
10. **Verify**: every row and every column of the completed matrix contains all 12 pitch classes exactly once.

### Phase 3: Composition (Realizing the Work)

11. **Select row forms for the overall formal plan**. A typical design:
    - **Exposition**: P0 (the prime row, establishing the reference).
    - **Contrast**: I5 or P5 (inversion or transposition offering complementary hexachord content).
    - **Development**: rapid alternation of row forms (e.g., change every 2–4 bars), simultaneous statements in different voices, partitioning of a single row across instruments.
    - **Recapitulation**: return to P0 at its original transposition level.
    - **Coda**: R0 or RI0 for a symmetrical closing gesture.
12. **Assign row forms to voices**: Each voice (or instrument in a texture) carries its own linear presentation of a row form. When two or more voices state different row forms simultaneously, vertical sonorities emerge from the alignment of their respective row positions.
13. **Partition the row** (advanced technique): Take a single row statement and distribute its 12 notes across multiple voices according to a fixed pattern:
    - *Trichordal partition*: 4+4+4 — each of 3 voices plays 4 notes.
    - *Hexachordal partition*: 6+6 — two voices share the row.
    - *Cross partition* (Dallapiccola, Schoenberg Op. 33a): distribute row pitches across a grid of voices × registers such that each vertical slice through the partition also forms a complete aggregate.
14. **Compose rhythm, register, dynamics, and articulation** freely. These non-pitch parameters supply the phrase shape, expression, and formal pacing. Without deliberate shaping here, the row sounds mechanical (the "bingo-ball" effect).
15. **Use combinatoriality for seamless transitions**: When P0 and I5 share hexachordal content, the end of P0's first hexachord can overlap with the beginning of I5's first hexachord, creating an unbroken 12-tone flow between sections.
16. **Cadence via aggregate completion**: A formal boundary is often marked by the completion of a full aggregate — all 12 pitch classes have sounded in the current row form(s), and the next row form begins.

## Practitioner Examples

### Arnold Schoenberg (Founder, 1874–1951)
- **Suite for Piano, Op. 25** (1923) — first fully 12-tone work. Row: E–F–G–C♯–G♯–A–D–B–C–A♯–D♯–F♯. Uses all four row forms across a Baroque suite structure. Row presented thematically: each movement opens with the row as a melodic statement.
- **Variations for Orchestra, Op. 31** (1928) — landmark orchestral 12-tone work. Theme presents the row monophonically. 9 variations + finale demonstrate how a single row generates an orchestral work's melodic, harmonic, and contrapuntal content.
- **String Quartet No. 4, Op. 37** (1936) — mature combinatorial practice. Row: D–C♯–A–B♭–F–E♭–E–C–A♭–G–F♯–B. Hexachordally combinatorial with I5. The finale is a double fugue using different row forms in four voices simultaneously.
- **Mature practice** (1950 theoretical summary): (1) hexachordal inversion combinatoriality, (2) partitioning, (3) isomorphic partitioning (partition structure preserved across transformations), (4) invariance as structural pivot.

### Anton Webern (Symmetry & Pointillism, 1883–1945)
- **Symphony, Op. 21** (1928) — row: C–D♯–F♯–E–G♯–F–A–B♭–C♯–A♯–D–G. The row exposes a 4-note trichord structure; the entire work derives from this small seed.
- **Variations for Piano, Op. 27** (1936) — symmetric row (retrograde of inversion = prime at some transposition). Symmetry reduces distinct row forms from 48 to 24. Pointillistic texture: each note of the row assigned to a unique register/instrument, making register a structural parameter.
- **String Quartet, Op. 28** (1938) — the BACH motif (B♭–A–C–B♮) is embedded at positions 9–12 of the row. Webern's procedure: durations proportional to intervals, registral displacement, Klangfarbenmelodie (melody distributed across changing timbres).
- **Webern hallmarks**: palindromic/symmetrical rows, derived-row technique (all material from a tiny seed), registral displacement as primary structural parameter, Klangfarbenmelodie.

### Alban Berg (Lyric Serialism, 1885–1935)
- **Lyric Suite** (1926) — multiple rows derived from a single hexachord pair. Mixes tonal triads (G minor opening) within the 12-tone framework. Row succession follows a dramatic program across 6 movements.
- **Violin Concerto** (1935) — row: G–D–A–E–B♭–C♯–E♭–F♯–G♯–A♯–B–C. First four notes = violin open strings. Quotes Bach chorale (Es ist genug, BWV 60). Berg's trademark: tonal references coexist with atonal serialism; rows serve dramatic/narrative ends; flexible treatment (notes may be repeated or skipped for expression).
- **Lulu** (1937) — opera-length serial composition. Each character has a derived row from a 4-note "basic series." Dramatic relationships encoded as intervals between character rows.

### Later Practitioners
- **Milton Babbitt** — all-partition arrays: pre-compositional designs specifying set classes for each vertical slice. *Partitions*, *All Set*.
- **Pierre Boulez** — *Structures I* (1952): integral serialism — a single row governs pitch, duration, dynamics, and attack mode. The human craft: designing the pre-compositional grid.
- **Igor Stravinsky** — late-period serialism (1953–1970). Rotational arrays and vertical spacing derived from the row. Works: *Agon*, *Requiem Canticles*, *Threni*.
- **Luigi Dallapiccola** — lyrical serialism. Cross partition technique: distributing row notes across instruments in consistent register/grouping patterns. *Il Prigioniero*, *Canti di Prigionia*.

## UnitMatrix Mapping

### Voices

| Voice | Role | Row-Form Responsibility |
|-------|------|----------------------|
| Voice 0 | **Row Spine / Thematic Voice** | Carries P0 (or primary row form) linearly. Establishes the row identity at section onset. The "prime" voice. |
| Voice 1 | **Contrast / I-Voice** | Carries I0 or I5 (the inversion). Provides harmonic complement. Often enters after V0 has stated first hexachord of P0, enabling combinatorial overlap. |
| Voice 2 | **Retrograde / Development Voice** | Carries R, RI, or derived-row forms. Active in development; thinner texture, higher register. |
| Voice 3 | **Partition / Cross-Partition Voice** | Carries a segment (trichord/tetrachord/hexachord) of a single row form in cross-partition distribution. Together with other partition voices, completes the aggregate per time unit. |
| Voice 4–N | **Additional Partition or Free Voices** | Additional row-form carriers or timbral/registral layers. In dense textures, each voice may carry a different row form simultaneously. |
| Percussion | Freely composed | Not pitch-serialized (unless integral serialism). |

### Sections

| Section | Description | Active Voices | Row Forms |
|---------|------------|--------------|-----------|
| S0_Exposition_P0 | Monophonic/homophonic row statement | V0 (may double at octave) | P0 |
| S1_Contrast_I | Inversion entry, new register | V0 (P0 or P5) + V1 (I0 or I5) | P0/P5 + I0/I5 |
| S2_Development | Rapid row alternation, simultaneous forms | V0–V3 active | Multiple, changing every 2–4 bars |
| S3_Stretto_Partition | Cross partition, max density | All voices, each carrying a segment of the row | Single row form partitioned |
| S4_Recap_P0 | Return to prime | V0 (+ V1 for harmonic filling) | P0 |
| S5_Coda | Aggregate completion, dissolution | V0 or V0 + V2 (R form) | R0 or RI0 |

### Rules (for musicom implementation)

1. **Row-order constraint**: Pitches in a voice must follow the active row form's order. No pitch may recur within a voice before the row completes (exception: immediate repetition, trill, tremolo).
2. **Aggregate completeness (vertical)**: The set of pitches sounding simultaneously across all voices at any moment should normally be a subset of the aggregate; the union of sequential aggregates should complete all 12 pitch classes per formal segment.
3. **Row-form pool**: Only the 48 permutations of the original row (12P × 4 transformations) supply pitch material. No other pitch classes.
4. **Combinatoriality filter**: P0 and I5 must be hexachordally complementary for Schoenbergian practice (their first hexachords together = all 12 pitch classes).
5. **Partition invariance**: If cross partition is used, partition segments must sum to the full row across all active voices, and the pattern must be consistent or itself serialized.
6. **Cadence via aggregate**: A formal section boundary is marked by aggregate completion — the point at which all 12 pitch classes of the current row form(s) have been stated.

## Quirks & Pitfalls

- **Expressive risk**: Row-order discipline without active rhythm/register/dynamics shaping produces mechanical-sounding music ("bingo-ball effect"). The human craft is in what you do *beyond* the row.
- **False combinatoriality**: A random row rarely has hexachordal combinatoriality. You must *design* the row for it. Most beginners' rows are non-combinatorial.
- **Schoenberg vs. Webern vs. Berg divergence**: These three composers used the same method with starkly different results. Schoenberg: row as theme. Webern: row as cell-generator. Berg: row as dramatic/narrative tool (tonal references allowed).
- **The 48-row trap**: Beginners think they must use all 48 forms. In practice, a well-crafted work uses 4–8 carefully chosen forms that relate combinatorially.
- **Integral serialism is NOT Schoenberg**: Boulez's *Structures I* serialized all parameters, removing the human free-choice that Schoenberg preserved. This is a separate (derived) method.
- **Analytical vs. creative confusion**: Music theorists analyze a 12-tone work by labeling every note with its row position. The composer did not necessarily work this way — Schoenberg often composed melody first and checked row compliance afterward.

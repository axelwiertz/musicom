## Description

Javanese gamelan gending composition is a **cyclically-punctuated architectural craft** built on nested colotomic structures (gendhing). The composer (empu karawitan) does not write a continuous score. Instead they fix three layers:

1. **The colotomic skeleton** — the gong cycle (gongan) that defines the form: how many beats per section, which gong/kempul/kenong/kethuk hits at which subdivision.
2. **The balungan** — the skeletal melody (literally "skeleton" or "outline") written in kepatihan cipher notation (numbers 1–7). 4-beat gatras, final note of each gatra is the structural seleh (cadence).
3. **The pathet** — the modal constraint set: which notes are prominent (tonic/dasar), which are avoided, what cadence formulas fit, and what cengkok (elaboration patterns) to draw from.

The composer then decides the **section order**: buka (introduction by rebab or bonang) → mérong (first section, slower) → ompak (bridge transition) → ngelik (rising contrasting section with vocals) → minggah/inggah (second main section, faster irama) → optional return → suwuk (ending cued by kendang). Each section is a multiple of complete gongan cycles.

## Craft Procedure (step by step)

### 1. Fix the tuning system (laras)
Choose **sléndro** (5-tone: 1-2-3-5-6, roughly equidistant ≈ 240¢ each) or **pélog** (7-tone: 1-2-3-4-5-6-7, unequal intervals, used as 5-tone subsets omitting either 4 or 7 depending on pathet). This is the pitch-material universe.

### 2. Fix the pathet (modal category)
Select one of 6 pathet (3 per laras) and internalize its constraints:

| Pathet | Tonic (dasar) | Strong | Avoided | Cadence formula |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sléndro nem | 2 | 5 | 1 | 6-5-3-2 |
| Sléndro sanga | 5 | 1 | 3 | 2-1-6-5 |
| Sléndro manyura | 6 | 2 | 5 | 3-2-1-6 |
| Pélog lima | 2 | 5 | 7 | 5-3-2-1 |
| Pélog nem | 5 | 1 | 7 | 2-1-6-5 |
| Pélog barang | 6 | 2 | 1 | 3-2-7-6 |

The tonic (dasar) is the pitch most often arrived at by seleh (cadence point) at phrase ends. Avoiding the "banned" pitch in the balungan except for special effect is a key constraint.

### 3. Choose the gendhing structure (colotomic form)
Decide the gongan cycle length and which interpunctuating instruments play where. Major forms from simplest to longest:

- **Lancaran**: 16-beat gongan. Pattern: `TWTN TPTN TPTN TPTG` (ketuk/wela/kenong → kempul/ketuk/kenong ×3 → ketuk/kempul/kenong/gong). The fastest, simplest form.
- **Ketawang**: 16-beat gongan, kempul on beats 4 and 12. Pattern: `p.Tp. TpN p.Tp. TpG` (kempyang+ketuk+kenong ×2 → gong). Used for vocal-heavy pieces.
- **Ladrang**: 32-beat gongan (8 gatras × 4 beats). Pattern: `pT pN pT pN ... pT pG` (kempyang/ketuk every beat pair, kenong every 2 gatras, gong after 8 gatras). The most common form.
- **Ayak-ayakan, Srepegan, Sampak**: wayang-derived variable-length forms. Sampak uses repeated single notes, srepegan is medium, ayak-ayakan is slower with variable gongan length.
- **Gendhing kethuk 2 arang/kerep, etc.**: The longest form. Mérong section has kethuk playing only on odd-numbered gatras (kerep) or doubled-odd gatras (arang). Minggah section uses kempyang. A single gongan may last 64 beats or more.

### 4. Compose the balungan (skeletal melody)
Write the core melody in kepatihan cipher (numbers) in 4-beat gatras. Each gatra ends on a seleh note that conforms to the pathet's cadence hierarchy. The balungan is the **shared invariant** that all elaborating instruments decorate differently. General rules:
- Final note of each gatra (seleh) must be a structurally important pathet note.
- The final gatra of each section ends on the tonic (dasar) of the pathet.
- Contour should have a gradual rise/fall profile across the section.
- Avoid the pathet's forbidden note in structural positions.

### 5. Write the buka (introduction)
The buka is a solo introduction, typically played by the rebab (bowed string, soft style) or bonang (horizontal gong-chime row, loud style). It presents the pathet's character, states the tonic, and ends on the note that is the first note of the balungan (the buka's final note flows into the first gatra's final note). Length: usually 2–4 gatras, free meter.

### 6. Set the section architecture
Standard gending progression:

```
Buka (solo intro, free meter)
  → Mérong (first main section, slow irama I, kempyang absent)
     [Optional Ngelik in mérong: a rising contrasting sub-section, same gongan structure, higher register]
  → Ompak (bridge, 1 nongan, contrasting balungan, kendang cue, transitions to minggah)
  → Minggah / Inggah (second main section, faster irama, kempyang now active)
     [May switch to ladrang structure: "minggah ladrang"]
  → Suwuk (ending cue: kendang signals final gong, kempul/kethuk/kenong pattern sequence, final gong ageng)
```

Each section consists of a whole number of gongan cycles (usually 1 gongan per section, repeated). The ngelik sub-section, when present, sits inside mérong — it is higher in register, more animated, and has its own balungan but shares the same colotomic structure.

### 7. Determine irama (temporal density level)
Choose the irama level that controls the ratio between balungan pace and elaborating instruments:
- **Irama I** (lancar): balungan = 1 note per beat, elaborating instruments at 2× or 4×.
- **Irama II** (tanggung): balungan = 1 note per 2 beats, elaborators at 4× or 8×. Quarter-note feel.
- **Irama III** (dadi): balungan = 1 note per 4 beats, elaborators at 8× or 16×. Slow, spacious.
- **Irama IV** (wiled): balungan = 1 note per 8 beats, elaborate cengkok. Extremely slow.

### 8. Determine the garap (realization rules per instrument)
The balungan is realized differently by each instrument section — this is the domain of the performer, not the composer, but the composer's choices constrain it:

- **Saron family** (demung, saron, peking): Play the balungan as-is (demung/saron) or in 2× diminution (peking). The skeleton itself.
- **Bonang** (barung, panerus): Play imbal (interlocking elaboration), gembyangan (octave doubling on offbeats), anticipating the seleh note of each gatra.
- **Gendèr**: Play cengkok (standardized elaboration patterns keyed to the seleh note of each gatra). Each seleh has ~6–10 named cengkok variants.
- **Rebab**: Play lagu (melody) — a nuanced, phrase-shaped version of the balungan with slides, bends, and micro-timing. The rebab leads the ensemble.
- **Suling** (bamboo flute): Elaborates with breath-phrased melodic contours, doublings at the octave.
- **Gambang** (xylophone): Rapid 16th-note running elaboration across the full range, respecting pathet constraints.
- **Kendhang** (drum): Leads tempo, irama changes, section transitions, and the suwuk cue. The kendhang player is the conductor.
- **Vocal** (pesindhèn female solo / gerong male chorus): Sings cengkok-derived melodic lines, gerong provides harmonic block texture in parallel thirds/fourths.

### 9. Rehearse, iterate, refine
Because gamelan is traditionally learned orally (no notation in the classical method), the composer teaches each section by singing or playing the balungan. The elaborating musicians apply their cengkok vocabulary on the spot. The composer adjusts the balungan when the "feel" (rasa) does not match the intended pathet mood. After enough passes, the piece becomes codified.

## UnitMatrix Integration

### Voices

| Voice | Name | Instrument | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voice 0 | **Gong Spine (Colotomy)** | gong ageng, kenong, kempul, kethuk, kempyang | **Invariant colotomic cycle** — the form skeleton. Gong ageng = gongan boundary. Kenong = nongan (¼ gongan). Kempul = subdivides nongan. Kethuk interlock with kempyang. Never deviates from the chosen bentuk. |
| Voice 1 | **Balungan Core** | saron, demong, slenthem | **Skeletal melody** — the composed pitch spine. 4-beat gatra grid, seleh on beat 4. Invariant per section. |
| Voice 2 | **Elaboration Layer** | gendèr, bonang, gambang, peking | **Cengkok/imbal realization** — pattern-based elaboration of Voice 1. Each seleh triggers a pre-composed cengkok pattern. Peking = 2× diminution. Bonang = offbeat octave anticipation. Gendèr = multi-octave figuration. |
| Voice 3 | **Lead Interpretation** | rebab, suling, pesindhèn | **Lagu** — nuanced human-shaped melody. Slower than Voice 2, phrase-contoured, microtonal bends (rebab), breath phrasing (suling). The expressive soul. |
| Voice 4 | **Pulse & Transition** | kendhang (kendhang ageng + ketipung), kepyak | **Temporal director** — kendang patterns set irama level, signal section breaks (ompak, suwuk), drive tempo changes. Cypher (kepyak) hits for cueing. |

### Sections

| Section | irama | Colotomy | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| S0 | Buka | (free meter, solo) | Reb introduces pathet, ends on balungan's first seleh. |
| S1 | Mérong (S1 = irama I–III, slow) | Gong cycle per bentuk (no kempyang) | Main statement, repeated. Establishes pathet. |
| S2 | Ngelik (inside Mérong, same/slightly faster) | Same colotomy as S1 | Rising contrasting sub-section, higher register. |
| S3 | Ompak (bridge) | 1 nongan, kendang cues | Transition balungan, contrasting contour, leads to minggah. |
| S4 | Minggah (Inggah, irama I–II, medium-fast) | Same gongan length, kempyang now active | Second main section, animated, higher density. |
| S5 | Suwuk (end cue) | Trumpet sequence: kendang → final gong ageng | Complete the cycle. Final gatra selects pathet tonic. |

### Rules

1. **Colotomy Invariance**: Voice 0's gong cycle pattern is fixed per bentuk — never altered mid-piece.
2. **Zero-drift**: All sections = integer number of gatra (4-beat units). Total voice lengths are multiples of the gongan length in beats.
3. **Pathet Pitch Set**: Voice 1 (balungan) only uses notes within the 5-tone subset of the pathet. Pitches outside this set are forbidden in skeletal positions.
4. **Seleh Hierarchy**: The final note of each gatra (beat 4) must be a pathet-allowed pitch. Final gatra of each section = tonic (dasar).
5. **Buka Final → Balungan First**: The buka's last note is the same as the first gatra's seleh note — seamless entry.
6. **Ngelik Register**: Voice 1 in ngelik operates one octave higher than mérong.
7. **Irama Density Grid**: Voice 2 (elaboration) density = irama_multiplier × Voice 1 density. Irama I = 2×, II = 4×, III = 8×, IV = 16×.
8. **Kendang Cue Lock**: Section transitions (ompak, suwuk, irama shift) only happen at a kendang stroke pattern. Piece cannot transition without it.
9. **Minggah Kempyang Rule**: Voice 0's kempyang is only active in minggah/inggah, never in mérong.
10. **Suwuk Convergence**: The final gatra returns to the pathet tonic (dasar). The gong ageng strikes simultaneously with the final kenong — the piece is complete only when the gong's resonance fades.

## Quirks & Pitfalls

- **Pélog is not 7-tone**: Though pélog has 7 tones in the tuning system, any single composition only uses 5 (omitting either 4 or 7). The unused tones appear only in rare ornamental passages.
- **Colotomic ≠ simple meter**: Lancaran's 16-beat cycle is NOT 4 bars of 4/4 — the strong beat is the final beat of each gatra (seleh), not the downbeat. Western-trained composers routinely misplace the accent.
- **Balungan ≠ melody**: The balungan is NOT the melody in the Western sense. It's a skeleton from which each instrument derives its own part. What sounds like "the tune" comes from the rebab or pesindhèn, not the saron.
- **Irama is not tempo**: Irama III (dadi) is much slower in density but the underlying pulse (keteg) may not change much. Western musicians confuse the two.
- **Garap is performer domain**: The composer of a traditional gendhing does not write out what the gendèr or gambang play — they rely on the performer's knowledge of cengkok repertoire. A new composition that violates known cengkok grammar sounds "wrong" to Javanese ears.
- **Pathet six-slot rules are simplifications**: Real pieces often mix pathet, especially in wayang. Martopangrawit himself noted that many gendhing "stray" from their assigned pathet to create emotional contrast — the avoidance rule is a guide, not a jail.

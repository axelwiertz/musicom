## 2. What the method is

**Isorhythm** ("the same rhythm", Greek; term coined 1904 by Friedrich Ludwig) organizes the most ambitious 14th-century music on **two independent repeating cycles applied to one voice**: a repeating rhythm pattern (**talea**) and a repeating melody pattern (**color**). The cycles are deliberately of different lengths, so the chant melody is heard under ever-changing rhythmic disguises until the cycles realign.

Three structural facts make it a genuine human composition method:
1. **Melody borrowed, rhythm invented.** The color is a pre-existing Gregorian chant melisma (*Terribilis est locus iste*, *In omnem terram*, the "David" melisma). The craft is *cutting* the chant into a color, *patterning* time into a talea, and *planning* how many statements to build before realignment.
2. **Form is arithmetic before it is sound.** Sections = color statements; each carries an integer number of taleae (Machaut's Kyrie: color 28 ÷ talea 4 = 7). Unequal cycles (color 9, talea 5) realign only after the LCM — the ratio *is* the duration.
3. **Diminution written once, heard many times.** Later statements re-read the *same notation* under new mensuration signs (Alanus 9/8→6/8→4/8; Dufay 6:4:2:3). The composer writes the tenor once; the signs are the speed changes.

## 3. Craft procedure in detail

### Step 0 — Select the cantus firmus
Pre-existing plainchant melisma (introit, antiphon, gradual/alleluia melisma, Office antiphon) as the tenor's material; transposition normal (Alanus +5th).

### Step 1 — Carve the color (pitch cycle)
Fixed ordered pitch sequence from the chant: Machaut's Kyrie color = 28 pitches; Dufay's = 14 notes of *Terribilis* (period Florentine reading; modern version has 17).

### Step 2 — Compose the talea (rhythm cycle)
Pattern of note values *and rests* (longa/breve/semibreve/minima): Machaut's Kyrie talea = 4 durations; Dufay's = 28 longae of which the first 14 are rests. Rests are structural.

### Step 3 — Fix the ratio and alignment schedule
- Equal division (28:4 → 7 taleae per color): identical alignment each color statement.
- Unequal (9:5): drift; realigns after 5 colors (LCM 45) — composer plans statement count.
- Double tenor: two tenors, one color, interlocking taleae (Dufay: a fifth apart, 28-longa cycles).

### Step 4 — Lay out macro-structure
Free introitus + N color statements = N large sections; Dufay: 4 statements × 2 sub-sections (bicinium → full ensemble) = 8 parts.

### Step 5 — Rhythmize the tenor
Color notes against talea durations; talea overrun into the next color when unequal → audible slip; talea rests silence the tenor at fixed cycle points.

### Step 6 — Plan diminution
Same tenor re-read under new mensurations: Alanus 9/8→6/8→4/8 (threefold, 3 taleae/color, 9-part structure); Dufay 6:4:2:3 (tempus perfectum → imperfectum → imperfectum diminutum → perfectum diminutum). Proportional operator, not new notes.

### Step 7 — Compose the upper voices
Triplum + motetus, each with its **own text** (polytextuality): motetus ≈ 5th above tenor, triplum ≈ 8ve–12th; contratenor (4th voice, Vitry's generation) as free filler. Vertical style: imperfect consonances (3rds/6ths) mid-phrase, perfect at cadence with decorated approach. **Panisorhythm** (late style): upper voices get their own taleae — Pycard's Old Hall mass: 4 upper taleae vs recurring lower color+talea, 4:9 desync, then Pythagorean contraction 12:9:8:6 realigns the parts.

### Step 8 — Apply hocket
1–2-note fragments traded rapidly between voices (hocket = "hiccup"): Machaut's *Hoquetus David* is a whole untexted 3-voice piece built this way; inside motets it punctuates phrase ends/climaxes.

### Step 9 — Fit text underlay
Syllable counts govern upper-voice phrase rhythm; melismas on penultimate/cadential syllables; poetry deliberately *not* aligned with tenor seams (Dufay: 4 strophes × 7 lines of 7–8 syllables).

### Step 10 — Notate in mensural notation & copy
Tempus/prolatio signs double as diminution instructions; Machaut curated his complete-works manuscripts (rare for the era); modern editions transcribe into bar-lines with the color/talea structure diagrammed.

|---|---|
| **Roman de Fauvel** (BN fr. 146, c. 1316–17) | 169 interpolated pieces; among the earliest isorhythm-organized works; several linked to Vitry. |
| **Philippe de Vitry** (1291–1361) | Ars Nova theorist-composer; four-voice isorhythmic motets (*Garrit Gallus / In nova fert*; Fauvel motets) with contratenor. |
| **Guillaume de Machaut** (c. 1300–1377) | Master poet-composer. *Messe de Nostre Dame* (c. 1360): Kyrie tenor = **color 28 pitches × talea 4 durations × 7** — canonical example. | **Johannes Alanus** (late 14th c.) | *Sub arturo plebs / Fons citharizantium / In omnem terram*: antiphon *In omnem terram* transposed +5th; **threefold diminution** (9/8→6/8→4/8); 3 colors × 3 taleae = 9-part structure. |
| **Pycard** (early 15th c., Old Hall MS) | Panisorhythmic mass: lower parts recurring color+talea; upper parts **four different taleae**; 4:9 ratio desynchronizes, then 12:9:8:6 Pythagorean contraction realigns. |
| **John Dunstable / Johannes Ciconia** (early 15th c.) | Panisorhythmic motets and Mass movements with unequal color/talea drift. |
| **Guillaume Du Fay** (c. 1397–1474) | *Nuper rosarum flores* (1436, Florence Cathedral consecration): **double tenor** on the 14-note color of *Terribilis est locus iste* (a fifth apart, interlocking taleae 28 longae w/ 14 rests), stated 4× under **6:4:2:3**; proportions = Solomon's Temple (Wright 1994; Warren's "dome" claim refuted); 8-part structure (bicinium → tutti). | **20th-c. afterlife** | Isorhythm as analytical lens for non-Western cyclic musics (peyote songs, Indian/African traditions); cyclic misalignment resurfaces in minimalist phasing (Reich). |

**Canonical repertory:** Machaut *Messe de Nostre Dame* (Kyrie/Sanctus/Agnus), M2, *Hoquetus David*; Vitry *Garrit Gallus / In nova fert*; Alanus *Sub arturo plebs*; Pycard Old Hall mass; Dufay *Nuper rosarum flores*, *Ecclesie militantis*.

## 5. UnitMatrix mapping (Musicom engine)

**Layer:** concrete. Tenor cells = one talea statement (color pitch vector × talea duration vector); sections = color statements; upper voices = free/panisorhythmic note streams in fixed range strata.

**Voices (rows):**
- **V0 — Tenor (cantus firmus spine):** color pitch vector rhythmized by talea duration vector; lowest register; piece identity.
- **V1 — Contratenor (optional):** free filler between tenor and motetus; may carry own cyclic organization (panisorhythm).
- **V2 — Motetus:** upper texted voice, independent text; range ≈ tenor+3..+7.
- **V3 — Triplum:** highest texted voice; hocket fragments with V2; range ≈ tenor+8..+14.
- *(V4 optional: instrumental doubling of V0 — sackbut/curved trumpet/organ, period practice.)*

**Sections (columns):**
- **S0 — Introitus:** free, un-isorhythmic opening (1–2 phrases).
- **S1…SN — Color statements:** one per section; k talea repetitions each; section tick-lengths scaled by the diminution proportions (6:4:2:3; 9:8→6:8→4:8).

**Rules to encode:**
1. **Color lock:** V0 pitch sequence fixed per statement (transposition allowed, never permuted).
2. **Talea lock:** V0 duration vector repeated k×; section tick-len = k × talea-len; LCM governs unequal-ratio spans; piece ends at (or deliberately before) realignment.
3. **Diminution operator:** per-section tick-scale factor pᵢ (6:4:2:3) — same duration vector, scaled section lengths, not new note values.
4. **Range strata:** motetus +3..+7, triplum +8..+14 above tenor; no crossing.
5. **Cadence gate:** section-final sonority = perfect (unison/8ve/5th) with decorated approach; mid-vertical imperfect (3rds/6ths); no functional progression — HARMONY emergent.
6. **Hocket operator:** 1–2-note fragment alternation between two voices on the subdivision grid (pure case: *Hoquetus David*).
7. **Polytextuality:** V2/V3 independent syllable budgets; phrase seams need not align with section seams.
8. **Talea rests = explicit silence cells** (Dufay 14-of-28 longae) — a cell type, not a gap.
9. **Zero-drift:** all cells end at the section terminal landmark (section_len from diminution); validate() must pass.
10. **Emergent harmony check:** vertical interval classes constrained only (imperfect mid-phrase, perfect at cadence) — never a composed chord layer.

## 8. Quirks & pitfalls

- **"Isorhythm" is an analyst's term** (Ludwig 1904); medieval composers thought in *talea* and *color* — don't reify the ism (Bent's critique).
- **"Color" ≠ timbre** — here it means melodic repetition/embellishment (medieval Latin sense).
- **Rests are structural** (Dufay's tenor silent 14-of-28 longae) — engines must model silence as a cell type.
- **Diminution is proportional, not recomposed** — implement as per-section tick scaling (6:4:2:3), not rewritten note values.
- **Numerology:** Wright (1994) refuted Warren's "Brunelleschi's dome" reading of *Nuper rosarum flores*; the 6:4:2:3 = Solomon's Temple (1 Kings 6).
- **Chant source versions differ** — Dufay's 14-note *Terribilis* is the period Florentine reading, not the modern 17-note one.
- **Mensural arithmetic is tricky** (tempus/prolatio, alteration) — transcribe via established editions.
- **Upper voices are not generated** until panisorhythm — auto-cycling them produces the late style, not the 14th-century default.
- **Text independence is the point** — aligning poetry to tenor seams destroys the polytextual counterpoint of two time-grids.

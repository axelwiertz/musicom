## 1. Method name & tradition

**Canon composition** — the contrapuntal craft of deriving one or more whole voices from a *single composed
line* (the **dux**, "leader") by strict, rule-governed imitation at a fixed temporal distance (Δt) and
pitch interval (Δp): the follower(s) — the **comes** (plural *comites*) — is the same melody transposed,
shifted in time, and optionally transformed (retrograde, inversion, augmentation/diminution, mensuration,
modulation-by-degree). In Renaissance/Baroque usage the word *canon* (Greek κανών = "law", "rule") named
*the rule itself* — the instruction stating how many parts, at which entry points, and by which
transposition the other voices are derived from the one written line; such a canon was often appended as a
motto or sign in the score. Before the 16th century strict imitation was called *fuga ligata* ("fettered
fugue"); the *canon* of the early era is the rule, not the piece.

- The method is the **strictest form of imitation**: where fugue (HC-055) states the subject and then
  *departs* into episodes and key areas, a canon never departs — from the moment the comes enters, every
  new note of the dux must remain in good counterpoint with the already-sounding notes of the comes. The
  whole art is the **engineering of one line that harmonizes with itself**.
- Canons come in a graded family: **simple canons/rounds** (comes at the unison/octave — *Row, Row, Row
  Your Boat*), **interval canons** (comes at the 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th … 9th), **inversion/mirror canons**
  (*al rovescio*, contrary motion), **retrograde/crab canons** (*cancrizans* — the follower runs the
  dux backwards), **augmentation/diminution canons** (follower in longer/shorter note values), **sloth
  canons** (extreme augmentation), **mensuration/prolation canons** (the same notation read under
  different time-signature proportions — different speeds), **spiral/perpetual canons** (the famous
  "endlessly rising" canon), **double/triple canons** (two/three simultaneous dux lines), and
  **puzzle/riddle/enigma canons** (only one line notated; the entry rule must be solved).
- It is a *composition/derivation procedure*, not an algorithm and not sound production: the human writes
  the dux by ear, drafts the comes against it note-by-note at the keyboard or desk, and tests the
  interlock by ear until the two strands sing together.

## 3. The craft procedure, step by step (how the human does it)

1. **Choose the frame** — number of voices (2, 3, 4, 6…), the imitative interval **Δp** (unison, 2nd,
   3rd, 4th, 5th, octave … or octave+), the temporal distance **Δt** (a beat, a bar, two bars), and the
   mode/key. Decide whether the canon is *finite* (ends on a cadence) or *perpetual* (a round, looping).
2. **Compose the dux** — a complete, shapely melody of 2–8 bars with a clear contour, a single climax,
   and a cadence-shaped tail. The dux must be *dual-role*: it will sound as the top line AND as the
   transposed/transformed inner or bottom line, so it must be singable in both registers and must not
   begin on a note that makes the comes entry clash with the dux's own accompaniment note.
3. **Fix the operator** — decide the transformation of the comes: exact transposition (strict canon:
   same interval qualities), **diatonic/free adjustment** (same interval *numbers* but with qualities
   adjusted to keep the mode — e.g. a major 3rd becomes minor where the scale demands), or a named
   operator: *contrary motion* (inversion around an axis note), *retrograde/crab* (cancrizans),
   *retrograde-inversion*, *augmentation/diminution* (double/halve note values), *mensuration/prolation*
   (same glyphs, different proportion signs → different speeds), *per tonos* (each loop ends a step
   higher — the spiral canon).
4. **Draft dux + comes together, note by note** — this is the heart of the craft. From the moment the
   comes enters, the composer works on the two lines *simultaneously* as a piece of two-part
   counterpoint, checking every vertical sonority:
   - **Forbidden parallels** between the strands: consecutive perfect consonances (octaves, fifths,
     unisons) approached in similar motion are rejected — the collision audit runs at every tick where
     both voices sound.
   - **Contrary motion bias**: where the strands run against each other, dissonance resolves by step;
     imitation at the 4th/5th is the hardest because the transposition makes perfect intervals stack —
     this is where diatonic adjustment (step 3) does its work.
   - **Cadence discipline**: when the comes approaches a cadence, the dux's line must be doing something
     that lets the two cadence *together* (or the dux must suspend/rhythmicize to make room).
5. **Engineer the terminal point** (finite canons) — the classic method, taught from Fux through
   Albrechtsberger: *plan the ending first, compose backwards*. The leader's final phrase is written so
   that when the comes reaches its cadence, the dux is simultaneously arriving at its own — typically by
   shortening the dux's tail, letting the dux drop out for a beat or two, or adding a short **free
   coda** after the strands converge (the "emancipated" tail, standard in the Goldberg canons).
6. **Add the free bass/accompaniment** (optional — the *accompanied canon*): when the canonic strands
   alone leave the harmony thin (e.g. two voices at the octave), a free bass or inner voice is composed
   underneath that harmonizes the composite; Bach's Goldberg canons all have such a bass (except the
   9th, which is a strict two-voice canon at the ninth).
7. **Dress it as a puzzle** (optional, the *puzzle/riddle canon*): write only the single dux line and
   append the rule as a motto or sign — e.g. Bach's "Quaerendo invenietis" ("seek and ye shall find"),
   the Musical Offering's "Notulis crescentibus crescat Fortuna Regis" (augmentation) and "Ascendenteque
   Modulatione ascendat Gloria Regis" (per tonos). The solver derives the entries; Mozart did exactly
   this against Padre Martini's puzzles (K. 73r) and Albrechtsberger defended the practice as
   "serv[ing] to sharpen acumen".
8. **Test by ear** — play/sing the strands together; verify the interlock is consonant, the cadence
   lands, and (for rounds) that the end of the melody flows seamlessly into its beginning — the
   "wheel" (rota) seam. Rounds add a final check: every staggered collision between voice N and N+1
   must be consonant, not just the first pair.

## 4. Practitioner examples

- **Sumer is icumen in** (Reading Rota, c. 1250, BL Harley MS 978) — the earliest surviving *six-voice*
  polyphony: a four-voice round at the unison (staggered entries over ~12 bars) above two free "pes"
  ostinato voices; the manuscript explicitly labels it *rota* ("wheel") and *canon*.
- **Guillaume de Machaut**, *Ma fin est mon commencement* (Rondeau 14, c. 1360s) — a **crab canon with a
  text palindrome**: "my end is my beginning / and my beginning my end" is literally enacted — the
  tenor is the retrograde of the cantus, the contratenor is its own retrograde, and the first couplet
  reads the same backwards.
- **Johannes Ockeghem**, *Missa prolationum* (c. 1460s) — the legendary **mensuration canon** mass:
  *every* movement is a canon; pairs of voices share one written line under different proportion signs
  (four voices, four different mensurations/speeds), and the imitation interval grows systematically
  movement by movement — unison → 2nd → 3rd … reaching the octave at the Osanna of the Sanctus.
- **Josquin des Prez**, *Missa L'homme armé super voces musicales*, Agnus Dei II — inscribed *"ex una
  voce tres"* ("three voices from one"): a triple mensuration canon from a single notated line.
- **J.S. Bach** — the summit of the craft: the **Goldberg Variations** place a canon every third
  variation at ascending intervals (Var. 3 = canon at the unison, 6 = 2nd, 9 = 3rd, 12 = 4th,
  15 = 5th, 18 = 6th, 21 = 7th, 24 = octave, 27 = 9th), each with a free bass; the **Musical
  Offering** (1747) collects ten canons on Frederick the Great's theme, including the celebrated
  **crab canon** (Canon a 2, cancrizans), the **endlessly rising canon** (*Canon a 2 per tonos*,
  modulating up a whole tone per loop with no final cadence), the *per augmentationem, contrario motu*
  canon, and the riddle canons *Quaerendo invenietis* and *Canon a 4*; the **Art of Fugue** closes with
  mirror canons/fugues (*Contrapunctus inversus* XII–XIII).
- **Mozart** — dozens of vocal canons K. 553–562 (the "social canons": *Bona nox*, *O du eselhafter
  Martin*, *Difficile lectu*, *Leck mich im Arsch*), the four Latin puzzle canons K. 73r (written after
  solving Padre Martini's), and a **mirror canon** in the trio of the Wind Serenade K. 388/384a.
- **Haydn** — the famous **canonic minuet** of the "Quinten" Quartet Op. 76 No. 2 (the two violins
  chase each other at the octave through the minuet's paired phrases), plus the six canons Hob.
  XXVIIb (*Thy voice, o Harmony*).
- **Beethoven** — canons WoO 159–198 (occasional/table canons, some riddle canons), a celebrated
  **canonic passage in Symphony No. 4** (the slow introduction's flute/bassoon exchange), and the
  strict canonic writing he produced under Albrechtsberger's instruction (1794–95) — Albrechtsberger
  having published the era's most thorough canon treatise (1790).
- **20th century** — Webern's *Five Canons* Op. 16 (soprano, clarinet, bass clarinet); Stravinsky's
  *Canon on a Russian Popular Tune* and *Double Canon*; **Conlon Nancarrow's** player-piano
  **tempo/mensural canons** (voices in different tempi, physically unplayable by humans, punched by
  hand); Arvo Pärt's mensuration canons *Cantus in Memoriam Benjamin Britten*, *Arbos*, *Festina
  Lente*.
- **English round tradition** — Purcell's "catches" (17th-c. rounds with bawdy double meanings) keep
  the perpetual canon alive in popular practice; the children's rounds *Row, Row, Row Your Boat* and
  *Frère Jacques* are the form's modern folk residue.

## 5. Musical Elements Framework reading

- **STRUCTURE** — the *frame*: entry groups (dux solo → comes entries at Δt), the terminal point /
  cadence convergence, sectioning for interval cycles (Goldberg-style: one canon per section at
  ascending Δp); rounds loop end→start (the rota seam).
- **PITCH** — the *operator plane*: Δp transposition, strict vs diatonic/free adjustment mask,
  inversion axis for mirror canons, retrograde = pitch-sequence reversal, mode/key constraint on the
  composite.
- **RHYTHM** — the *time plane*: Δt entry offset, augmentation/diminution duration scales,
  mensuration/prolation proportion factors (per-voice tempo ratios), syncopation/suspension placement
  for dissonance resolution against the other strand.
- **TEXTURE** — the *strand count*: one dux + N comites = N+1 canonic strands; free bass/accompaniment
  in accompanied canons; double/triple canon adds parallel canonic pairs.
- **HARMONY** — the *emergent* plane: the vertical sonorities arising where strands collide (the
  composer's counterpoint audit), cadence formulas at the terminal point, the free bass's harmonization
  of the composite.

## 6. UnitMatrix mapping

**Voices:**
- **V0 — Dux (leader).** The composed spine — the piece's identity anchor. Stated alone in the first
  entry group; continues through every later group as the reference strand.
- **V1 — Comes (follower).** The rule-derived strand: a copy of V0 shifted by Δt ticks and transposed
  by Δp semitones (with the diatonic-adjustment mask applied for free canons), or carrying the chosen
  operator (inversion, retrograde, augmentation/diminution, mensuration, per tonos).
- **V2 — Free bass / accompaniment.** Composed (not derived) harmonization under the canonic strands —
  the Goldberg-style bass that completes the harmony; active in accompanied canons, silent in strict
  unaccompanied ones.
- **V3 — Second canon pair / transformed strand (optional).** For double/triple canons (a second
  independent dux + its comes), for the Ockeghem-style second mensuration pair, or for a simultaneous
  inverted/retrograde variant (the "table canon" layout: one line, two readers).

**Sections** (equal length — zero-drift invariant; at 480 tpb / 4/4, one bar = 1920 ticks):
- Simple finite canon: S0 = Dux solo (lead-in, Δt long enough to establish the line), S1 = Comes entry
  (both strands, collision audit active), S2 = Terminal point (cadence convergence + optional free
  coda).
- Interval-cycle canon (Goldberg model): each section = one canon at the next ascending Δp (unison,
  2nd, 3rd … 9th), each with its free-bass layer and its own cadence.
- Perpetual canon / round: a single cyclic section whose end seam loops into its start; N voices
  staggered at equal Δt.

**Rules (what the engine would encode):**
- **Δt onset-lock:** comes events are V0 events delayed by exactly Δt ticks (480/960/1920…); the delay
  is a logged parameter, not free.
- **Δp transposition-lock:** comes pitches = V0 pitches + Δp semitones; strict canons use exact
  transposition, free canons apply a diatonic-adjustment mask (interval quality softened to the mode)
  — the mask must be logged so analysis can tell strict from free.
- **No-parallel-perfects audit:** at every tick where V0 and V1 both sound, consecutive perfect
  consonances (P1/P5/P8) by similar motion are rejected; the audit runs on the *composite*, including
  V2 in accompanied canons (bass doubling rules).
- **Contrary-motion bias:** when strands converge, stepwise resolution of dissonance; cadence
  approached by 2–3 or 7–1 in the outer voices.
- **Terminal-point convergence:** both strands must end on the same tick (finite canons) — the dux tail
  is shortened or a free coda added; this is the structural twin of the engine's zero-drift
  validate() gate (all rows equal length).
- **Operator transforms:** retrograde = reverse the V0 event list (pitch+duration, onset mirrored);
  inversion = pitch mirror about a logged axis with Δt preserved; augmentation = duration ×2 (×k);
  diminution = ÷2; mensuration = per-voice proportion factors (e.g. 2:1, 3:2) implemented as per-voice
  duration scaling; per tonos = each loop transposed +1 whole step and re-seamed.
- **Rota seam:** for rounds, the section's terminal event must voice-lead into its first event
  (end-to-start seam check); every staggered pair (V0/V1, V1/V2 … VN/V0) must pass the collision audit
  — not just the first pair.
- **Puzzle mode:** only V0 is written into the matrix; the entry rule (Δt, Δp, operator) is stored as
  section metadata — realization resolves the puzzle, exactly as a human solver would.
- **Playability/register:** comes transposition must stay within instrument range (the composer checks
  the dux's extremes against the chosen Δp).

## 7. Quirks & pitfalls

- **The hard interval is the 4th/5th.** Imitation at the 4th or 5th stacks perfect intervals between
  the strands; this is where diatonic (free) adjustment is not a cheat but the standard craft —
  Renaissance "canon" practice treated the adjusted answer as normal.
- **Canon vs fugue confusion.** Baroque terminology called all imitation "fugue" (*fuga ligata* for
  strict canon); a canon has *no episodes and no key areas* — if the imitation breaks, it's no longer a
  canon. Analyzers must not blur the boundary with HC-055.
- **The terminal point is the failure point.** Most student canons collapse at the end; the
  end-first/backwards method is the craft fix. A canon that ends by simply stopping (not cadencing
  together) is unfinished — except *perpetual* canons, which by definition don't end.
- **Perpetual/round seams are subtle.** *Sumer is icumen in* works because every staggered pair passes
  the audit; naive rounds collide at the loop point. Test all N pairs, not just the first.
- **Puzzle canons are a notation genre, not a compositional one** — "the enigmatical character … does
  not consist of any special way of composing it, but only of the method of writing it down"
  (Richter); even Morley grumbled that a solved puzzle "was scant worth the hearing". The unit of
  craft is the *line*, not the wrapper.
- **Mensuration canons need proportional reading.** Ockeghem's mass is unreadable under modern uniform
  time-signature assumptions; the proportion signs ARE the canon. In the UnitMatrix this means
  per-voice duration scaling must be applied before the zero-drift check.
- **Retrograde canons invert contour but not intervals.** A crab canon reverses the interval
  *sequence*; an inversion (mirror) canon reverses interval *directions*; retrograde-inversion does
  both — conflating them is the classic beginner error.
- **"Canon" the word is a rule, not a piece.** Medieval/Renaissance scores say "canon" meaning "here is
  the law by which the other voices are made" — the piece itself is the resulting polyphony. Treating
  the word as a genre name only became normal in later theory.

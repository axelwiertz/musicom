## 1. Method Name & One-Line Definition

**Improvisation-to-Notation Composition Workflow** — "capture the gesture, fix it on paper." The composer improvises first (at the instrument/voice, over a borrowed or minimal frame), then captures, selects, refines, and locks the best material into notation, producing a repeatable "work" that keeps the imprint of spontaneous gesture.

This is the inverse of write-then-play: the music is *played*, then *written*. It is simultaneously a Baroque/Classical desk discipline (C.P.E. Bach's free fantasy, Beethoven's improvisation→sketchbooks) and the core of modern jazz composition (Parker's notated improvised lines, Davis's studio-born modal heads, Jarrett's authorized transcription of a fully improvised concert).

## 2. Why It Is Human Craft (not algorithmic/AI)

- The essential human act is **selection under time pressure**: generating material is the easy half; deciding what to keep and what to discard is the craft.
- The frame (changes/modal cell) is a *musical* choice, not a computation: Parker's Cherokee, Davis's D-7/Eb-7.
- Taste judgments — how much to fix vs. leave free (lead-sheet minimalism vs. fully-notated score), whether to keep rubato/ornament in the notation — are explicitly human calls.
- The boundary between improvisation and composition is deliberately blurred (per musicological studies of Parker's processes: MTO 24.2, Martin 2018).

## 3. Craft Procedure (detail)

**Phase 0 — Prepare the improvising frame.**
Choose a scaffold: (a) borrowed stock progression (contrafact: Parker over *Cherokee*, *How High the Moon*, *I Got Rhythm*); (b) modal one/two-chord cell (Davis's D-7/Eb-7; Milestones' D-Dorian); (c) ground/ostinato (Baroque chaconne practice); (d) free-fantasy declaration (a key, tempo, mood, no fixed form). Set instrument, register, tempo — improvisation is physical; the frame must sit under the hands.

**Phase 1 — Improvise (generate).**
Play in real time, letting learned vocabulary (scales, arpeggios, licks, ornaments, rhythm patterns) flow over the frame. Shape phrases, vary rhythm profiles, aim for memorable contour. Test gestures by immediate varied repetition — a motif that survives repetition is a candidate.

**Phase 2 — Capture.**
Write down or record immediately; never trust memory for long. Capture media vary by practitioner: Beethoven's sketchbook jottings after concerts; Mozart's immediate notation; Parker's in-studio 8-bar scribbles; Davis's studio tape; Jarrett's concert recording; a trusted copyist/arranger (Gil Evans) as live transcriber.

**Phase 3 — Review & select.**
Listen/read back. Keep what has: memorable contour (distinctive leap, registral high point, surprising interval), rhythmic identity (survives without the harmony), repeatability (functions as a head/theme/motif). Discard ruthlessly — a 60-second improvisation may yield one 8-bar head.

**Phase 4 — Transcribe & regularize.**
Fix pitches and rhythm to the grid; choose meter and barring; simplify fluent fills; write the changes (or modal cell) under the line so others can play it.

**Phase 5 — Refine & develop.**
Edit the captured line, re-shape endings to cadence, add a bridge/channel (Parker revising *Ornithology*), extend into form (A sections repeat, contrast section, intro/outro), fill the texture (bass motion, comping voicings — or deliberately leave them to improvised practice).

**Phase 6 — Orchestrate/arrange (optional).**
Assign the captured line to a lead voice, add counter-voices, pads, percussion (Gil Evans orchestrated Davis's improvised lines into *Sketches of Spain* / *Miles Ahead*).

**Phase 7 — Lock the work.**
Freeze as lead sheet, score, or authorized transcription. Good practitioners preserve the gesture: rubato, swing, ornament remain in the notation as performance instruction instead of being erased by over-quantization (Jarrett's *Köln Concert* transcription keeps rhythmic flow; Parker lead sheets keep swing as convention).

## 4. Practitioner Examples

- **C.P.E. Bach** — *stylus fantasticus* / *freye Fantasie*: written Fantasias notate the improvisatory process (unbarred passages, sudden registral shifts); his *Versuch* teaches improvisation as the root of composition.
- **Mozart** — Fantasias K. 475 / K. 396-385g as notation of improvisatory flow; the *sprezzatura* ideal ("as if improvised") governed performance of even fully written music.
- **Beethoven** — archetype of improvise-then-write: legendary improvisations (Steibelt duel 1800 — took Steibelt's trio theme, inverted it, improvised ~1 hour) → "as soon as possible" jotted themes/motifs into sketchbooks (Kafka, Eroica sketchbooks) → refined over years into finished works (Fifth Symphony rhythm among traced examples).
- **Chopin** — composed at the keyboard after hours of salon improvisation; *Fantaisie-Impromptu* genre and rubato-laden surfaces preserve improvised gesture in fixed notation.
- **Charlie Parker** — *KoKo* (1945): an improvised alto line over *Cherokee* changes *is* the head. *Ornithology* / *Anthropology* = contrafacts from improvisation; MTO 24.2 (Martin 2018) documents composition *in the studio* ("Red Cross", "Blues (Fast)") with audible revisions, and Parker revising "Ornithology". Tommy Potter: "He'd write out eight bars, usually just for the trumpet... The channel could be ad libbed." No working manuscripts left — composed aloud.
- **Miles Davis** — "So What" / "Milestones" (1958): modal heads from studio humming/playing over minimal cells; melody over bass pedal with Red Garland comping. *Sketches of Spain* / *Miles Ahead*: Gil Evans notated and orchestrated Davis's improvised lines.
- **Keith Jarrett** — *The Köln Concert* (24 Jan 1975): entirely improvised solo concert, recorded live; published 1991 by Schott as *The Köln Concert: Original Transcription* — a canonized, learnable score of an improvisation.
- **Sonny Rollins** — heads (*Doxy*, *St. Thomas*) as improvised riffs fixed into song form.
- **Folk/oral** — collectors' notation of a singer's spontaneous variation re-enters tradition as a "new" fixed tune (improvisation feeds the oral canon; notation/recording is the freeze).

## 5. UnitMatrix Mapping (Voices & Sections, Rules)

**Two-phase pipeline over one matrix** (both inside the zero-drift UnitMatrix discipline):

### Phase A — CAPTURE (provisional matrix)
| Voice | Role | Contents |
|-------|------|----------|
| Voice 0 | Gesture/Lead | Loose improvisation seeds: rhythm profile + contour skeleton (exact rhythm fuzzy until fixation) |
| Voice 1 | Skeleton harmony | Chord-symbol frame cells — borrowed progression or modal cell (D-7→Eb-7) |
| Voice 2 | Bass/pedal skeleton | Root motion or pedal points (So What-style) |
| Voice 3 | Riff/comp skeleton (opt.) | Ostinato/comping pattern cell, repeated |

Sections = improvised phrase slots: `A1 A2 B A3` / 12-bar / modal 24-bar, each with a bar budget + active frame.

### Phase B — FIX (final matrix)
1. Memorability filter selects kept cells (contour highlight, registral climax, distinctive rhythm profile); rest discarded.
2. Quantize/regularize + pad every cell to its section terminal landmark → `validate()` zero-drift gate (the notation-freeze equivalent).
3. Realize skeleton chord cells → voicings with smooth voice-leading (`realize_progression(..., smooth=True)`); bass → root/pedal lines.
4. Fill texture: comp voice (rhythmic chord hits), bass voice, optional drums/ride pattern.
5. Repeated sections reuse identical fixed cells (head-repeat move).
6. Bridge/development = transformed A (varied motive or new frame).

### Encoded Rules
1. **Frame rule** — skeleton voices only use pitches of the declared frame pool.
2. **Seed-generation rule** — gesture candidates from frame pool with contour preservation + rhythm-profile preservation + 2–4 bar phrase budget.
3. **Memorability filter** — keep only cells with registral high point / distinctive interval / recurring rhythm profile (≥2).
4. **Fixation rule** — snap to grid, separate ornaments, pad to terminal landmark before validate; no over/under-fill.
5. **Realization rule** — common-tone retention, minimal motion voicings; bass root/fifth or pedal per frame type.
6. **Reuse rule** — repeated sections reference identical cells (structural repetition, not regeneration).
7. **Style-lee rule** — human decides lead-sheet minimal (skeleton comp/bass left free) vs fully-notated score (realize all voices).

## 8. Quirks & Pitfalls

- Selection is the craft — 90% of generated gesture is discarded; generation without the filter produces noodling.
- Over-fixation kills the gesture: full quantization + ornament erasure removes the as-if-improvised quality the method exists to preserve.
- Contrafact trap: the melody must stand alone; the head needs independent memorability even over borrowed changes.
- The frame is load-bearing: weak scaffold → weak head regardless of improvisation quality.
- In-studio capture is perishable: Parker's scribbles worked because the rhythm section knew the progressions by heart.
- Capture medium shapes the result: memory-notation (Beethoven) drifts toward refinement; tape-capture (Jarrett/Davis) preserves raw gesture.

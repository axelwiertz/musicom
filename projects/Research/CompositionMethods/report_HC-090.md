## 1. Method name & tradition

**Kalevala-Meter Rune Song Composition — Oral-Formulaic Parallelistic Song Craft**
(Finnish/Karelian/Izhorian *runolaulu* / *runonlaulanta*, Estonian *regilaul* / *regivärss*, Seto *leelo*).

- **Tradition / Culture:** Baltic-Finnic folk song, Proto-Finnic heritage estimated 1000–2000 years
  old; the living method behind the Finnish national epic *Kalevala* (Elias Lönnrot, Old 1835 / New
  1849, 22,795 verses) and the Estonian *Kalevipoeg* (F. R. Kreutzwald, 1857–61).
- **UNESCO ICH:** *Seto Leelo, Seto polyphonic singing tradition* — Representative List 2009
  (ID 00173); *Kihnu cultural space* (Estonia) — Representative List 2003.
- **Primary elements (Musical Elements Framework):** STRUCTURE, PITCH, RHYTHM, TEXTURE — plus TEXT as
  a first-class constraint (the meter rules operate on the text itself). HARMONY is not primary
  (drone-only, no functional harmony).

## 3. The craft procedure in detail (how the human does it)

1. **Learn the formulaic stock by ear** — no notation; oral, collective transmission. Apprentice
   internalizes the region's song corpus (hundreds–thousands of lines; Larin Paraske commanded
   >32,000 verses) and its **group tunes** (*rühmaviisid*): a small bank of short, small-range melodic
   formulas, each shared by many texts by function (swing tune, herd tune, wedding tune…).
2. **Compose in the Kalevala meter (regivärss)** — 8-syllable trochaic tetrameter, **no end rhyme**:
   - Strong syllables (long vowel/diphthong/consonant-closed, main stress) only in the **rising part**
     of feet 2–4; weak syllables (short, stressed) only in the **falling part**; unstressed = neutral,
     placeable anywhere; first foot free (even 3–4 syllables); no one-syllable word at line end.
   - Alternate **normal tetrameter** (stress = foot, caesura after foot 2: *Veli / kulta, // veikko/seni*)
     with **broken tetrameter** (*murrelmasäe*: stressed syllable in falling position, no caesura:
     *Miele/ni mi/nun te/kevi*) at roughly equal frequency — the alternation is the meter's signature.
3. **Bind each line by alliteration** — echo of initial consonant (weak) or initial vowel/vowel+consonant
   cluster (strong) within the line: *vaka vanha Väinämöinen*.
4. **Build parallelistic couplets** — the compositional unit is the idea-line + one synonymous parallel
   line (*In these dismal Northern regions / In the dreary land of Pohja*); chiasmus as variant.
   Narrative pauses during the parallel; the singer dwells, then launches the next idea.
5. **Set text to the melodic formula** — each 8-syllable line = two 4-syllable half-phrases on the
   **first five notes of the scale** (pentachord; ambitus = kantele range); recitation rhythm
   characteristically **5/4-oriented** (3+2 from the broken tetrameter), duple/sextuple regional
   variants; half-phrase and line ends cadence on the reciting tonic.
6. **Perform antiphonal lead–chorus = compose in performance** — lead (*runonlaulaja*/*eeslaulja*)
   sings the new line, chorus repeats it; the repeat is the **compositional thinking time** for the
   next line. Karelians held hands singing call-and-response; Ingrian tradition adds choral parallel
   voices. Solo performance for herd songs/lullabies; two-chorus alternation also occurs.
7. **Instrumental support (Finnish side)** — 5-string **kantele**, five-limit just intonation
   (D major/minor), gapped diatonic, lowest string = drone a fourth below tonic; plucked/strummed in
   the pentachord. **Estonian regilaul is unaccompanied** — rhythm marked by body sway, foot stamp,
   or dance (Seto *Pöörajooks*, Kihnu ring songs).
8. **Seto variant — polyphonic leelo with drift** — lead carries the **torrõ** main voice (several
   singers; older style deliberately **not strict unison**; men may add low **alumine torrõ**, lower
   scale notes, no fixed variation rules); solo **killõ** high descant above; the piece's **pitch
   level rises** during the song, relieved by **kergütamine** — lead abruptly restarts the next line
   lower (shared with Mordvin practice).

## 4. Practitioner examples

- **Arhippa Perttunen** (Viena Karelia, d. c. 1840s) — Lönnrot's principal Kalevala source (Sampo cycle).
- **Ontrei Malinen** (White-Sea Karelia) — source of the central Väinämöinen/Sampo songs.
- **Larin Paraske** (Izhorian, 1833–1904) — "Finnish Mnemosyne"; ~1,200 poems, 1,750 proverbs, 336
  riddles transcribed (Neovius); >32,000 verses; her hypnotic rune singing is said to feed Sibelius's
  *Kullervo* Op. 7.
- **Poavila & Triihvo Jamanen** (Karelian rune-singer brothers, photographed Uhtua 1894).
- **Anne Vabarna** (Seto, d. 1957; "Mother of Song") — composed the oral twin epic *The Maiden's Death
  Song & The Great Wedding*, written down by A. O. Väisänen (1923): a documented large-scale
  rune-song composition by a traditional singer.
- **Compilers/refractions:** Elias Lönnrot (*Kalevala*), F. R. Kreutzwald (*Kalevipoeg*),
  Jean Sibelius (*Kullervo*), Veljo Tormis (regilaul choral cycles), Värttinä (contemporary runo-song
  ensemble), Metsatöll (runo-metal), Martti Pokela (kantele revival).

## 5. Musical Elements Framework reading

- **STRUCTURE:** the parallelistic strophe (idea + parallel + chorus repeat) is the formal unit; songs
  chain strophes episodically — no fixed song form.
- **PITCH:** pentachord formula tunes; line-end cadence on reciting tonic; Seto upward drift;
  kantele drone a fourth below tonic.
- **RHYTHM:** quantitative Kalevala-meter syllable grid; 5/4-oriented recitation (3+2); duple/sextuple
  variants; sway/stamp accents.
- **TEXTURE:** monophonic lead + antiphonal chorus (Finnic norm); kantele drone + plucks (Finnish);
  Seto polyphony (torrõ block + killõ descant + optional low torrõ).
- **HARMONY:** not primary — drone only; vertical sonorities incidental.

| Row (Voice) | Role | Cell content |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Voice 0 | Lead singer | Melodic-formula units: 8-syllable line = two 4-syllable half-phrase events over the pentachord, text-locked (cell length = Σ syllable ticks), line-end tonic cadence. |
| Voice 1 | Chorus | Antiphonal copy of Voice 0's previous line (offset = one line) — the repeat/thinking seam. |
| Voice 2 | Kantele (Finnish variant, optional) | Invariant drone (4th below tonic) + pentachord pluck ostinato across all sections. |
| Voice 3 | Killõ descant / low torrõ (Seto variant, optional) | Higher/lower pentachord block voices; register follows drift curve. |

**Sections (Columns):** one section per strophe = idea-line + parallel + chorus repeat (S0 S1 S2 … Sn),
chained episodically.

**Rules to encode:**
1. Syllable-count lock: 8 syllables/line; no one-syllable line-final word (text constraint).
2. Stress-position map: strong → rising parts of feet 2–4; weak → falling parts; foot 1 free;
   normal/broken tetrameter alternation ~50/50.
3. Alliteration constraint: ≥2 stressed words per line share initial consonant/vowel echo.
4. Parallelism rule: section n+1 line 1 restates section n's idea line with synonyms before narrative
   advance; chiasmus variant allowed.
5. Pentachord ambitus (≤ 5 scale degrees); line-end cadence on reciting tonic.
6. Antiphonal offset = one line (Voice 1 = Voice 0 delayed).
7. Drone invariance (Voice 2 constant).
8. Seto drift option: tonic +1 semitone per strophe until kergütamine reset (Voice 3 register clamp).
9. No end-rhyme; Estonian regilaul variant: Voice 2 empty (unaccompanied).

## 8. Quirks & pitfalls

- Parallelism ≠ literal repetition — must restate with synonyms (Lönnrot's editorial overuse of
  parallelism is a documented distortion of the source tradition).
- The meter is **quantitative**, not stress-meter; English imitations drift to 7-syllable lines.
- 5/4 is a recitation pulse from the broken tetrameter, not a dance meter; forcing 4/4 erases the
  murrelmasäe character.
- The melody is a vehicle, not the work — reusing one group tune for many texts is correct.
- Seto drift must be reset via kergütamine or the killõ strand is stranded.
- Older Seto torrõ is deliberately not strict unison — grid-quantizing to exact unison loses the grain.

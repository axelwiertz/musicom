## 1. What it is

The **hardanger fiddle (hardingfele)** is the national instrument of Norway: a
violin-family fiddle with **8–9 strings — 4 bowed melody strings plus 4–5
sympathetic understrings** running through a hollow fingerboard under the
bridge. The earliest surviving instrument is by **Ole Jonsen Jaastad,
Hardanger, 1651**. Its craft signature is *composition by tuning*: a slått
(tune) is written **for a specific scordatura**, and the retuning changes the
entire musical world — fingerboard geography, available drones, and above all
**which sympathetic strings ring**.

Key facts anchoring this entry:

- **D transposing instrument** — written C sounds D; more than **20 recorded
  tunings**. Standard (highest/normal) tuning: **A–D–A–E** (written) with
  understrings tuned **B–D–E–F♯–A** to ring with that tuning.
- **Named tunings bind to tune families**: "troll tuning" **A–E–A–C♯** for the
  *fanitullen* devil's-tunes and the *Kivlemøyane* suite; **gorrlaus**
  (F-string tuning) in some regions; **E-scale tunings** (B–E–B–F♯,
  Annbjørg Lien); **F-scale** (C–F–C–G, Knut Buen). In Valdres, playing in
  troll tuning was called **"greylighting"** — the fiddler had played through
  his tunings until morning.
- **Flat bridge** (taller, wider, flatter than violin) → strings nearly level →
  the normal bow texture is **double-stops on two, sometimes three, top
  strings**: melody + drone (bordun) sounded together, always.
- **Bowing** is lighter and bouncier than violin; the dancer's loud
  **foot-stomping (trapping)** is the percussion instrument.
- Repertoire = **slått** (plural slåttar): named tunes transmitted orally,
  learned by ear from a master; each tune family "belongs" to a valley and
  often to a lineage. Tune types follow the dance: **springar** (3/4 couple
  dance with *unequal* beats — Telemark: longer–long–short; Valdres:
  short–longer–long), **gangar** (walking dance, 2/4 or 3/8; living only in
  Telemark and Setesdal), **halling** (fast solo acrobatic dance, 2/4 or 6/8,
  95–106 bpm, building to the *kast* — kicking a hat held ~230–280 cm up).
- Bygdedans form is a three-part sequence: **figuring part → lausdans (free
  dancing) → samdans (closed hold)**; the fiddler's variations track the
  dancers.
- **Grieg** transcribed hardanger tunes: *Slåtter* Op. 72 (1903, arranged by
  Johan Halvorsen for violin), and the opening of "Morning" (*Peer Gynt*) is
  derived from the sympathetic-string tuning **A–F♯–E–D–E–F♯**.
- The church banned it for centuries ("devil's instrument" folklore — fiddlers
  taught by the nix/devil, esp. *fanitullen* tunes); first church performances
  by Johannes Dahle (1920s). UNESCO-listed tradition; Liza Lim (*Philtre*,
  1997) and Nils Økland/Erlend Apneseth carry it into contemporary music.

## 3. Craft procedure (how a human does it, step by step)

1. **Choose the scordatura for the tune** — the piece and its tuning are one
   decision: standard A–D–A–E for most; low bass (G–D–A–E) for flatfele-style
   pieces; gorrlaus for certain regional repertoires; troll tuning
   (A–E–A–C♯) for *fanitullen* / *Kivlemøyane* material; raised E- or
   F-scale tunings for brighter, higher-set tunes. **The understrings are
   retuned to match** (e.g. B–D–E–F♯–A under A–D–A–E) so the resonance box is
   built for exactly this tune.
2. **Re-learn the fingerboard** — scordatura means every position moves; the
   fiddler re-maps hand shapes per tuning. This is why one tuning = one
   repertoire family, and why switching tunings mid-session ("greylighting")
   marks the end of a playing night.
3. **Learn the slått orally** — rote from a master fiddler: phrase by phrase,
   sung/vocalized first, then bowed; notation (when used at all) is a memory
   aid, never the source. Tunes carry names, owners, and stories
   (*Fanitullen* = learned from the devil under the table at the inn).
4. **Assemble the tune from gang/motif stock** — a slått is built from a
   shared vocabulary of short stereotypic melodic formulas and turns
   (regional "dialect"), combined and sequenced rather than invented from
   nothing. Composition = selection + order + ornament.
5. **Set the dance meter** — pick the pulse pattern the dance demands:
   - **Springar**: 3/4 with *unequal beats* — Telemark longer–long–short,
     Valdres short–longer–long; beat 1 carries the dancers' weight.
   - **Gangar**: 2/4 or 3/8, stately walking pulse.
   - **Halling**: fast 2/4 or 6/8 (95–106 bpm), sharp driving pulse for
     acrobatic solo dancing (nakkespretten, kast).
6. **Bow the double-stop composite** — flat bridge, two (sometimes three)
   strings at once: melody line on top, **bordun drone** (open string, often
   tonic or fifth) underneath. The drone is not accompaniment; it is half of
   the melody's body. Bounce the bow for triple-stroke figures; light touch,
   baroque-weight bow.
7. **Let the sympathetic strings ring** — the untouched understrings resonate
   with every bowed note; the fiddler *chooses fingerings and voicings so the
   resonance blooms* (this is audible tuning design: Grieg lifted the
   A–F♯–E–D–E–F♯ understring scale directly into "Morning"). Resonance is a
   compositional parameter, not a side effect.
8. **Lay the foot-stomp grid** — for dance slått the fiddler or dancers stamp
   the weight beat (beat 1 of springar), giving the tune its percussive
   floor.
9. **Vary strophe-by-strophe across the dance phases** — first pass plain,
   then increasingly ornamented and rhythmically driven; variation intensifies
   with the dance: figuring part (steady) → **lausdans** (freer, wider) →
   **samdans** (drive to cadence). In halling, variations build toward the
   **kast** moment — the music peaks exactly when the hat is kicked.
10. **Close with a vurving/turn** — a cadential twist figure that both
    finishes the dance phase and re-launches the cycle if the dance
    continues; a wedding slått (**bruremarsj**) instead walks the procession
    to the church door and stops there.

## 4. Practitioner examples

- **Torkjell Haugerud** (Telemark, 1876–1954) — canonical springar/gangar
  source recordings; benchmark of the Bø style.
- **Knut Buen** — recorded whole albums in raised F-scale tuning (C–F–C–G);
  master of regional slått "dialects".
- **Hauk Buen** — Telemark springar tradition bearer.
- **Leif Rygg** — Voss hardanger lineage.
- **Johannes Dahle** (Tinn) — first to play hardanger fiddle inside a church
  (1920s).
- **Knut Hamre & Benedicte Maurseth** — *Rosa i Botnen*: oldest playable
  hardanger fiddles with Norway's oldest playable church organ.
- **Edvard Grieg** — *Slåtter* Op. 72 (17 hardanger peasant dances notated via
  Johan Halvorsen); "Morning" (*Peer Gynt*) built from the understring tuning
  A–F♯–E–D–E–F♯; *Fanitullen*-style material in theatrical settings.
- **Johan Halvorsen** — transcriber/arranger of Op. 72 slåttar.
- **Arne Bjørndal** & **Eivind Groven** — collectors/systematizers; Groven
  built his just-intonation organ (renstemt) to render the microtonal reality
  of slått playing.
- **Annbjørg Lien** — E-scale tunings, cross-over recordings (with organist
  Iver Kleive).
- **Nils Økland, Erlend Apneseth** — contemporary/experimental hardanger
  voices; **Liza Lim** — *Philtre* (1997) solo hardanger commission.
- Canonical tunes: *Fanitullen* (devil's tune, troll tuning), *Kivlemøyane*
  suite, *Fykerudens gångar/springar*, *Bruremarsj* repertoire, Valdres
  hallingar.

## 5. UnitMatrix mapping (Musicom engine)

### Voices & Sections

| Voice | Role | Content | Invariance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voice 0 | **melody double-stop lead** | top-string melody built from gang/motif stock; ornaments accrue per pass | gang formula identity, fixed per slått |
| Voice 1 | **bordun/drone partner** | lower double-stop voice (open-string tonic/5th pedal under the melody) | drone-locked to the tuning's open strings |
| Voice 2 | **sympathetic understrings** | sustained ghost layer ⊂ understring pitch set of the chosen scordatura; rings with, never doubles, the melody | subset of tuning set, low velocity |
| Voice 3 | **foot-stomp (trapping)** | dance weight on beat 1 (springar), or drive pulse in halling | continuous, invariant |

**Sections** = dance phases, not abstract blocks: **Figuring → Lausdans →
Samdans** per dance cycle (springar/gangar), or **Halling build → Kast
climax → Close** for the solo dance; bridal context = **Bruremarsj
processional → stop at church door**. Ornaments/variation index rises with
each section (plain → ornamented → drive).

### Rules to encode

- **Scordatura-per-tune lock** — one tuning per piece; melody + drone +
  sympathetic layer all derive from that tuning's pitch set (understrings ⊂
  tuned resonance set; e.g. A–D–A–E ↔ B–D–E–F♯–A).
- **Unequal-beat grid** — springar 3/4 realized with explicit beat
  tick-lengths (Telemark longer–long–short; Valdres short–longer–long);
  never uniform 3/4 quantization.
- **Double-stop composite bias** — Voices 0+1 sound together by default;
  a monophonic render is a degraded case, not the target.
- **Sympathetic-set constraint** — Voice 2 pitches must lie in the
  understring set of the active tuning; onsets ride melody events, velocity
  low (ring, not line).
- **Motif-stock composition** — melodic material assembled from a finite
  formula vocabulary + sequence/ornament operators; no freely invented
  material (variation-not-invention).
- **Dance-phase form** — section order and variation intensity track
  figuring → lausdans → samdans; halling peaks at kast.
- **Foot-stomp invariance** — weight beat never disappears while dancing.
- **Cadential vurving** — section closes with the turn figure; can re-launch
  (dance continues) or stop (processional ends at the door).
- **Range/tessitura constraint** — 4 bowed strings, melody concentrated on
  the top two; ~1.5-octave melodic range typical.

### Element mapping

**PITCH** (scordatura pitch set + drone/sympathetic derivation, primary),
**RHYTHM** (unequal dance-beat grids + halling drive + stomp weight,
primary),
**TEXTURE** (double-stop composite + sympathetic ring, primary),
**STRUCTURE** (dance-phase form + variation passes, secondary).
**HARMONY** is emergent only — drone + melody imply I/5 open sonorities;
there is no functional progression.

## 6. Quirks / pitfalls

- **Uniform 3/4 kills the springar** — the genre's core information is beat
  asymmetry; encode beat lengths as explicit tick spans, not a global tempo.
- **Tuning is the piece, not a knob** — rendering a slått in the wrong
  scordatura changes fingerings, drones AND the sympathetic bloom; treat
  tuning as an immutable section/piece parameter (Valdres "greylighting"
  shows how strongly tunings structure a whole playing night).
- **Transposing instrument** — hardanger written C sounds D; store absolute
  concert ticks/pitches in the engine, keep the scordatura mapping as a
  named preset, never hand-shift by ear.
- **Sympathetic layer is a ghost, not a voice** — if Voice 2 reads as a
  played line, the render is wrong: low velocity, sustained, subset of
  understrings only.
- **Double-stops are the default** — single-line-only output loses the
  bordun, which is half the instrument's identity (flat bridge exists for
  this).
- **Motif stock ≠ laziness** — slått composition is selection and order from
  a shared formula vocabulary; "inventing" novel themes per generation is
  off-craft. Constrain the pitch/rhythm generator to the stock.
- **Dance function gates form** — halling peaks at the kast, springar
  tracks lausdans/samdans, bruremarsj stops at the church door; use the
  dance's social script, not abstract ABA.

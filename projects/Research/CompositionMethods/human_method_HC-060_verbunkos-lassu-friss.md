## 1. What it is

Verbunkos (from German *Werbung*, "recruiting") is the Hungarian dance-music genre that accompanied Habsburg army recruitment ceremonies between the 1720s and the abolition of conscription-avoidance recruiting in 1849. A dozen hussars danced in ranks while a Romani band played: the sergeant opened with slow, dignified movements, the lower officers joined with livelier steps, and the youngest soldiers finished with jumps and **spur-clicking** (*bokázó*). The music mirrored the choreography exactly — starting slow and rhetorical, then accelerating section by section to virtuosic brilliance. By c. 1784–1810 the *published* verbunkos literature (Bihari, Lavotta, Csermák, Kossovits) had turned this ritual into a fixed composition craft: a two-part (or extended alternating) slow–fast form built on a characteristic scale and a family of signature rhythms. It became Hungary's national style of the 19th century and the basis of the *style hongrois* used by Haydn, Schubert, Berlioz, Brahms, Liszt, Erkel, and later Kodály and Bartók.

The human craft is: **compose a melody in the verbunkos scale, hang it on a slow dotted-rhythm dance (lassú) and a fast virtuoso dance (friss), underpin it with the cigányzenekar accompaniment lock (bőgő dűvő + kontra strokes + cimbalom), and seal every section with the bokázó cadence.** It is an *architecture of tempo and ornament density*, not of motivic transformation.

## 2. The craft procedure (how a human does it)

1. **Fix the meter** — strictly duple: 2/4 or 4/4. No asymmetric meters (contrast HC-019). The military march background governs the step.
2. **Choose the scale** — the "Hungarian minor" / "Gypsy scale": 1–2–♭3–♯4–5–♭6–7 (harmonic minor with raised 4th), whose two **augmented seconds** (♭3–♯4, ♭6–7) are the instant identity markers. Practitioners also use verbunkos-dorian, verbunkos-phrygian, verbunkos-lydian and major-with-augmented-second variants (Loya's taxonomy), but the raised 4th is the fingerprint. Plan a **parallel-major resolution** for the final friss.
3. **Compose the hallgató** (optional but traditional) — a wordless slow introduction ("melody without words"), free and rhapsodic, rubato/parlando, heavily ornamented, no dance pulse. It is the prímás's showpiece, a cadenza-like prelude.
4. **Compose the lassú** — a majestic, lyric slow dance in dotted rhythm: dotted-quarter + eighth (♩.♪) predominates; the genre-fingerprint cell is the "verbunkos rhythm" = **dotted-eighth + sixteenth** (♪.♫). Wide-arched melody phrase (a bar or more of arch), single climax, ending on a half or full cadence stamped with the **bokázó figure** — a cadential turn derived from heel-clicking, of the *cambiata* type (leap up then step down to the cadence note), usually over a V–I with sharp dotted rhythm.
5. **Lay the accompaniment lock** — the cigányzenekar (3–6 players) pattern:
   - **Bőgő** (double bass): dűvő strokes — root/fifth on the beat, an invariant on-the-beat pulse that never rests.
   - **Kontra** (3-string viola or second violin): chordal strokes on off-beats and in the gaps, marking harmony rhythmically ("dűvő" = striking).
   - **Cimbalom**: percussive chordal punctuation; tremolo resonance at cadences and holds.
   - The prímás (lead violin) improvises ornaments over the composed skeleton: trills, double-stops, pizzicato, rapid runs.
6. **Compose the first friss** — a fast, *giusto* (strict-time) dance: triplet girdles, 16th-note running passages, sharply accented syncopations. Where the lassú was parlando-flexible, the friss is metronomic and propulsive.
7. **Add further friss sections — each faster** — the recruiting ceremony's escalation. Multiple fast sections, each quicker than the last (accelerando at transitions), with increasing virtuosity: the *cifra* (ornamented) figures, high-register runs, double stops. This monotonic tempo/density ramp is the structural core of the genre.
8. **Resolve to the parallel major for the final friska** — the Romantic convention (Liszt's rhapsodies, Kodály): the climax moves to the tonic major with cimbalom-like figuration, "stable major-mode resolutions".
9. **Close with the bokázó cadence** — rapid closing figure + the heel-click cadential formula (V–I with cambiata turn), often on a held pedal/trill first.
10. **Optional extension**: alternate lassú/friss pairs at greater length (a slow pair, a fast pair, another slow, faster fast...) — the direct ancestor of the csárdás's lassú→friska and of Kodály's *Dances of Galanta* (lassú introduction → four swift dances).

The composition is mostly **choreographed social ritual translated into music**: rank order of dancers = section order; the youngest soldiers' spur-clicks = the bokázó cadence; the recruiting bribe of food/wine/spectacle = the accelerando.

## 3. Real practitioner examples

- **János Bihari (1764–1827)** — "father of verbunkos", Romani violinist and bandleader; ~84 surviving compositions; formed his Pest band c. 1801, played through the Congress of Vienna (1814); credited (probably wrongly but tellingly) with the *Rákóczi March*; Liszt heard him and never forgot the style.
- **János Lavotta (1764–1820)** & **Antal Csermák (1774–1822)** — the other two of the "national trio"; Lavotta wrote the first verbunkos *programme music* (*Nota insurrectionalis hungarica*, 1797); Csermák pioneered the Hungarian chamber dance set.
- **József Kossovits (d. c. 1819)** — 12 *Danses Hongroises* (c. 1800), rhythmic/structural standardization. **Ignác Ruzitska** — *Magyar nóták Veszprém Vármegyéből* (1823–32), the first great printed collection. **Márk Rózsavölgyi (1789–1848)** — late-verbunkos master who led the transition into the csárdás; member of the National Theatre.
- **Ferenc Erkel (1810–1893)** — Hungarian national opera (*Hunyadi László* 1844, *Bánk bán* 1861) built on verbunkos idiom.
- **Style hongrois abroad**: Haydn *Piano Trio Hob. XV:25 "Gypsy Rondo"* (1795); Schubert *Divertissement à la hongroise* D. 818 (1824); Beethoven (Rákóczi-inflected episodes, *König Stephan*); Brahms *Hungarian Dances* (1869 ff., based on Bihari/Lavotta/Csermák/Ruzitska material); **Liszt** *Hungarian Rhapsody No. 2* — the canonical lassú→friska (unmetered cadenza opening, dotted lassú theme, friska accelerando to major climax).
- **20th century**: **Kodály** *Dances of Galanta* (1933 — childhood memory of the Galánta Gypsy band, lassú introduction + four accelerating dances); **Bartók** *Contrasts* (1938) mvmt. I titled "Verbunkos", Violin Concerto No. 2; the **táncház** (dance-house) revival (1970s–present, e.g. Muzsikás) continues the performance craft; the Czech **Slovácko verbuňk** (recruit dances) is UNESCO-listed (2008) with its three-part song–slow–fast structure.

## 4. UnitMatrix integration (Musicom engine)

### Voices

| Voice | Role | Content |
|---|---|---|
| V0 | **Bőgő bass spine** | Dűvő strokes: root/5th on the beat, invariant; never rests (like the oompah of HC-051, the surdo of HC-053, the bőgő of the recruiting band) |
| V1 | **Prímás melody lead** | The composed skeleton: hallgató, lassú, all friss themes — the piece's identity |
| V2 | **Kontra harmonic layer** | 3-voice chordal strokes on off-beats / gaps, harmony rhythm, voice-leading fills |
| V3 | **Cimbalom layer** | Percussive chordal punctuation + tremolo at cadences and holds |
| V4 | **Ornament/cifra layer** | Prímás surface: trills, double-stops, pizzicato, 16th/triplet runs; sparse in lassú, dense in friss; no new pitch classes in lassú |
| V5 | **Clarinet/tárogató** (optional, urban bands) | Counter-melody / doubling, fills |

### Sections

`S0 Hallgató` (free rubato, no pulse) → `S1 Lassú I` (dotted, minor) → `S2 Lassú II` (contrasting strain, same tempo family) → `S3 Friss I` (giusto, triplet/16th runs) → `S4 Friss II` (faster, cifra virtuosity) → `S5 Friska climax` (max tempo, parallel-major resolution, cimbalom figuration) → `S6 Closing tag` (held trill/pedal + bokázó V–I).

### Rules (what would encode it)

1. **Verbunkos-scale lock** — pitch set = 1–2–♭3–♯4–5–♭6–7; ♯4 mandatory as identity marker; two augmented seconds as contour-grammar fences.
2. **Lassú rhythm profile** — dotted-note dominance: ♩.♪ and the fingerprint ♪.♫ cell; no even-note monotony.
3. **Friss rhythm profile** — giusto; triplet girdles + 16th runs; strict accents; syncopation on off-beats.
4. **Tempo map** — monotonic ramp: lassú ≈ 50–70 bpm, friss ≈ 120–160+ bpm; each successive friss section strictly faster (accelerando at section transitions).
5. **Bőgő dűvő invariance** — V0 fires on the beat in every bar, never rests (except the hallgató's free opening, where V0 is silent).
6. **Kontra interlock** — V2 strokes never coincide with V0 onsets (off-beat slot), mirroring the hand-partition rule of HC-053.
7. **Bokázó cadence formula** — every section ends with the cambiata-type cadential figure (leap-up → step-down to cadence tone) over V–I, in dotted rhythm.
8. **Ornament-skeleton separation** — V1's composed pitches are invariant; V4's ornaments are surface only (no new pitch classes in lassú; free figuration in friss).
9. **Major-resolution gate** — final friss resolves to the parallel major (Liszt/Kodály convention), with cimbalom-like resonant figuration in V3.
10. **Hallgató gate** — S0 is exempt from tempo map and V0 pulse; free rhythm, ornament-dense; models the prímás's rubato prelude.
11. **Zero-drift lengths** — all voiced sections equal bar counts per the UnitMatrix invariant.

## 5. Quirks & pitfalls

- **Not klezmer**: both are East-Central European violin-band musics, but klezmer (HC-037) uses shteyger modes with *neutral* seconds, a wedding script of *distinct dance types*, and no internal slow→fast ramp; verbunkos is one piece with a tempo-arc and an augmented-2nd scale. Keep separate.
- **The augmented second is the whole weight of the style**: encoding verbunkos with plain harmonic minor + occasional ♯4 loses the identity; the ♯4 must be a *structural* scale degree (Loya: it forms its own modal families — verbunkos minor/dorian/phrygian/lydian).
- **"Gypsy" is a style label, not an ethnicity claim**: the melodies are Hungarian; Romani musicians were the carriers/performers. Reports should not conflate performer ethnicity with musical origin (Bellman's caution).
- **Bihari ≠ composer-in-the-Viennese-sense**: self-taught, repertoire largely improvised and orally transmitted; the 84 surviving pieces were written down by others. Any "Bihari composition" is at least partly a transcription. The Rákóczi March attribution is legendary, not factual.
- **Hallgató vs lassú blur**: early sources use hallgató for any slow verbunkos melody; treat "free prelude" and "slow dance" as separate stages (S0 vs S1) in the model.
- **Friss sections are not variations**: unlike HC-026 (pibroch) or HC-039 (Irish setting), each friss presents *new* figuration and often new material — density and tempo carry the arc, not theme-transformation.

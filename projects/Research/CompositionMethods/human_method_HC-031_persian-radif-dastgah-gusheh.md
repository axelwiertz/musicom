## 1. Method Name

Persian Radif — Dastgah–Gusheh Ordering & Improvisation. The craft of
navigating the **radif** (ردیف, "row/order"), the orally transmitted canon of
several hundred short melodic figures (**gusheh**, گوشه "corner/melody") that a
master strings together, in a fixed **order** within a modal system
(**dastgāh**, دستگاه "sonic space"), to compose a live performance that is
simultaneously a recitation of a fixed canon and a fresh improvisation.

## 2. Tradition / Culture

Persian classical music — Iran, the **dastgah** system. The radif crystallized
in the 19th century (attributed to Ali Akbar Farahani at the Qajar court) and
was codified by his sons **Mirza Abdollah** and **Agha Hosseinqoli** (tar
players, Qajar period). It descends from an older **maqam** pedagogy and is
carried forward purely by oral transmission. UNESCO Intangible Cultural
Heritage (2009, inscribed on the Representative List).

The radif is **not a set of finished pieces** — it is a *taxonomy of melodic
atoms plus their canonical ordering*. Mastery = internalizing the whole canon
so deeply that any gusheh can be recalled at any moment, and then re-assembling
it into a unique performance ("each nightingale's song is unique" — Nour-Ali
Boroumand's metaphor).

## 4. Craft Procedure (how the human does it, step by step)

1. **Internalize the radif by rote.** Years of repetition under a master; the
   master may appoint a **khalifeh** (successor/assistant) who drills the other
   students. No notation in the tradition (notation came later — Jean During,
   Pirniakan, Ma'rufi). The canon lives in memory.
2. **Choose a dastgah.** Seven principal dastgahs (Shur, Mahur, Homayun,
   Segah, Chahargah, Nava, Rast-Panjgah) plus derivative **avaz** modes. A
   dastgah is a *sonic space* defined by interval relations + the characteristic
   *movement* of its melodies — not just a scale. Shur is the mother-mode;
   Homayun always precedes its child Bayat-e Esfahan (class-system hierarchy).
3. **Open with the daramad.** The introductory gusheh, lowest in register,
   establishes the **shahed** (شاهد, the gusheh's central/reciting note — the
   note "upon which the gusheh is based"). The daramad's shahed is the
   fundamental that anchors the whole dastgah.
4. **Walk the gusheh sequence.** Each dastgah holds 10–30 (up to 50+) gushehs,
   performed in a **canonical order** fixed by the masters' judgment. Each
   gusheh has its own shahed, register, and melodic identity. Longer gushehs
   split into **ghesmat** (sub-sections) taught separately but performed
   together; some exist in multiple **no'** (types) giving the performer
   freedom.
5. **Modulate (pardeh-gardani).** Certain gushehs have *modulatory potential*:
   Delkash (Mahur: 6th & 7th lowered), Hesar (Chahargah: 4th lowered, 5th
   raised), Mouyeh (4th lowered). The **shahed moves** when you pass through
   them, and "this movement creates a new sonic space." Modulatory gushehs sit
   in the **middle** of the dastgah, never the beginning or end.
6. **Climb to the awj (peak)** — the highest-register, most intense gusheh
   cluster — then descend.
7. **Return home via the forud.** The cadential gusheh that descends back to
   the daramad's shahed, closing the tonal journey. Almost every dastgah has a
   distinct forud.
8. **Improvise within the frame.** The radif is the *material and the route*,
   not the destination — each performance re-assembles gushehs with fresh
   ornamentation (tahrir vocal melisma, riz/abshar pick ornaments), microtonal
   inflection, and personal shape. Individualism is valued: imitation is *not*
   admired; being different is.
9. **Add rhythmic contrast sparingly.** Most gushehs are free/meterless
   (rhythm follows Persian poetry meter). Occasional **zarbi** (metric)
   gushehs — reng (dance), chaharmezrab (fast plucked showpiece), kereshmeh,
   naghmeh — are inserted after non-metric gushehs, borrowing their melodic
   character into a fixed pulse.

## 5. Practitioner Examples

- **Mirza Abdollah** (tar) — the most authoritative radif; transmitted to many
  students; keeps pre-Qajar legacy alive; notated by Jean During (1970s, from
  Nour-Ali Boroumand's by-heart recording).
- **Agha Hosseinqoli (Mirza Hossein-Qoli)** (tar) — brother's parallel radif;
  first to record the radif on gramophone (1905 Tehran, 1907 Paris, 1912);
  notated by Dariush Pirniakan (2001).
- **Ali-Akbar Shahnazi** (tar) — son of Agha Hosseinqoli; first to record the
  full radif by heart; his audio is still a reference for tar masters.
- **Nour-Ali Boroumand** — studied with Darvish Khan, Musa Ma'rufi, Esma'il
  Qahremani; the canonical by-heart source behind During's Mirza Abdollah
  notation.
- **Abdollah Davami** — brought the radif into vocal performance; the vocal
  radif reference.
- **Majid Kiani, Dariush Talai, Mohammad-Reza Lotfi, Hossein Alizadeh** —
  modern interpreters/transmitters.
- **Kayhan Kalhor** — contemporary kamancheh master extending radif-based
  improvisation into global collaboration.
- **Bruno Nettl** — the definitive Western scholarship (*The Radif of Persian
  Music*, 1987; identified 152 gushehs in Maroufi's radif, 470 gushehs in the
  1963 Ma'rufi canon).

## 6. UnitMatrix Mapping (Voices & Sections)

### Voices
- **Voice 0 = drone + shahed spine** — tanbur/tar low string (and vocal
  sustaining) holds the current shahed; a moving invariant that *shifts its
  center* only at modulation points. The tonal-anchor reference.
- **Voice 1 = lead (tar/setar/santur/kamancheh/vocal)** — the gusheh sequence:
  daramad → gushehs → modulatory gusheh → awj → forud; ornamented, microtonal,
  free-rhythm dominant.
- **Voice 2 = heterophonic echo / ornament layer** — idiomatic pick/vocal
  ornament (riz, tahrir) shadowing Voice 1; no new pitch classes, only
  decoration.
- **Voice 3 = percussion (tombak/daf), zarbi-only** — silent during free
  gushehs; enters only for reng/chaharmezrab/kereshmeh metric gushehs.

### Sections
Each **gusheh = one section**, in canonical order:
**Daramad → Gusheh₂ → … → Modulatory gusheh (pardeh-gardani) → Awj (peak) →
Forud (return to daramad shahed)**. Metric zarbi gushehs punctuate the
free-rhythm flow as contrast sections. The section sequence *is* the
composition.

### Rules (what would encode it)
- **Shahed tracking** — a scalar "current central note" that only changes when
  a modulatory gusheh is entered; all melodic events gravitate to it.
- **Gusheh-order constraint** — sections drawn from an ordered gusheh list;
  daramad first, forud last; modulatory gushehs only mid-sequence.
- **Daramad anchoring** — opens lowest register, states the dastgah's shahed.
- **Forud return** — final section descends to the daramad shahed = closure.
- **Pardeh-gardani (modulation)** — only a whitelisted subset of gushehs
  (Delkash/Hesar/Mouyeh type) may shift the shahed; shift = new sonic space.
- **Microtonal interval set** — quarter-tone (koron/sori) inflections within
  the dastgah's interval skeleton; not 12-TET.
- **Free-rhythm dominance** — most sections unmetered (events snap to melodic
  contour + poetry meter, not a grid); zarbi sections carry a fixed pulse.
- **Individualism / variation-not-invention** — same gusheh material, fresh
  ornamentation each pass; no two realizations identical.

## 7. Quirks / Pitfalls (engine-fit notes)

1. **Shahed is not a tonic.** The "center" moves *within* a dastgah and across
   dastgahs; a fixed key/tonic model will fail. Encode shahed as a mutable
   per-section scalar that the melodic generator gravitates to.
2. **Free rhythm vs the zero-drift grid.** Most gushehs are unmetered;
   UnitMatrix's absolute-tick cells must host non-gridded events — quantize
   onset *loosely* (or treat each gusheh as a section of "free" events padded
   to section length) to keep track-length symmetry.
3. **Microtonality.** Koron (lowered) / sori (raised) quarter-tones need
   pitch-bend or a >12 pitch-class grid; naive 12-TET rounding erases the
   dastgah's identity.
4. **Ordering *is* the method.** A radif is defined by gusheh *sequence*, not
   by a set of gushehs; a bag-of-gushehs generator is not the method. The
   canonical order (per dastgah) is a hard constraint.
5. **Modulation is whitelist-gated.** Only specific gushehs may move the
   shahed; a free "modulate anywhere" rule produces non-Persian wandering.
   Gate pardeh-gardani through the modulatory-gusheh set.
6. **Rhythm contrast is sparse.** Zarbi gushehs are the exception; the
   percussion voice is tacet for most sections — do not default a continuous
   groove.

## 1. Executive Summary & One-Line Description

**TR-808 Analog Cymbal Physical-Circuit Synthesis (TACS)** synthesizes the iconic Roland TR-808 crash and ride cymbal voices directly from the 1980 analog hardware circuit schematic: an astable multivibrator bank of six CMOS Schmitt-trigger square-wave oscillators summed into twin high-order active bandpass filters (~3.4 kHz and ~7.1 kHz), gated by three independent swing-type non-linear transistor VCAs driven by multi-stage RC envelope generators, shaped by Sallen-Key highpass filters and an interconnected passive tone stage, and equalized by an active differentiator buffer (+6 dB/octave). It delivers an authentic, responsive metallic sheen with continuous decay/tone control and zero sampled waveforms.

## 3. Historical & Theoretical Provenance

The **Roland TR-808 Rhythm Composer** (released in 1980, designed under Tadao Kikumoto and Ikutaro Kakehashi) remains one of the most influential instruments in modern electronic, hip-hop, and dance music. Unlike later digital machines (such as the Linn LM-1 or Roland TR-909) which relied on PCM sample ROMs for cymbals, the TR-808 generated every drum voice through dedicated analog circuit topologies.

The cymbal voice circuit represented a remarkable design breakthrough:
1. **The Tea Spill Discovery:** Engineering lore (recounted by synth designer Don Lewis) states that while experimenting with prototype breadboards at Roland's Tokyo lab, chief engineer Tadao Kikumoto accidentally spilled tea across the board. The resulting burst of noisy, sizzling oscillations inspired the engineering team to reproduce that exact character through CMOS Schmitt triggers and active filtering.
2. **Academic Circuit Deconstruction:** In 2014, researchers Kurt James Werner, Jonathan S. Abel, and Julius O. Smith at CCRMA (Stanford University) published the definitive circuit analysis: *"The TR-808 Cymbal: a Physically-Informed, Circuit-Bendable, Digital Model"* (Proc. ICMC|SMC|2014). Their work proved that the cymbal voice's metallic texture is mathematically generated by non-linear intermodulation of six square waves rather than stochastic white noise.
3. **Roland Service Manual Specifications:** The official *Roland TR-808 Service Notes* (June 1981, 1st ed.) document component-level schematics and trimming procedures, establishing exact component values for the oscillator bank, bandpass filters, and swing-type VCAs.

## 5. Mathematical Mechanics & State Equations

### 5.1 Schmitt-Trigger Astable Multivibrators
A single CMOS HD14584 hex Schmitt-trigger inverter IC implements six astable multivibrators. Each inverter exhibits positive-going threshold $V_{T^+}$ and negative-going threshold $V_{T^-}$ (nominally $V_{T^+} \approx 2.9\text{ V}$, $V_{T^-} \approx 1.9\text{ V}$ at $V_{DD} = 5.0\text{ V}$).

Charging and discharging intervals for the $k$-th oscillator governed by $R_k C_k$:
$$t_{\mathrm{charge}, k} = R_k C_k \ln\left(\frac{V_{OH} - V_{T^-}}{V_{OH} - V_{T^+}}\right)$$
$$t_{\mathrm{discharge}, k} = R_k C_k \ln\left(\frac{V_{OL} - V_{T^+}}{V_{OL} - V_{T^-}}\right)$$

Total period $T_k = t_{\mathrm{charge}, k} + t_{\mathrm{discharge}, k}$; frequency $f_k = 1 / T_k$.
Duty cycle $D_k$:
$$D_k = \frac{t_{\mathrm{charge}, k}}{T_k} = \frac{\ln\left(\frac{V_{OH} - V_{T^-}}{V_{OH} - V_{T^+}}\right)}{\ln\left(\frac{(V_{OH} - V_{T^-})(V_{OL} - V_{T^+})}{(V_{OH} - V_{T^+})(V_{OL} - V_{T^-})}\right)} \approx 47.98\%$$

The six oscillators produce square waves at nominal frequencies:
- $f_1 = 205.3\text{ Hz}$ ($R_{36}=180\text{ k}\Omega, C_{21}=15\text{ nF}$)
- $f_2 = 369.6\text{ Hz}$ ($R_{38}=100\text{ k}\Omega, C_{22}=15\text{ nF}$)
- $f_3 = 304.4\text{ Hz}$ ($R_{45}=120\text{ k}\Omega, C_{23}=15\text{ nF}$)
- $f_4 = 522.7\text{ Hz}$ ($R_{47}=68\text{ k}\Omega, C_{24}=15\text{ nF}$)
- $f_5 \in [359.4, 1149.9]\text{ Hz}$ (trimpot $TM_2$, factory tuned to $800\text{ Hz}$)
- $f_6 \in [254.3, 627.2]\text{ Hz}$ (trimpot $TM_1$, factory tuned to $540\text{ Hz}$)

Summed output across passive resistive mixing network:
$$V_{\mathrm{sum}}(t) = \sum_{k=1}^6 w_k \cdot \mathrm{sqr}(2\pi f_k t + \phi_k, D_k)$$

### 5.2 Active Bandpass Filtering
The active bandpass filters utilize bridged-T feedback topologies around operational amplifiers. The continuous-time transfer function is:
$$H_{bp}(s) = \frac{\beta_2 s^2 + \beta_1 s}{\alpha_3 s^3 + \alpha_2 s^2 + \alpha_1 s + \alpha_0}$$

For Bandpass Filter #1:
$$\beta_2 = -R_{56}R_{57}(C_{13} + C_{14})C_{10}, \qquad \beta_1 = -R_{57}C_{10}$$
$$\alpha_3 = R_{56}R_{57}R_{52}C_{13}C_{14}C_{10}, \qquad \alpha_2 = R_{56}R_{57}C_{13}C_{14} + R_{56}R_{52}(C_{13} + C_{14})C_{10}$$
$$\alpha_1 = R_{56}(C_{13} + C_{14}) + R_{52}C_{10}, \qquad \alpha_0 = 1$$

Center frequencies are tuned to $f_{c1} \approx 3440\text{ Hz}$ and $f_{c2} \approx 7100\text{ Hz}$. Discretization is carried out via bilinear transform with oversampling or center-frequency pre-warping:
$$\omega_a = \frac{2}{T_s} \tan\left(\frac{\omega_d T_s}{2}\right)$$

### 5.3 Trigger Logic & Attack Smoother
CPU trigger pulse $V_{\mathrm{trig}}$ is smoothed via a single-pole RC network ($Q_{19}, R_{84}\text{--}R_{86}, C_{35}$):
$$H_{as}(s) = \frac{V_{\mathrm{smooth}}}{V_{\mathrm{trig}} - V_{BE}} = \frac{1}{1 + \tau s}, \qquad \tau \approx 102.44\ \mu\text{s}, \quad V_{BE} \approx 0.7258\text{ V}$$

### 5.4 Envelope Generators
The smoothed trigger drives diode-switched RC networks. For the primary user-controlled decay stage (EG #1.1):
$$\frac{dV_B}{dt} = \begin{cases}
\frac{V_{\mathrm{smooth}} - V_{\mathrm{on}} - V_B}{R_{\mathrm{charge}} C_{41}}, & V_B < V_{\mathrm{smooth}} - V_{\mathrm{on}} \quad (\text{attack}, \sim 1\text{ ms}) \\
-\frac{V_B}{(R_{93} + k \cdot VR_2) C_{41}}, & V_B \ge V_{\mathrm{smooth}} - V_{\mathrm{on}} \quad (\text{release/decay})
\end{cases}$$
where $k \in [0.0, 1.0]$ is the decay knob setting and $V_{\mathrm{on}} \approx 0.5899\text{ V}$.

### 5.5 Swing-Type Voltage-Controlled Amplifiers (VCAs)
Unlike conventional linear VCAs, the 808 swing-type VCA replaces the fixed $V_{CC}$ rail with the instantaneous envelope voltage $V_{\mathrm{env}}(t)$. The output exhibits large-signal exponential clipping and sharp diode gating:
$$V_{\mathrm{est}} = V_{\mathrm{env}} - R_c I_{ES}\left(\exp\left(\frac{V_{BE}}{V_T}\right) - 1\right)$$
$$V_{\mathrm{VCA}} = \frac{V_{\mathrm{est}} - V_{\mathrm{env}}}{\left(1 + \left|\frac{V_{\mathrm{est}} - V_{\mathrm{env}}}{V_{\mathrm{env}} - V_{\mathrm{lowerEdge}}}\right|^\alpha\right)^{1/\alpha}} + V_{\mathrm{env}}$$
where $\alpha \approx 3.5$, $V_T \approx 25.85\text{ mV}$, and $V_{\mathrm{lowerEdge}}$ is modeled by:
$$V_{\mathrm{lowerEdge}} = \alpha_0 e^{-\beta_0 V_{\mathrm{env}}^{\gamma_0}} + \alpha_1 e^{-\beta_1 V_{\mathrm{env}}^{\gamma_1}} + \alpha_2$$
with fit coefficients $\alpha_0 = -1.396, \beta_0 = 1.063, \gamma_0 = 1.000, \alpha_1 = 0.825, \beta_1 = 0.837, \gamma_1 = 1.447, \alpha_2 = 0.566$.

### 5.6 Tone Network & Differentiator Output Stage
The signals from the three VCA bands pass through Sallen-Key highpass filters (5.5 kHz, 8.2 kHz, and a resonant 10.5 kHz stage). The passive Tone pot ($VR_5$) crossfades between the lower highpass bands and the upper resonant band. The summed signal is buffered by an op-amp with an inverting differentiator configuration ($+6\text{ dB/octave}$ slope):
$$H_{diff}(s) \approx K \cdot \frac{s}{1 + s / \omega_p}$$
which accurately mimics the acoustic radiation resistance of a vibrating bronze disc into 3D free space.

## 6. Musical Elements Framework

- **PITCH:** Inharmonic metallic cluster. Fundamental frequencies ($205.3, 304.4, 369.6, 522.7, 540, 800\text{ Hz}$) intentionally avoid harmonic ratios, creating an acoustic illusion of metallic chime and high-frequency dispersion. Internal trimpots $TM_1$ and $TM_2$ enable microtonal detuning.
- **RHYTHM:** Point-process impulsive trigger. Attacks are instantaneous ($<1\text{ ms}$), providing tight transient response for intricate 16th-note ride rhythms or crashing downbeat accents. Decay is variable from $150\text{ ms}$ (splash/hi-hat feel) to $2.5\text{ s}$ (open sustained crash).
- **HARMONY:** Broad-spectrum metallic tone without definite tonal pitch center. Blends neutrally over any harmonic context without clashing against chord progressions or modal centers.
- **STRUCTURE:** Macro-structural markers. Serves as structural punctuation marking section changes (downbeats of Intro, Verse, Chorus, Outro) or providing continuous groove momentum.
- **TEXTURE:** Sizzling, airy, non-masking high-frequency texture. Peak spectral energy is concentrated between $3.5\text{ kHz}$ and $12\text{ kHz}$, keeping low-mid register clear for basslines and chordal instruments.

## 8. Implementation Pitfalls & Engineering Directives

1. **Nyquist Warping in Discrete Filters:** With active bandpass and Sallen-Key highpass cutoffs located between 7.1 kHz and 10.5 kHz, direct bilinear transformation at standard sample rates (44.1 kHz) suffers from severe frequency compression near Nyquist. **Directive:** Render at $2\times$ or $4\times$ oversampling ($88.2\text{ kHz}$ or $176.4\text{ kHz}$) before decimation, or apply tangent pre-warping to each filter pole.
2. **VCA Idle Bleed:** If the swing-type VCA is implemented as a pure mathematical multiplier, residual low-level square wave harmonics will bleed into the output continuously. **Directive:** Enforce a hard threshold cutoff when envelope voltage drops below diode drop $V_{\mathrm{on}} \approx 0.59\text{ V}$.
3. **Square Wave Aliasing:** Raw digital step transitions in the square wave oscillators introduce high-frequency foldover. **Directive:** Oversampling combined with PolyBLEP edge smoothing prevents harsh digital aliasing while preserving the authentic analog bite.
4. **Free-Running Oscillator Phases:** In original hardware, the six oscillators run continuously and independently of note triggers. Digital triggers that reset oscillator phases to zero produce identical, sterile transient clicks. **Directive:** Randomize or track continuous oscillator phases across successive hits to produce natural, organic analog variance.

## 9. Python / NumPy Implementation Sketch

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy import signal

def tr808_cymbal_voice(
    decay=0.5,
    tone=0.5,
    tune_tm1=540.0,
    tune_tm2=800.0,
    velocity=100,
    duration_sec=1.5,
    sample_rate=44100
):
    """
    Renders a single Roland TR-808 cymbal hit (Method SP-089 TACS).
    
    Parameters:
        decay: float [0.0, 1.0] - EG#1 decay length (0.15s - 2.5s)
        tone: float [0.0, 1.0] - Tone balance between low/high bands
        tune_tm1: float - Oscillator 6 frequency (default 540 Hz)
        tune_tm2: float - Oscillator 5 frequency (default 800 Hz)
        velocity: int [1, 127] - Hit velocity / accent
        duration_sec: float - Buffer duration in seconds
        sample_rate: int - Output sample rate (Hz)
    
    Returns:
        np.ndarray: Mono audio float buffer normalized to [-1.0, 1.0]
    """
    # 2x oversampling to preserve high-frequency filter slopes
    os_rate = sample_rate * 2
    n_samples = int(duration_sec * os_rate)
    t = np.arange(n_samples) / os_rate
    
    # 1. Hex Schmitt-trigger oscillator bank
    freqs = [205.3, 369.6, 304.4, 522.7, tune_tm2, tune_tm1]
    weights = [0.18, 0.18, 0.16, 0.16, 0.16, 0.16]
    
    # Free-running randomized phases
    phases = np.random.uniform(0, 2 * np.pi, len(freqs))
    
    v_sum = np.zeros(n_samples)
    for f, w, phi in zip(freqs, weights, phases):
        # 47.98% duty cycle square wave per HD14584 specification
        phase_acc = (t * f + phi / (2 * np.pi)) % 1.0
        sqr = np.where(phase_acc < 0.4798, 1.0, -1.0)
        v_sum += w * sqr
        
    # 2. Dual Active Bandpass Filters
    # BPF 1: ~3440 Hz (Q ~ 3.5)
    sos_bp1 = signal.iirpeak(3440.0, 3.5, fs=os_rate, output='sos')
    bp1 = signal.sosfilt(sos_bp1, v_sum)
    
    # BPF 2: ~7100 Hz (Q ~ 4.0)
    sos_bp2 = signal.iirpeak(7100.0, 4.0, fs=os_rate, output='sos')
    bp2 = signal.sosfilt(sos_bp2, v_sum)
    
    # 3. Envelopes: Attack smoother + multi-stage RC decay
    tau_decay = 0.15 + decay * 2.10  # 150ms to 2.25s decay
    env_decay = np.exp(-t / tau_decay)
    env_attack = 1.0 - np.exp(-t / 0.0001)  # 100 microsecond attack smoother
    env_click = np.exp(-t / 0.012)          # 12ms punch transient
    
    env_vca1 = env_attack * (0.85 * env_decay + 0.15 * env_click)
    env_vca2 = env_attack * (0.65 * env_decay + 0.35 * env_click)
    
    # Hard diode threshold cutoff (zero idle bleed)
    env_vca1 = np.where(env_vca1 > 0.005, env_vca1, 0.0)
    env_vca2 = np.where(env_vca2 > 0.005, env_vca2, 0.0)
    
    # 4. Swing-type VCAs (diode clipping & non-linear saturation)
    vca1 = np.tanh(bp1 * env_vca1 * 3.0)
    vca2 = np.tanh(bp2 * env_vca2 * 3.0)
    
    # 5. Sallen-Key Highpass Filters
    sos_hp1 = signal.iirfilter(2, 5500.0, btype='highpass', ftype='butter', fs=os_rate, output='sos')
    sos_hp2 = signal.iirfilter(2, 8200.0, btype='highpass', ftype='butter', fs=os_rate, output='sos')
    hp1 = signal.sosfilt(sos_hp1, vca1)
    hp2 = signal.sosfilt(sos_hp2, vca2)
    
    # 6. Tone blending stage
    gain1 = 1.0 - 0.55 * tone
    gain2 = 0.35 + 0.85 * tone
    mixed = hp1 * gain1 + hp2 * gain2
    
    # 7. Rising +6 dB/octave differentiator output buffer
    sos_diff = signal.iirfilter(1, 3500.0, btype='highpass', ftype='butter', fs=os_rate, output='sos')
    out_diff = signal.sosfilt(sos_diff, mixed)
    
    # Decimate back to base sample rate
    audio_out = signal.decimate(out_diff, 2)
    
    # Velocity scaling and normalization
    gain_vel = (velocity / 127.0)
    peak = np.max(np.abs(audio_out)) + 1e-8
    return audio_out * (gain_vel / peak)
```

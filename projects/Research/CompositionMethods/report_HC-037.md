## 1. Method name & one-paragraph description

**Klezmer Ornament-Led Ensemble Craft.** The professional instrumental music of
Ashkenazi Jews (*k'lei zemer* = "instruments of melody"), played by guild
musicians (klezmorim) at weddings. Craft signature for the engine: **a tune is
not a fixed melody — it is a modal shell (shteyger) + a dance-meter container +
a governed ornament/variation practice**. Ornaments have names and rules
(krekhts, kneytsh, kvetsh, tshok, glitshn), imitate the human voice (hazzan
cantorial + Hasidic nigun aesthetics), and are applied with *taste/gust* —
variation and restraint, never mechanical. Form is driven by the wedding
function: rubato **doina** intros land directly into metric **dance tunes**
(freylekhs, bulgar, sher, khosidl, hora/zhok, terkisher); parting pieces
(dobriden, zay gezunt) close the set. Ensemble = lead (violin 19th c. /
clarinet American era) + seconding voice (heterophonic double-time
elaboration, off-beat bias) + bass (baran) + poyk (bass drum + cymbal).

## 4. Craft procedure (how a human actually does it)

1. **Pick the function and dance type first** — the wedding script decides
   form: circle dances (freylekhs/redl/hopke/dreydl 2/4; bulgar = Moldavian
   circle dance with syncopated 2/4 or 4/4, THE American dance type after
   Tarras), figure dance (sher "scissors", 4/4 march-like, four couples in
   square formation — number of musical sections must fit the called
   figures), dignified feature (khosidl 2/4 or 4/4), listening/display
   (terkisher 4/4 Ottoman-style, skotshne), non-metrical intro (doina; also
   taksim, volekhl, fantasia), processional (gas-nign — always 3/4 per
   Beregovski; tsum tish), parting (dobriden, zay gezunt, dobranotsh), ritual
   dances (kosher-tants/mitsve-tants 3/4 polonaise-like; broygez-tants
   anger/reconciliation pantomime).
2. **Choose the shteyger (mode)** — one mode per tune: **Freygish** (Phrygian
   dominant; ~25% of Beregovsky's corpus; = maqam Hijaz; "Hava Nagila",
   "Ma yofus"), **Mi Sheberakh** (Ukrainian Dorian, raised 4th; the doina
   mode; shares pitch classes with Freygish), **Mogen Ovos** (natural minor;
   greeting/parting pieces + dance tunes; ≈ Bayati), **Adonoy Molokh**
   ("Jewish major", flatted 7th ≈ Mixolydian), **Yishtabach** (Mogen Ovos
   variant flattening 2̂ and 5̂). Mode is organized as **pentachord regions,
   not octave scales**; **modal progression** between shared-pitch regions
   (e.g. Freygish upper pentachord → Mogen Ovos lower) is a compositional
   resource (Joshua Horowitz, "The Klezmer Ahava Rabboh Shteyger"). Beregovsky
   used the word **gust ("taste")** for mode — taste as analytic category.
3. **Learn the tune orally** — rote through klezmer dynasties/apprenticeship;
   tunes rarely attributed to a composer; each player owns his version;
   notation (when used) is a memory aid. Brandwein could not read sheet music.
4. **Assemble the skeleton from cadence + formula stock** — phrases built
   around **typical klezmer cadences**; cadence type identifies a tune as
   klezmer even when the broader structure is borrowed from non-Jewish
   sources. Freylekhs/sher skeletons = AABB section pairs.
5. **Plan the seconding** — 2nd violin and tsimbl play **double-time
   elaboration emphasizing off-beats**; clarinet at lower volume "sneaks in
   with ornamented secondings" of the violin lead. Roles swap when clarinet
   leads (American era). This is heterophony, not chordal comping.
6. **Lay the floor** — bass (baran) root-fifth on strong beats; **poyk**
   (bass drum with cymbal mounted, struck with beater/hand cymbal) marks
   dance weight; percussion minimal in early recordings (wood block/snare).
7. **Ornament by taste** — named vocal-imitative ornaments: **krekhts**
   ("groan/sob" — pitch scoops after landing on a note, especially before
   cadences), **kneytsh** ("wrinkle/fold" — bent/pinched note), **kvetsh**
   ("pressure/stress"), **tshok** (cackle-like bent note), **glitshn**
   (glissandos), plus trills, grace notes, appoggiaturas, mordents, slides,
   flageolets (string harmonics), pedal notes. Governance = **taste,
   self-expression, variation and restraint**: the same phrase is never
   ornamented identically twice; ornaments cluster at cadences/phrase ends.
8. **Doina intro when function calls for it** — free-rhythm, highly ornamented
   (melismatic) rubato improvisation "on a more or less fixed pattern (usually
   a descending one), stretching the notes in a rubato-like manner"; prolonged
   notes are the 4th/5th above the floor note; adopted from Romanian
   lăutari (Bartók linked doina family to Arabo-Persian makam; taksim→doina
   as intro-to-dance is recorded practice for both lăutari and klezmorim;
   klezmer doinas influenced by Hasidic nigunim). Accompaniment = sparse
   held chords/pedal so the soloist breathes. **The segue is the form**: the
   doina's final phrase lands directly into the dance tune's first bar.
9. **Replay with pass-variation** — AABB cycles; plain statement → ornamented
   → virtuosic double-time seconding; material varies, cycle structure never
   does (variation-not-invention, as in mbira/slått entries).
10. **Follow the room** — sher sections cut/extended to fit called figures;
    leader watches dancers; badkhn's kale-bazetsn gets free-form support over
    held chords; broygez-tants mirrors the pantomime (angry → reconciliation
    music).
11. **Close with a parting piece** — dobriden/zay gezunt (Mogen Ovos family)
    ends the set; gas-nign walks the procession (3/4).

## 5. Practitioner examples

- **Naftule Brandwein** (1884–1963) — Galicia→NY; Stretiner Hasidic dynasty;
  Victor sides 1923–27 of virtuosic doinas + dance pieces; krekhts-laden
  cantorial style; could not read music; career decline as big-band sound
  rose; 1941 self-label return.
- **Dave Tarras** (c. 1895–1989) — Podolia→NY 1921; ~500 recordings; read
  music, military-band precision; "smooth and dignified, deliberate and
  rhythmical phrasing"; **credited (Zev Feldman) with "Bessarabianizing"
  Jewish dance music and replacing the freylekh with the bulgar** as the
  dominant American dance form; *Tanz!* (1956, with Sam Musiker) fused jazz
  + klezmer; mentored Andy Statman; NEA National Heritage Fellowship 1984.
- **Pedotser** (Aron-Moyshe Kholodenko) & **Stempenyu** (Yosef Drucker) —
  19th-century klezmer violin virtuosos fusing classical technique
  (Khandoshkin) with Bessarabian folk violin; their pieces spread
  anonymously — repertoire without named composers.
- **Moisei Beregovsky** (1892–1961) — corpus systematizer; ~25% Freygish,
  ~⅕ major, majority minor-family; used **gust ("taste")** for mode; wrote
  "Yidishe klezmer, zeyer shafn un shteyger" (1941).
- **Joshua Horowitz** — "The Klezmer Ahava Rabboh Shteyger: Mode, Sub-mode,
  and Modal Progression" — pentachord-based modal system; basis for the
  engine's mode rules.
- **Michael Alpert** (Brave Old World) — sher figure-dance account
  ("similar to the American square dance... richest venues for social
  interaction", Kraków 1994, on Pessl's Sher).
- **Walter Zev Feldman** — scholarship + 1979 LP with Andy Statman that
  popularized "klezmer" as the genre name.
- Canonical repertoire: "Hava Nagila", "Ma yofus" (Freygish), Brandwein's
  doina-labelled Victor sides + "Odessa Bulgar"-type bulgars, Pessl's Sher.

doina's final phrase = dance's first phrase (pitch-locked handoff).

## 9. Quirks / pitfalls

- **Pentachords, not octave scales** — shteyger as fixed 8-note scale breaks
  modal progression; represent as stacked pentachord regions with a floor
  note (Horowitz).
- **Ornament without restraint = parody** — tradition's own category is
  *taste (gust)*; cap density, cluster at cadences, reserve krekhts for
  pre-cadential spots.
- **Krekhts is a bend, not a note** — implement as pitch-bend-like offset or
  grace-note pair; it imitates a sob, which lives *between* pitches.
- **Doina rubato ≠ free timing everywhere** — only the solo layer is rubato;
  encode as explicit non-uniform tick spans padded to section_len or
  zero-drift gate fails. Bass/drum drop to sparse pedal/tacet.
- **The segue is the form** — doina that doesn't land into the dance = taksim;
  encode handoff as a section-boundary rule.
- **Sher section count serves the dance figures** — cut/extend to fit; a
  fixed form ignoring the figure script is off-function.
- **Don't chromaticize the mode** — pre-war craft core is modal + diatonic;
  ornaments live between pitches (bends), not as chromatic passing tones;
  chordal chromaticism = later American stratum.
- **Second ≠ harmony part** — seconding voice heterophonically elaborates
  (double-time, off-beats, echo); 3rds/6ths harmony-part render is a
  revival-era stratum.
- **Lead instrument era** — 19th c. violin lead (Pedotser/Stempenyu) vs
  post-1900 American clarinet lead (Brandwein/Tarras); pick era when
  assigning GM programs.

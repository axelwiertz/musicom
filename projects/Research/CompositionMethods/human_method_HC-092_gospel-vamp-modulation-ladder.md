## Description

The **gospel vamp** is the African-American Black-church arranging practice of building an open-ended, repeating harmonic loop — the *praise break* / vamp section — and using it as the engine of escalation: choir layers stack one by one, the soloist ad-libs melismatic "runs", drums and hand-claps tighten, and — the signature device — the **whole loop climbs key by key (the modulation ladder / "key climb")** until the director cuts it and the ensemble lands a cadential **tag**. The method is the *song-form architecture* of gospel music (verse → chorus → vamp → tag, with optional fast **shout music**), a living oral/aural tradition still practiced in thousands of churches and studios.

It is a **human arranging/composition craft**, not an algorithm: every climb, layer entry, and cut is a human decision negotiated in real time between soloist, choir, organist, and director.

## Tradition / Culture

- **African-American gospel music** — Black church, USA.
- Roots: ring shout of enslaved Central/West Africans (Kongo-derived) → lined-out hymnody / spirituals → Pentecostal revival (Azusa Street 1906) → **Thomas A. Dorsey** codified the blues-based *gospel song* form in 1930s Chicago ("Father of Gospel Music") → 1940s–50s soloists (Mahalia Jackson) and quartets → **mass-choir arranging** era (James Cleveland, Rev. James Moore, 1960s–80s) → **Twinkie Clark / Clark Sisters** shout sound (1970s–80s) → contemporary urban gospel (Kirk Franklin) and worldwide influence (soul, R&B, rock, funk).
- Scholarship: Horace Clarence Boyer, *How Sweet the Sound: The Golden Age of Gospel* (1995); Anthony Heilbut, *The Gospel Sound* (1971, rev. 1997); Mellonee V. Burnim & Portia K. Maultsby (eds.), *African American Music: An Introduction* (2006/2015).
- Transmission: oral/aural — learned by singing in church, choir rehearsal, and imitation; notation is a convenience, never the source.

## Craft Procedure (how the human does it, step by step)

1. **Compose or choose the song** — Dorsey's classic form: a **verse** (narrative, soloist-led) and a **chorus** (refrain, full ensemble). New gospel songs are still made the old way: a tune hummed or sung, a testimony text, then an arrangement. (The vamp method is also applied to existing hymns — e.g. "Amazing Grace".)
2. **Set the choir voicing** — SATB, but with gospel alterations: added 6ths, 9ths, major 7ths, altered dominants, and the famous "gospel clusters". Piano and **Hammond organ** comp with rhythmic **stabs** (short repeated chords), organ often adds **scoops** (glissandi into the chord) and pedal bass.
3. **Build the intro vamp** — a short loop (often ii–V–I, IV–I, I–vi–ii–V, or a single chord pedal) played by organ/piano, free until the soloist is cued. This is the *breathing* before the verse.
4. **Verse → Chorus** — soloist carries the narrative with call-and-response punctuation from the choir; the chorus hits full. Each repeat can be ornamented (runs, key lifts on the chorus ending — a miniature version of the ladder).
5. **Enter the vamp / praise-break section** — the core device:
   - pick a **1–4 chord loop** (a "vamp chord progression", typically short and diatonic with a strong cycle: ii–V–I, IV–V–I, or I–vi–IV–V);
   - repeat the loop as the engine; **stack layers per pass**: pass 1 = soloist + rhythm; pass 2 = sopranos join; pass 3 = altos; pass 4 = tenors; the choir's answering phrase grows thicker each time (layer-stacking density gradient);
   - soloist **ad-libs runs** (melismas, ornaments) over the loop — "running the vamp";
   - choir interjects ("yeah", "oh", "Hallelujah", "clap your hands") — call-and-response intensifies;
   - drums + **hand-claps on upbeats**, bass tightens, tempo pushes slightly — the **drive**.
6. **Execute the modulation ladder (key climb)** — the signature:
   - on each repeat of the loop, the **whole band lifts a half step or whole step**;
   - the organist/pianist executes the lift as the **V7 of the new key** (the "modulating dominant"/"scoop" — e.g. loop in C, at the climb the band plays G7→C then re-enters the loop in D♭ or D);
   - typical climbs: 2–6 rungs; whole-step = gradual elevation, half-step = dramatic; each rung re-energizes the loop (the crowd's response is part of the composition — the human loop listens and climbs again);
   - **Clark Sisters' "You Brought the Sunshine"** (1981) is the canonical recorded ladder — a chain of upward key changes in the vamp.
7. **Cut the vamp → land the tag** — on the director's cue the loop stops and the ensemble lands a **cadential tag**: IV–V–I (or ii–V–I) with a fermata, often on a sustained "oh"/title phrase, frequently built as a **chord pyramid** (voices enter one by one from the bottom or top: tenor → alto → soprano → lead, stacking on one chord).
8. **Optional shout / praise-break finale** — fast-tempo instrumental-vocal section: chromatic basslines, dominant-7 chords, snare hits + hand-claps on **upbeats**; organists improvise over the changes. **Twinkie Clark** is credited as originator of the classic contemporary shout sound; trombone-led instrumental shouts ("UHOP") mimic vocal techniques with vibrato/slurs/glissando.
9. **Rehearsal pedagogy** — taught by ear in choir rehearsal: section leaders learn their line, the vamp order is negotiated ("we'll take it up two times then cut to the tag"), everything held by memory and cue — a fully human, in-the-room compositional process.

## Practitioner Examples

- **Thomas A. Dorsey** (1899–1993) — "Father of Gospel Music"; codified verse–chorus gospel song form with blues harmony; "Precious Lord, Take My Hand" (1932).
- **Mahalia Jackson** (1911–1972) — the archetypal soloist; improvised ornamentation and drive in concert.
- **Rev. James Cleveland** (1931–1991) — "King of Gospel"; created the modern mass-choir gospel sound (blending traditional gospel, soul, pop, jazz in arrangements); classic vamps on "Peace Be Still", "I Don't Feel No Ways Tired", "Lord, Help Me to Hold Out"; pioneered the 1970s choir-recording model.
- **Aretha Franklin + Southern California Community Choir** — *Amazing Grace* (1972, live, with James Cleveland) — gospel vamping, ad-lib spoken/sung testimony, key lifts.
- **The Clark Sisters / Twinkie Clark** — "You Brought the Sunshine" (1981) — the signature modulation-ladder record; Twinkie Clark credited as originator of the classic contemporary shout sound (fast tempo, chromatic bass, dominant-7 chords, up-beat claps).
- **Shirley Caesar** ("Jesus, I Love Calling Your Name"), **Walter Hawkins & Love Center Choir** ("Changed", "Oh Happy Day" tradition), **Rev. Milton Brunson & Thompson Community Singers**, **Mississippi Mass Choir**, **Kirk Franklin** ("Stomp", "I Smile") — contemporary vamp/ladder practice.
- **Sister Rosetta Tharpe** — gospel guitar showmanship; crossed gospel into rock/R&B.
- Scholarship/pedagogy: Boyer (1995), Heilbut (1971), Burnim & Maultsby (2006); modern piano pedagogy (e.g. "GospelKeys"-style courses) teach the vamp + modulation ladder as core vocabulary.

## UnitMatrix Integration (Musicom engine)

### Voices
- **Voice 0 = Lead / soloist** — verse melody + ad-lib run cells (ornament surface over the loop; no new structural tones beyond the chord scale).
- **Voice 1 = Choir soprano** — top line of the gospel-voiced chord stack; enters on its assigned layer pass.
- **Voice 2 = Choir alto** — middle-voice fill with added 6ths/9ths; enters next.
- **Voice 3 = Choir tenor** — inner voice; enters next; also carries the pyramid's first entry in the tag.
- **Voice 4 = Choir bass (vocal)** — roots/fifths of the stack; enters early (foundation-first stacking).
- **Voice 5 = Hammond organ** — stab comping on the loop, scoop glissandi at climbs, pedal bass; chordal rhythm locked to the clap grid.
- **Voice 6 = Piano** — comping stabs + solo fills between phrases.
- **Voice 7 = Bass guitar** — walking/chromatic line; approaches each new key root from a half-step below at the climb.
- **Voice 8 = Drums / percussion** — snare + hand-claps on **upbeats**, ride/hat drive; intensity crescendo per vamp column.
- **Voice 9 = Metadata** — key-ladder log (each vamp column's key + climb interval), layer-stack count, section tags, cue points.

### Sections
`S0 Intro vamp (organ loop, free)` → `S1 Verse` → `S2 Chorus` → `S3 Vamp_1 → Vamp_2 → … → Vamp_n (each column one loop pass, transposed +1/+2 semitones)` → `S4 Tag (IV–V–I fermata + chord pyramid)` → `S5 Shout (optional fast finale)`.

### Rules (encoder sketch)
- **Vamp-loop rule** — the harmonic loop (chord cell set) is constant within a vamp column; columns differ only by transposition and layer count.
- **Key-ladder rule** — consecutive vamp columns are +1 or +2 semitones from the previous; enforce by transposing the whole column's pitch content; the climb cell = V7 of the new key (modulating dominant).
- **Layer-stack rule** — each vamp column adds one more choir voice (density gradient 1→4), zero-drift safe (every voice still fills the full section tick span; unused voices rest).
- **Call-response rule** — soloist phrase cells end where a choir interjection cell begins; interjection vocabulary constrained to the loop harmony.
- **Comping rule** — organ/piano stab rhythm locked to up-beat clap grid; pedal bass on loop roots; scoop cells allowed only at climb boundaries.
- **Tag rule** — S4 = cadence (IV–V–I or ii–V–I) with terminal fermata; pyramid entry order tenor→alto→soprano→lead; all voices converge to the final sonority before the terminal landmark.
- **Shout rule** — S5 fast tempo, chromatic bass, dominant-7 chords, snare + up-beat claps (Twinkie-style).
- **Humanization note** — traditional gospel eighths swing, contemporary straight; the engine's style-driven humanizer (jazz/blues loose timing) fits traditional gospel; keep the up-beat claps tight (rhythm-section anchors ~2 ms).
- **Zero-drift** — every section equal length in bars; vamp columns equal-length cells; ad-lib run cells pad to the section landmark like any ornament surface.

## Quirks / Pitfalls

- **Real-time form** — the vamp's length and climb count are decided *in performance*, not fixed in advance; a fixed-score encoding must snapshot one negotiated run (the recording practice: the 1972 *Amazing Grace* captures exactly such a run).
- **Transposition mechanics** — climbing by semitone moves every chord's pitch content; naive per-cell absolute pitches break — the encoder must transpose column content as a block (relative chord shapes), the same reason sheet-music transposition is done on chord symbols, not note-by-note.
- **Claps on upbeats** — off-grid relative to the downbeat grid; must be placed on the tick of the "and" (e.g. beat+240 at 480 tpb) or as absolute-tick humanized events, never snapped to beat starts.
- **Pyramid entry vs equal-length invariant** — entering voices must still fill their section span; use rest-prefixed cells (like kotekan/antiphony entries in HC-002/HC-067).
- **Swing vs straight** — get the style flag right; applying jazz-style loose timing to a contemporary straight gospel track is a genre error.
- **Overlap check** — no existing HC entry covers gospel arranging; HC-067 (call-and-response) and HC-006 (big-band shout chorus) are neighboring but distinct (generic antiphony; jazz arranging), HC-015 shape-note hymnody is a different (dispersed-harmony, white) hymn tradition, HC-044 isicathamiya is South African choral — none duplicates the vamp/ladder/tag craft.

## 1. Overview & Human Historical Craft

The **Andalusi Nūbah** (literally "turn" or "shift", referring to court musicians awaiting their turn to perform before the caliph) is one of the oldest surviving monumental classical suite traditions in the world. Originating in medieval Islamic Spain (al-Andalus, 711–1492 CE) and famously systematized in Córdoba by the polymath court musician **Ziryāb** (Abu al-Hasan 'Alī Ibn Nāfi', 789–857 CE), the tradition crossed the Strait of Gibraltar with waves of Andalusi exiles and Moriscos, settling in North Africa into distinct regional branches: **al-Āla** in Morocco (centered in Fes, Tetouan, and Chefchaouen), **Gharnātī** and **San'a** in Algeria, and **Ma'lūf** in Tunisia and Libya.

In the Moroccan *al-Āla* school, the tradition preserved 11 monumental nūbāt (out of an idealized cosmological 24, one for each hour of the day). A full nūbah is an expansive multi-hour architectural suite constructed entirely within a single overarching modal realm (*tab'*, plural *tubū'*), moving through a rigorous macro-structural sequence of five distinct rhythmic-metric movements called **mawāzīn** (singular **mīzān**).

Rather than Western harmonic drama or sonata-allegro tonal tension, the human craft of the Nūbah organizes time through **unidirectional temporal acceleration and rhythmic compression**:
1. It begins out of time with unmetered instrumental exposition (*bughya* / *mshālyā*).
2. It anchors the mode with an instrumental overture (*tushiya*).
3. It marches through five strictly ordered *mawāzīn*, each with its own cyclical metric signature (*wazn*).
4. Within *each individual mīzān*, the tempo escalates systematically through three internal tempo phases:
   - **Muwassa'** ("spacious" / slow): broad, solemn, highly ornamented vocal stanzas (*san'āt*).
   - **Mahzūz** ("shaken" / agitated): medium tempo, syncopations tighten.
   - **Insirāf** ("departure" / accelerated finale): rapid, driving, ecstatic metric resolution.
5. Movements are bound together seamlessly by brief instrumental bridges (*qantara*, plural *qanātir*, literally "bridges") that pivot the ensemble into the next meter without breaking modal continuity.

The entire repertory of over 900 poetic-musical units was codified by the Tetouani scholar **Muhammad ibn al-Husayn al-Hā'ik** in his seminal 1789 songbook, the **Kunnāsh al-Hā'ik**, which froze the poetic texts (*muwashshahāt* and *azjāl*) and their performance order into a sacred living oral-written canon (*turāth*).

## 2. The Step-by-Step Human Craft Procedure

How a master *sheikh* (ensemble leader playing the *rabāb* or *'ūd*) and the *jawq* (classical ensemble) assemble and realize a Nūbah:

### Step 1: Select the Mode (*Tab'*) & Tuning
The leader fixes a single overarching *tab'* (e.g., *Raml al-Māya*, *'Ushshāq*, *Hijāz al-Kabīr*, *Rasd*, *Istihlāl*). The entire suite remains anchored to this modal center. Microtonal inflections, characteristic pentachords/tetrachords (*ajnas*), resting tones (*qarār*), and dominant reciting tones are established. The stringed instruments (*'ūd ramal*, *rabāb*, *kamānja* / Moroccan violin played vertically on the knee, *qānūn*, *cello*) are tuned to the open pitches of the *tab'*.

### Step 2: Atmospheric Opening — *Bughya* (or *Mshālyā*)
Before any meter begins, the ensemble performs the **bughya**: a short, non-metered improvisatory prelude played in unison and heterophony.
- The *rabāb* or *kamānja* outlines the characteristic motifs, leaps, and resting degrees of the *tab'*.
- Establishes the modal ethos, emotional state, and pitch intonation for both players and audience.

### Step 3: Formal Instrumental Overture — *Tushiya*
The ensemble enters the first metric cycle with a composed, through-composed instrumental overture called **tushiya** (plural *twāshi*).
- Played in fixed meter (often associated with the first mīzān).
- Introduces the rhythmic pulse and displays the virtuosic coordination of the string players and percussionists (*tār* tambourine and *darbūka*).

### Step 4: Traversing the Five *Mawāzīn* Cycle
The master navigates the suite through the five canonical Moroccan metric suites in rigid succession:
1. **Mīzān al-Basīt**: 6/4 compound slow meter. Stately, noble, expansive.
2. **Mīzān al-Qā'im wa-Nisf**: 8/4 asymmetrical additive meter (grouped as 3+2+3 or 5+3).
3. **Mīzān al-Btāyhī**: 8/4 fast symmetrical / flowing pulse.
4. **Mīzān ad-Darj**: 4/4 walking rhythmic drive.
5. **Mīzān al-Quddām**: 3/4 or 6/8 brisk, driving triple/compound sprint.

### Step 5: Realizing Vocal Stanzas (*San'āt*) within a Mīzān
Within each *mīzān*, vocal settings of strophic poetry (*muwashshah* in classical Arabic or *zajal* in Andalusian dialect) are sung. Each song is called a **san'a** (literally "piece of craft"):
- All instrumentalists sing in unison chorus (*jawq*) while simultaneously playing their instruments.
- Solo vocal improvisations (*inshād*) can be interjected by master singers over sustained instrumental drones.
- The *san'āt* are performed in ascending tempo phases:
  - **Muwassa'**: Extended syllables across wide metric beats.
  - **Mahzūz**: Half-tempo acceleration; ornamental density increases.
  - **Insirāf**: Percussion drops subdivisions, tempo doubles/triples, clapping (*tarshīq*) and ecstatic driving rhythms propel the melody to the climax.

### Step 6: Modulatory Bridges (*Qantara*)
To transition between movements or when modulating between related modes (*inshiqāq*), the leader plays an instrumental transition (*qantara*). The percussion shifts its *dum-tek* cadence, aligning the ensemble into the new metric division without stopping.

### Step 7: Heterophonic Ornamentation
No Western functional chordal harmony exists. Richness is generated entirely through **heterophonic stratification**:
- The *rabāb* provides the archaic, guttural, sustained, slightly nasal melodic skeleton with bowed portamento.
- The *'ūd* ornaments with plucked tremolos, rapid neighboring-tone flourishes, and octave displacement.
- The *kamānja* adds fluid slides, microtonal inflections, and upper-register shimmer.
- The *tār* (snare-ringed frame drum) strikes crisp edge *tek* and deep center *dum* accents, dictating the metric cycle.

## 3. Real Practitioner Examples

1. **Ziryāb (Abu al-Hasan 'Alī Ibn Nāfi', 789–857 CE, Córdoba)**:
   The foundational figure who brought Abbasid Baghdad court music to the Umayyad court of Córdoba. He established the 24 nūbāt linked to the humors and hours of the day, added a fifth string to the lute, and invented the pedagogical progression: singing poetry on a simple rhythm → unmetered modal phrase → metered instrumental melody → full suite.
2. **Muhammad ibn al-Husayn al-Hā'ik (fl. late 18th century, Tetouan, Morocco)**:
   Compiler of the *Kunnāsh al-Hā'ik* (c. 1789), the single most influential anthology in Moroccan music history. Al-Hā'ik recorded the complete texts, melodies, modes, and performance sequences of the 11 surviving nūbāt, preserving the tradition against oblivion during Ottoman expansion and European pressure.
3. **Haj Abdelkrim al-Raïs (1912–1996, Fes, Morocco)**:
   Legendary master of the *rabāb* and director of the *Orchestre Al-Brihi de Fès*. He preserved the purity of the Fasi style, spearheaded the definitive Ministry of Culture recordings, and authored critical notation treatises bridging oral rote memory with modern ethnomusicology.
4. **Moulay Ahmed Loukili (1907–1988, Rabat, Morocco)**:
   Renowned director of the Moroccan Radio Orchestra of Andalusian Music in Rabat. Loukili was famous for his expressive vocal interpretation (*inshād*), refined lute playing, and elegant tempo pacing through the *mawāzīn* suites.
5. **Mohamed Larbi Temsamani (1920–2001, Tetouan, Morocco)**:
   Innovator of the Tetouani school who directed the Conservatoire of Tetouan, incorporating subtle polyphonic voicings, string section precision, and formalizing the orchestral staging of *al-Āla*.

## 4. Musical Elements Framework (Musicom Engine Mapping)

### PITCH
- **Modal Unity (*Tab'*)**: A piece remains anchored in a single *tab'* (e.g., *Raml al-Māya*: D–E–F–G–A–B–C–D; or *Hijāz al-Kabīr*: D–E♭–F♯–G–A–B–C–D).
- Pitch centers are hierarchically ranked: resting finalis (*qarār*), dominant structural reciting degree (*nawā* / *istigrār*), and peak octave climax.
- Melodic motion is predominantly conjunct (stepwise) with characteristic triadic leaps (fourths and fifths) marking section entries.

### RHYTHM
- Governed by cyclical metric patterns (**mawāzīn**):
  - *Basīt* (6/4): 6 beats per cycle with primary downbeat *dum* and syncopated *tek*.
  - *Qā'im wa-nisf* (8/4): Asymmetric additive metric cycle (3+2+3).
  - *Btāyhī* (8/4): Flowing 8-beat walking cycle.
  - *Darj* (4/4): 4-beat walking march.
  - *Quddām* (3/4 or 6/8): Fast, driving hemiola / triple meter.
- **Acceleration Principle**: Systematic tempo escalation from *Muwassa'* (~60–72 BPM) through *Mahzūz* (~96–112 BPM) to *Insirāf* (~136–168 BPM).

### HARMONY
- **Zero Functional Harmony**: Pure modal monophony and heterophony. Vertical intervals are incidental results of heterophonic delays, octave doublings, and fifth pedal resonances.
- Tonal gravity is enforced via the persistent returning cadence to the *qarār* tonic at the conclusion of every poetic stanza (*bait*).

### STRUCTURE
- **Macro-Form**:
  - `Bughya` (Arrhythmic modal exposition)
  - `Tushiya` (Instrumental metered overture)
  - `Mīzān 1: Basīt` (Muwassa' → Mahzūz → Insirāf)
  - `Qantara` (Metric bridge)
  - `Mīzān 2: Qā'im wa-Nisf` (Muwassa' → Mahzūz → Insirāf)
  - `Qantara` (Metric bridge)
  - `Mīzān 3: Btāyhī` (Muwassa' → Mahzūz → Insirāf)
  - `Qantara` (Metric bridge)
  - `Mīzān 4: Darj` (Muwassa' → Mahzūz → Insirāf)
  - `Qantara` (Metric bridge)
  - `Mīzān 5: Quddām` (Muwassa' → Mahzūz → Insirāf → Climax)

### TEXTURE
- **Stratified Heterophony**:
  - **Skeletal Core**: *Rabāb* playing elongated pitches with microtonal inflection and drone strings.
  - **Plucked Elaboration**: *'Ūd* playing running semiquavers, broken chords, and octave touches.
  - **Melismatic Vocal/Bowed Upper Layer**: *Kamānja* and unison chorus singing poetry.
  - **Percussion Foundation**: *Tār* and *Darbūka* executing the *dum* (low) and *tek* (high) rhythmic syntax.

## 5. UnitMatrix Mapping (Voices & Sections)

In Musicom's `UnitMatrix` model (where rows = voices, cols = sections, cells = `MusicUnit`, with strict zero-drift equal track lengths):

### Voices Architecture (Rows)
- **Voice 0: Skeletal Spine / Rabāb (`program=MidiInstrument.CELLO` or `VIOLIN`)**
  - Carries the unadorned structural modal melody.
  - Acts as the invariant pitch backbone; every note corresponds to a structural poetic syllable.
- **Voice 1: Plucked Heterophonic Elaboration / 'Ūd (`program=MidiInstrument.ACOUSTIC_GUITAR_NYLON` or `OUDFALLBACK`)**
  - Ornaments Voice 0 with octave leaps, mordents, tremolos, and arpeggiated neighbor tones. Never strays from Voice 0's modal target pitches.
- **Voice 2: Unison Vocal / Kamānja Lead (`program=MidiInstrument.VIOLIN`)**
  - Delivers the *san'a* vocal line with legato phrasing, slurs, and poetic declamation. In *Muwassa'*, holds long tones; in *Insirāf*, rapid syllabic hits.
- **Voice 3: Tār & Darbūka Percussion Grid (`channel=9, MidiPercussion`)**
  - Enforces the active *mīzān* cycle. Low bass drum / conga hits model *dum* (center), open rimshot / tambourine hits model *tek* (edge).
  - Drives the tempo shift at section transitions.

### Sections Architecture (Columns)
- **Section 0: Bughya (Arrhythmic Prelude)** — Emulated via rubato-quantized long structural notes, sparse free-form contour.
- **Section 1: Tushiya (Instrumental Overture)** — Fixed metric entry (e.g. Basīt 6/4).
- **Section 2: San'a Basīt Muwassa' (Slow 6/4)** — Stately vocal poetry.
- **Section 3: San'a Basīt Insirāf (Fast 6/4)** — Rhythmic acceleration and energetic drive.
- **Section 4: Qantara Bridge (Transition to Darj or Quddām)** — 2-bar percussion and unison link.
- **Section 5: San'a Quddām Muwassa' (Moderate 3/4 or 6/8)** — Triple meter swaying motif.
- **Section 6: San'a Quddām Insirāf (Fast 6/8 Climax)** — High-velocity finale accelerating to final stinger cadence.

### Deterministic Encoding Rules
1. **Modal Invariance Rule**: All voices strictly draw pitch classes from the assigned *tab'* (e.g., D-Dorian or D-Phrygian Major). No foreign chromatic pitches allowed.
2. **Heterophonic Subservience Rule**: Every event in Voice 1 and Voice 2 must share pitch class or consonances (octave, fourth, fifth) with Voice 0 on the downbeat of each metric pulse.
3. **Mīzān Metric Mask Rule**: Percussion strikes in Voice 3 are locked to the specific *dum-tek* formula of the section's active *wazn*.
4. **Acceleration Invariant**: Track length per bar scales down (or BPM scales up monotonically) across sections from Muwassa' to Insirāf.
5. **Zero-Drift Padding Guard**: Every section cell in every voice terminates precisely at `section_len` ticks.

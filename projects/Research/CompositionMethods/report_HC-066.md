## 1. Method name & tradition line

**Ground-Bass Variation Set — Chaconne & Passacaglia Continuous-Variation Craft
(Ostinato Spine, Texture-Rotation Variations, Climactic Coda).**

Lineage: Spanish street origins (*chacona* / *pasacalle*, ~1600) → Italian keyboard
redefinition by Frescobaldi (late 1620s–1637) → French *passacaille* & English *chacony*
(Purcell, Couperin) → German/Austrian organ and instrumental apex (Buxtehude, Pachelbel,
Kerll, Biber, Bach, Handel) → Romantic use as symphonic finale (Beethoven 32 Variations
WoO 80 influence; Brahms Symphony No. 4 "Ciaccona") → 20th-c. revival (Webern Op. 1,
Reger Op. 127, Ravel Piano Trio passacaille, Shostakovich Violin Concerto No. 1,
Britten *Peter Grimes* passacaglia, Vaughan Williams *Passacaglia on B–G–C*, Glass
*Satyagraha*). One engine, two regulation strengths: **passacaglia** = strict recurring
melodic bass line (usually minor); **chaconne** = recurring harmonic progression with
freer bass (usually major). Bukofzer: "modern attempts to arrive at a clear distinction
are arbitrary and historically unfounded" — composers deliberately mixed them.

`human (→concrete)` — identical to all other HC rows. This is human-craft knowledge:

## 3. The craft procedure (as a human executes it)

1. **Compose the ground** — 2–8 bars (canonical 4/6/8), triple meter (3/4 or 3/2),
   often entering on the second beat. Two required properties: (a) its own distinct
   melodic profile (recognizable on every return), (b) an implied harmonic progression
   launching from an unambiguous tonic. Set cadence policy: authentic close per
   statement (self-contained) or half-cadence (dovetail into next statement).
2. **Choose the ground mode** — passacaglia mode (literal bass repetition; BWV 582) or
   chaconne mode (harmonic skeleton locked, bass free to move/be implied; BWV 1004).
   Decide ground migration schedule: when the ground may move to an inner/top voice
   (BWV 582 var. 11; Brahms 4 vars. 12–15 buried in a flute melody).
3. **Compose the exposed opening statement ("theme")** — transparent texture, no
   elaborate non-harmonic tones: bare ground on organ pedals (BWV 582), winds/brass
   statement (Brahms 4). The listener's anchor.
4. **Lay the variation chain — one texture stance per ground statement:**
   - *Subdivision rotation:* whole/half → quarter → dotted → eighth → triplet → 16th.
   - *Texture archetype rotation:* homophonic chords, two-voice counterpoint, imitative
     canons, perpetual-motion figuration, melody-in-bass, chorale, toccata passagework,
     ornamented aria line.
   - *Register/voicing moves:* low/high melody, wide/close spacing, octave transfer.
   - *Harmony-side variation:* chromatic inflection, tonicization, relative-key
     excursions (Buxtehude modulating ostinato; Brahms vars. 26–28 C major).
5. **Build paired variations** — bookend statements (plain ←→ elaborated, original ←→
   inverted): BWV 582 vars. 10–11 in double counterpoint at the 15th; Brahms pairs
   4/5, 8/9.
6. **Architect the large arc** — group variations into sections; **reset complexity to
   near zero at structural pivots** (Bach Chaconne: D-major reset at ~var. 34, D-minor
   reset at ~var. 53); offset climaxes with sudden activity drops ("waves": simple →
   complex → drop → greater climax).
7. **Compose the coda — break the loop** — strict repetition suspended at the end:
   Brahms breaks the 8-bar units, hemiolas (3/4 regrouped as 3/2), "pseudo-variations"
   in the Più Allegro coda, German-sixth apex on the theme's chromatic note, definitive
   E-minor punctuation; Purcell's "remember me" leap over the 11th ground statement;
   BWV 582 closes on a cadential pedal then launches the fugue.
8. **Optional framing** — append an independent movement on the same theme (BWV 582
   passacaglia → fugue) or embed the set as a cycle movement (Brahms 4 finale; Biber
   closing passacaglia).

**Pedagogical doctrine** (Rubin, *Music Composition & Theory*, U. Minnesota 2024):
unassuming → complex in waves; thin to one/two voices for perpetual-motion middle
sections (licenses more non-harmonic tones/arpeggiation); alternate block chords with
figuration; coda drawn from first-variation material or new quasi-improvised material;
paired variations (German model) or ritornello/refrain variations (English model —
Purcell's grounds); keep variety alive via bass-transfer, relative-key modulation,
metric alteration of the ostinato, and imitation over the ground.

## 4. Practitioner examples (real human use)

- **Girolamo Frescobaldi** — *Cento partite sopra passacagli* (1637): first
  comparative chaconne/passacaglia treatment; metric & rhythmic alteration of the
  ostinato across a long variation series.
- **Henry Purcell** — "When I am laid in earth" (*Dido and Aeneas*, 1689): chromatic
  fourth ground (G–F♯–F–E♭–D, *passus duriusculus*) stated **11 times**, 3/2, G minor;
  strict unvaried ground under a freely varied vocal line with word-painting; also
  *Chacony in G minor* Z. 730 (ritornello-variation model).
- **Heinrich Ignaz Franz Biber** — Passacaglia closing the *Mystery (Rosary) Sonatas*
  (~1676): solo violin over descending tetrachord, implicit four-part harmony.
- **Arcangelo Corelli** — *La Folia* (Op. 5 No. 12, 1700): archetypal harmonic-ground
  set; written-out ornamental variations codifying improvisation practice.
- **J.S. Bach** — Passacaglia & Fugue BWV 582: 8-bar ground (head borrowed from André
  Raison) + 21 variations + fugue; ground migrates to top voice (var. 11); canons in
  the middle; double counterpoint pairs; pedal entries/exits in waves. Chaconne
  BWV 1004: 256 bars = 64 four-bar phrases over a harmonic ground, each phrase
  tonic → dominant; ABA D minor → D major → D minor; complexity resets at pivots.
- **G.F. Handel** — Passacaille HWV 432 (Suite No. 7 in G minor, 1720): paired
  keyboard variations over a 4-bar ground.
- **Dieterich Buxtehude** — Passacaglia BuxWV 161: *modulating* ostinato.
- **François Couperin** — Passacaille, *Ordre No. 8*: French passacaille in mixed
  chaconne/passacaglia practice.
- **Johannes Brahms** — Symphony No. 4 finale (1885), which he called a chaconne:
  8-bar rising theme with a chromatic 5th (A♯ in E minor; after Bach Cantata 150
  *Nach dir, Herr, verlanget mich*), **30 continuous variations + coda** in four
  sections (vars. 1–11 in 3/4; 12–15 slow 3/2 interlude incl. E-major trombone
  chorale; 16–23 developmental 3/4; 24–30 recapitulatory with C-major excursion;
  Più Allegro coda breaks the loop with hemiolas, pseudo-variations 31–34, and a
  German-sixth resolution to E minor).
- **Benjamin Britten** — Passacaglia from *Peter Grimes* (Op. 33b); **Dmitri
  Shostakovich** — Passacaglia from Violin Concerto No. 1 (3rd mvt); **Anton Webern**
  — Passacaglia Op. 1 (serial-era ground); **Maurice Ravel** — Passacaille, Piano
  Trio; **Ralph Vaughan Williams** — *Passacaglia on B–G–C* (1933); **Philip Glass**
  — *Satyagraha* Act I Sc. 1.
- **Cousin practice:** jazz chorus-taking over blues/modal grooves (Coltrane, Miles,
  Fela) is the same invariance-contract discipline; analysts routinely compare Bach's
  variation sets to jazz soloing over static harmony.

## 5. UnitMatrix mapping (Voices & Sections)

**Voices**
- **V0 Ground/Ostinato spine** — invariant anchor. Passacaglia mode: literal recurring
  bass cell (pitch-class skeleton locked). Chaconne mode: locked harmonic-skeleton row
  with hinted bass. Length = one ground statement; drives zero-drift.
- **V1 Cantus/surface melody** — per-section top line; primary variation locus.
- **V2 Harmonic realization** — inner voices from the ground's implied progression,
  smooth voice-leading; implied (arpeggiated) in solo-instrument mode.
- **V3 Counterpoint/imitation layer** — second contrapuntal line; canonic entries at
  fixed offset/interval; invertible pairs.
- **V4 Figuration/diminution layer** — perpetual-motion streams, triplet/16th
  passagework; subdivision profile rotates per section.
- **V5 Pedal/coda extension** — sustained pedals, hemiola cross-rhythms, final
  cadential punctuation; active in coda (and under optional fugal appendage).

**Sections**
S0_Ground_Statement (exposed) → S1..S30_Variation blocks (one texture stance each,
each exactly one ground statement long) → S_Major_Interlude (optional parallel-major
block, chaconne ABA model) → S_Break_Coda (loop suspended: climax, hemiola, terminal
cadence) → optional S_Fugue on the ground's head (BWV 582 model).

**Rules the engine would encode**
- Ground invariance (pitch skeleton locked in passacaglia; harmonic row locked in
  chaconne); ground-migration gate for designated sections.
- Zero-drift: section length = N × ground statement length; all voices padded to
  section terminal landmark.
- Texture-rotation lock (no adjacent same stance); subdivision density gradient per
  group with reset rule at pivots.
- Cadence lock (each variation ends on the ground's cadence; half-cadence flag
  permits dovetail); seam voice-leading continuity.
- Imitation offset table (canon at 1 beat / 1 bar; interval 4th/5th/octave).
- Key-mode guard (minor until major block/coda); coda-break rule (loop suspended;
  hemiola allowed; terminal cadence punctuation).

**Integration notes for the Musicom engine** (per AGENTS.md): use
`UnitMatrixComposer`; `create_matrix(num_voices=6, num_sections=N)`;
`fill_voice_section` per cell; `validate()` zero-drift gate MUST pass before
`to_midi()`; BAR = 1920 ticks at 480 TPQ / 4/4 — ground statements in 3/4 or 3/2
set via `create_section(bars=...)` multiples. Ensemble suggestion: V0 cello/bass,
V1 violin/flute, V2 viola/keys, V3 second violin, V4 woodwind figuration, V5 pedal
brass/continuo — or solo-violin mode (V0 implied, others arpeggiated).

## 8. Quirks & pitfalls

- **Chaconne/passacaglia distinction is historically unenforceable** (Bukofzer;
  Goetschius and Lucas defined them in exactly opposite ways). Implement as ONE engine
  with a strictness parameter, not two forms.
- **Ground must stay short** (≤8 bars; 4/6/8 canonical). Oversized grounds blur the
  invariance perception that IS the form.
- **Variation ≠ new piece.** Abandoning the ground's phrase rhythm or cadence breaks
  form coherence; the skeleton must remain audible.
- **Complexity is NOT monotonic.** Masters reset to near-zero at pivots (Bach Chaconne
  at var. 34 and 53); a relentless build destroys the two-channel listening design.
- **Paired variations are structural**, not decoration — Baroque practice repeats each
  variation with modification; encode pair design deliberately.
- **Solo-instrument chaconnes imply the ground** (Biber, BWV 1004): V0 must exist as an
  internal row even when not literally struck — keep it for voice-leading/harmony
  derivation (and for transcription-friendly export).
- **Zero-drift discipline:** with N × ground-length sections, section boundaries fall
  exactly on ground cadences; use the half-cadence flag to create dovetails rather than
  stretching bars.
- Numeric fine print on BWV 582: theme + 21 variations + fugue (some earlier analyses
  count 20; the 21-count is the standard modern figure); treat as "~8-bar ground with a
  ~20-variation chain" in engine configs.

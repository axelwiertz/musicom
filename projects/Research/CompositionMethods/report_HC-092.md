## 1. Method name & tradition

**Gospel Music Vamp & Modulation-Ladder Arrangement — Praise-Break Key-Climb Craft**
(Verse → Chorus → Vamp → Tag architecture; choir layer-stacking; call-response ad-lib runs; shout music)

- **Tradition / Culture:** African-American gospel music — Black church, USA. Lineage: ring shout (Kongo-derived, enslaved Central/West Africans, southeastern US) → lined-out hymnody / spirituals → Pentecostal revival (Azusa Street, 1906) → **Thomas A. Dorsey** codifies the blues-based gospel song form (1930s Chicago) → soloist era (Mahalia Jackson) and quartets (Golden Gate Quartet) → **mass-choir arranging** (James Cleveland, Rev. James Moore, 1960s–80s) → **Twinkie Clark / Clark Sisters** classic shout sound (1970s–80s) → contemporary urban gospel (Kirk Franklin). ## 3. The craft procedure in detail (how the human does it)

1. **Compose or choose the song.** Dorsey's classic form: **verse** (narrative, soloist-led) + **chorus** (refrain, full ensemble); blues-based harmony with religious lyrics. New gospel songs still arise orally — tune + testimony text — then get arranged. The vamp method also re-arranges existing hymns (Aretha's "Amazing Grace").
2. **Set the choir voicing.** SATB with gospel alterations: added 6ths/9ths, major 7ths, altered dominants, "gospel clusters". Piano and **Hammond organ** comp with rhythmic **stabs**; organ adds **scoops** (gliss into the chord) and pedal bass.
3. **Build the intro vamp.** A short loop (ii–V–I, IV–I, I–vi–ii–V, or one-chord pedal) played free by organ/piano until the soloist is cued — the breathing before the verse.
4. **Verse → Chorus.** Soloist carries the narrative with choir call-and-response punctuation; the chorus hits full. Chorus endings often get a miniature key lift.
5. **Enter the vamp / praise-break section** — the core device:
   - pick a 1–4 chord loop (short, strongly cyclic: ii–V–I, IV–V–I, I–vi–IV–V);
   - repeat the loop as the engine; **stack layers per pass** (pass 1 soloist + rhythm → sopranos → altos → tenors); the choir's answer thickens each pass;
   - soloist **runs** (melisma ad-libs) over the loop;
   - choir interjections ("yeah", "oh", "Hallelujah", "clap your hands");
   - drums + **hand-claps on upbeats**, bass tightens, slight tempo push = the **drive**.
6. **Execute the modulation ladder (key climb)** — the signature device:
   - on each repeat the **whole band lifts a half step or whole step**;
   - executed as the **V7 of the new key** (modulating dominant / "scoop": loop in C, climb plays G7→C then re-enters in D♭/D);
   - 2–6 rungs typical; whole step = gradual, half step = dramatic; each rung re-energizes the loop; the crowd's response feeds the next climb (real-time negotiation).
   - **Clark Sisters, "You Brought the Sunshine"** (1981) = canonical recorded ladder.
7. **Cut the vamp → land the tag.** Director's cue stops the loop; ensemble lands **IV–V–I or ii–V–I with fermata**, often on a sustained "oh"/title phrase, frequently as a **chord pyramid** (voices enter one-by-one: tenor → alto → soprano → lead, stacking onto one chord).
8. **Optional shout / praise-break finale.** Fast tempo, **chromatic basslines**, dominant-7 chords, snare hits + **hand-claps on upbeats**; organ improvises. **Twinkie Clark** credited as originator of the classic contemporary shout sound; brass "UHOP" shouts (trombone lead) mimic vocal vibrato/slurs/glissando.
9. **Rehearsal pedagogy & transmission.** Learned by ear in church and choir rehearsal; section leaders learn lines, vamp order negotiated verbally ("we'll take it up two times, then cut to the tag"); all held by memory and cue. Notation is a convenience, never the source.

## 4. Practitioner examples

- **Thomas A. Dorsey** (1899–1993) — "Father of Gospel Music"; codified verse–chorus gospel song form; "Precious Lord, Take My Hand" (1932).
- **Mahalia Jackson** (1911–1972) — archetypal soloist; improvised ornamentation and drive.
- **Rev. James Cleveland** (1931–1991) — "King of Gospel"; built the modern mass-choir gospel sound (traditional gospel + soul/pop/jazz arrangements); classic vamps on "Peace Be Still", "I Don't Feel No Ways Tired", "Lord, Help Me to Hold Out".
- **Aretha Franklin + Southern California Community Choir** — *Amazing Grace* (1972, live, with James Cleveland): gospel vamping, ad-lib testimony, key lifts.
- **The Clark Sisters / Twinkie Clark** — "You Brought the Sunshine" (1981): the signature modulation-ladder record; Twinkie Clark = originator of the classic shout sound.
- **Shirley Caesar** ("Jesus, I Love Calling Your Name"), **Walter Hawkins & Love Center Choir** ("Changed"), **Rev. Milton Brunson & Thompson Community Singers**, **Mississippi Mass Choir**, **Kirk Franklin** ("Stomp", "I Smile") — contemporary vamp/ladder practice.
- **Sister Rosetta Tharpe** — gospel guitar showmanship bridging to rock/R&B.
- Pedagogy/scholarship: Boyer (1995), Heilbut (1971), Burnim & Maultsby (2006); modern gospel piano courses teach the vamp + modulation ladder as core vocabulary.

## 5. UnitMatrix mapping (Musicom engine)

### Voices
- **Voice 0 = Lead / soloist** — verse melody + ad-lib run cells (ornament surface over the loop; stays inside the chord scale).
- **Voice 1 = Choir soprano** — top of the gospel-voiced stack; enters on its assigned layer pass.
- **Voice 2 = Choir alto** — added-6th/9th fill; enters next.
- **Voice 3 = Choir tenor** — inner voice; also opens the tag pyramid.
- **Voice 4 = Choir bass (vocal)** — roots/fifths; enters early (foundation-first stacking).
- **Voice 5 = Hammond organ** — stab comping on the loop, scoop glissandi at climbs, pedal bass; chordal rhythm locked to the clap grid.
- **Voice 6 = Piano** — comping stabs + fills between phrases.
- **Voice 7 = Bass guitar** — walking/chromatic; approaches each new key root from a half-step below at the climb.
- **Voice 8 = Drums / percussion** — snare + hand-claps on **upbeats**; intensity crescendo per vamp column.
- **Voice 9 = Metadata** — key-ladder log (each vamp column's key + climb interval), layer counts, section tags, cue points.

### Sections
`S0 Intro vamp (organ loop, free)` → `S1 Verse` → `S2 Chorus` → `S3 Vamp_1 → Vamp_2 → … → Vamp_n (each column one loop pass, transposed +1/+2 semitones)` → `S4 Tag (IV–V–I fermata + chord pyramid)` → `S5 Shout (optional fast finale)`.

### Rules (encoder sketch)
- **Vamp-loop rule** — harmonic loop (chord cell set) constant within a vamp column; columns differ only by transposition and layer count.
- **Key-ladder rule** — consecutive vamp columns +1/+2 semitones; transpose the whole column's pitch content as a block; climb cell = V7 of the new key (modulating dominant).
- **Layer-stack rule** — each vamp column adds one more choir voice (density gradient 1→4); unused voices rest-pad, preserving the equal-length invariant.
- **Call-response rule** — soloist phrase cells end where choir interjection cells begin; interjections constrained to loop harmony.
- **Comping rule** — organ/piano stabs locked to the up-beat clap grid; pedal on loop roots; scoops only at climb boundaries.
- **Tag rule** — S4 = cadence (IV–V–I / ii–V–I) with terminal fermata; pyramid entry tenor→alto→soprano→lead converging before the terminal landmark.
- **Shout rule** — S5: fast tempo, chromatic bass, dominant-7 chords, snare + up-beat claps.
- **Humanization note** — traditional gospel eighths swing, contemporary straight; rhythm-section anchors stay tight (claps ~2 ms, bass lays back); style-driven humanizer (jazz/blues loose) fits traditional gospel.
- **Zero-drift** — every section equal length in bars; vamp columns equal-length cells; run cells pad to the section landmark like any ornament surface.

## 8. Quirks / pitfalls

- **Real-time form** — vamp length and climb count are decided *in performance*; a fixed-score encoding must snapshot one negotiated run (exactly what live albums like *Amazing Grace* 1972 capture).
- **Transposition mechanics** — a semitone climb moves every chord's pitch content; the encoder must transpose column content as a block (relative chord shapes), never per-cell absolute pitches.
- **Claps on upbeats** — off-grid relative to beat starts; place at the "and" tick (beat+240 @ 480 tpb) or as absolute-tick humanized events; never snap to beat starts.
- **Pyramid entry vs zero-drift** — entering voices must still fill their section span; use rest-prefixed cells (pattern already used by HC-002 kotekan / HC-067 antiphony).
- **Swing vs straight** — style flag matters: traditional gospel swings eighths, contemporary urban gospel is straight; applying jazz-loose timing to a straight contemporary track is a genre error.
- **Sibling-job note** — `methods_db.md` (algorithmic DB) is untouched; only `human_methods_db.md` was appended (one row, bold style matching HC-055+; older HC-001..054 rows use non-bold IDs — pre-existing formatting split, left as-is).

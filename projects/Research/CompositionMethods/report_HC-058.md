## Full Craft Procedure

### Step 1: Song selection (Samba de Enredo)
Each year the samba school announces its *enredo* (theme/story) around April. The school runs a competitive song selection process (*disputa*) over several months — multiple composition teams (compositores) submit entries, and the school votes on a winner. The winning *samba de enredo* is a 2–4 minute song in A–B–A–B or A–B–B–C form with:
- Part A verse (8 bars, narrative)
- Part B / Refrão chorus (4–8 bars, catchy repeated hook)
- Bridge / Terceira parte (optional)
- Lyrics follow Portuguese *quadra* (4-line stanza) or *décima* (10-line) metre, 6–8 syllables per line.

### Step 2: Set the Andamento (tempo anchor)
The *mestre de bateria* fixes the parade tempo — the *andamento* — typically **100–140 bpm**. Grupo Especial schools (the top 12) usually run 120–130 bpm. Once set, the tempo is **isochronous**: no acceleration, no ritardando. This is the first rule — the surdo beat 1+2 pattern must lock to this tempo and never deviate.

### Step 3: Compose the Surdo Spine (the "feet")
The surdo section is structured as three distinct drum tones:
- **Surdo 2 (Segunda)**: smaller drum, higher pitch. Plays on **beat 1** — the "call." (Pitch ~D2–F2.)
- **Surdo 1 (Primeira)**: largest drum, deepest pitch. Plays on **beat 2** — the "response"/answer. (Pitch ~C2–Eb2.)
- Together Surdo 1+2 form an invariant **two-beat "heartbeat"** pattern that runs continuously for the entire ~90-minute parade. Never stops, never changes.
- **Surdo 3 (Terceira / Cortador)**: smallest surdo, highest pitch. Plays **syncopated "cutting" variations** between beats 1 and 2. Adds swing density. Can be active or inactive per section (corte on in chorus, off in verse).

The mestre notates (mentally or on paper) the surdo 1+2 anchor first as the tempo spine, then composes the surdo 3 corte line to fill the gaps.

### Step 4: Layer the Caixa Engine
The caixa (snare drum, metal shell, snares engaged) is the **subdivisional engine**:
- Continuous 16th-note subdivision (4 notes per beat, or *semínima pontuada* feel).
- The *pegada* (accent profile) is school-specific:
  - **Caixa em cima** (Mangueira style): snare held high, bright dry sound, strict 16ths, accent on & of beats 2&4.
  - **Caixa marcado** (Salgueiro style): heavier, more marcato attack, accent on the "and" of every beat.
- The caixa NEVER stops (except in paradinha breaks). It provides the "swing" feel that makes samba distinctive.
- Every school's caixa style is a sonic fingerprint.

### Step 5: Repinique — the Conductor
The repinique (tenor drum, stick+hand technique) is the **mestre's executive instrument**:
- Reinforces the basic pulse and doubles caixa subdivision in fills.
- Plays **chamadas** (opening calls): distinct solo phrases that start the samba.
- Leads **paradinhas** (breaks): call-and-response figures where the mestre plays the call phrase and the full bateria responds with a composed unison rhythm.
- The mestre often carries the repique while conducting, cuing the bateria through the piece.

During full-groove sections (verse, chorus, bridge), the repinique plays time-keeping pulse. Only during calls, breaks, and viradas does it play the distinctive solo figures.

### Step 6: Tamborim Colour/Design
The tamborim section is the **design voice** — the mestre writes specific rhythmic patterns (*levadas* / *designs*) that are syncopated "drawings" over the groove:
- **Carreteiro**: the standard 4-beat pattern: hit on beats 1's &, 2, 2's &, 4. (Tamborim turned for timbre variation.)
- **Partido-alto**: more open, off-beat heavy, derived from partido-alto samba.
- **Custom designs**: written per chorus section to mark the form.

Each tamborim player can *virar* (turn/handle-tilt the drum during play) to alter pitch/timbre. The mestre may design a specific tamborim line that only plays in the Refrão (chorus) — this makes the tamborim a **form-marking device**.

### Step 7: Shimmer Layer — Chocalho / Ganza / Agogô
High-frequency colour fills the audible "sheen" of the bateria:
- **Chocalho / Ganza**: continuous shake (8th-note triplet feel or 16th-note grid). Never stops during non-break sections. The "shimmer blanket."
- **Agogô**: two-note bell (high–low) playing a syncopated pattern. Active in chorus (Refrão) sections only. Marks chorus boundaries.
- **Reco-reco**: scrape rhythm accenting clave-like phrases. Optional.

The mestre decides which shimmer instruments are **on** in which section section (mask on/off).

### Step 8: Compose the Breaks — Paradinha & Virada
Breaks are the bateria's punctuation marks:
- **Paradinha**: The mestre plays a call on repique → all instruments stop except Surdo 1+2 heartbeat → bateria plays a composed unison phrase (1–2 bars) → silence for 1–2 beats → groove resumes.
- **Virada**: A 1–2 bar "turn" phrase leading into the next section. All voices accent on **beat 3** — the universal "turn" signal in Rio samba.
- **Parada Final**: The final cadence — a composed unison figure (2–4 bars) landing on beat 3 as a simultaneous crash.

The mestre composes each break as a rhythmic phrase that must be teachable by ear (memorable, singable pattern).

### Step 9: Arrange the Section Arc
The mestre designs a **dynamic and textural arc** over each song cycle:

| Section | Bars | Density | Key Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S0 — Intro/Cabeça** | 4–8 | LOW → MID | Repique call → surdo anchors alone → caixa enters → tamborim enters → full |
| **S1 — Part A (verse 1)** | 8 | MEDIUM | Full groove. Carreteiro tamborim. Standard surdo 3. |
| **S2 — Refrão (chorus 1)** | 4–8 | HIGH | Full density. Optional tamborim design change. Agogô on. Surdo 3 max. |
| **S3 — Part B (verse 2)** | 8 | MEDIUM | Virada at bar 7–8 leading into next chorus. |
| **S4 — Refrão (chorus 2)** | 4–8 | HIGH | Full density. |
| **S5 — Ponte (bridge)** | 4–8 | LOW–MED | Paradinha mid-way for contrast. Reduced shimmer. Tamborim response call. |
| **S6 — Refrão Final** | 4–8 | HIGHEST | Maximum density + register. Surdo 3 most active. Tamborim decorated. |
| **S7 — Parada Final (coda)** | 2–4 | CRASH | Composed unison cadence, all voices crash on beat 3. |

### Step 10: Oral Transmission & Cue System
The mestre teaches the arrangement **by ear** over months:
- Mestre plays each part on demo instrument at rehearsal.
- *Ritmistas* repeat by rote (*orelhada* method — "by ear").
- Section directors (*diretores de naipe*) drill their instrument group in parallel.
- Full bateria rehearsals every weekend June→Feb, then nightly in Carnaval week.
- **Apito (whistle) signals**:
  - Single blast = attention
  - Double blast = paradinha
  - Long trill (liiiii) = virada
  - Three shorts = parada final

### Step 11: Walking Order
In parade, the bateria walks in a strict order (highest-pitch closest to mestre, lowest farthest back):
1. Mestre (repique + apito)
2. Chocalho/Ganza (shakers)
3. Tamborim
4. Caixa
5. Repinique section
6. Agogô, Cuica (tuned/colour)
7. Surdo 3
8. Surdo 2 / Surdo 1 (largest drums at the back)

This ensures the lower frequencies don't mask the higher-pitch cue signals from the mestre.

### Rules (12 rules encoded)

1. **Surdo invariance** — V0 always plays beat 1 + beat 2. Tick offsets: `bar_start` (Surdo 2) and `bar_start + half_bar` (Surdo 1). Never rests.
2. **Caixa continuous-subdivision** — V2 fires every 16th-note tick. Zero gaps. Grid intensity = `BAR / 4` ticks per 16th in 4/4.
3. **Repique entrance gate** — V3 active ONLY in S0 (chamada), S5 (paradinha call), S7 (parada final). Tacet in S1–S4, S6.
4. **Tamborim design-per-section** — V4 pattern fixed within section. 4 design variants max: D0=carreteiro, D1=partido-alto, D2=chorus-special, D3=bridge-response.
5. **Shimmer continuity** — V5 active continuously in all non-break sections. Muted ONLY during paradinha break bars.
6. **Paradinha break composition** — When triggered: V0 continues surdo anchor; V1, V2, V3, V4, V5, V6 play composed 1-bar unison phrase then cut to silence for 1–2 beats. Groove resumes after silence gap.
7. **Virada beat-3 accent** — At section-end virada (S3 bar 8, S6 bar 8 -> S7): all voices accent on tick `bar_start + 1920*(3/4)` (beat 3 crotchet).
8. **Density arc monotonic** — Density level per section: S0=1/4, S1=2/4, S2=3/4, S3=2/4, S4=3/4, S5=1/4, S6=3/4 (with surdo3 max), S7=all crash.
9. **Agogô section mask** — V6 active in S2 and S4 (Refrão sections) only. Off in all others.
10. **Parada final convergence** — In S7, all voices land on beat 3 as a simultaneous crash. Zero tick drift between voices.
11. **Tempo isochronous** — No tempo change within the arrangement. ±0 bpm drift.
12. **Zero-drift pad** — All voices equal-length padded to section length. Every voice's units end at the same terminal tick.

## Quirks & Pitfalls

1. **No pitch melody** — The bateria arrangement is purely rhythmic/timbral. In a UnitMatrix encoding, pitch exists only as register stratification (low→high = surdo→caixa→tamborim). The musical content is entirely in onsets, duration, velocity, and percussion instrument ID. This stretches the normal UnitMatrix model which expects pitch-as-melody.

2. **Oral tradition vs rule system** — Many mestre choices ("this design sounds right here") are aural/heuristic. The 12 rules above are a formal approximation for software — in reality, a mestre's craft involves decades of internalized rhythm grammar that resists total codification.

3. **No song melody** — The bateria does NOT play the samba de enredo's sung melody. That's handled by the *puxador* (lead singer) and *cordas* (string section: cavaquinho, guitar, violão). The UnitMatrix here covers only bateria percussion.

4. **Paradinha timing is brutal** — In a real 300-person bateria, the break's stop-gap-restart must be perfectly synchronized. Even ±1 tick offset between voices = audible trainwreck. Software must enforce exact break-length ticks.

5. **Surdo numbering confusion** — Some escolas invert the labels: "surdo 1" = highest, "surdo 2" = lowest. This document uses the majority convention (1=lowest, 2=middle, 3=highest). Always check the school's convention.

6. **Cuica excluded** — The cuica friction drum is a characteristic samba colour but is not core to the structural arrangement described here. Portela especially features cuica. Can be added as V8 in extended models.

7. **Carnival loop extension** — The arrangement is designed for a ~2-minute song repeated ~30× over 90 minutes. The UnitMatrix model covers one song-cycle; real performance is cycle×N with identical arrangement per cycle plus endurance fade.

8. **Walking order acoustics** — The physical arrangement of the bateria (high→low front→back) is a performance-space concern that doesn't map to MIDI. The pitch register in the UnitMatrix maps surdo=low, caixa=mid, tamborim=high to approximate the stratification.

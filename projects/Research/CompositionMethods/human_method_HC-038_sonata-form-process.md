# HC-038 — Sonata Form Process (Galant Exposition–Development–Recapitulation Craft)

**Method ID:** HC-038
**Layer:** concrete (target when implemented) — human-craft knowledge; realizes material through the UnitMatrix
**Tradition:** Western Classical — Viennese Classical / galant tradition (Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven, Schubert), ~1750–1830; pedagogy from Fux/Koch through Czerny, A.B. Marx; modern systematization by Caplin (*Classical Form*, 1998) and Hepokoski/Darcy (*Elements of Sonata Theory*, 2006)
**Primary Elements:** STRUCTURE, HARMONY, PITCH, TEXTURE
**Status:** ✅ Documented

## 1. What it is

The **sonata form process** is the human craft of building a large instrumental
movement as a *tonal argument*: present material in two contrasting keys,
destabilize it through modulation and fragmentation, then resolve the conflict by
bringing everything home. It is not a mold poured over finished themes — the
18th-century composer *composed into* the form, making the form's cadential
goals drive the writing.

The method has two intertwined layers, which is what distinguishes it from
HC-009 (motivic development, which supplies the *cell-transformation operators*
used inside this form):

1. **Tonal plot** — a key journey: tonic → (transition) → secondary key in the
   exposition, remote keys in the development, return to tonic with the second
   group *restated in the home key* in the recapitulation.
2. **Rhetorical zones** — Hepokoski/Darcy's "action spaces" **P–TR–'–S–C**,
   each with a *generic goal*: P proposes the tonic (provisionally), TR builds
   energy toward a **medial caesura (MC)**, S confirms the new key with the
   **essential expositional closure (EEC)** — the first *satisfactory* perfect
   authentic cadence that "proceeds onward to differing material" — and C
   reinforces. The recapitulation repeats the same rotation with the S-zone
   cadence now landing on the tonic: the **essential structural closure (ESC)**,
   the goal of the whole movement.

The human craft is **dialogic**: at every zone boundary the composer picks
among *defaults* (most-typical options), *lower-level defaults* (less common but
still idiomatic), or **deformations** (deliberate atypical surprises). Sonata
Theory: the form is "a constellation of normative and optional procedures that
are flexible in their realization." The composer's skill = knowing the norm
well enough to bend it meaningfully.

**Distinct from:**
- **HC-009 Motivic Development** — that method supplies the transformation
  *operators* (invert/fragment/augment); sonata form supplies the *tonal and
  rhetorical architecture* those operators serve. A sonata development uses
  HC-009's toolkit inside its P-based rotation.
- **HC-021 Species Counterpoint** — note-against-note craft, not large-scale
  tonal plotting.
- **HC-001 Partimento** — the *schema-level* (2–8 bar) craft a galant
  composer used to improvise the surface; sonata form is the movement-level
  frame those schemata fill.

## 2. Craft steps — how the human actually does it

The documented workflow of a Classical composer (reconstructable from
treatises — Koch's *Versuch einer Anleitung zur Composition* 1782–93,
Czerny's *School of Practical Composition* 1848, Marx — and from the
autograph/sketch record):

1. **Choose the movement's key, type, and layout.** Fix the home key; decide
   the sonata *Type* (Hepokoski/Darcy):
   - **Type 1** — exposition + recap only, no development (sonatina; common in
     slow movements).
   - **Type 2** — "reversed" rotation: development-like opening, segues into
     S; tonic restored by S's arrival (binary descendant).
   - **Type 3** — the textbook full design: exposition → development →
     recapitulation.
   - **Type 4** — sonata-rondo: rotation ends with a retransition, second
     rotation begins with P in tonic, obligatory P-based coda.
   - **Type 5** — concerto design: extra orchestral ritornello rotation,
     solo/tutti alternation.
   Also decide **Continuous vs Two-Part exposition**: if TR reaches a medial
   caesura, the exposition is articulated P|TR ' S|C; if TR flows straight into
   S, it is continuous.

2. **Compose P in the tonic — provisionally.** Write the primary theme
   (often a Schoenberg **sentence**: 2-bar basic idea + varied repetition,
   then continuation → cadence) that *defines* the tonic with a cadence. P is
   tonally provisional: the form will require departure, and P's return later
   marks every new rotation.

3. **Compose TR — the energy pump.** TR's chief goal is to *build energy*
   toward the medial caesura; it usually modulates on the way (dominant if the
   movement is major, relative major/III or v if minor). Fortspinnung
   technique: sequence, fragmentation of P's tail, model-sequence work.
   Decide: fortspinnung-style continuous TR, or TR ' with a **medial
   caesura** — the "hammer-blow" chords + grand pause (or light filler) that
   announce the secondary theme. Per Sonata Theory, a piece "cannot have a
   secondary theme without an MC to prepare it (except in highly deformational
   circumstances)."

4. **Compose S in the new key — the cadential quest.** S typically contrasts:
   quieter, more lyrical, piano, new melodic character. S's whole job is to
   *delay and then deliver* the perfect authentic cadence in the new key — the
   EEC. Humans dramatize the delay: trimodular blocks (a false second MC),
   evaded cadences, "S-space tries again." After the EEC lands, write **C**
   (closing zone): new or recycled material, reinforcing cadences, sometimes a
   "tutti-punch" suffix. Then mark the exposition repeat.

5. **Compose the development — tonal instability workshop.** Start in the key
   the exposition ended in (first-level default: reopen with P material in a
   new key; second-level default: begin episodically with new material).
   Run the exposition's themes through remote keys — sequence, fragmentation,
   juxtaposition (this is where HC-009's operator set is deployed). Keep the
   texture unstable: harmonic rhythm speeds up, cadences evade. End with the
   **retransition (RT)**: a dominant pedal / standing-on-the-dominant that
   thins out and prepares P's return. Optional human tricks: a **false
   recapitulation** (P starts in the wrong key, then veers off — comic or
   dramatic); retransitions to the "wrong" dominant (Brahms Op. 1; Schubert
   Great Symphony finale).

6. **Compose the recapitulation — same rotation, new tonic.** Restate P
   unchanged in the tonic. Recompose the *transition* — the famous
   "secondary development": the original TR modulated away; now it must stay
   home. **The crux is S**: it must return *in the tonic* (mode may flip —
   Beethoven V's S recast in C major; Mozart 40/i mode change) because the
   tonal conflict resolves *exactly here*. Land the ESC (the tonic PAC),
   which "confirms the piece's tonic as a whole, solidifying the tonal
   promise first made by P." Material extensively worked over in the
   development may be shortened or dropped (Mozart K. 457, Haydn Op. 77/1).

7. **Compose the coda — the post-argument.** After the ESC the harmonic
   argument is complete; the coda reinforces: new cadential themes, P-based
   apotheosis, or (Beethoven) a *second development* — his Eroica's enormous
   coda, the Eighth Symphony finale's exceptional length.

8. **Performance/revision pass.** Bar-by-bar score check: is every cadence
   weighted by its structural rank (Tovey/Rosen: cadence emphasis = formal
   importance; false cadences get real-cadence characteristics then undercut)?

### The defaults ladder (the human decision table)

| Moment | 1st-level default | Lower default | Deformation example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Development opening | P-based, new key | Episodic new material | Haydn Op. 76/1 false reprise |
| S in minor movement | III or v | — | Deformational S-keys (H/D) |
| MC | Half cadence in new key + caesura-fill | HC in tonic | Direct continuous flow to S |
| Retransition | V pedal → P in tonic | Subdominant return | Brahms Op. 1 C-dominant |
| Recap S | Tonic, same mode | Mode flip (major↔minor) | "Wrong-key" recapitulations |

## 3. Practitioner examples

- **Joseph Haydn** — Op. 76 No. 1 (false recapitulation in the retransition);
  Symphony No. 103 "Drumroll" (slow introduction too weighty to skip; material
  returns in the coda). Haydn's monothematic expositions reuse P's shape for S
  — the key contrast, not the theme, carries the contrast.
- **W.A. Mozart** — String Quartet K. 465 "Dissonance" (adagio introduction);
  Symphony No. 40/i (S recast major→minor mode flip in recap; trimmed S after
  development work); K. 457 (S-space omission after tonic-elaboration in the
  development); trimodular blocks in many S-zones.
- **Ludwig van Beethoven** — Piano Sonata Op. 13 "Pathétique" (grave
  introduction reappears at the development's start and in the coda);
  Symphony No. 5/i (S in the parallel major at the recap's ESC);
  Symphony No. 3 "Eroica" (monumental development + coda-as-second-development).
- **Franz Schubert** — Symphony No. 9 finale (retransition lands the theme in
  the flat mediant E♭ — deformational retransition); Great Symphony's
  three-key exposition practice.
- **Johannes Brahms** — Piano Sonata No. 1 (retransition builds on the
  *tonic's* dominant seventh, then takes the theme in C major — documented
  exception).
- **Hepokoski/Darcy codification** — *Elements of Sonata Theory: Norms, Types,
  and Deformations in the Late-Eighteenth-Century Sonata* (OUP 2006; SMT
  Wallace Berry Award 2008): dialogic form, defaults/deformations, P–TR–'–S–C
  action spaces, Types 1–5, EEC/ESC as the form's two cadential goals.
- **Pedagogical lineage** — Fux *Gradus* (1725, species craft underneath),
  Koch (1782–93, periodic/melodic-cadential formal functions), Czerny (1848),
  A.B. Marx (form as plot), Caplin (1998, formal functions: initiating/
  mediating/concluding), Schoenberg's sentence/period as the standard theme
  types fed into the zones.

## 4. UnitMatrix integration (Musicom)

Layer: **concrete (target when implemented)**. The sonata is the canonical
Section-skeleton method: the Section axis *is* the tonal plot; the Voice axis
carries the zone rhetoric. Every section is one action space; zone-goal rules
gate section transitions.

### Voices & Sections

- **Voice 0 — P-theme spine (primary voice).** States P at every rotation
  start (Exposition P, Development P-entry, Recap P, Coda P-fragment).
  The rotation marker: any P statement = new rotation. Invariant identity
  anchor (cf. HC-009's Voice 0).
- **Voice 1 — TR/S melodic voice (primary contrast).** TR entries = energy
  build (fragmentation of V0's tail, rising sequence); S entries = lyrical
  contrast (slower harmonic rhythm, piano dynamic, new contour). In the recap,
  V1's S-material is *transposed to tonic* — the matrix's single most
  rule-governed edit.
- **Voice 2 — accompaniment/comping texture.** Galant Alberti bass /
  sustained pads; density parameter tracks zone (sparse under S, driving
  sixteenths in TR, max in C).
- **Voice 3 — bass/tonal-gravity voice.** Carries the key plan: root motion
  I→V (or I→III/v) in exposition, wandering roots in development, V-pedal in
  RT, I in recap. Cadential formula ownership (PAC targets at EEC/ESC).
- **Voice 4 (optional) — MC punctuation + filler.** Hammer-blow chords at the
  caesura, caesura-fill (light echo into the gap), tutti C-zone reinforcement.
  Doubles as the "orchestra vs solo" alternation for a Type 5 rendering.

### Sections (the tonal plot)

```
(Intro opt) → P(tonic) → TR(→V/III) ' MC → S(V/III/v) → C(EEC)
→ [exposition repeat marker]
→ DEV (P-entry in new key → fragments, remote keys → RT: V-pedal)
→ P(tonic) → TR'(secondary development, no modulation) → S'(tonic) → C(ESC)
→ (Coda: P-based reinforcement / apotheosis)
```

Type parameter selects skeleton: Type 1 drops DEV; Type 2 = DEV + S + C;
Type 4 interleaves RT/P; Type 5 prepends an orchestral rotation.

### Rules (engine-encodable)

1. **Key-plan rule:** section key sequence is hard-coded per Type —
   `Expo: T→X (X = V if major else III/v); Dev: free (≥2 remote keys); Recap:
   T throughout`. S-in-recap = transpose S-content by the exposition's
   modulation interval, inverted.
2. **Cadence-goal rule:** EEC = first PAC in key X that is followed by
   differing material; ESC = same predicate in tonic. Cadence-evasion gate
   (allowed: evade/deceive N times in S before the goal fires).
3. **MC rule:** S may not begin without a preceding caesura event (gap or
   filler) except under an explicit deformation flag.
4. **Rotation rule:** P-recall starts a rotation; recap must replay the
   exposition's zone order (partial-rotation development allowed).
5. **Energy-gradient rule:** TR density/tempo-weight monotonic rise to MC;
   RT monotonic decay (thinning) to P.
6. **Contrast rule:** S = dynamic drop (≤ piano), slower harmonic rhythm,
   lyrical contour vs TR's fragmentation — measurable as onset-density drop +
   interval-variance drop.
7. **Default/deformation ladder:** each zone-boundary decision gets a level
   (1st default / 2nd default / deformation); profile *determinism* picks the
   default path, an explicit `deformations=[...]` parameter injects surprises
   (false recapitulation, wrong-key RT, trimodular block).
8. **Secondary-development rule:** recap-TR recomposed — its modulation
   contour is flattened (stays in tonic) while keeping the exposition TR's
   rhythmic profile.
9. **Zero-drift:** intro material returning in the coda uses absolute-tick
   landmarks; silent padding to `total_section_ticks − 1` per the tail-truncation
   pitfall so the exposition-repeat bar doesn't shift tracks.

### Method hybrids

- **HC-009 inside:** development sections run the motive operator set on
  P/S fragments (this form supplies *where*, HC-009 supplies *how*).
- **HC-001 partimento schemata** generate the surface (Prinner in TR,
  Fonte in S, Fenaroli in RT) — the actual galant procedure.
- **ABS-001 Tension Curve Planner** maps naturally: exposition tension rise
  (P→TR peak→S release→C re-rise), development peak, RT decay, recap resolve —
  the sonata is a tension curve with cadential pinpoints.
- **ABS-002 Subset Walker** can drive the development's key walk; the
  retransition gate forces return to the dominant of T.

## 5. Quirks / pitfalls

- **Recap-TR is the failure point:** naively replaying the exposition
  re-modulates to V and never returns. The engine must *recompose* TR
  (flatten its modulation). This is the #1 rule humans teach ("secondary
  development").
- **EEC ≠ any PAC:** the first S-zone PAC often gets evaded/repeated
  ("S tries again"); the EEC predicate requires *proceeding onward to
  differing material*. Naive cadence-counting overcounts.
- **Mode vs key:** recap S may change mode (Beethoven 5: minor→major) but
  never key. Encode key-plan as pitch-class center, mode as scale-set toggle.
- **Continuous expositions** have no MC — a selector that assumes P|TR S|C
  will mis-segment continuous designs (e.g. many first-movement concertos).
- **False recapitulation** fools pitch-based P-detectors; detect via the
  *following modulation away* rather than theme-match alone.
- **Monothematic expositions** (Haydn) reuse P for S — contrast-by-key only;
  a contrast-scored gate would reject Haydn's own expositions.
- **Count assertions:** development length varies from tiny (Eine kleine
  Nachtmusik) to colossal (Eroica) — never gate on proportional duration;
  gate on the cadential landmarks instead.

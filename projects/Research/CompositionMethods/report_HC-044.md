## 2. Tradition Context

Isicathamiya ("walk softly / tip-toe", from Zulu *-cathama*) and its louder ancestor **mbube** ("lion") are the a cappella choral crafts of South African Zulu migrant workers. Origin chain:

1. 1920s–30s: rural Zulu men migrate to the coal mines/factories of Natal, Johannesburg, Durban; form hostel singing societies to rebuild community; weekend competitions in assembly halls (8pm–8am, up to 30 choirs, prize = goat/honour).
2. Forerunners: *ingoma* stomp-dance, mission four-part hymnody, American black minstrel troupes (Orpheus McAdoo 1890s) + ragtime.
3. 1939: Solomon Linda & the Original Evening Birds record "Mbube" — first African record to sell 100,000+; names the genre.
4. 1960: Joseph Shabalala founds Ladysmith Black Mambazo; softens the style into modern isicathamiya; 1986 *Graceland* (Paul Simon) makes it global.
5. Global lineage: Weavers "Wimoweh" (1951) → Tokens "The Lion Sleeps Tonight" (#1, 1961) → Disney *The Lion King* (1994); Linda's estate credited/compensated 2006 after Rian Malan's *Rolling Stone* exposé.

Mbube vs isicathamiya: mbube = loud, forceful, dense low chant + soaring improvised lead; isicathamiya = soft, blended, tip-toed, overlapping. Both share the same craft spine documented here.

## 3. The Craft Procedure (how a human actually does it)

Ten-stage human workflow (composition → orchestration → rehearsal → performance, one by-ear process):

1. **Dream-trance composition.** Shabalala: "my body is sleeping, my spirit is at work." Visited for six months by 24 spiritual elders singing isicathamiya; ended with a musical examination he passed perfectly. The whole song (all parts) is heard at once, then taught from memory. Songs answer current troubles (politics, love, religion, worker dignity) — socially conscious by design.
2. **Fix Zulu text (amagama).** Short aphoristic lines; fixed short refrains ("wembube", "hamba kahle") = the chorus-response cells.
3. **Massed bass spine.** Largest section of the choir; homophonic rhythmic-unison chant; in mbube a booming root progression — Coplan's **I–IV–I6/4–V** "repeating harmonic segments or root progressions"; folkworks analysis of "Mbube": simple four-chord loop **I–IV–I–V**; Erlmann: subsequent South African styles all adopted its booming I-IV-V bass.
4. **Lead call.** High tenor (soprano-register in early mbube). Linda's "Mbube" melody was **improvised on the third studio take** over the frozen chant (Rian Malan: "a dense meshing of low male voices above which Solomon yodeled and howled... improvising occasionally").
5. **Middle block fill.** Alto + 2nd tenor fill close-position chord tones, homophonic rhythmic unison with the bass ("groups of voices singing homophonically in rhythmic unison... to create intricate harmonies and textures", Mbube genre).
6. **Crossover (izilomo / "zoom").** High falsetto leaps/sustains **above the block** at phrase peaks and cadences (signature of "Homeless"/"Diamonds on the Soles of Her Shoes"); chord tones only.
7. **Overlapping call-and-response.** Chorus answers each lead line with the fixed refrain, **entering before the lead finishes** (Wikipedia: "the phrases of each section overlap") — imitative polyphony, not sterile alternation.
8. **Dance layer.** Semicircle, leader front; first song standing still (makwaya), second with synchronized i-steps/tip-toe (stylized quiet *ingoma*; historically to avoid disturbing hostel guards — "cothoza mfana", tip-toe guys). Choreography judged.
9. **Rote rehearsal.** No notation; composer teaches each part separately by ear — basses first, then middles, then lead/crossover; rehearsal circle = symbolic cattle enclosure (ancestor presence); prayer opens competitions.
10. **Farewell tag.** "Hamba kahle" closing — cadential descent/hold resolving to I, often with a final crossover flourish.

## 4. Practitioner Examples

- **Solomon Linda & the Original Evening Birds** (1939) — "Mbube": Linda soprano lead; Madondo & Sibiya middles; Mlangeni, Mkhize, Sikhakhane basses.
- **Joseph Shabalala & Ladysmith Black Mambazo** (1960–) — "Homeless", "Diamonds on the Soles of Her Shoes" (*Graceland* 1986), *Nomathemba*, "World in Union"; 5 Grammys; Oscar-nominated doc *On Tiptoe: Gentle Steps to Freedom* (2000); Shabalala d. 2020.
- **Competition circuit** — Durban Royal Entertainers, King Star Brothers, Empangeni Home Tigers, Brave Lion Singers, Elim A Cappella Choir.
- **Lineage** — Seeger/Weavers "Wimoweh" (1951, credits under pseudonym "Paul Campbell"); Tokens #1 "The Lion Sleeps Tonight" (1961, melody taken from Linda's improvisation); Miriam Makeba "Mbube"; Disney *The Lion King* (1994); 2006 estate settlement.
- **Scholarship** — Rian Malan ("In the Jungle", *Rolling Stone* 2000); Veit Erlmann (*Nightsong*, 1995); Carol Muller (*Focus: Music of South Africa*, 2008); Christopher Ballantine ("Chronicles of an African Composer", 1996); Angela Impey (Smithsonian 1997); David B. Coplan (*In Township Tonight!*, 1985 — I–IV–I6/4–V).

## 8. Quirks & Pitfalls

1. **Naming trap**: "mbube" names BOTH the song and the genre; "isicathamiya" and "mbube" are two styles of one craft — do not model them as unrelated methods. The blend-style mask rule captures the difference inside one UnitMatrix.
2. **Crossover terminology**: "crossover"/"zoom" is practitioner and liner-note vocabulary (Shabalala's groups, *On Tiptoe*), not a formalized ethnomusicological term; the *function* (high falsetto chord-tone peak above the block) is unambiguous in recordings. Document as function, not as an institutionalized term.
3. **Overlap, not alternation**: naive call-and-response implementations alternate strictly; the actual craft overlaps entries (chorus enters before lead finishes). Encode with an overlap allowance, not a strict mask.
4. **Improvisation boundary**: the lead's freedom is *decorative variation over a frozen harmonic cycle* — variation-not-invention. The block and bass never improvise. This is a hard rule to preserve when realizing.
5. **Dream-trance composition** is documented as the composers' own account (Shabalala; Zulu traditionalist path) — include it as the human cognitive-described workflow, not as mysticism to strip out; it is part of *how humans actually compose* here.
6. **Dance is structural**: i-step placement marks downbeats and section turns — the dance layer is not decorative; competition judging includes choreography. Keep Voice 4's downbeat accents aligned to the section grid.
7. **Copyright history is part of the craft record**: "The Lion Sleeps Tonight" lineage is a documented appropriation case (Linda paid £0.87 for the copyright; estate settled 2006). Historical context, not a composing step — include in provenance notes only.
8. **UNESCO date**: isicathamiya inscribed 2024 (19.COM); avoid earlier UNESCO claims — the 2014 inscription in the neighboring Cante Alentejano entry is a different tradition.
9. **Zero-drift**: several sections have different active-voice sets; silent voices must be padded to full section length (30,720 ticks total) or `validate()` fails.

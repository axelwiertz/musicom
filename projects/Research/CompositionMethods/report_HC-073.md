## 2. What the method is

Sutartinės (from *sutarti*, "to be in concordance") are the archaic 2–3-voice polyphonic songs of northeastern Lithuania — the canonical **second-as-consonance** craft. Built on heterophony, parallelism, canon, and free imitation, they sound "sharp and capricious": constant parallel seconds between voices, syncopated rhythms, short 2–5-pitch melodies with a narrow ambitus, two equal-length parts, and text that is mostly repeated words plus vocable refrains (sodauto, lylio, ratilio, tonarilio, dauno, kadujo, čiūto, tūto, rūto…).

The genre classifies itself by *who enters when* (≈40 documented performance styles, UNESCO):
- **Dvejinės** — 2 singers/groups, begin AND end together, different lines → parallel seconds. Example: *Kaip sėjau linelį*.
- **Trejinės** (most popular) — 3 voices in strict staggered canon; 2nd voice one phrase behind, 3rd two behind; when the 3rd enters the 1st rests → only two voices sound at once. Example: *Ka tu, bitele*.
- **Keturinės** — 2 pairs; pair 1 sings the stanza, pair 2 repeats the same words; within each pair the partners sing different lines. Example: *Žvingia žirgas*.
- **Instrumental** — men transferred the formulas to skudučiai (one pitch per pipe → hocket by construction), kanklės, ragai, daudytės.

Melody and text rotate "on a spiral" (mantra-like); voices are equal, so no voice owns the ending. Termination is the **ūktelėjimas**: all singers stop together on a shout (or a gesture; instruments hold a long sustained unison).

## 3. Craft procedure in detail

### Step 0 — Oral acquisition
Learned by ear inside women's work/ritual communities (no notation). Knowledge = melody cells + vocable refrains + entry formulas. Written record begins c. 1849 (Miežinis); Antanas Juška collected in the 19th c.; Zenonas Slaviūnas published the canonical 3-volume *Sutartinės* (1958–59).

### Step 1 — Fix the text block
Two parts: meaningful main text (short, repetitive, visual — e.g. *Kaip sėjau linelį*) + vocable refrain (*Tūto dijūto*…). Model: *Dijūta kalnali* = "Dijūta kalnali, Dijūta berželi" / "Tūto dijūto, Tūto dijūto". The two parts are assigned to different singers.

### Step 2 — Compose the melody cell
2–5 pitches; narrow ambitus; two equal-length parts; accented syncopated rhythm; short spiral-lockable phrases. The melody is the invariant spine.

### Step 3 — Choose deployment (the craft decision)
Dvejinė (simultaneous different lines → parallel seconds) / Trejinė (strict staggered canon with rest rotation; subtypes strict-canon *griežtinės* and round-canon *ratelio* per Slaviūnas typology) / Keturinė (two pairs, stanza echo, in-pair word split) / Instrumental (hocket-transfer of the same formulas).

### Step 4 — Set rhythm, accent, movement
Syncopation is the engine; refrains are distinctly articulated ("driving"). Dance sutartinės: circle/star formation, linked arms, moderate stamping; work sutartinės take pulse from the task. Revival tempo band ≈ 60–100 BPM.

### Step 5 — Cycle the spiral
Repeat the phrase+refrain loop; variation is in energy (accent/dynamics/dance), not pitch. Rotations may swap which voice/pair leads.

### Step 6 — Close with the ūktelėjimas
Anyone triggers it anywhere in the loop: all stop together on a shout (gesture variant; instrumental = long sustained unison tone). The only "cadence" is social convergence, not harmonic.

## 5. UnitMatrix mapping

**Voices (rows):**
- **V0 — Melody spine:** invariant 2–5-pitch cell (statement part); identity anchor; repeats every section (spiral).
- **V1 — Deployment-dependent partner:** dvejinė = heterophonic parallel partner forming parallel seconds (same rhythm, diverging contour); trejinė = 2nd canon entry (offset = one phrase unit); keturinė = pair-partner line with different words.
- **V2 — Trejinė = 3rd canon entry (offset = two phrase units) + rest-rotation mask (exactly 2 of 3 voices active); keturinė = second pair's echo (repeats V0's stanza).**
- **V3 — Stamp/dance layer** (accent grid; dance sutartinės) or skudučiai hocket part (instrumental version; one pitch per pipe).
- *(Instrumental variant: V1/V2 = interlocking panpipe/zither/horn parts obeying the same entry formulas.)*

**Sections (columns):** one phrase+refrain cycle per section (two equal-length parts). Sections repeat as spiral rotations; termination only via ūktelėjimas gate — no harmonic cadence column.

**Rules:**
1. Pitch-set constraint: ≤5 pitches, narrow ambitus; all voices from the same set.
2. Second-as-consonance: parallel seconds legal/required at any beat; no resolution rule, no tertian function.
3. Entry-formula lock (trejinė): offsets ∈ {0,1,2} phrase units; rest-rotation mask ⇒ exactly 2 of 3 voices active per column.
4. Dvejinė simultaneity: V0/V1 share start AND end ticks, different contours.
5. Keturinė pair-echo: pair-2 repeats pair-1 words/pitches at same cell length; partners never identical.
6. Equal-length parts; all sections equal length (zero-drift; validate() must pass).
7. Vocable-refrain slots: refrain cells use only the piece's vocable set.
8. Syncopation bias: accent mask per style; stamp accents only the syncopated grid.
9. Spiral repetition: sections cell-identical; variation of energy, not pitch.
10. Ūktelėjimas end-gate: final cell = unison convergence (shout / sustained unison), not a chord.

## 8. Quirks & pitfalls

- **"Concord" is dissonant:** correcting the seconds toward triads destroys the genre — the second must be the stable sonority.
- **No leader, no cadence:** loops must not auto-append V–I; termination is the social ūktelėjimas.
- **Trejinė rest mask is the signature:** naive always-3-voice canon gives the wrong density.
- **Text ≠ lyrics:** mostly vocables; syllable count matters more than semantics.
- **Instrumental ≠ accompaniment:** skudučiai hocket transfers the same entry formulas (one pitch per pipe).
- **Source caution:** *griežtinė/ratelio* subtype terms follow the Slaviūnas typology as summarized in musicology; primary English documentation (UNESCO) names only the three main types + ≈40 styles.

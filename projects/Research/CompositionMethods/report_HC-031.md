## 1. Method Name

Persian Radif — Dastgah–Gusheh Ordering & Improvisation. The craft of navigating the **radif** (ردیف, "row/order"), the orally transmitted canon of hundreds of short melodic figures (**gusheh**, گوشه "corner/melody") strung together, in a fixed **order** within a modal system (**dastgāh**, دستگاه "sonic space"), to compose a live performance that is at once a recitation of a fixed canon and a fresh improvisation.

## 2. Tradition

The radif is **not a set of finished pieces** — it is a *taxonomy of melodic atoms plus their canonical ordering*. Mastery = internalizing the whole canon so deeply that any gusheh can be recalled at any moment, then re-assembling it into a unique performance. Nour-Ali Boroumand's metaphor: traditional Iranian music is "like the song of the nightingale — each nightingale's song is unique."

- **Formation** attributed to **Ali Akbar Farahani** (Qajar court), who classified Iranian music to preserve it amid cultural decline. His original radif did not survive; his sons' radifs carry it.
- **Mirza Abdollah's radif** = the most important canon (notated by Jean During, 1970s, from Nour-Ali Boroumand's by-heart recording). **Agha Hosseinqoli's** parallel radif = the first gramophone-recorded (1905/1907/1912), notated by Dariush Pirniakan (2001).
- **Transmission** is oral, with the master sometimes appointing a **khalifeh** (successor) who drills other students (e.g. Morteza Neydavoud was Darvish Khan's khalifeh).
- **Scale of the canon**: Musa Ma'rufi's authoritative 1963 canon = 470 gushehs (some repeated across dastgahs); Bruno Nettl identified 152 gushehs in Maroufi's radif.

## 4. Craft Procedure (how the human does it, step by step)

1. **Internalize the radif by rote.** Years of repetition under a master (khalifeh drills). No notation in the living tradition. The canon lives in memory.
2. **Choose a dastgah.** Seven principal dastgahs (Shur, Mahur, Homayun, Segah, Chahargah, Nava, Rast-Panjgah) plus derivative **avaz** modes. A dastgah is a *sonic space* defined by interval relations + the characteristic *movement* of its melodies — not just a scale. Shur is the mother-mode; Homayun always precedes its child Bayat-e Esfahan.
3. **Open with the daramad.** The introductory gusheh, lowest in register, establishes the **shahed** (شاهد, the gusheh's central/reciting note — "the note upon which the gusheh is based"). The daramad's shahed anchors the whole dastgah.
4. **Walk the gusheh sequence.** Each dastgah holds 10–30 (up to 50+) gushehs, performed in a **canonical order** fixed by the masters' judgment. Each gusheh has its own shahed, register, melodic identity. Longer gushehs split into **ghesmat** (sub-sections) taught separately but performed together; some exist in multiple **no'** (types) giving performer freedom.
5. **Modulate (pardeh-gardani).** Certain gushehs have *modulatory potential*: Delkash (Mahur: 6th & 7th lowered), Hesar (Chahargah: 4th lowered, 5th raised), Mouyeh (4th lowered). The **shahed moves** when passing through them, "and this movement creates a new sonic space." Modulatory gushehs sit **mid-sequence**, never at beginning or end.
6. **Climb to the awj (peak)** — the highest-register, most intense gusheh cluster — then descend.
7. **Return home via the forud.** The cadential gusheh descending back to the daramad's shahed, closing the tonal journey. Almost every dastgah has a distinct forud.
8. **Improvise within the frame.** Each performance re-assembles gushehs with fresh ornamentation (tahrir vocal melisma, riz pick ornaments), microtonal inflection, personal shape. Individualism is valued — imitation is *not* admired.
9. **Add rhythmic contrast sparingly.** Most gushehs are free/meterless (rhythm follows Persian poetry meter). Occasional **zarbi** (metric) gushehs — reng, chaharmezrab, kereshmeh, naghmeh — are inserted after non-metric gushehs, borrowing their melodic character into a fixed pulse.

## 5. Practitioner Examples

- **Mirza Abdollah** (tar) — the most authoritative radif; transmitted to many students; notated by Jean During (1970s).
- **Agha Hosseinqoli (Mirza Hossein-Qoli)** (tar) — first to record the radif on gramophone (1905 Tehran, 1907 Paris, 1912); notated by Dariush Pirniakan (2001).
- **Ali-Akbar Shahnazi** (tar) — son of Agha Hosseinqoli; first to record the full radif by heart; still a reference for tar masters.
- **Nour-Ali Boroumand** — studied with Darvish Khan, Musa Ma'rufi, Esma'il Qahremani; the canonical by-heart source behind During's notation.
- **Abdollah Davami** — brought the radif into vocal performance; the vocal radif reference.
- **Majid Kiani, Dariush Talai, Mohammad-Reza Lotfi, Hossein Alizadeh** — modern interpreters/transmitters.
- **Kayhan Kalhor** — contemporary kamancheh master extending radif-based improvisation into global collaboration.
- **Bruno Nettl** — definitive Western scholarship (*The Radif of Persian Music*, 1987).

## 6. UnitMatrix Mapping (Voices & Sections)

### Voices
- **Voice 0 = drone + shahed spine** — tanbur/tar low string (and vocal sustaining) holds the current shahed; a moving invariant that *shifts its center* only at modulation points.
- **Voice 1 = lead (tar/setar/santur/kamancheh/vocal)** — the gusheh sequence: daramad → gushehs → modulatory gusheh → awj → forud; ornamented, microtonal, free-rhythm dominant.
- **Voice 2 = heterophonic ornament echo** — idiomatic pick/vocal ornament (riz, tahrir) shadowing Voice 1; no new pitch classes, only decoration.
- **Voice 3 = percussion (tombak/daf), zarbi-only** — silent during free gushehs; enters only for reng/chaharmezrab/kereshmeh metric gushehs.

### Sections
Each **gusheh = one section**, in canonical order: **Daramad → Gusheh₂ → … → Modulatory gusheh (pardeh-gardani) → Awj (peak) → Forud (return to daramad shahed)**. Metric zarbi gushehs punctuate the free-rhythm flow as contrast sections. The section sequence *is* the composition.

### Rules (what would encode it)
- **Shahed tracking** — a scalar "current central note" that only changes when a modulatory gusheh is entered; all melodic events gravitate to it.
- **Gusheh-order constraint** — sections drawn from an ordered gusheh list; daramad first, forud last; modulatory gushehs only mid-sequence.
- **Daramad anchoring** — opens lowest register, states the dastgah's shahed.
- **Forud return** — final section descends to the daramad shahed = closure.
- **Pardeh-gardani (modulation)** — only a whitelisted subset of gushehs (Delkash/Hesar/Mouyeh type) may shift the shahed.
- **Microtonal interval set** — quarter-tone (koron/sori) inflections within the dastgah's interval skeleton; not 12-TET.
- **Free-rhythm dominance** — most sections unmetered; zarbi sections carry a fixed pulse.
- **Individualism / variation-not-invention** — same gusheh material, fresh ornamentation each pass.

## 9. Quirks / Pitfalls (engine-fit notes for the implementer)

1. **Shahed is not a tonic.** The "center" moves *within* a dastgah and across dastgahs; a fixed key/tonic model will fail. Encode shahed as a mutable per-section scalar that the melodic generator gravitates to.
2. **Free rhythm vs the zero-drift grid.** Most gushehs are unmetered; UnitMatrix's absolute-tick cells must host non-gridded events — quantize onset *loosely* (or treat each gusheh as a section of "free" events padded to section length) to keep track-length symmetry.
3. **Microtonality.** Koron (lowered) / sori (raised) quarter-tones need pitch-bend or a >12 pitch-class grid; naive 12-TET rounding erases the dastgah's identity.
4. **Ordering *is* the method.** A radif is defined by gusheh *sequence*, not by a set of gushehs; a bag-of-gushehs generator is not the method. The canonical order (per dastgah) is a hard constraint.
5. **Modulation is whitelist-gated.** Only specific gushehs may move the shahed; a free "modulate anywhere" rule produces non-Persian wandering. Gate pardeh-gardani through the modulatory-gusheh set.
6. **Rhythm contrast is sparse.** Zarbi gushehs are the exception; the percussion voice is tacet for most sections — do not default a continuous groove.

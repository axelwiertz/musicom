# HC-049: Kecak (Ramayana Monkey Chant) — Balinese Vocal Interlocking & Dramatic Architecture

## Description

**Kecak** (also spelled ketjak/kécak) is a Balinese vocal chant-drama in which a large circle of male performers (50–150+) interlock a percussive "cak-cak-cak" syllable into complex polyrhythmic patterns — entirely without instruments. Created in the 1930s by I Wayan Limbak and German artist Walter Spies from the older Sanghyang trance ritual, the form dramatises the Ramayana epic. The chanting circle serves as both orchestra and chorus, while costumed character-dancers act out scenes in the centre. Kecak is called *gamelan suara* ("voice orchestra") because the vocal interlocking recreates the stratified sound of a bronze gamelan ensemble.

## Tradition / Culture

**Balinese Hindu — Indonesia (Bali).** Roots in the Sanghyang trance-exorcism dance (pre-20th c.), transformed into a secular Ramayana drama for tourist audiences in the 1930s. From the 1970s onward, *kecak kreasi/kontemporer* emerged as an artistic form valued by Balinese audiences themselves. UNESCO-adjacent (not yet inscribed, but widely recognised as representative Indonesian intangible heritage).

## The Craft Procedure (Step by Step)

### 1. Internalise the invariant pulse (the "cak" clock)
Every chorus member learns to maintain a steady, unchanging pulse — the "cak" syllable as a continuous stream. The syllable is percussive, short, and sharp (glottal stop + unvoiced vowel), enabling precise interlock without pitch or sustain overlap. Novices sit in the outer ring and imitate; seniors move inward.

### 2. Master the named rhythmic patterns
Each chorus member memorises at least four core "cak" patterns, each named by the number of syllables per cycle:

- **Cak telu** (3-syllable): syncopated 3-count within a 4/4 or 8-beat frame — creates cross-rhythmic tension.
- **Cak lima** (5-syllable): 5-count pattern, asymmetric against the grid.
- **Cak nem** (6-syllable): 6-count pattern (two groups of 3), more regular.
- **Cak pitu** (7-syllable): 7-count shift pattern.
- **Cak polos** (plain unison): all chanters together on the same "cak-cak-cak" subdivision.
- **Cak lesung**: a variant pattern named after the rice-pounding mortar rhythm.

These patterns are NOT written; they are transmitted from senior to junior by rote imitation in rehearsal sessions lasting months to years before a chanter performs publicly.

### 3. Learn the juru gending (song leader) lines
A few senior chanters have specialised vocal roles:

- **Juru gending**: a soloist ("pengong") who sings melodic vocal phrases over the cak tapestry — melodic filigree called "shiiir-yang-ngger-yang-nggur-yang-nggeer" — using a Balinese pentatonic mode, filling the texture without melody instruments.
- **Juru kawen**: maintains the pulse with "po-po-po-po" syllables (a bare steady beat marker).
- **Juru (kecak) leader**: shouting command cries ("Diih!" / "Chiaaat!") to cue section changes, tempo accelerations, or the entry of new patterns.
- **Dalang**: the narrator who recites the Ramayana story in Balinese/Sanskrit between scenes.

### 4. Assign chorus to concentric circles
The inner circles chant the most complex patterns (cak telu, cak pitu, alternations); the outer rings chant simpler cak-cak-cak or cak nem. The spatial arrangement matches the density hierarchy — inner = more syncopation, outer = ground.

### 5. Interlock the patterns (the hocket mechanism)
Each pattern occupies a defined subdivision slot. Because the "cak" syllable has zero sustain and a sharp attack, patterns interlock like gamelan kotekan: when one sub-group is silent, another sub-group fills the gap. The composite rhythm sounds continuous, faster and denser than any single chanter could produce. The effect is a single "voice orchestra" with no silence between onsets.

### 6. Layer in character-dancers and dramatic scenes
The Ramayana unfolds in fixed scenes. Costumed dancer-actors (Sita, Rama, Lakshmana, Ravana, Hanuman, Jatayu, the Vanara monkey army, the gold deer) enter the circle centre and perform stylised dance-drama. The chanting circle shifts pattern, intensity, and tempo to match the dramatic mood:

- **Gentle scenes** (exile, Sita and Rama in Dandaka forest): softer dynamics, slower cak pulse, juru gending sings melodic phrases.
- **Dramatic tension** (golden deer, abduction): gradual tempo increase, transition to syncopated patterns.
- **Battle** (Rama vs. Ravana, Hanuman's army vs. demons): maximum tempo, full cak telu/cak lima/cak pitu composite interlock, loudest dynamic, hand/arm movements become agitated tremolo.
- **Fire scene** (Hanuman's tail burns Lanka): dancer enters trance, fire-kicking, chorus at peak intensity.

### 7. Escalate through the performance arc
About 1 hour total. The arc follows a Jo-Ha-Kyū-like acceleration pattern (Balinese equivalent):

1. **Pembukaan (Opening)**: slow cak-cak-cak, swaying in circles, establishing pulse.
2. **Pengembangan (Development)**: cak telu/cak lima/cak nem patterns introduced, tempo gradually rises.
3. **Konflik (Conflict)**: battle scenes, maximum speed and density, trance states.
4. **Penutup (Closing)**: resolution of Ramayana (Ravana defeated, Sita rescued), cak fades back to slow pulse, chorus dissolves.

### 8. Cue transitions via vocal commands
The juru leader cries "Diih!" (stop) and "Chiaaat!" (start/resume). Senior chanters also signal tempo modulations by shifting their own pattern ahead of the chorus. The chorus follows by ear — no conductor, no notation.

## Practitioner Examples

- **I Wayan Limbak (1913–2003)**: co-creator of Kecak Ramayana. Master dancer and choreographer from Singapadu village. First international tour (1930s) popularised the form.
- **I Wayan Dibia (b. 1950)**: foremost scholar-practitioner. Author of *Kecak: the Vocal Chant of Bali* (1996). Codified the vocal patterns and performance structure.
- **Walter Spies (1895–1942)**: German painter/musician who lived in Bali and collaborated with Limbak to adapt Sanghyang into the Ramayana drama.
- **Kendra Stepputat**: ethnomusicologist who documented the *kecak kreasi* movement and distinguished tourist-kecak from contemporary-art-kecak.
- **Gamelan Batara Bayu / Kecak groups at Uluwatu, Tanah Lot, Ubud, Batubulan**: contemporary touring groups performing daily at temple venues.
- **Contemporary kecak kreasi choreographers**: expanding the form with new compositions for Balinese audiences.

### Rules (Constraints to Encode)

1. **Cak syllable mask**: each "cak" event = 1 short tick (~120–240 ticks at 480ppqn), zero sustain. No pitch — single attack per event.
2. **Interlock constraint**: For any subdivision slot t, at most one voice among V1/V2 may have an active onset (strict hocket).
3. **Pulse invariance**: V0 (cak pulse) AND V4 (po-po-po) maintain constant tempo baseline unless drum-led acceleration cues.
4. **Cross-rhythmic multiplicity**: composite grid must produce at least two different periodicity layers simultaneously (e.g. 3-against-4, 5-against-8).
5. **Pattern switching only at scene boundaries**: a pattern (cak telu/cak lima/cak nem) locks for the duration of the scene section. No mid-scene pattern mutation.
6. **Juru leader cue mask**: V6 events clear all other voices for exactly 1 beat (the "Diih!" break), then re-enter on V6 event "Chiaaat!".
7. **Pentatonic pitch set for V3**: only pitches from the pelog/slendro pentatonic subset are allowed for the juru gending melodic line.
8. **Density monotonic increase**: across S0→S5, number of active voices must monotonically increase. S6→S7 monotonic decrease.
9. **Scene boundary check**: V7 (dalang) text markers must fall between section transitions.
10. **Zero-drift**: all voice rows must end at the same total tick count — section lengths are pre-determined and padded.
11. **Trance/fire escalation at S5**: V6 triggers a trance escalation flag (could map to a density floor override — all voices must be ON during the fire scene, no rests).

# HC-061: Corsican Paghjella Polyphony — Cantu in Paghjella Three-Voice Staggered-Entry Craft

## Description
Paghjella (Cantu in Paghjella) is a traditional Corsican a cappella polyphonic singing practice, combining three male vocal registers that always enter a song in the same fixed order: **a secunda** (the leading voice, medium register, carries the main melody), **u bassu** (the lowest voice, provides the harmonic foundation with sustained tones), and **a terza** (the highest voice, enriches with ornamentation and melismas). The tradition is both secular and sacred, performed in churches, village squares, bars, processions, agricultural fairs, and social gatherings. It is transmitted orally by ear — no notation. UNESCO inscribed it on the List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding in 2009.

## Tradition / Culture
- **Corsican (Mediterranean)** — island of Corsica, France
- Roots: pre-Christian Mediterranean singing + Pisan/Gregorian chant influence (11th c.) + confraternal brotherhood traditions
- UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding (2009)
- Oral transmission within families, confraternities, and village communities
- Related to Sardinian *cantu a tenore* (4-part) and other Mediterranean polyphonies

## Layer

## Craft Procedure (how the human does it)

### 1. Internalise the repertoire by ear
No notation, no sheet music. The apprentice singer absorbs the repertoire by constant exposure — during church masses, confraternity rehearsals, village fairs, family gatherings, and (in past centuries) shepherds' vigils. Master singers correct intonation, vowel colour, entry timing, and phrase shape by ear, like a vocal coach.

### 2. Assemble the trio
Three male singers form the core unit:
- **A secunda** (also *secondu* / *siconda* / *sigonda*): the most experienced singer. Medium register. Carries the main melody. Sets the pitch, tempo, and phrasing.
- **U bassu**: the deep, steady voice. Sings the lowest notes, long sustained tones, acts as the harmonic anchor. A physically demanding role requiring consistent breath support.
- **A terza** (also *terzu*): the highest, most flexible voice. Weaves ornamentation, melismas, vocal flourishes above the secunda's melodic line. Requires wide range and agility.

### 3. Fix the mode and the text
The secunda selects:
- A **text** in Corsican (occasionally Sardinian, Latin, or Greek) — octosyllabic or decasyllabic lines, either sacred (prayers, confraternal hymns) or secular (love poetry, laments, work songs, political commentary)
- A **modal contour** (often Phrygian-inflected, with flattened second degrees, open fifths, and characteristic tierce de Picardie final cadence where a minor-key song resolves to a major chord)

### 4. Staggered entry — the fixed order
The entry order is invariant:
1. **Secunda** begins alone, singing the opening phrase of the melody, establishing pitch, mode, and tempo.
2. **Bassu** enters after a short delay (typically 2–4 beats), joining on a sustained low note or a simple harmonic support — the 5th, the tonic, or a third below the secunda's current note.
3. **Terza** enters last, above both, weaving a melismatic ornamentation line. The terza may swoop in after the bassu has stabilised or may wait for a phrase boundary.

This staggered entry sequence is repeated at each structural section or phrase boundary throughout the piece.

### 5. Voice-leading and harmonic convergence
Once all three voices are active, they follow established procedures:
- The **bassu** tends to start mid-range in a phrase and descend to the lowest tone by the cadence — giving a feeling of gravitational pull and centering.
- The **terza** moves freely above, often on open vowels, with melismatic flourishes. It may echo the secunda's phrase tail or create a contrasting line.
- The three voices produce a dense, beating texture from natural intervals and slight microtonal inflections (since they sing in just intonation, not equal temperament). This creates the famous "fourth sound" — an emergent overtone that seems to come from nowhere, called *a voce di l'anghjuli* (the voice of the angels), especially audible in resonant stone churches.
- Dissonances (2nds, 4ths, 7ths) are stable; they are not resolved the way functional harmony would require. Tension and release come from the beating and convergence to open intervals (5ths, octaves).

### 6. The cadence — tierce de Picardie
The hallmark paghjella cadence: the song may be in a minor or Phrygian mode throughout, but it **always ends on a major chord** (tierce de Picardie). The secunda raises the third, the bassu lands on the tonic, and the terza converges on the major third above. This is the traditional "close" — never a minor ending.

### 7. Ornamentation and variation
- The secunda may vary the melody slightly on each repeat (partial improvisation within boundaries).
- The terza freely ornaments — mordents, turns, melismatic runs, held high notes.
- The bassu may shift between sustained pedal and simple melodic descent over the course of a phrase.
- In the *mimofonia* system (Nando Acquaviva, A Cumpagnia), the lead singer uses hand/finger gestures to direct real-time harmonic choices — indicating when to move up or down, hold, or leap — while singing. This is a living, gestural composition method.

### 8. Form
- **Secular paghjella**: strophic — the same melody repeats for each verse of poetry, with the staggered entry pattern resetting at each strophe.
- **Sacred paghjella**: through-composed or strophic, following the liturgical text. Often includes antiphonal elements (two half-choirs answering each other).
- **Architecture**: the macro-form is a breath-arch of staggered entries, building to full three-voice texture, then dissolving at the cadence. Each phrase is shaped as a mini-arch: entry → full chord → descent → cadence convergence.

### 9. Performance context
- **No instruments** — a cappella only in the strict tradition.
- **Close physical proximity** — singers stand in a tight circle, facing each other or with one arm around each other's shoulders, to blend timbres and reinforce overtones.
- **Natural acoustics** — churches, stone rooms, village squares with reverberation enhance the "fourth sound."
- **Flexible rhythm** — speech-like rubato, no strict metre. Singers breathe together and shape phrases collectively. It is not conducted; it is breathed.

## UnitMatrix Integration (Musicom Engine)

### Voice Mapping

| Voice | Role | Description | Invariant Properties |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Voice 0 (V0)** | **Secunda Spine** | The main melody — the only voice that carries the composed/remembered melody in its entirety. Opens every section. | Invariant pitch/rhythm skeleton per song. Never altered. Anchor of piece identity. |
| **Voice 1 (V1)** | **Bassu Foundation** | Sustained low notes, harmonic root/5th drone, descends to lowest tone at each phrase end. Enters second, after secunda's opening gesture. | Harmonic stability target. Tonic/5th pedal or slow stepwise descent. |
| **Voice 2 (V2)** | **Terza Ornament** | Highest voice. Ornamented, melismatic line above secunda. Enters last, after bassu has stabilised. | Freer contour, but pitches derived from modal scale of V0. No new pitch classes outside mode. |
| **Voice 3 (V3)** | **Echo/Response Layer** | Optional antiphonal second trio or the same voices echoing the last phrase. Captures the *chjamà è rispondi* (call-and-response) dimension. | Same role structure as V0/V1/V2 but delayed by one phrase. |

### Section Mapping (Macro-Form as Breath-Arches)

| Section | Description | Voice Activity |
|---|---|---|
| **S0 — Secunda Solo (Intonation)** | Secunda opens alone, establishing pitch, mode, and tempo. 1–2 phrases. | V0 alone. V1/V2/V3 silent. |
| **S1 — Bassu Entry (Staggered)** | Bassu enters on sustained low tone or harmonic support. Full harmonic foundation sets in. | V0 active, V1 enters after 2–4 beats. V2/V3 silent. |
| **S2 — Terza Entry (Full Texture)** | Terza enters above, with melismatic ornamentation. Full three-voice polyphony. Density max. | V0 + V1 + V2 active. V3 optional (echo delayed). |
| **S3 — Echo/Response (if antiphonal)** | Second group or same trio repeats/varies the phrase. Call-and-response alternation. | V3 echoes V0/V1/V2 pattern, or V0–V2 thin to let V3 be heard. |
| **S4 — Cadence Convergence (Tierce de Picardie)** | All voices converge on tonic + major third. Bassu lands on tonic. Terza holds major third. | V0 = tonic, V1 = tonic or 5th, V2 = major 3rd. Full convergence. |
| **S5 — Dissolve** | Optional re-silence or hummed fade. The "release" after the resonant cadence hangs in the room. | Decay to silence. |

### Rules for UnitMatrix Implementation

1. **Fixed entry order**: V0 must always begin each section/verse. V1 may enter after a minimum offset of 2 beats. V2 must wait until V1 is established (min 1 bar after V1 entry). This staggered-entry rule is inviolable.

2. **Harmonic convergence**: at every cadence point, V1 must land on the tonic (or 5th), V0 must land on the tonic (or 3rd of the final chord), and V2 must converge on a consonant interval above (major 3rd in the tierce de Picardie). The final chord is always major, even if the modal content throughout was minor/Phrygian.

3. **Modal pitch set**: All voice pitches must belong to the chosen Corsican mode (typically Dorian, Aeolian, or Phrygian with a flattened 2nd). Microtonal inflection is tolerated as a performance parameter (just intonation offset) but at the UnitMatrix level, pitch classes are quantised to the mode scale.

4. **Bassu descent gradient**: V1 must start each phrase at a mid-range pitch and descend monotonically (allowing stays on the same pitch) to the cadence low note. This is the "gravitational pull" rule.

5. **Non-overlap entry**: V0, V1, and V2 should never all enter simultaneously at a section start. V0 must have a solo phrase before V1 enters.

6. **No instruments/no percussion rule**: UnitMatrix must not assign any percussion or instrumental voices. All voices are vocal (MidiInstrument voice-class or left as pure note data with no instrument program).

7. **Rubato / flexible timing**: Sections should have flexible duration. Zero-drift padding may use fermata-style holding (sustained chord) rather than metronomic fill.

8. **Tierce de Picardie filter**: The final cadence of every piece must check the major-third condition. If the secunda/melody ends on the tonic, the terza must supply the major third, creating a Picardy-third convergence.

9. **Echo rule (V3 antiphonal)**: If an echo voice (V3) is used, it must exactly mirror the pitch content of V0 for its section but delayed by one full phrase (phrase-length offset). No new pitch material in echoes.

10. **Zero-drift**: Every section must have equal total duration in ticks for all active voices, maintaining the zero-drift invariant. Sustained notes (bassu drones) are padded to section length.

# Consonant Spectral Signatures

## 1. Sibilants (Fricatives)
Sibilance is generated by narrow air turbulence against the teeth.
- **'S'**: High-frequency energy. Power concentrated above 5000Hz. Sharp, metallic. 4k-8k range dominant.
- **'SH'**: Lower spectral centroid than 'S'. Energy starts around 2000Hz, peaking near 3000Hz-4000Hz. "Softer" white noise profile.

## 2. Plosives (Stops)
Plosives are momentary blockages of airflow followed by a sudden release.
- **'T'**: Sharp, high-frequency burst. Energy >3000Hz. Very fast decay (40ms-60ms). No sustained noise.
- **'P'**: Low-frequency component (200Hz "thump") combined with a broad but muted noise burst. The "lip pop" effect.

## 3. Implementation Parameters
| Consonant | Filter Type | Frequency Range | Gain (Rel) | Duration |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|------------|----------|
| S         | High-Pass   | >5000Hz         | 0.4        | 150ms    |
| SH        | Band-Pass   | 2500 - 4500Hz   | 0.5        | 150ms    |
| T         | High-Pass   | >3000Hz         | 0.6        | 40ms     |
| P         | Low-Pass    | <200Hz + Noise  | 0.7        | 50ms     |

## Findings - 2026-06-05
- 'S' synthesis via High-Pass (10th order) @ 5kHz provides sharp, clean sibilance.
- 'SH' requires lower peak (Band-Pass 2.5k-4.5k) to differentiate from 'S'.
- CV transition (S -> A) requires volume balancing; sibilants are perceived louder than periodic signals of same amplitude. Reduced 'S' gain to 0.4.
- Sequential concatenation of noise followed by resonance-filtered pulse wave successfully creates "SA" phoneme.

  - i — bVII — IV — i (Dm — C — G — Dm)

### 3) Laouto/lyra accompaniment voicings & patterns
- Tuning/common approach: laouto often tuned A D A D (or variations). Use open fifths, double-stop octaves, and simple chordal strums.
- Basic laouto voicings (root-position triads; play as dyads/octaves for traditional sound):
  - Dm: D–A (open 5th), add F on higher string for fullness
  - G: G–D (open 5th), add B if available
  - Am: A–E (open 5th), add C for color
  - F: F–C (open 5th)
  - C: C–G (open 5th)

- Two typical patterns:
  1) Alternating drone + chord (common):
     - Pulse: | D(drone) — D–A strum — D(drone) — D–A strum |
     - On G change the drone to G, alternate G–D strum.
  2) Ostinato arpeggiated pattern (slow dances / verses):
     - Play root on beat 1 (low), fifth on beat 2 (higher), root on 3, third on 4 — repeat.
     - Example over Dm: low D — A — D — F (keeps modal color)

- Lyra (melodic instrument) approach:
  - Play the melody with ornamentation (slides, mordents, short grace notes) and let laouto hold the drone/ostinato.
  - Use short bowed double-stops (drone on D plus melody note) to mimic vocal timbre.

---
### 4) Arrangement tips & ornamentation
- Emphasize the raised 6th (B) in melodic lines to signal Dorian; use it as a passing tone and in leaps into melodic peaks.
- Keep chord changes sparse on fast dances — use the i—IV vamp and let soloists improvise.
- Use short mordents, slides of a semitone or whole tone, and accented grace notes on strong beats typical to Cretan playing.
- For modern flavor, add Dm7 (D F A C) or Am7 (A C E G) and tasteful 7th chords on bVII (C7) to link sections.
